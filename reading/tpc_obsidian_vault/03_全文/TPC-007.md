## Abstract

For a cutoff $D$, write the von Mangoldt function as the exact sum of its short source-divisor part and its complementary defect, $$a_D(n)=-\sum_{\substack{d\mid n\\d\le D}}\mu(d)\log d,  \qquad  r_D(n)=\Lambda(n)-a_D(n).$$ The preceding paper in this series isolated $$\mathcal T_{h,>D}(X)=\sum_{X<n\le2X}r_D(n)\Lambda(n+h)$$ as the complete large-divisor remainder left after a Bombieri–Vinogradov evaluation of the prime-pair correlation. For a fixed nonzero even shift, estimating this remainder by $o(X)$ is equivalent to the expected Hardy–Littlewood asymptotic and is therefore open.

Here the quantifier on the shift is changed and nothing else is removed from the defect. We first prove, uniformly for $1\le |h|\le 2X^{1-\varepsilon}$, that the short-divisor correlation with $D=X^{1/2}(\log X)^{-B}$ equals $\mathfrak S(h)X+O_A(X(\log X)^{-A})$. Combining this uniform transfer with the averaged Hardy–Littlewood theorem of Matomäki, Radziwiłł, and Tao shows that, whenever $$X^{8/33+\varepsilon}\le H\le X^{1-\varepsilon},$$ the full defect satisfies $$\mathcal T_{h,>D}(X)=O_{A,\varepsilon}(X(\log X)^{-A})$$ for all but $O_{A,\varepsilon}(H(\log X)^{-A})$ shifts in every permitted window of length $2H$. The deep almost-all prime-pair theorem is an external input; the contribution here is the uniform defect transfer and its exact interpretation. Fixed $h$, in particular $h=2$, is not controlled.

We place the result in additive Fourier coordinates: the defect correlations are matrix coefficients of the integer translation representation and their global square sum is an exact weighted Fourier energy. This is a spectral identity, not a new dispersion estimate. Finally, if $V_q$ denotes the space of $q$-periodic functions on $(N,2N]$, we prove uniformly for $q\le N^{1-\delta}$ that $$\|P_{V_q}(\Lambda-1)\|_2^2  \ll_\delta N\frac q{\varphi(q)}  \ll_\delta N\log\log(3q),$$ whereas $\|\Lambda-1\|_2^2\sim N\log N$. Thus sublinear finite-conductor models capture a vanishing proportion of the prime fluctuation’s linear $L^2$ energy. This is a periodic-approximation boundary, not an impossibility theorem for nonlocal sieve or Type II methods. No fixed-shift prime-pair lower bound is proved.

The Hardy–Littlewood prediction is $$\label{eq:intro-hl-prediction}  \mathcal C_h(X)\sim\mathfrak S(h)X$$ for each fixed even $h$, where $$\label{eq:intro-singular-series}  \mathfrak S(h)=  \begin{cases}  \displaystyle  2\prod_{p>2}\left(1-\frac1{(p-1)^2}\right)  \prod_{\substack{p\mid h\\p>2}}\frac{p-1}{p-2},&2\mid h,\\[1.3ex]  0,&2\nmid h.  \end{cases}$$ Even an $o(X)$ error in [\[eq:intro-hl-prediction\]](TPC-007.md#ref-3ce664d2d6f15b41) is not known for any single nonzero even shift; the case $h=2$ would imply the twin-prime conjecture. This paper does not alter that fixed-shift fact.

The elementary identity $$\label{eq:intro-mobius-log}  \Lambda(n)=-\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)\log d$$ gives, for every $1\le D\le2X$, the exact decomposition $$\begin{aligned}  a_D(n)&=-\sum_{\substack{d\mid n\\d\le D}}\mu(d)\log d,  \label{eq:intro-short-weight}\\  r_D(n)&=\Lambda(n)-a_D(n)        =-\sum_{\substack{d\mid n\\d>D}}\mu(d)\log d,  \label{eq:intro-defect-weight}\end{aligned}$$ and hence $$\label{eq:intro-exact-split}  \mathcal C_h(X)=\mathcal C_{h,\le D}(X)+\mathcal T_{h,>D}(X),$$ where $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal C_{h,\le D}(X)  &=\sum_{X<n\le2X}a_D(n)\Lambda(n+h),  \label{eq:intro-short-correlation}\\  \mathcal T_{h,>D}(X)  &=\sum_{X<n\le2X}r_D(n)\Lambda(n+h).  \label{eq:intro-large-defect}\end{aligned}$$ The terminology *source divisor* refers to a divisor introduced by expanding the first von Mangoldt factor in [\[eq:intro-mobius-log\]](TPC-007.md#ref-b2d75f20cdb90d04).

TPC-6 proved for each fixed nonzero even $h$ that classical Bombieri–Vinogradov theory evaluates [\[eq:intro-short-correlation\]](TPC-007.md#ref-548d5114ecc82862) at $D=X^{1/2}(\log X)^{-B}$, and consequently that [\[eq:intro-hl-prediction\]](TPC-007.md#ref-3ce664d2d6f15b41) is equivalent to $\mathcal T_{h,>D}(X)=o(X)$ [`\citep{WangTPCSix2026}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-007.md#cite-fa35c8724b85bf3a). That equivalence named the missing object but supplied no estimate for it. The present paper retains the same complete remainder and changes the topology in the shift variable: pointwise control is replaced by control outside a set of asymptotic density zero in each sufficiently long shift window.

## Main contributions and provenance

The logical inputs and outputs are separated as follows.

1.  We prove a singular-series coefficient estimate uniform for $1\le|h|\le z^K$. Together with the maximal Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem, it yields $$\mathcal C_{h,\le D}(X)   =\mathfrak S(h)X+O_A(X(\log X)^{-A})$$ simultaneously for all $1\le|h|\le2X^{1-\varepsilon}$. The analytic ingredients are classical; the point is the uniformity needed for the growing shift window.

2.  We use the averaged Hardy–Littlewood theorem of [`\citet[Theorem~1.3(i)]{MatomakiRadziwillTao2019}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-007.md#cite-03024995d598450c). It gives the same main term for the full correlation outside $O_{A,\varepsilon}(H(\log X)^{-A})$ shifts when $H\ge X^{8/33+\varepsilon}$. Subtraction proves that the *same full defect* from TPC-6 is small at almost all such shifts. The exponent $8/33$ and the deep prime-correlation estimate are entirely due to those authors; we do not claim a new circle-method, dispersion, or Type II estimate.

3.  We record the exact Fourier representation of the defect and its Plancherel energy. This gives a rigorous translation-dynamical interface, but the identity alone contains no cancellation estimate.

4.  We prove a finite-conductor energy-escape theorem. Periodic functions of every modulus $q\le N^{1-\delta}$ capture only $O_\delta(\log\log(3q)/\log N)$ of the $L^2$ energy of $\Lambda-1$. This quantifies a linear periodic-approximation boundary and does not extend to arbitrary nonlinear or nonlocal information.

Earlier almost-all-shift prime-pair results culminated, for the present purpose, in the theorem of Matomäki, Radziwiłł, and Tao, which improved the shift-window threshold from $X^{1/3+\varepsilon}$ to $X^{8/33+\varepsilon}$ [`\citep{Mikawa1991,MatomakiRadziwillTao2019}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-007.md#cite-8351006b93f422ec); [\[MatomakiRadziwillTao2019\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-007.md#cite-03024995d598450c). Our result is not a further improvement of that threshold. It transfers their theorem to the exact large-source-divisor coordinate singled out by the preceding operator and sieve analysis.

## What is not claimed

For clarity, none of the following is asserted.

1.  The exceptional set is not shown to avoid any prescribed shift. In particular, it may contain $h=2$ for every $X$.

2.  No individual dyadic layer of [\[eq:intro-defect-weight\]](TPC-007.md#ref-42fa82b8aa80f943) is shown to be small. Cancellation may occur between different divisor scales.

3.  The Fourier identity is not a spectral gap, an operator isomorphism, or an estimate for a fixed Fourier coefficient.

4.  The finite-conductor theorem concerns orthogonal approximation by a single periodic linear space. It is not a universal parity-barrier theorem and does not rule out growing bilinear, dispersion, automorphic, or other nonlocal methods.

5.  No lower bound for twin primes, no fixed-shift asymptotic, and no new level of distribution for the primes is proved.

# A uniform small-divisor transfer theorem

The fixed-shift coefficient calculation in TPC-6 is not by itself sufficient for a window in which $h$ grows with $X$: its displayed error constant was allowed to depend on $h$. We first remove that dependence over every polynomial range. Uniform truncated singular-series estimates of this type also occur in the divisor-sum correlation work of [`\citet[Lemma~2.1]{GoldstonYildirim2003}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-007.md#cite-d35d90466075cefc); a coefficient-level proof adapted to the present weight is included for completeness.

> **Proof** Set $$a_h(d)=\mathbf 1_{(d,h)=1}\frac{\mu(d)}{\varphi(d)}$$ and, initially for $\Re s>0$, write $$\label{eq:Fh-factorization}  F_h(s)=\sum_{d\ge1}\frac{a_h(d)}{d^s}  =\frac{G_h(s)}{\zeta(1+s)}.$$ The Euler factors of $G_h$ are $$\label{eq:Gh-local-factors}  G_{h,p}(s)=  \begin{cases}  \displaystyle  \frac{1-p^{-s}/(p-1)}{1-p^{-1-s}},&p\nmid h,\\[1.2ex]  (1-p^{-1-s})^{-1},&p\mid h.  \end{cases}$$ Let $g_h$ be the multiplicative function whose Dirichlet series is $G_h$. Expanding [\[eq:Gh-local-factors\]](TPC-007.md#ref-bb918093461bc168) coefficient by coefficient gives, for $\nu\ge1$, $$\label{eq:gh-prime-powers}  g_h(p^\nu)=  \begin{cases}  p^{-\nu},&p\mid h,\\[0.6ex]  \displaystyle-\frac1{p^\nu(p-1)},&p\nmid h.  \end{cases}$$ Thus, in the half-plane of absolute convergence and hence as a coefficient identity, $$\label{eq:uniform-coefficient-convolution}  a_h(n)=\sum_{rk=n}g_h(r)\frac{\mu(k)}k.$$
>
> We need two uniform bounds for $g_h$. The absolute Euler product at $s=0$ and its first logarithmic derivative give $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_{r\ge1}|g_h(r)|  &\ll \log\log(3|h|),  \label{eq:gh-zero-moment}\\  \sum_{r\ge1}|g_h(r)|(1+\log r)  &\ll \bigl(1+\log\log(3|h|)\bigr)^2.  \label{eq:gh-first-log-moment}\end{aligned}$$ Indeed, the factors with $p\nmid h$ form an absolutely convergent product, while those with $p\mid h$ contribute at most $\prod_{p\mid h}(1-p^{-1})^{-1}\ll\log\log(3|h|)$ and $\sum_{p\mid h}\log p/(p-1)\ll1+\log\log(3|h|)$.
>
> For the tail we use a fixed Rankin exponent. If $0<\delta<1/2$, then $$\label{eq:gh-rankin-product}  \sum_{r\ge1}|g_h(r)|r^\delta  \ll_\delta  \exp\!\left(C_\delta(1+(\log(3|h|))^\delta)\right).$$ For $p\nmid h$ the relevant local product converges because its nontrivial part is $O_\delta(p^{-2+\delta})$. For $p\mid h$ it is $(1-p^{-1+\delta})^{-1}$. The standard prime-rearrangement bound $\sum_{p\mid h}p^{-1+\delta}\ll_\delta 1+(\log(3|h|))^\delta$ proves [\[eq:gh-rankin-product\]](TPC-007.md#ref-27336da0beda901e). We emphasize that no assertion about arbitrary high logarithmic moments is needed here.
>
> Put $$U(y)=\sum_{k\le y}\frac{\mu(k)}k,  \qquad  V(y)=\sum_{k\le y}\frac{\mu(k)\log k}{k}.$$ The classical zero-free-region form of the prime number theorem, followed by partial summation, gives $$\label{eq:UV-uniform-pnt}  U(y)\ll \mathrm e^{-c\sqrt{\log y}},  \qquad  V(y)=-1+O\bigl(\mathrm e^{-c\sqrt{\log y}}\bigr)  \quad(y\ge3),$$ with a possible change in the positive absolute constant $c$. Using [\[eq:uniform-coefficient-convolution\]](TPC-007.md#ref-ae590f538df9debc), the left side of [\[eq:uniform-singular-coefficient\]](TPC-007.md#ref-2c02a17fdf1c5518) equals $$\label{eq:uniform-coefficient-r-sum}  \sum_{r\le z}g_h(r)  \left\{-\log r\,U(z/r)-V(z/r)\right\}.$$
>
> Split at $r=z^{1/2}$. On the lower range, [\[eq:UV-uniform-pnt\]](TPC-007.md#ref-1e1b3952b5262b54) and [\[eq:gh-first-log-moment\]](TPC-007.md#ref-3611804c8eff58b5) replace the quantity in braces by $1$ with error $O_{A,K}((\log z)^{-A})$. On the upper range, the elementary bound $$|\log r\,U(z/r)+V(z/r)|\ll(\log(2z))^2$$ is sufficient. From [\[eq:gh-rankin-product\]](TPC-007.md#ref-27336da0beda901e), the assumptions $|h|\le z^K$ and $0<\delta<1/2$ give $$\sum_{r>z^{1/2}}|g_h(r)|  \ll_{\delta,K}z^{-\delta/2}  \exp\bigl(O_{\delta,K}((\log z)^\delta)\bigr)  \ll_{\delta,K}z^{-\delta/3}$$ for sufficiently large $z$. The same estimate replaces $\sum_{r\le z^{1/2}}g_h(r)$ by $G_h(0)$. Consequently [\[eq:uniform-coefficient-r-sum\]](TPC-007.md#ref-b9201aec7c101598) is $G_h(0)+O_{A,K}((\log z)^{-A})$.
>
> Finally, evaluation of [\[eq:Gh-local-factors\]](TPC-007.md#ref-bb918093461bc168) at zero gives $$\label{eq:Gh-zero-singular-series}  G_h(0)=  \prod_{p\mid h}\frac p{p-1}  \prod_{p\nmid h}\left(1-\frac1{(p-1)^2}\right)  =\mathfrak S(h).$$ If $h$ is odd, the second product has a zero factor at $p=2$; if $h$ is even, separating the factor at $2$ gives [\[eq:intro-singular-series\]](TPC-007.md#ref-573cbf68019a0882). This proves the lemma.

> **Remark: Why the uniform proof is separate** The fixed-$h$ estimate with an $O_{A,h}$ constant does not imply [\[lem:uniform-singular-coefficient\]](TPC-007.md#ref-98eb1b0d76265f7e) when $h$ grows. The Rankin tail in the proof is what absorbs the mild growth of the Euler factors belonging to primes dividing $h$.

## Uniform evaluation of the short source-divisor part

For real $y\ge0$ and a reduced residue class $a\pmod q$, put $$\label{eq:AP-error-definition}  E(y;q,a)=  \sum_{\substack{n\le y\\n\equiv a\, (\mathrm{mod}\,q)}}\Lambda(n)  -\frac y{\varphi(q)}.$$

> **Lemma: Uniform non-coprime contribution** \[lem:uniform-noncoprime\] Fix $0<\varepsilon<1/2$. Uniformly for $1\le|h|\le2X^{1-\varepsilon}$ and $1\le D\le2X$, $$\label{eq:uniform-noncoprime-bound}  \sum_{\substack{d\le D\\(d,h)>1}}  |\mu(d)|\log(2d)  \sum_{X<dm\le2X}\Lambda(dm+h)  \ll_{\varepsilon,\eta}X^\eta$$ for every $\eta>0$, provided $X$ is sufficiently large.

> **Proof** Write $n=dm$. If $(d,h)>1$, choose a prime $p\mid(d,h)$. Then $p\mid n+h$. The condition $\Lambda(n+h)\ne0$ forces $n+h=p^j$. For sufficiently large $X$, the target interval lies in $(X/2,3X]$. Consequently only $O_\varepsilon(1)$ powers $p^j$ lie in $(X+h,2X+h]$ for each $p\mid h$. For each resulting $n$, $$\sum_{d\mid n}|\mu(d)|\log(2d)  \ll\tau(n)\log(2n)\ll_\eta X^\eta.$$ The number of prime divisors of $h$ and the von Mangoldt weight cost only powers of $\log X$, which are absorbed after decreasing $\eta$.

> **Theorem: Uniform sharp small-divisor transfer** \[thm:uniform-small-divisor-transfer\] Fix $A>0$ and $0<\varepsilon<1/2$. There is $B=B(A,\varepsilon)>0$ such that, with $$\label{eq:theoretical-D-cutoff}  D=\left\lfloor\frac{X^{1/2}}{(\log X)^B}\right\rfloor,$$ one has $$\label{eq:uniform-short-main}  \mathcal C_{h,\le D}(X)  =\mathfrak S(h)X  +O_{A,\varepsilon}\left(\frac{X}{(\log X)^A}\right)$$ uniformly for all integers $$\label{eq:uniform-h-range}  1\le|h|\le2X^{1-\varepsilon}.$$

###### ref-7279c3a6b5a536b8

###### ref-e41f044d857059d3

###### ref-7820b3e7b6bfcee4

> **Proof** Expanding $a_D$ and writing $n=dm$ gives $$\label{eq:sharp-small-expanded}  \mathcal C_{h,\le D}(X)  =-\sum_{d\le D}\mu(d)\log d    \sum_{X<dm\le2X}\Lambda(dm+h).$$ The terms with $(d,h)>1$ contribute $O_{A,\varepsilon}(X(\log X)^{-A})$ by [\[lem:uniform-noncoprime\]](TPC-007.md#ref-bf4094ef1e0cee7c), after choosing a fixed small $\eta<1$.
>
> If $(d,h)=1$, the inner sum is $$\label{eq:sharp-AP-row}  \frac X{\varphi(d)}  +E(2X+h;d,h)-E(X+h;d,h).$$ The maximal Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem states, after taking $B$ large enough in terms of the requested logarithmic saving, that $$\label{eq:maximal-BV-used}  \sum_{d\le D}  \max_{(a,d)=1}\max_{y\le3X}  |E(y;d,a)|  \ll_{A,\varepsilon}\frac{X}{(\log X)^{A+2}}.$$ The range [\[eq:uniform-h-range\]](TPC-007.md#ref-594ef54c61372671) ensures that both endpoints in [\[eq:sharp-AP-row\]](TPC-007.md#ref-e41f044d857059d3) lie below $3X$ for sufficiently large $X$. Multiplication by $\log d$ costs at most one logarithm, so the total progression error in [\[eq:sharp-small-expanded\]](TPC-007.md#ref-7279c3a6b5a536b8) is $O_{A,\varepsilon}(X(\log X)^{-A-1})$.
>
> The continuous main term is exactly $$-X\sum_{\substack{d\le D\\(d,h)=1}}  \frac{\mu(d)\log d}{\varphi(d)}.$$ Because $D=X^{1/2+o(1)}$ and $|h|\le2X^{1-\varepsilon}\le D^3$ for all sufficiently large $X$, [\[lem:uniform-singular-coefficient\]](TPC-007.md#ref-98eb1b0d76265f7e) with $K=3$ evaluates it as $\mathfrak S(h)X+O_{A,\varepsilon}(X(\log X)^{-A})$. Combining the three parts proves the theorem.

> **Remark: Classical analytic input** The estimate [\[eq:maximal-BV-used\]](TPC-007.md#ref-7820b3e7b6bfcee4) is the classical maximal Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem [`\citep{Bombieri1965,Vinogradov1965, IwaniecKowalski2004}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-007.md#cite-072be3f1fcf63a7d); [\[Vinogradov1965\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-007.md#cite-91320d5c0c0e4130); [\[IwaniecKowalski2004\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-007.md#cite-49dfea87eaada156). is a uniform application of that theorem, not a new distribution result for primes.

###### ref-5d7e78d1d586a481

> **Theorem: Complete-defect closure at almost all shifts** \[thm:complete-defect-almost-all\] Fix $A>0$ and $0<\varepsilon<1/2$. There exists $B=B(A,\varepsilon)>0$ such that, for $$\label{eq:closure-D}  D=\left\lfloor\frac{X^{1/2}}{(\log X)^B}\right\rfloor,$$ and every $(H,h_0)$ in the range [\[eq:MRT-H-range\]](TPC-007.md#ref-f130e8ab2d005962), one has $$\label{eq:complete-defect-small}  \mathcal T_{h,>D}(X)  =O_{A,\varepsilon}\left(\frac{X}{(\log X)^A}\right)$$ for all but $$\label{eq:complete-defect-exceptions}  O_{A,\varepsilon}\left(\frac{H}{(\log X)^A}\right)$$ nonzero integers $h$ with $|h-h_0|\le H$.

###### ref-cda38cb7e1bc8b3a

> **Proof** Every shift in the stated window satisfies $$|h|\le h_0+H\le2X^{1-\varepsilon}.$$ Choose the logarithmic saving in [\[thm:uniform-small-divisor-transfer\]](TPC-007.md#ref-d8052ed31f323bc3) slightly larger than $A$. Uniformly for every nonzero $h$ in the window, it gives $$\label{eq:short-part-window}  \mathcal C_{h,\le D}(X)  =\mathfrak S(h)X  +O_{A,\varepsilon}\left(\frac{X}{(\log X)^A}\right).$$ Outside the exceptional set in [\[thm:MRT-averaged-HL\]](TPC-007.md#ref-6315c6618fba04c6), the full correlation satisfies the same formula. Subtract [\[eq:short-part-window\]](TPC-007.md#ref-cda38cb7e1bc8b3a) from [\[eq:MRT-pair-asymptotic\]](TPC-007.md#ref-48a38e781e641395) and use the exact identity [\[eq:intro-exact-split\]](TPC-007.md#ref-b26c85a9b8f7b241).

> **Remark: What is genuinely controlled** estimates the entire sum over all source divisors $d>D$. It is not a surrogate tail, a truncated Type II packet, or a statement in which an extra coefficient has been removed. On the other hand, the proof does not estimate any prescribed divisor layer separately; different layers may cancel in the complete sum.

> **Remark: Provenance and priority** The prime-pair asymptotic and the exponent $8/33$ in [\[thm:MRT-averaged-HL\]](TPC-007.md#ref-6315c6618fba04c6) are not new. Earlier work treated longer shift windows, and [`\citet{MatomakiRadziwillTao2019}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-007.md#cite-03024995d598450c) obtained the quoted threshold. Large-/small-divisor decompositions in parity problems also have important precedents, including [`\citet{MurtyVatwani2017}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-007.md#cite-4d26dbf0bf7f180e). The theorem here is a transfer statement for the exact TPC-6 defect coordinate, supported by the uniform small-divisor theorem of [2](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-007.md#src-172a40103eeb86ae); it is not presented as a new almost-all prime-pair theorem.

## Density and fixed-$L^p$ formulations

###### ref-2eec9a2a32d2cea6

Let $$\label{eq:shift-window-set}  \mathcal H(X;H,h_0)=  \{h\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}:|h-h_0|\le H\}.$$

###### ref-32df80bf28106b1a

###### ref-6d2a79da738f665f

> **Corollary: Shift-density convergence** \[cor:shift-density-convergence\] Fix $A>0$ and $0<\varepsilon<1/2$. There is $B=B(A,\varepsilon)>0$ such that, with $D$ as in [\[eq:closure-D\]](TPC-007.md#ref-13a350229a1df091), for every $(H,h_0)$ in [\[eq:MRT-H-range\]](TPC-007.md#ref-f130e8ab2d005962), $$\label{eq:density-tail-bound}  \frac1H\#\left\{h\in\mathcal H(X;H,h_0):  \frac{|\mathcal T_{h,>D}(X)|}{X}>(\log X)^{-A}\right\}  \ll_{A,\varepsilon}(\log X)^{-A}.$$

> **Proof** Apply [\[thm:complete-defect-almost-all\]](TPC-007.md#ref-d04643fc2ffa5e13) with requested saving $A+2$. For sufficiently large $X$, its implicit constant times $(\log X)^{-A-2}$ is at most $(\log X)^{-A}$. Every shift above the displayed threshold therefore belongs to that theorem’s exceptional set, whose relative size is $O_{A,\varepsilon}((\log X)^{-A-2})$. This proves the stated, weaker bound.

###### ref-c1aef0306f6d3f8d

###### ref-aed5f1be003abde3

> **Corollary: Fixed-$L^p$ shift closure** \[cor:fixed-Lp-closure\] Fix $p\ge1$, $A>0$, and $0<\varepsilon<1/2$. There is $B=B(A,p,\varepsilon)>0$ such that, with $D$ as in [\[eq:closure-D\]](TPC-007.md#ref-13a350229a1df091), for every $(H,h_0)$ in [\[eq:MRT-H-range\]](TPC-007.md#ref-f130e8ab2d005962), $$\label{eq:fixed-Lp-closure}  \left(   \frac1{\#\mathcal H(X;H,h_0)}   \sum_{h\in\mathcal H(X;H,h_0)}   \left|\frac{\mathcal T_{h,>D}(X)}X\right|^p  \right)^{1/p}  \ll_{A,p,\varepsilon}(\log X)^{-A}.$$

###### ref-99a8db49be47dd73

> **Proof** The elementary pointwise bounds $$\begin{aligned}  |\mathcal C_h(X)|&\ll X(\log(3X))^2,\\  |\mathcal C_{h,\le D}(X)|  &\le \log(3X)  \sum_{d\le D}\log(2d)\left(\frac Xd+1\right)  \ll X(\log(3X))^3\end{aligned}$$ give $$\label{eq:defect-crude-pointwise}  |\mathcal T_{h,>D}(X)|\ll X(\log(3X))^3.$$ Choose a fixed auxiliary exponent $R>p(A+3)+1$ and apply [\[thm:complete-defect-almost-all\]](TPC-007.md#ref-d04643fc2ffa5e13) with requested saving $R$. On the regular shifts use that theorem, and on its $O(H(\log X)^{-R})$ exceptional set use [\[eq:defect-crude-pointwise\]](TPC-007.md#ref-99a8db49be47dd73). Since $$\frac1{\#\mathcal H(X;H,h_0)}  \sum_{h\in\mathcal H(X;H,h_0)}  \left|\frac{\mathcal T_{h,>D}(X)}X\right|^p  \ll_{R,p,\varepsilon}(\log X)^{-Rp}+(\log X)^{3p-R},$$ taking the $p$th root and absorbing the fixed constants proves [\[eq:fixed-Lp-closure\]](TPC-007.md#ref-aed5f1be003abde3).

The freedom to request arbitrary logarithmic saving in [\[thm:MRT-averaged-HL\]](TPC-007.md#ref-6315c6618fba04c6) is what allows every fixed $p$. No claim is made for $p$ growing with $X$ or for the supremum norm.

# Translation dynamics and additive spectral coordinates

The shift variable has an exact and elementary dynamical realization. This section records it in order to distinguish the resulting identity from the analytic estimate imported in [\[thm:MRT-averaged-HL\]](TPC-007.md#ref-6315c6618fba04c6).

###### ref-a02f8d2e80e72fe8

Put $\mathrm e(x)=\exp(2\pi i x)$. For a finitely supported sequence $f:\mathbb Z\to\mathbb C$, define $$\label{eq:additive-Fourier-transform}  \widehat f(\theta)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}f(n)\mathrm e(n\theta),  \qquad \theta\in\mathbb T=\mathbb R/\mathbb Z.$$

###### ref-d615be07a0d359b3

###### ref-35438a3648fc4252

###### ref-88f8307af263cede

###### ref-a38772fd3ea2fb9c

###### ref-782c743f4f972df4

> **Proposition: Exact Fourier representation of correlations** \[prop:exact-correlation-spectrum\] Let $f,g:\mathbb Z\to\mathbb R$ be finitely supported and set $$\label{eq:generic-shift-correlation}  C_h(f,g)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}f(n)g(n+h).$$ Then $$\begin{aligned}  C_h(f,g)  &=\int_{\mathbb T}    \overline{\widehat f(\theta)}\widehat g(\theta)    \mathrm e(-h\theta)\,d\theta,  \label{eq:correlation-Fourier-coefficient}\\  \sum_{h\in\mathbb Z}|C_h(f,g)|^2  &=\int_{\mathbb T}|\widehat f(\theta)|^2                   |\widehat g(\theta)|^2\,d\theta.  \label{eq:correlation-Plancherel}\end{aligned}$$ Equivalently, if $U^h g(n)=g(n+h)$ is the unitary integer-translation representation on $\ell^2(\mathbb Z)$, then $$\label{eq:translation-matrix-coefficient}  C_h(f,g)=\langle f,U^h g\rangle_{\ell^2(\mathbb Z)}.$$

> **Proof** Expand the integrand in [\[eq:correlation-Fourier-coefficient\]](TPC-007.md#ref-88f8307af263cede). Fourier orthogonality retains exactly the pairs $t=n+h$. Formula [\[eq:correlation-Plancherel\]](TPC-007.md#ref-a38772fd3ea2fb9c) is Parseval applied to the Fourier coefficients in [\[eq:correlation-Fourier-coefficient\]](TPC-007.md#ref-88f8307af263cede), and [\[eq:translation-matrix-coefficient\]](TPC-007.md#ref-782c743f4f972df4) is the definition of $U^h$.

###### ref-394697b4e23fb5df

###### ref-e9bae404bb06e5ad

###### ref-866f878452fb9b2c

###### ref-4add13e2c9c758fc

###### ref-2a795b7e7e7ab327

For the defect application, let $Y=2X^{1-\varepsilon}$ and define $$\begin{aligned}  f_{X,D}(n)&=r_D(n)\mathbf 1_{X<n\le2X},  \label{eq:defect-Fourier-sequence}\\  g_{X,Y}(t)&=\Lambda(t)\mathbf 1_{X-Y<t\le2X+Y}.  \label{eq:prime-Fourier-sequence}\end{aligned}$$ For every $|h|\le Y$, $$\label{eq:defect-as-matrix-coefficient}  \mathcal T_{h,>D}(X)  =C_h(f_{X,D},g_{X,Y})  =\langle f_{X,D},U^h g_{X,Y}\rangle.$$ Thus the complete defect is an inverse Fourier coefficient of $$\label{eq:defect-spectral-product}  \overline{\widehat f_{X,D}(\theta)}  \widehat g_{X,Y}(\theta).$$ The global energy identity is $$\label{eq:defect-global-Fourier-energy}  \sum_{h\in\mathbb Z}|C_h(f_{X,D},g_{X,Y})|^2  =\int_{\mathbb T}|\widehat f_{X,D}(\theta)|^2              |\widehat g_{X,Y}(\theta)|^2\,d\theta.$$

> **Remark: Identity versus estimate** Neither [\[eq:defect-spectral-product\]](TPC-007.md#ref-4add13e2c9c758fc) nor [\[eq:defect-global-Fourier-energy\]](TPC-007.md#ref-2a795b7e7e7ab327) shows that a coefficient is small. The analytic content in [\[thm:complete-defect-almost-all\]](TPC-007.md#ref-d04643fc2ffa5e13) comes from Bombieri–Vinogradov for the short divisor coordinate and from the theorem of Matomäki, Radziwiłł, and Tao for the full correlation. Parseval does not replace those inputs.

> **Remark: A triangular family, not a fixed mixing system** Both vectors in [\[eq:defect-as-matrix-coefficient\]](TPC-007.md#ref-866f878452fb9b2c), the cutoff $D$, and the window grow with $X$. Therefore [\[cor:fixed-Lp-closure\]](TPC-007.md#ref-c1aef0306f6d3f8d) is a statement about a triangular family of translation matrix coefficients. It is not a mixing theorem for one fixed pair of vectors, a spectral gap for $U$, or a spectral isomorphism with a logistic map.

## Why density convergence does not select a fixed shift

The topology in [\[cor:fixed-Lp-closure\]](TPC-007.md#ref-c1aef0306f6d3f8d) deliberately forgets a set of vanishing relative size. Evaluation at one coordinate is not uniformly continuous in that topology.

###### ref-c43f965a31e2e33a

###### ref-2bc3f2d91aab25be

###### ref-c00c3db069382f27

> **Proposition: Fixed-coordinate spike** \[prop:fixed-coordinate-spike\] Let $\mathcal H_X\subset\mathbb Z$ be finite sets with $|\mathcal H_X|\to\infty$, all containing a fixed integer $h_\ast$. Define $$\label{eq:spike-sequence}  s_X(h)=\mathbf 1_{h=h_\ast}.$$ Then, for every fixed $1\le p<\infty$, $$\label{eq:spike-Lp-vanishing}  \left(\frac1{|\mathcal H_X|}\sum_{h\in\mathcal H_X}|s_X(h)|^p\right)^{1/p}  =|\mathcal H_X|^{-1/p}\longrightarrow0,$$ while $s_X(h_\ast)=1$ for every $X$.

Consequently, no abstract passage from [\[eq:fixed-Lp-closure\]](TPC-007.md#ref-aed5f1be003abde3) to a bound at $h=2$ is possible. In fact the allowance $O(H(\log X)^{-A})$ in [\[eq:complete-defect-exceptions\]](TPC-007.md#ref-5d7e78d1d586a481) grows without bound in the permitted power-sized windows, so the exceptional set may contain any preassigned finite collection of shifts at every scale.

# Finite-conductor energy escape and a layerwise boundary

The almost-all-shift theorem uses genuinely nonlocal information about prime correlations. We next quantify why simply fitting the prime fluctuation by a periodic observable does not recover comparable $L^2$ energy.

## Orthogonal projection onto periodic observables

###### ref-d05b6be8b28a55b3

Let $$\label{eq:dyadic-integer-interval}  I_N=\{n\in\mathbb Z:N<n\le2N\}$$ and equip $\ell^2(I_N)$ with counting measure. Let $V_q(N)$ be the subspace of restrictions to $I_N$ of $q$-periodic complex functions, and let $P_q$ be the orthogonal projection onto $V_q(N)$.

###### ref-a8e809b772faea80

###### ref-2f18bb75d932970d

###### ref-cafcc2d28eeff24c

###### ref-df6f3eab260aefa8

###### ref-a1fac4406e89ca0f

###### ref-b35d2d8ca5ef9a2f

> **Theorem: Finite-conductor energy escape** \[thm:finite-conductor-energy-escape\] Fix $0<\delta<1$. Uniformly for integers $$\label{eq:energy-q-range}  1\le q\le N^{1-\delta},$$ one has $$\label{eq:periodic-projection-bound}  \|P_q(\Lambda-1)\|_{\ell^2(I_N)}^2  \ll_\delta N\frac q{\varphi(q)}  \ll_\delta N\log\log(3q).$$ On the other hand, $$\label{eq:prime-fluctuation-energy}  \|\Lambda-1\|_{\ell^2(I_N)}^2\sim N\log N.$$ Consequently, uniformly in [\[eq:energy-q-range\]](TPC-007.md#ref-2f18bb75d932970d), $$\begin{aligned}  \frac{\|P_q(\Lambda-1)\|_2^2}{\|\Lambda-1\|_2^2}  &\ll_\delta\frac{\log\log(3q)}{\log N}=o_\delta(1),  \label{eq:captured-energy-ratio}\\  \inf_{b\in V_q(N)}\|\Lambda-1-b\|_2^2  &=(1-o_\delta(1))N\log N.  \label{eq:periodic-best-approximation}\end{aligned}$$

###### ref-92f8fd5fe937ac0b

###### ref-8ec3b550f1c24b35

###### ref-6275d6d8cc5a403e

###### ref-54b7f1a1a4b13b5d

###### ref-9c13e5c7085d8325

> **Proof** For $a\pmod q$, let $$\label{eq:residue-class-count}  N_a=\#\{n\in I_N:n\equiv a\pmod q\}.$$ The range [\[eq:energy-q-range\]](TPC-007.md#ref-2f18bb75d932970d) gives $N_a=N/q+O(1)\asymp_\delta N/q$ uniformly for sufficiently large $N$. For any $f\in\ell^2(I_N)$, the projection is the residue-class average, so $$\label{eq:projection-exact-formula}  \|P_qf\|_2^2  =\sum_{a\, (\mathrm{mod}\,q)}   \frac1{N_a}   \left|\sum_{\substack{n\in I_N\\n\equiv a\, (\mathrm{mod}\,q)}}   f(n)\right|^2.$$
>
> Let $\Lambda_{\mathrm{pr}}(n)=\log n$ when $n$ is prime and zero otherwise. If $(a,q)=1$, the Brun–Titchmarsh inequality and $\log(N/q)\ge\delta\log N$ imply $$\label{eq:BT-weighted-row}  \sum_{\substack{N<p\le2N\\p\equiv a\, (\mathrm{mod}\,q)}}\log p  \ll_\delta\frac N{\varphi(q)}.$$ We use here only the classical Brun–Titchmarsh inequality; the other standard prime-number estimates in this proof may be found, for example, in [`\citet{MontgomeryVaughan2007}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-007.md#cite-3c91c7e57f203d4b). Thus the residue sum of $\Lambda_{\mathrm{pr}}-1$ has absolute value $O_\delta(N/\varphi(q))$. If $(a,q)>1$, no prime $p>N>q$ lies in that class, and the residue sum is simply $-N_a$. Substitution into [\[eq:projection-exact-formula\]](TPC-007.md#ref-8ec3b550f1c24b35) yields $$\label{eq:prime-only-projection}  \|P_q(\Lambda_{\mathrm{pr}}-1)\|_2^2  \ll_\delta N\frac q{\varphi(q)}+N  \ll_\delta N\frac q{\varphi(q)}.$$
>
> The prime-power remainder $R_{\mathrm{pp}}=\Lambda-\Lambda_{\mathrm{pr}}$ satisfies $$\label{eq:prime-power-L2}  \|R_{\mathrm{pp}}\|_2^2  \le\sum_{\substack{N<p^k\le2N\\k\ge2}}(\log p)^2  \ll N^{1/2}(\log N)^2.$$ Orthogonal projection is a contraction. Combining [\[eq:prime-only-projection\]](TPC-007.md#ref-54b7f1a1a4b13b5d) and [\[eq:prime-power-L2\]](TPC-007.md#ref-9c13e5c7085d8325), using $\|u+v\|^2\le2\|u\|^2+2\|v\|^2$, proves the first bound in [\[eq:periodic-projection-bound\]](TPC-007.md#ref-cafcc2d28eeff24c). The standard estimate $q/\varphi(q)\ll\log\log(3q)$ proves the second.
>
> The prime number theorem and partial summation give $$\sum_{n\in I_N}\Lambda(n)\sim N,  \qquad  \sum_{n\in I_N}\Lambda(n)^2\sim N\log N,$$ which proves [\[eq:prime-fluctuation-energy\]](TPC-007.md#ref-df6f3eab260aefa8). Finally, the orthogonal decomposition $$\inf_{b\in V_q(N)}\|\Lambda-1-b\|_2^2  =\|(I-P_q)(\Lambda-1)\|_2^2  =\|\Lambda-1\|_2^2-\|P_q(\Lambda-1)\|_2^2$$ gives [\[eq:captured-energy-ratio\]](TPC-007.md#ref-a1fac4406e89ca0f) and [\[eq:periodic-best-approximation\]](TPC-007.md#ref-b35d2d8ca5ef9a2f).

> **Remark: Exact boundary of the theorem** The space $V_q(N)$ is a linear finite-conductor model. A small projection onto $V_q(N)$ says nothing by itself about bilinear forms, adaptive weights, growing collections of moduli, or nonlinear sieve information. Thus [\[thm:finite-conductor-energy-escape\]](TPC-007.md#ref-a8e809b772faea80) is not a general impossibility theorem for analytic number theory.

> **Remark: Computational boundary** The finite values do not verify the hypothesis of an unproved conjecture, choose the exceptional set in [\[thm:complete-defect-almost-all\]](TPC-007.md#ref-d04643fc2ffa5e13), or provide evidence that $h=2$ is regular. Their role is reproducibility and finite algebraic validation only.

## Conclusion and the remaining route

###### ref-31bf559208acefc0

TPC-6 isolated the full large-source-divisor remainder and proved that its cancellation at a fixed nonzero even shift is exactly the missing prime-pair content. The present paper replaces the fixed-shift quantifier in that ledger by an almost-all-shift quantifier: $$\label{eq:conclusion-ledger} \begin{gathered} \text{uniform Bombieri--Vinogradov}\\[-2pt] \text{short-divisor transfer} \quad+\quad \text{MRT almost-all prime-pair theorem} \\ \Longrightarrow \text{complete large-divisor defect is small at almost all shifts}. \end{gathered}$$ This gives an explicit estimate for that object within the TPC-6 ledger, but only in shift density. The fixed-coordinate spike in [\[prop:fixed-coordinate-spike\]](TPC-007.md#ref-c43f965a31e2e33a) proves the quantifier gap. The periodic energy escape in [\[thm:finite-conductor-energy-escape\]](TPC-007.md#ref-a8e809b772faea80) and the shifted-prime Möbius slice [\[eq:outer-slice-shifted-primes\]](TPC-007.md#ref-31af4e776bb89b76) mark boundaries for two natural strategies that might otherwise be proposed to specialize [\[eq:conclusion-ledger\]](TPC-007.md#ref-31bf559208acefc0) to a prescribed even shift.

One plausible route toward a subsequent fixed-shift advance would seek a coefficient-stable nonlocal estimate. A rigorous but narrower intermediate target is a low-conductor second-coefficient transfer, in which a periodic factor observable is passed through the prime target by Bombieri–Vinogradov theory. Such a theorem can describe exact arithmetic involutions at growing polylogarithmic conductor, but [\[thm:finite-conductor-energy-escape\]](TPC-007.md#ref-a8e809b772faea80) shows why it must not be confused with approximation of the full weight $\Lambda-1$. The broad fixed-shift Type II problem remains open.
