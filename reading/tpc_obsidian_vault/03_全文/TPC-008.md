## Abstract

Let $x=MN$, let $h\ne0$ be fixed, and let $q\geq3$ be odd with $(q,h)=1$. For a bounded $q$-periodic function $\beta$ supported on the unit classes modulo $q$, we study the two-scale prime-target form $$\sum_{M<m\leq2M}\alpha_m  \sum_{n\geq1}\beta(n)\Lambda(mn+h)W(n/N),  \qquad |\alpha_m|\leq1.$$ Allowing an arbitrary bounded second coefficient leads to the unresolved bilinear/Type II interface isolated in the preceding work. In the low-period range $$q\leq(\log x)^C,  \qquad  2Mq\leq x^{1/2}(\log x)^{-B},$$ we prove instead an exact coefficient-transfer law, with arbitrary logarithmic saving, by a direct application of the classical maximal Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem. The main term is operator-valued: $$N\!\int W\,  \sum_m\alpha_m\frac{m}{\varphi(m)}  (\mathsf P_{q,h}\beta)(m),  \qquad  (\mathsf P_{q,h}\beta)(a)  =\frac1{\varphi(q)}  \sum_{\substack{b\in(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^\times\\(ab+h,q)=1}}\beta(b).$$ Thus the periodic coefficient is not replaced by its mean: it is transported by an explicit finite arithmetic operator.

We determine this operator completely. After row normalization it is a self-adjoint Markov operator. Character modes with a conductor divisible by $p^2$ are annihilated. A surviving character of exact squarefree conductor $r$ is exchanged with its conjugate and multiplied by $$(-1)^{\omega(r)}\overline{\chi(-h)}  \prod_{p\mid r}(p-2)^{-1}.$$ This is exactly the finite-conductor multiplier found for the centered rough kernel in the preceding work, now transferred through the genuine prime target $\Lambda(mn+h)$. We also give the corresponding prime-weighted source corollary and an exact finite certificate.

The analytic input is classical; no new level of distribution is asserted. The modulus is $mq$, not $m$, and residue splitting costs the period $q$. Moreover, the finite-conductor energy-escape theorem from the preceding paper shows that polylogarithmic periodic spaces capture a vanishing proportion of the $L^2$ energy of $\Lambda-1$. Consequently one cannot substitute $\beta=\Lambda-1$, and the result gives neither a fixed-shift prime-pair asymptotic nor a twin-prime lower bound or a breach of the parity barrier.

# Introduction and exact scope

###### ref-26db7c8539f9337b

The transition from a rough target to a prime target changes the analytic problem. A finite sieve kernel can test the congruences $mn+h\not\equiv0\pmod p$ for prescribed primes $p$, but primality is encoded by the von Mangoldt weight $\Lambda(mn+h)$. The preceding paper [`\citep{WangTPCSix2026}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-008.md#cite-fa35c8724b85bf3a) made this distinction explicit: a one-sided Bombieri–Vinogradov argument controls $$\label{eq:intro-one-sided}  \sum_{M<m\leq2M}\alpha_m  \sum_{n\geq1}\Lambda(mn+h)W(n/N),  \qquad |\alpha_m|\leq1,$$ in the Type I range $M\leq x^{1/2}(\log x)^{-B}$, where $x=MN$. Inserting an arbitrary bounded coefficient $\beta_n$ in the $n$-sum destroys the direct reduction to one residue class modulo $m$. The resulting form leads to the unresolved bilinear/Type II interface isolated there.

###### ref-4e214d6c217b0acb

###### ref-0c3dbddb3d456973

This paper treats a deliberately narrower coefficient class. We suppose that $\beta$ is periodic modulo a small integer $q$ and is supported on the unit classes. Splitting $n$ modulo $q$ turns each row into finitely many prime sums in reduced residue classes modulo $$\label{eq:intro-combined-modulus}  mq.$$ The classical maximal Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem can then be applied after two costs have been recorded: the level condition concerns $Mq$, and the triangle inequality over the residue classes costs at most $q$. Both costs are harmless when $$\label{eq:intro-safe-range}  q\leq(\log x)^C,  \qquad  2Mq\leq\frac{x^{1/2}}{(\log x)^B}.$$ They are not harmless for a general power-sized period.

###### ref-6cf02099cbb1b1ae

###### ref-dd8ed0ce91dddaaa

The principal point is not merely that a periodic coefficient can be handled. Its main term retains a finite operator. For an odd $q$ with $(q,h)=1$, put $$\label{eq:intro-local-operator}  (\mathsf P_{q,h}\beta)(a)  =\frac1{\varphi(q)}   \sum_{\substack{b\in G_q\\(ab+h,q)=1}}\beta(b),  \qquad  G_q=(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^\times.$$ The transfer theorem proved below has the rowwise main term $$\label{eq:intro-row-main}  \frac{mN}{\varphi(m)}\left(\int_0^\infty W(t)\,dt\right)  (\mathsf P_{q,h}\beta)(m).$$ Thus the prime target does not average $\beta$ to a scalar. It transports the finite residue observable by $\mathsf P_{q,h}$.

## Results and logical status

The paper contains four groups of statements.

###### ref-1a60bd0043ae3902

###### ref-da441b58baebfea5

1.  We determine $\mathsf P_{q,h}$ exactly. After division by its row density $$\label{eq:intro-kappa}   \kappa_q=\prod_{p\mid q}\frac{p-2}{p-1},$$ it is a self-adjoint Markov operator. If a local character has conductor exponent at least two at some $p\mid q$, that mode is annihilated. Every surviving nonprincipal mode has squarefree exact conductor $r$ and is exchanged with its conjugate with modulus $$\label{eq:intro-damping}   d(r)=\prod_{p\mid r}\frac1{p-2}.$$ The phase is the arithmetic involution phase $(-1)^{\omega(r)}\overline{\chi(-h)}$. This is the same exact multiplier that occurs in the rough centered kernel of [`\citet{WangTPCFive2026}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-008.md#cite-a95f215ba0ba0f17). The structural contribution here is to identify and prove the occurrence of this multiplier in the main term of the two-scale prime-target form.

2.  We prove an $\ell^1$ row-transfer theorem in the range [\[eq:intro-safe-range\]](TPC-008.md#ref-0c3dbddb3d456973). It is uniform over all bounded unit-supported $q$-periodic coefficients and immediately gives coefficient-uniform bilinear forms. The only analytic input is the classical maximal Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem [`\citep{Bombieri1965,Vinogradov1965,IwaniecKowalski2004}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-008.md#cite-072be3f1fcf63a7d); [\[Vinogradov1965\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-008.md#cite-91320d5c0c0e4130); [\[IwaniecKowalski2004\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-008.md#cite-49dfea87eaada156).

3.  We give exact character corollaries and add a Mangoldt weight in the $m$-coordinate. Siegel–Walfisz then removes every nonprincipal periodic output mode. This produces a two-prime/periodic-factor asymptotic in a strongly unbalanced Type I range. It does not make the periodic factor prime.

4.  We combine the transfer theorem with the finite-conductor energy-escape result of [`\citet{WangTPCSeven2026}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-008.md#cite-a66c165fb5c69ac7). Periods of size $(\log x)^C$ capture a vanishing proportion of the $L^2$ energy of $\Lambda-1$. Therefore the present theorem cannot be specialized to the missing prime coefficient. We record this as a strict theorem-interface statement, not as a universal impossibility result.

The finite computations have the same separation. An integer/rational certificate verifies the local identities, conductor multipliers, involutions, and exact arithmetic-progression reindexing. A separate floating-point experiment displays finite Mangoldt rows. No analytic theorem depends on either computation.

## Relation to known distribution results

The estimate in this paper is an organized consequence of the standard square-root level of distribution. It is not a new Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem. Results beyond the square-root level are known for special well-factorable weights and fixed residue structures [`\citep{BombieriFriedlanderIwaniec1986,Maynard2025WellFactorable}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-008.md#cite-5cb6d7e2408bcfd5); [\[Maynard2025WellFactorable\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-008.md#cite-6cf00aa083c8ad8b); those hypotheses do not directly cover the residue $mb+h$ modulo the moving combined modulus $mq$ with an arbitrary periodic coefficient.

Much deeper cancellation estimates are known for isolated shifted-prime character sums, beginning with earlier shifted-prime work and continuing through stronger bounds of Kerr, as well as for general trace functions over primes [`\citep{FriedlanderGongShparlinski2010,Kerr2021, FouvryKowalskiMichel2014}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-008.md#cite-087e9040da968282); [\[Kerr2021\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-008.md#cite-0d9b3056a5e43081); [\[FouvryKowalskiMichel2014\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-008.md#cite-35ebc0dfa8ff5ce8). Our object and purpose are different: the source scale $m$ moves, the modulus is $mq$, arbitrary row coefficients are retained, and the desired output is the complete finite operator $\mathsf P_{q,h}$. We make no priority claim for a new estimate of an isolated character sum.

An $L^2$ treatment using Barban–Davenport–Halberstam or variational forms [`\citep{LewkoLewko2012}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-008.md#cite-bf132f8ade8e2045) may reduce the elementary residue-splitting loss in some ranges. In the small-modulus regime, however, standard formulations also contain terms that do not automatically yield the power-sized-period version of our coefficient-uniform statement. Such an extension is left as an open analytic problem rather than inferred from an inapplicable mean square bound.

## What is not proved

For clarity, none of the following is a consequence of this paper.

-   There is no estimate with an arbitrary bounded second coefficient.

-   There is no bound for the Vaughan Type II hard packet identified in [`\citet{WangTPCSix2026}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-008.md#cite-fa35c8724b85bf3a).

-   There is no asymptotic for $\sum_n\Lambda(n)\Lambda(n+2)$ and no twin-prime lower bound.

-   The parity obstruction is not broken. The relevant limitations of finite sieve information remain those discussed in the asymptotic-sieve and parity literature [`\citep{FriedlanderIwaniec1998,Polymath2014}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-008.md#cite-a670a8e9d85764df); [\[Polymath2014\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-008.md#cite-68dbb6c61a10972e).

-   The finite Markov spectrum below is not a spectral realization of the primes. It is the exact Fourier form of a local congruence operator.

The gain is narrower but rigorous: one nontrivial second-coefficient class crosses the prime target at a prescribed shift, and the surviving arithmetic dynamics is computed exactly.

# The finite prime-target operator

###### ref-fe7ee5c6d72ce4ef

###### ref-3e15c35990b606fa

###### ref-c5e20d37f0f277d9

Throughout this section $q\geq3$ is odd, $(q,h)=1$, and $G_q=(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^\times$. We use normalized counting measure on $G_q$. For $\beta:G_q\to\mathbb C$, define $$\begin{aligned}  (\mathsf P_{q,h}\beta)(a)  &=\frac1{\varphi(q)}    \sum_{\substack{b\in G_q\\(ab+h,q)=1}}\beta(b),  \label{eq:P-definition}\\  \kappa_q  &=\prod_{p\mid q}\frac{p-2}{p-1},  \qquad  \mathsf T_{q,h}=\kappa_q^{-1}\mathsf P_{q,h}.  \label{eq:T-definition}\end{aligned}$$ For each $a$, exactly $$\label{eq:row-degree}  R_q=\varphi(q)\kappa_q  =\prod_{p^e\parallel q}p^{e-1}(p-2)$$ values of $b$ are admissible. Consequently $\mathsf T_{q,h}$ is a Markov operator. Its kernel is real and symmetric, because $ab+h=ba+h$, so it is self-adjoint on $L^2(G_q)$.

## Inclusion-exclusion and the arithmetic involutions

###### ref-2b84a031150f8152

###### ref-b8c42e400b05e297

For every squarefree $d\mid\operatorname{rad}(q)$ let $$\label{eq:J-definition}  J_{h,d}(a)=-h a^{-1}\pmod d,  \qquad a\in G_d,$$ and let $U_{h,d}f=f\circ J_{h,d}$. Then $J_{h,d}^2$ is the identity, so $U_{h,d}$ is a self-adjoint unitary involution. Define the normalized fiber average $$\label{eq:fiber-average}  (E_{q,d}\beta)(u)  =\frac{\varphi(d)}{\varphi(q)}   \sum_{\substack{b\in G_q\\b\equiv u\pmod d}}\beta(b).$$

###### ref-f42a492e06f2eb7e

###### ref-9c6690492f6b5689

> **Proposition: Exact involution bundle** \[prop:involution-bundle\] For every $\beta:G_q\to\mathbb C$ and $a\in G_q$, $$\label{eq:involution-bundle}  (\mathsf P_{q,h}\beta)(a)  =\sum_{d\mid\operatorname{rad}(q)}    \frac{\mu(d)}{\varphi(d)}    \bigl(U_{h,d}E_{q,d}\beta\bigr)(a\bmod d).$$ All terms in this identity are finite and exact.

> **Proof** Inclusion-exclusion gives $$\mathbf 1_{(ab+h,q)=1}  =\sum_{\substack{d\mid\operatorname{rad}(q)\\ab\equiv-h\pmod d}}\mu(d).$$ For fixed $a$ and $d$, the congruence selects the fiber $b\equiv J_{h,d}(a)\pmod d$. By [\[eq:fiber-average\]](TPC-008.md#ref-b8c42e400b05e297), its unnormalized sum is $$\frac{\varphi(q)}{\varphi(d)}  (E_{q,d}\beta)(J_{h,d}(a)).$$ Divide by $\varphi(q)$ and sum over $d$.

This formula identifies the exact link with the target-divisor involutions of [`\citet{WangTPCSix2026}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-008.md#cite-fa35c8724b85bf3a). It is not a mixing assertion: each $U_{h,d}$ is an involution, while the damping comes from the normalized combination of all divisor layers and from the fiber averages.

## Complete character-block decomposition

###### ref-ededb3f9f28b3a21

Let $\widehat G_q$ be the character group. We use the Fourier convention $$\label{eq:Fourier-convention}  B(\chi)=\frac1{\varphi(q)}\sum_{b\in G_q}\beta(b)\chi(b),  \qquad  \beta(b)=\sum_{\chi\in\widehat G_q}B(\chi)\overline{\chi(b)}.$$ Write $q=\prod_{p^e\parallel q}p^e$. Let $f_p(\chi)$ be the conductor exponent of the local component $\chi_p$ modulo $p^e$.

###### ref-45dd3cd13496e1bf

###### ref-4544c5e8cf316ccb

Call $\chi$ *surviving* if $f_p(\chi)\leq1$ for every $p\mid q$. For such a character put $$\label{eq:exact-squarefree-conductor}  r_\chi=\prod_{\substack{p\mid q\\f_p(\chi)=1}}p,  \qquad  d(r_\chi)=\prod_{p\mid r_\chi}\frac1{p-2},$$ and $$\label{eq:c-multiplier}  c_h(\chi)  =(-1)^{\omega(r_\chi)}d(r_\chi)    \overline{\chi(-h)}.$$ The principal character is surviving, with $r_\chi=1$ and $c_h(\chi)=1$.

###### ref-02b8dcab6a4c730a

###### ref-21bc96248fd17982

###### ref-d5f8542297f41669

###### ref-a22836570f822db8

###### ref-616584853992ef39

###### ref-b65de3d7c0ed2482

> **Theorem: Exact local spectrum** \[thm:exact-local-spectrum\] For every $\beta:G_q\to\mathbb C$, $$\label{eq:P-Fourier-formula}  (\mathsf P_{q,h}\beta)(a)  =\kappa_q   \sum_{\substack{\chi\in\widehat G_q\\\chi\ \mathrm{surviving}}}   B(\chi)c_h(\chi)\chi(a).$$ Equivalently, $$\label{eq:T-on-character}  \mathsf T_{q,h}\overline\chi  =\begin{cases}    c_h(\chi)\chi,&\chi\text{ surviving},\\    0,&\chi\text{ not surviving}.   \end{cases}$$ In particular, on a surviving mode, $$\label{eq:T-square-character}  \mathsf T_{q,h}^{2}\chi=d(r_\chi)^2\chi.$$
>
> The nonzero spectrum consists of $1$, the signed eigenvalues on real characters, and the pairs $\pm d(r_\chi)$ on nonreal conjugate pairs. Zero has multiplicity $$\label{eq:zero-multiplicity}  \varphi(q)-\varphi(\operatorname{rad}(q)).$$ For every integer $k\geq1$, $$\label{eq:even-trace}  \operatorname{tr}(\mathsf T_{q,h}^{2k})  =\prod_{p\mid q}\left(1+(p-2)^{1-2k}\right).$$

> **Proof** It is enough to compute locally. Fix $p^e\parallel q$, write $a$ for a unit modulo $p^e$, and consider the sum of $\overline{\chi_p(b)}$ over unit $b$ with $ab+h\not\equiv0\pmod p$.
>
> If $f_p=0$, the summand is $1$ and the sum is $p^{e-1}(p-2)$. If $f_p=1$, the complete unit sum is zero and the excluded fiber has $p^{e-1}$ points, on all of which the character has the same value. The allowed sum is therefore $$-p^{e-1}\overline{\chi_p(-h)}\chi_p(a).$$ If $f_p\geq2$, both the complete unit sum and the sum over the excluded fiber vanish: the restriction of $\chi_p$ to the kernel of reduction modulo $p$ is nonprincipal. Hence this local factor is zero.
>
> Multiplying the local sums and dividing by $\varphi(q)$ proves [\[eq:P-Fourier-formula\]](TPC-008.md#ref-21bc96248fd17982) and [\[eq:T-on-character\]](TPC-008.md#ref-d5f8542297f41669). Since $c_h(\overline\chi)=\overline{c_h(\chi)}$ and $|c_h(\chi)|=d(r_\chi)$, applying $\mathsf T_{q,h}$ twice proves [\[eq:T-square-character\]](TPC-008.md#ref-a22836570f822db8). There are $p-1$ local characters with conductor exponent at most one, so the nonzero subspace has dimension $\prod_{p\mid q}(p-1)=\varphi(\operatorname{rad}(q))$. Finally, group the surviving characters by exact conductor. At a prime $p$, there is one principal choice and $p-2$ nonprincipal choices. Thus $$\sum_{\chi\ \mathrm{surviving}}d(r_\chi)^{2k}  =\prod_{p\mid q}\left(1+(p-2)(p-2)^{-2k}\right),$$ which is [\[eq:even-trace\]](TPC-008.md#ref-b65de3d7c0ed2482).

###### ref-f2e9bae2d39d1346

###### ref-be8b735e24d50a0e

> **Corollary: Gap and the prime $3$ obstruction** \[cor:local-gap\] Suppose $q$ is squarefree. If $3\mid q$, a nonprincipal conductor-$3$ mode has modulus-one eigenvalue, so there is no spectral gap on the mean-zero subspace. If $3\nmid q$ and $p_{\min}$ is the least prime dividing $q$, then $$\label{eq:mean-zero-norm}  \|\mathsf T_{q,h}\|_{\mathbf 1^\perp\to\mathbf 1^\perp}  =\frac1{p_{\min}-2}<1.$$ For nonsquarefree $q$, the same norm formula holds on $\mathbf 1^\perp$; equivalently, it holds on the mean-zero part of the surviving subspace. The orthogonal complement of the surviving subspace is killed in one step.

The finite chain can therefore have a genuine local gap. This does not say that the primes, or the sequence $\Lambda(mn+h)$, form a mixing dynamical system. The distinction will be maintained throughout.

# A periodic coefficient through the prime target

###### ref-ca84b4a7a1b9e6de

Fix a nonzero integer $h$ and a function $W\in C_c^1((0,\infty))$. Write $$\label{eq:I-W}  \mathcal I_W=\int_0^\infty W(t)\,dt.$$ All constants below may depend on $h$ and $W$. The parameter $h$ is fixed; in particular, $h=2$ is permitted. The assertion is nevertheless a theorem about a periodic coefficient in a prime-target form, not a prime-pair theorem.

###### ref-473cc9f725426d85

Let $x=MN$, where $M,N\geq2$. For an odd integer $q$ coprime to $h$, let $\beta:G_q\to\mathbb C$, extend it periodically to the integers, and set it equal to zero on the nonunit residue classes. For $(m,hq)=1$, define the row $$\label{eq:prime-target-row}  \mathcal R_{m,h}^{(N)}(\beta)  =\sum_{n\geq1}\beta(n)\Lambda(mn+h)W(n/N).$$

## The rowwise transfer theorem

###### ref-2198446c20c298cf

###### ref-893b9a53636dab0c

We first state the classical input in the form used in the proof. Put $$\label{eq:Delta-definition}  \Delta(Y;r)=  \max_{\substack{a\bmod r\\(a,r)=1}}  \max_{y\leq Y}  \left|   \sum_{\substack{n\leq y\\n\equiv a\pmod r}}\Lambda(n)   -\frac{y}{\varphi(r)}  \right|.$$ For every $L>0$, the maximal Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem gives a $B_0=B_0(L)>0$ such that $$\label{eq:maximal-BV}  \sum_{r\leq Y^{1/2}(\log Y)^{-B_0}}\Delta(Y;r)  \ll_L \frac{Y}{(\log Y)^L}.$$ This is the classical theorem [`\citep{Bombieri1965,Vinogradov1965,IwaniecKowalski2004}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-008.md#cite-072be3f1fcf63a7d); [\[Vinogradov1965\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-008.md#cite-91320d5c0c0e4130); [\[IwaniecKowalski2004\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-008.md#cite-49dfea87eaada156), not a result proved here.

> **Theorem: Operator-valued periodic transfer** \[thm:operator-transfer\] Fix $A,C>0$. There is $B=B(A,C,h,W)>0$ such that the following holds for all sufficiently large $x=MN$. Suppose $$\label{eq:transfer-q-range}  3\leq q\leq(\log x)^C,  \qquad q\text{ odd},  \qquad (q,h)=1,$$ and $$\label{eq:transfer-modulus-range}  2Mq\leq\frac{x^{1/2}}{(\log x)^B}.$$ Uniformly for every $\beta:G_q\to\mathbb C$ with $\|\beta\|_\infty\leq1$, $$\label{eq:l1-row-transfer}  \sum_{\substack{M<m\leq2M\\(m,hq)=1}}  \left|   \mathcal R_{m,h}^{(N)}(\beta)   -\frac{mN}{\varphi(m)}\mathcal I_W    (\mathsf P_{q,h}\beta)(m\bmod q)  \right|  \ll_{A,C,h,W}\frac{x}{(\log x)^A}.$$ Consequently, for arbitrary complex coefficients $|\alpha_m|\leq1$ supported on the same rows, $$\begin{aligned}  &\sum_{M<m\leq2M}\alpha_m    \sum_{n\geq1}\beta(n)\Lambda(mn+h)W(n/N)  \notag\\  &\quad=  N\mathcal I_W  \sum_{M<m\leq2M}\alpha_m\frac{m}{\varphi(m)}  (\mathsf P_{q,h}\beta)(m)  +O_{A,C,h,W}\!\left(\frac{x}{(\log x)^A}\right).  \label{eq:bilinear-operator-transfer}\end{aligned}$$

The theorem is stronger than a single bilinear estimate: it controls the sum of the absolute row errors. This is exactly the supremum of the error in [\[eq:bilinear-operator-transfer\]](TPC-008.md#ref-1e721faebcf7dc98) over all row coefficients of modulus at most one.

###### ref-3434c137cbbc7b23

###### ref-9fa6e146762bf6ab

###### ref-5e1863e4e74e91d3

###### ref-b1270982ae46b2db

###### ref-d2f7a161670f1d77

###### ref-e0b70b749a38605f

###### ref-ecda7daf18a64bc8

###### ref-82180b344e2262bd

> **Proof** Put $$\label{eq:Lambda-q}  \Lambda_q(t)=\Lambda(t)\mathbf 1_{(t,q)=1}.$$ We first prove the result with $\Lambda_q$ in place of $\Lambda$. Split the $n$-sum into $b\in G_q$. If $(mb+h,q)=1$, the substitution $t=mn+h$ gives the exact correspondence $$\label{eq:AP-reindexing}  \left\{n\geq1:n\equiv b\pmod q\right\}  \longleftrightarrow  \left\{t\in\mathbb Z:t\equiv mb+h\pmod{mq},\ (t-h)/m\geq1\right\}.$$ The class on the right is reduced. Indeed, $(mb+h,m)=(h,m)=1$ and it is coprime to $q$ by assumption. On this class $\Lambda_q(t)=\Lambda(t)$ whenever $t\geq1$. Equivalently, after inserting the transformed weight $W((t-h)/(mN))$, the target sum is a weighted prime sum in that reduced class modulo $mq$.
>
> Stieltjes partial summation, applied to $W((t-h)/(mN))$, gives $$\begin{aligned}  &\sum_{\substack{n\geq1\\n\equiv b\pmod q}}   \Lambda_q(mn+h)W(n/N)  \notag\\  &\qquad=  \frac{mN}{\varphi(mq)}\mathcal I_W+E_{m,b},  \label{eq:one-residue-main}\end{aligned}$$ where, for a constant $C_W>0$, $$\label{eq:one-residue-error}  |E_{m,b}|\ll_W\Delta(C_Wx;mq).$$ For sufficiently large $x$, the support of the transformed weight lies in the positive $t$-axis. The continuous main term is exactly $$\frac1{\varphi(mq)}  \int_0^\infty W((t-h)/(mN))\,dt  =\frac{mN}{\varphi(mq)}\mathcal I_W.$$ If $(mb+h,q)>1$, the corresponding $\Lambda_q$ sum is identically zero.
>
> Because $(m,q)=1$, we have $\varphi(mq)=\varphi(m)\varphi(q)$. Summing the main terms in [\[eq:one-residue-main\]](TPC-008.md#ref-5e1863e4e74e91d3) against $\beta(b)$ therefore gives $$\begin{aligned}  \frac{mN}{\varphi(m)\varphi(q)}\mathcal I_W  \sum_{\substack{b\in G_q\\(mb+h,q)=1}}\beta(b)  =\frac{mN}{\varphi(m)}\mathcal I_W(\mathsf P_{q,h}\beta)(m).\end{aligned}$$
>
> For the errors, the triangle inequality and [\[eq:one-residue-error\]](TPC-008.md#ref-b1270982ae46b2db) give $$\begin{aligned}  &\sum_{\substack{M<m\leq2M\\(m,hq)=1}}   \left|    \sum_{\substack{b\in G_q\\(mb+h,q)=1}}\beta(b)E_{m,b}   \right|  \notag\\  &\qquad\ll_W  \left(\sum_{b\in G_q}|\beta(b)|\right)  \sum_{r\leq2Mq}\Delta(C_Wx;r).  \label{eq:raw-BV-error}\end{aligned}$$ Here we enlarged the moduli $mq$ to all $r\leq2Mq$. Choose the exponent in [\[eq:maximal-BV\]](TPC-008.md#ref-893b9a53636dab0c) larger than $A+C+2$ and then choose $B$ in [\[eq:transfer-modulus-range\]](TPC-008.md#ref-02e0e5d927d7b051) accordingly. Since $$\label{eq:l1-beta-cost}  \sum_{b\in G_q}|\beta(b)|\leq\varphi(q)\leq q\leq(\log x)^C,$$ the right side of [\[eq:raw-BV-error\]](TPC-008.md#ref-d2f7a161670f1d77) is $O_{A,C,h,W}(x(\log x)^{-A})$.
>
> It remains to restore the terms deleted in [\[eq:Lambda-q\]](TPC-008.md#ref-3434c137cbbc7b23). If $(mn+h,q)>1$ and $\Lambda(mn+h)\ne0$, then $$\label{eq:exceptional-prime-power}  mn+h=p^k$$ for some prime $p\mid q$. On the support of the two scales, $mn\asymp_W x$. For each $p$, only $O_W(1)$ powers $p^k$ lie in the resulting fixed-ratio interval. For each such power, the number of pairs $(m,n)$ is at most $\tau(|p^k-h|)$. Hence, for every fixed $\eta>0$, the total deleted contribution, even after absolute values over all rows, is $$\label{eq:prime-power-error}  \ll_{\eta,h,W}x^\eta\sum_{p\mid q}\log p  \ll_{\eta,h,W}x^\eta\log(2q).$$ The divisor bound $\tau(t)\ll_\eta t^\eta$ was used here. Under [\[eq:transfer-q-range\]](TPC-008.md#ref-d4d779cee7fdeb93), [\[eq:prime-power-error\]](TPC-008.md#ref-82180b344e2262bd) is absorbed by $x(\log x)^{-A}$. This proves [\[eq:l1-row-transfer\]](TPC-008.md#ref-a75c34e9ab37e009); multiplying the row errors by $\alpha_m$ proves [\[eq:bilinear-operator-transfer\]](TPC-008.md#ref-1e721faebcf7dc98).

###### ref-017407b9e0f2cdc5

> **Remark: The unsimplified complexity bound** \[rem:raw-complexity-bound\] Before imposing $q\leq(\log x)^C$, the proof gives, for every $L>0$ and in the corresponding Bombieri–Vinogradov modulus range, $$\label{eq:raw-complexity-bound}  \sum_m|\text{row error}_m|  \ll_{L,h,W}  \|\beta\|_{\ell^1(G_q)}\frac{x}{(\log x)^L}  +O_{\eta,h,W}(x^\eta\log(2q)).$$ This formula explains the polylogarithmic condition. The classical theorem controls the maximum of one reduced class for each modulus; summing an arbitrary observable over all $q$ classes is not free.

###### ref-181247ee9abfcc09

> **Remark: The combined-modulus boundary** \[rem:combined-modulus-boundary\] The modulus in [\[eq:AP-reindexing\]](TPC-008.md#ref-9fa6e146762bf6ab) is $mq$. It would be incorrect to retain the one-sided condition $M\leq x^{1/2}(\log x)^{-B}$ from [\[eq:intro-one-sided\]](TPC-008.md#ref-26db7c8539f9337b) after introducing a period $q$. The valid direct condition is [\[eq:transfer-modulus-range\]](TPC-008.md#ref-02e0e5d927d7b051). Likewise, a character of small exact conductor inflated to a much larger ambient period still contains the unit cutoff for that ambient period; its integer-period cost is the ambient $q$, unless the coefficient is rewritten at its genuinely smaller period.

# Modal transfer and a prime-weighted source

###### ref-e82e01acb2c4c143

The operator theorem becomes especially transparent in character coordinates. Continue to use the Fourier convention [\[eq:Fourier-convention\]](TPC-008.md#ref-ededb3f9f28b3a21). Combining \[[thm:operator-transfer](TPC-008.md#ref-c5f0c034952d497f),[thm:exact-local-spectrum](TPC-008.md#ref-02b8dcab6a4c730a)\] gives the exact main term $$\begin{aligned}  &\sum_m\alpha_m\sum_n\beta(n)\Lambda(mn+h)W(n/N)  \notag\\  &\quad=\kappa_qN\mathcal I_W  \sum_m\alpha_m\frac{m}{\varphi(m)}  \sum_{\substack{\chi\in\widehat G_q\\\chi\ \mathrm{surviving}}}  B(\chi)c_h(\chi)\chi(m)  +O_{A,C,h,W}\!\left(\frac{x}{(\log x)^A}\right).  \label{eq:modal-transfer}\end{aligned}$$ The sums over $m$ have the support and the range of [\[thm:operator-transfer\]](TPC-008.md#ref-c5f0c034952d497f).

## One character mode

###### ref-216eef0ab105ce0d

###### ref-6d687617402e66ed

> **Corollary: Primitive squarefree mode** \[cor:primitive-mode\] Let $q$ be odd and squarefree, let $\chi$ be primitive modulo $q$, and suppose the hypotheses of [\[thm:operator-transfer\]](TPC-008.md#ref-c5f0c034952d497f) hold. Then $$\begin{aligned}  &\sum_m\alpha_m\sum_{n\geq1}   \overline{\chi(n)}\Lambda(mn+h)W(n/N)  \notag\\  &\quad=  \frac{\mu(q)\overline{\chi(-h)}}{\varphi(q)}  N\mathcal I_W  \sum_m\alpha_m\frac{m}{\varphi(m)}\chi(m)  +O_{A,C,h,W}\!\left(\frac{x}{(\log x)^A}\right).  \label{eq:primitive-mode-transfer}\end{aligned}$$ Here every Dirichlet character is extended by zero away from the unit classes. Replacing $\overline\chi$ by $\chi$ conjugates the phase and the output character.

> **Proof** For a primitive character of squarefree modulus $q$, $r_\chi=q$ and $$\kappa_qc_h(\chi)  =\prod_{p\mid q}\frac{p-2}{p-1}   \frac{\mu(q)\overline{\chi(-h)}}{\prod_{p\mid q}(p-2)}  =\frac{\mu(q)\overline{\chi(-h)}}{\varphi(q)}.$$ Now apply [\[eq:modal-transfer\]](TPC-008.md#ref-e82e01acb2c4c143).

###### ref-a3966a472b58ecfd

This formula displays the arithmetic involution directly: $$\label{eq:mode-involution}  \overline\chi(n)  \quad\longmapsto\quad  \overline{\chi(-h)}\chi(m).$$ The scalar $\mu(q)/\varphi(q)$ is not a heuristic independence factor. It is the exact local main coefficient after the $q$ residue classes have been passed through the prime target.

###### ref-1ada4fed1eefacef

###### ref-7e25ef6adaa3e6c4

> **Corollary: Higher conductor-exponent annihilation** \[cor:higher-exponent-annihilation\] Let $\chi\in\widehat G_q$ have local conductor exponent at least two at some $p\mid q$. Under the hypotheses of [\[thm:operator-transfer\]](TPC-008.md#ref-c5f0c034952d497f), $$\label{eq:higher-mode-transfer}  \sum_m\alpha_m\sum_{n\geq1}  \overline{\chi(n)}\Lambda(mn+h)W(n/N)  \ll_{A,C,h,W}\frac{x}{(\log x)^A}.$$

The vanishing is a local algebraic cancellation in the leading term. It is not a zero-free-region statement about the $L$-function of $\chi$.

## Exact-conductor truncations

###### ref-3fd4ec32c004b545

For $1\leq R\leq\operatorname{rad}(q)$, define the surviving low-conductor part $$\label{eq:low-conductor-beta}  \beta_{\leq R}(b)  =\sum_{\substack{\chi\ \mathrm{surviving}\\r_\chi\leq R}}  B(\chi)\overline{\chi(b)}.$$ Its transferred main term is obtained from [\[eq:modal-transfer\]](TPC-008.md#ref-e82e01acb2c4c143) by restricting the same sum to $r_\chi\leq R$. There is, however, a distinction between exact conductor and actual integer period.

If [\[eq:low-conductor-beta\]](TPC-008.md#ref-3fd4ec32c004b545) is retained as a function on $G_q$ and extended by zero off the units modulo $q$, its period remains $q$ because of the ambient unit cutoff. The Bombieri–Vinogradov condition is then on $Mq$. If one instead starts with a primitive character of its genuine modulus $r$, as in [\[cor:primitive-mode\]](TPC-008.md#ref-216eef0ab105ce0d), the condition is on $Mr$. This prevents a low exact-conductor label inside an enormous ambient CRT space from being used to evade the combined-modulus boundary.

###### ref-34ec7731fdb3347e

For a finite sum of primitive modes of possibly different genuine periods, the proof applies term by term. A sufficient polylogarithmic complexity budget, with every $r_j$ odd and $(r_j,h)=1$, is $$\label{eq:mode-complexity-budget}  \sum_j|\gamma_j|r_j\leq(\log x)^C,  \qquad  2M\max_j r_j\leq x^{1/2}(\log x)^{-B}.$$ This is a convenient corollary of the residue-class proof, not an optimal large-sieve statement.

## Adding a prime weight in the source coordinate

The nonprincipal output modes in [\[eq:modal-transfer\]](TPC-008.md#ref-e82e01acb2c4c143) need not vanish for arbitrary $\alpha_m$: one may choose $\alpha_m$ to align with a character. They do vanish after placing a prime weight on the $m$-coordinate.

###### ref-76cf141e90cca8f4

###### ref-4840005deaad0e16

###### ref-c58de5684e587885

###### ref-a46907866573db19

> **Corollary: Prime source and periodic factor** \[cor:prime-source-periodic-factor\] Fix $\delta,A,C>0$, let $W_1,W_2\in C_c^1((0,\infty))$, and suppose $\operatorname{supp}W_1\subset[1,2]$. Let $q\geq3$ be odd with $(q,h)=1$, let $\beta:G_q\to\mathbb C$ satisfy $\|\beta\|_\infty\leq1$, and extend $\beta$ $q$-periodically to the integers and by zero on nonunit residue classes. Put $x=MN$ and assume $$\label{eq:prime-source-range}  M\geq x^\delta,  \qquad  q\leq(\log M)^C,  \qquad  2Mq\leq\frac{x^{1/2}}{(\log x)^B},$$ where $B=B(A,C,\delta,h,W_1,W_2)$ is sufficiently large. Then $$\begin{aligned}  &\sum_{\substack{m,n\geq1\\(m,hq)=1}}  \Lambda(m)W_1(m/M)\beta(n)  \Lambda(mn+h)W_2(n/N)  \notag\\  &\quad=  \kappa_q B(\mathbf 1)MN  \left(\int_0^\infty W_1(u)\,du\right)  \left(\int_0^\infty W_2(v)\,dv\right)  +O_{A,C,\delta,h,W_1,W_2}\!\left(\frac{x}{(\log x)^A}\right),  \label{eq:prime-source-asymptotic}\end{aligned}$$ where $$\label{eq:beta-unit-mean}  B(\mathbf 1)=\frac1{\varphi(q)}\sum_{b\in G_q}\beta(b).$$ In particular, a unit-mean-zero periodic factor gives an arbitrary logarithmic saving.

###### ref-c82221cb3fb2815d

###### ref-a9587df828848bd0

###### ref-b2de0486099e7729

> **Proof** Apply [\[thm:operator-transfer\]](TPC-008.md#ref-c5f0c034952d497f) with row coefficient $\Lambda(m)W_1(m/M)$. Dividing this coefficient by $O(\log x)$ first and requesting one additional logarithmic saving justifies the application. Substitute [\[eq:P-Fourier-formula\]](TPC-008.md#ref-21bc96248fd17982) in the main term.
>
> For the principal character, the prime number theorem and partial summation give $$\label{eq:principal-prime-source}  \sum_m\Lambda(m)W_1(m/M)\frac{m}{\varphi(m)}  =M\int_0^\infty W_1(u)\,du  +O_A\!\left(\frac{M}{(\log M)^A}\right).$$ The factor $m/\varphi(m)$ may be replaced by $1$: on primes the difference contributes $O_{W_1}(\log M)$, and higher prime powers contribute $O_{W_1}(M^{1/2}(\log M)^2)$.
>
> For every nonprincipal $\chi\in\widehat G_q$, the Siegel–Walfisz theorem, uniformly for $q\leq(\log M)^C$, gives $$\label{eq:nonprincipal-prime-source}  \sum_m\Lambda(m)W_1(m/M)\frac{m}{\varphi(m)}\chi(m)  \ll_{A,C,W_1}\frac{M}{(\log M)^A}.$$ The rows inherit the condition $(m,hq)=1$. Its excluded prime powers obey the uniform bound $$\label{eq:excluded-prime-source-powers}  \sum_{\substack{M<p^k\leq2M\\p\mid hq}}  \Lambda(p^k)\frac{p}{p-1}  \ll_h\log(2q),$$ because a fixed prime contributes only $O(1)$ powers to a dyadic interval. They are therefore absorbed in the same saving. There are at most $\varphi(q)\leq(\log M)^C$ surviving modes, while $|B(\chi)|,|c_h(\chi)|\leq1$. Increasing the Siegel–Walfisz saving absorbs their total. Finally $M\geq x^\delta$ makes $\log M$ comparable to $\log x$, proving [\[eq:prime-source-asymptotic\]](TPC-008.md#ref-c58de5684e587885).

###### ref-bc6f67ee69016c91

The range [\[eq:prime-source-range\]](TPC-008.md#ref-4840005deaad0e16) is strongly unbalanced. Ignoring logarithmic factors, the combined-modulus condition says $$\label{eq:type-I-imbalance}  N\gtrsim Mq^2.$$ This is still a Type I theorem. The periodic factor has crossed the prime target, but it has not become a second unrestricted prime weight.

# The finite-conductor boundary

The transfer theorem is uniform over a nontrivial coefficient class, but that class is small compared with the prime fluctuation. This section makes the missing step quantitative.

###### ref-560645c19ded1e79

###### ref-35e5033fe99fb339

For an interval $$\label{eq:I-N}  I_N=(N,2N]\cap\mathbb Z,$$ let $V_q(N)\subset\mathbb C^{I_N}$ be the restrictions of all $q$-periodic functions, and let $P_{q,N}$ be orthogonal projection onto $V_q(N)$ in counting-measure $\ell^2(I_N)$. Put $$\label{eq:prime-fluctuation}  g_N(n)=\Lambda(n)-1.$$ The preceding paper proves the following estimate [`\citep{WangTPCSeven2026}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-008.md#cite-a66c165fb5c69ac7).

###### ref-aae5dc7da5b7f83c

###### ref-f938cebffb5aa45c

###### ref-a7f484e15106b333

###### ref-29c9357237db26b0

###### ref-057005b519a326f2

> **Theorem: Finite-conductor energy escape** \[thm:recalled-energy-escape\] For every fixed $\delta>0$, uniformly for $q\leq N^{1-\delta}$, $$\label{eq:projection-energy}  \|P_{q,N}g_N\|_2^2  \ll_\delta N\frac{q}{\varphi(q)}.$$ Moreover, $$\label{eq:full-prime-energy}  \|g_N\|_2^2\sim N\log N.$$ Consequently, $$\label{eq:orthogonal-best-approximation}  \inf_{v\in V_q(N)}\|g_N-v\|_2^2  =\|g_N\|_2^2-\|P_{q,N}g_N\|_2^2,$$ and hence $$\label{eq:best-periodic-approximation}  \inf_{v\in V_q(N)}\|g_N-v\|_2^2  =N\log N+O_\delta\!\left(N\frac{q}{\varphi(q)}\right)  +o(N\log N).$$

###### ref-78472179a62484fe

For $q\leq(\log N)^C$, the standard estimate $q/\varphi(q)\ll\log\log(3q)$ gives $$\label{eq:polylog-energy-ratio}  \frac{\|P_{q,N}g_N\|_2^2}{\|g_N\|_2^2}  \ll_C\frac{\log\log\log N}{\log N}=o(1).$$ The precise triple logarithm is not important; the vanishing ratio is.

## Why the prime coefficient cannot be inserted

###### ref-df36723fea62d992

Let $v_q=P_{q,N}g_N$ and $\rho_q=g_N-v_q$. This is the best linear $q$-periodic approximation in $\ell^2(I_N)$. The present theorem can transport a bounded unit-supported periodic coefficient of period $q\leq(\log x)^C$. Even if one granted a separate extension of the transfer theorem covering $v_q$, [\[eq:best-periodic-approximation\]](TPC-008.md#ref-057005b519a326f2) shows that $$\label{eq:residual-energy}  \|\rho_q\|_2^2=(1-o(1))N\log N.$$ Thus the untransferred residual contains asymptotically all of the linear prime-fluctuation energy.

###### ref-514775ab164cb43b

> **Proposition: Strict non-specialization** \[prop:strict-nonspecialization\] Neither [\[thm:operator-transfer\]](TPC-008.md#ref-c5f0c034952d497f) nor [\[cor:prime-source-periodic-factor\]](TPC-008.md#ref-76cf141e90cca8f4) can be specialized, by periodic projection alone, to $\beta(n)=\Lambda(n)-1$. Doing so would require a new estimate for the residual $\rho_q$ in [\[eq:residual-energy\]](TPC-008.md#ref-df36723fea62d992). The finite-conductor transfer supplies no such estimate.

> **Proof** The coefficient $\Lambda-1$ is neither bounded nor periodic. More decisively, [\[eq:best-periodic-approximation\]](TPC-008.md#ref-057005b519a326f2) holds after optimizing over the entire linear space of $q$-periodic functions, which is larger than the unit-supported bounded class of [\[thm:operator-transfer\]](TPC-008.md#ref-c5f0c034952d497f). Hence no choice in that class makes the $\ell^2$ residual negligible. The transfer theorem contains no bilinear bound for the residual, so replacing it by zero would be an additional unproved assertion.

This is a boundary of the present method and coefficient class. It is not a proof that every nonlocal, nonlinear, dispersion, or Type II construction must fail. In particular, an $L^2$ energy statement alone does not determine the sign or cancellation of the residual prime-target form.

## The scale inherited from the rough prime window

###### ref-ad79091c96ad72e7

###### ref-aef0710f0e89da75

TPC-5 uses the squarefree rough modulus $$\label{eq:TPC5-Qw}  Q(w)=\prod_{w<p\leq w^2}p,  \qquad  \log Q(w)=(1+o(1))w^2.$$ To transfer an entire $Q(w)$-periodic observable through the present theorem, one needs $Q(w)\leq(\log x)^C$. Therefore $$\label{eq:w-polylog-scale}  w^2\leq(C+o(1))\log\log x,  \qquad  w=O(\sqrt{\log\log x}).$$ The local prime window may grow, but only to this polylogarithmic-conductor scale under the direct Bombieri–Vinogradov proof. Low exact-conductor modes inside a larger $Q(w)$ do not change its ambient unit cutoff, as noted in [\[rem:combined-modulus-boundary\]](TPC-008.md#ref-181247ee9abfcc09).

## A precise next-step ledger

###### ref-53d335f508fb9624

The current interface can be summarized as $$\label{eq:gap-ledger}  \underbrace{\text{periodic low-conductor part}}_{\text{transferred here}}  \quad+\quad  \underbrace{\text{energy-bearing nonperiodic residual}}_{\text{uncontrolled at fixed }h}.$$ Moving from [\[eq:gap-ledger\]](TPC-008.md#ref-53d335f508fb9624) toward a fixed prime-pair problem requires at least one genuinely new ingredient. Possible stages are:

1.  replace the residue-class triangle inequality by an $L^2$ or coefficient-sensitive estimate and determine the largest rigorously transferable periodic complexity;

2.  construct a structured nonperiodic coefficient class whose residual has provably smaller bilinear influence, not merely smaller local Fourier data;

3.  obtain Type II or dispersion control for the energy-bearing residual against $\Lambda(mn+h)$ at a prescribed $h$;

4.  only after such control, reconnect the exact divisor ledger to a fixed prime-pair correlation.

Stages (2) and (3) are not supplied by classical Bombieri–Vinogradov. They are the mathematical gap, rather than an omitted routine estimate. A negative result showing that a proposed coefficient class cannot reduce [\[eq:residual-energy\]](TPC-008.md#ref-df36723fea62d992) would also be a legitimate outcome of the next stage; it would narrow the route without implying a universal parity impossibility theorem.

## A separate finite Mangoldt diagnostic

###### ref-f03972bf54a90c91

It then compares the common row value with the exact rational local main term. This is a sharp-box diagnostic on $N<n\leq2N$, corresponding formally to $\mathcal I_W=1$, rather than an implementation of the smooth weight in the theorem. ## Conclusion

The paper advances the fixed-shift program by one controlled layer. An arbitrary second coefficient remains outside the one-sided Bombieri–Vinogradov reduction, but a bounded low-period coefficient can be passed through $\Lambda(mn+h)$ with an $\ell^1$ row error. The leading term is not merely a density: it is the exact finite operator $\mathsf P_{q,h}$. Its divisor-bundle involutions, conjugate-mode exchange, higher conductor-exponent kernel, damping factors, and even trace moments are all explicit.

The same result also draws a sharper boundary. The actual modulus is $mq$, the residue complexity costs $q$, and TPC-7 shows that the permitted polylogarithmic periodic spaces miss asymptotically all of the $\Lambda-1$ energy. Thus the theorem genuinely reaches a prime target at a prescribed $h$, including $h=2$, while stopping before the Type II residual that contains the fixed prime-pair difficulty. That separation is the main conclusion: a finite arithmetic dynamics survives the prime target exactly, but it is not a substitute for the missing parity-sensitive estimate.
