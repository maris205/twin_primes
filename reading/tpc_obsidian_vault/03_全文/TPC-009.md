## Abstract

Let $h\ne0$ be fixed and let $W\in C_c^1((0,\infty))$. We study the prime-target residual aggregated over all factor scales, $$\mathcal A_h(X;W)=  \sum_{m,n\geq1}(\Lambda(n)-1)\Lambda(mn+h)W(mn/X),$$ and its large-factor portion $\mathcal A_{h,>D}$ obtained by imposing $n>D$. Although $\Lambda-1$ has $L^2$ energy of order $N\log N$ on a dyadic interval, the complete factor aggregation has the exact collapse $$\sum_{n\mid r}(\Lambda(n)-1)=\log r-\tau(r).$$ Consequently $\mathcal A_h$ is a difference of a shifted prime number theorem and a Titchmarsh divisor sum.

Write $$C_h(s)=  \prod_{p\mid h}(1-p^{-s-1})  \prod_{p\nmid h}\left(1+\frac1{(p-1)p^{s+1}}\right),  \qquad C_h=C_h(0).$$ Using the uniform Titchmarsh theorem of Assing–Blomer–Li, we obtain a fixed-$h$ asymptotic with arbitrary logarithmic saving. Classical maximal Bombieri–Vinogradov then evaluates the whole range $n\leq D$, uniformly for $$D\leq X^{1/2}(\log X)^{-B}.$$ Subtracting gives an explicit asymptotic for the complementary range $n>D$, even though most of that range lies beyond the direct arithmetic-progression reduction. When $D=D(X)\to\infty$, its logarithmic leading term is $$(1-C_h)X\log(X/D)\int_0^\infty W(u)\,du.$$ Whenever $(1-C_h)I_0(W)\ne0$, its $X\log X$ coefficient at the square-root cutoff is one half of the complete-sum coefficient. For $h=2$, $$C_2=\frac13\frac{\zeta(2)\zeta(3)}{\zeta(6)}  =0.647865\ldots,$$ so the coefficient is explicitly nonzero.

The result is a scale-aggregation theorem, not a new Titchmarsh, Bombieri–Vinogradov, dispersion, or Type II estimate. The factor collapse is the zero Mellin mode of the multiplicative scale variable. Nonzero Mellin modes do not reduce to $\log r-\tau(r)$, and a single factor layer already contains fixed-shift prime-pair covariance. In particular, the paper proves no fixed prime-pair asymptotic, twin-prime lower bound, or breach of the parity barrier.

# Introduction and exact scope

The preceding finite-conductor transfer result separates a bounded periodic second coefficient from the energy-bearing remainder of $\Lambda-1$. The periodic part can be passed through $\Lambda(mn+h)$ in a one-sided Bombieri–Vinogradov range, but low-period spaces capture only a vanishing fraction of the $L^2$ energy of the prime fluctuation [`\citep{WangTPCSeven2026,WangTPCEight2026}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-009.md#cite-a66c165fb5c69ac7); [\[WangTPCEight2026\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-009.md#cite-03af9e93a15bc281). The next question is therefore not whether that remainder is large—it is—but whether a specified prime-target form can nevertheless average it.

This paper answers that question in one nonlocal coordinate. Fix $h\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}$ and $W\in C_c^1((0,\infty))$. We extend $\Lambda(t)$ by zero for $t\leq0$ and define $$\label{eq:intro-complete-residual}  \mathcal A_h(X;W)  =\sum_{m,n\geq1}    (\Lambda(n)-1)\Lambda(mn+h)W(mn/X).$$ For $D\geq1$, put $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal A_{h,\leq D}(X;W)  &=\sum_{\substack{m,n\geq1\\n\leq D}}    (\Lambda(n)-1)\Lambda(mn+h)W(mn/X),  \label{eq:intro-small-residual}\\  \mathcal A_{h,>D}(X;W)  &=\sum_{\substack{m,n\geq1\\n>D}}    (\Lambda(n)-1)\Lambda(mn+h)W(mn/X).  \label{eq:intro-large-residual}\end{aligned}$$ The split is exact: $$\label{eq:intro-exact-split}  \mathcal A_h=\mathcal A_{h,\leq D}+\mathcal A_{h,>D}.$$

###### ref-9e4b9a0524f86dab

The form [\[eq:intro-complete-residual\]](TPC-009.md#ref-a2530072a2cae421) does not localize $m$ and $n$ separately. It fixes their product scale and sums over every factorization. That distinction is the source of the theorem. If $r=mn$, then $$\label{eq:intro-zero-mode-collapse}  \sum_{n\mid r}(\Lambda(n)-1)  =\log r-\tau(r).$$ Thus the full factor coordinate collapses to the difference between an elementary logarithmic weight and the divisor function. The target remains prime-sensitive, but it is now precisely a Titchmarsh divisor problem.

## The fixed-shift result

###### ref-f497f617ec87b7ac

###### ref-b2c902662882970e

###### ref-e61cc51680d4fe59

Set $$\label{eq:intro-moments-W}  I_0(W)=\int_0^\infty W(u)\,du,  \qquad  I_1(W)=\int_0^\infty W(u)\log u\,du,$$ and define the Euler product $$\label{eq:intro-Chs}  C_h(s)=  \prod_{p\mid h}(1-p^{-s-1})  \prod_{p\nmid h}  \left(1+\frac1{(p-1)p^{s+1}}\right).$$ It is holomorphic in a neighborhood of $s=0$. We abbreviate $$\label{eq:intro-Ch-notation}  C_h=C_h(0),\qquad C_h'=C_h'(0).$$ Here and below, $\gamma$ denotes the Euler–Mascheroni constant.

###### ref-df34583e645b98df

###### ref-b6788599d77e3dbd

###### ref-9b3cc3be1227bae2

###### ref-015766d9b5c8ea79

Our main theorem, stated precisely in [\[thm:large-factor-tail\]](TPC-009.md#ref-e9fbc3bbed9e73c6), gives $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal A_{h,>D}(X;W) ={}&X\Big[(1-C_h)\{(\log X)I_0(W)+I_1(W)\}        -2(\gamma C_h+C_h')I_0(W)\Big]  \notag\\  &-XI_0(W)  \sum_{\substack{n\leq D\\(n,h)=1}}  \frac{\Lambda(n)-1}{\varphi(n)}  +O_{A,h,W}\!\left(\frac{X}{(\log X)^A}\right),  \label{eq:intro-tail-formula}\end{aligned}$$ uniformly for $$\label{eq:intro-D-range}  2\leq D\leq X^{1/2}(\log X)^{-B},$$ where $B=B(A,h,W)$ is sufficiently large. An elementary Dirichlet-series calculation then shows $$\label{eq:intro-coefficient-sum}  \sum_{\substack{n\leq D\\(n,h)=1}}  \frac{\Lambda(n)-1}{\varphi(n)}  =(1-C_h)\log D+\kappa_h+o_h(1)$$ for an explicit finite constant $\kappa_h$. Hence the leading tail is $$\label{eq:intro-tail-leading}  (1-C_h)XI_0(W)\log(X/D).$$

This is a fixed-shift theorem, including $h=2$. It evaluates the complete range beyond the direct Bombieri–Vinogradov cutoff, but only after all of those factor scales have been added with their original signs. No estimate for an individual dyadic $n$-layer follows.

## What is new and what is imported

The analytic estimates used here are established results. The complete sum is evaluated by the uniform Titchmarsh theorem of [`\citet{AssingBlomerLi2021}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-009.md#cite-8984a349ff307f96), whose work supplies strong errors and substantial uniformity in the shift. Earlier decisive forms of the Titchmarsh divisor problem include work of [`\citet{Fouvry1985}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-009.md#cite-fa64d467149629e5) and [`\citet{BombieriFriedlanderIwaniec1986}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-009.md#cite-5cb6d7e2408bcfd5). The range $n\leq D$ is evaluated by the classical maximal Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem [`\citep{Bombieri1965,Vinogradov1965}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-009.md#cite-072be3f1fcf63a7d); [\[Vinogradov1965\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-009.md#cite-91320d5c0c0e4130). We prove no improvement to either input.

The contribution is instead the exact residual coordinate and its boundary:

1.  the full prime fluctuation $\Lambda(n)-1$, not a bounded periodic approximation, is inserted into a genuine fixed-shift prime target;

2.  complete factor aggregation converts it exactly into $\log r-\tau(r)$;

3.  the small-factor Bombieri–Vinogradov evaluation is subtracted from the complete Titchmarsh evaluation, yielding the entire large-factor tail;

4.  Mellin twisting identifies why only the zero scale frequency collapses and why factor localization reintroduces the unresolved bilinear geometry.

This distinction parallels a general lesson of the asymptotic sieve: distribution information and the bilinear information needed to overcome parity are separate inputs [`\citep{FriedlanderIwaniec1998}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-009.md#cite-a670a8e9d85764df). Recent work on prime-producing sieves makes the Type I/Type II ledger still more explicit [`\citep{FordMaynard2024}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-009.md#cite-5b5e23d53c608306). Our theorem resolves one aggregated coordinate; it does not verify the bilinear hypotheses required in those frameworks.

For comparison, prime-pair asymptotics are known for almost all shifts in long windows, with the strongest two-point range relevant here due to [`\citet{MatomakiRadziwillTao2019}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-009.md#cite-03024995d598450c). Newer higher-uniformity results handle arbitrary fixed one-parameter prime-shift patterns $n+a_i h$ in an almost-all-$h$ regime [`\citep{MatomakiRadziwillShaoTaoTeravainen2026}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-009.md#cite-438ea47a51fd4bb1). Neither statement selects a prescribed fixed shift such as $h=2$.

## Strict claim boundary

The following statements are not consequences of this paper.

1.  We do not prove an asymptotic for $\sum_n\Lambda(n)\Lambda(n+h)W(n/X)$ at any prescribed admissible even shift $h$; in particular, we do not treat $h=2$.

2.  We do not estimate a fixed dyadic box $m\asymp M$, $n\asymp N$ with two unrestricted coefficients.

3.  The large-factor theorem permits cancellation between different factor scales. It supplies neither pointwise-in-scale control nor an $L^2$ estimate in the scale variable.

4.  No new level of distribution, dispersion theorem, Type II estimate, twin-prime lower bound, or breach of the sieve parity barrier is asserted.

5.  The phrase “zero Mellin mode” refers to multiplicative factor-scale aggregation. It is not a spectral realization of the primes or an isomorphism with a nonlinear map.

The value of the result is precisely that both sides of the boundary are rigorous: the zero scale frequency closes at every fixed $h$, while the localization step that would isolate a prime-pair layer is displayed rather than assumed.

# Complete factor aggregation and Titchmarsh closure

We first evaluate the complete sum [\[eq:intro-complete-residual\]](TPC-009.md#ref-a2530072a2cae421). The algebraic reduction is elementary, but it is the point at which the factor-scale geometry changes.

###### ref-40f874d96fa03fcc

###### ref-daa09df6fe8945d6

> **Proposition: Exact zero-mode collapse** \[prop:zero-mode-collapse\] For every $X>0$, $$\label{eq:complete-collapse}  \mathcal A_h(X;W)  =\sum_{r\geq1}    (\log r-\tau(r))\Lambda(r+h)W(r/X).$$

> **Proof** Only finitely many pairs $(m,n)$ occur because $W$ is compactly supported. Group them by $r=mn$. The coefficient of $\Lambda(r+h)W(r/X)$ becomes $$\sum_{n\mid r}(\Lambda(n)-1)  =\sum_{n\mid r}\Lambda(n)-\sum_{n\mid r}1.$$ The convolution identities $$\Lambda*\mathbf 1=\log,  \qquad  \mathbf 1*\mathbf 1=\tau$$ give [\[eq:complete-collapse\]](TPC-009.md#ref-daa09df6fe8945d6).

## The two analytic inputs

We record the smooth fixed-shift forms needed below. The first is an elementary consequence of the prime number theorem; the second follows from the uniform Titchmarsh divisor theorem of [`\citet{AssingBlomerLi2021}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-009.md#cite-8984a349ff307f96).

###### ref-4270ec9b7bbf1206

###### ref-ab74a4f9e3b831da

> **Lemma: Shifted logarithmic prime weight** \[lem:shifted-log-prime\] For every $A>0$, $$\label{eq:shifted-log-prime}  \sum_{r\geq1}\log r\,\Lambda(r+h)W(r/X)  =X\{(\log X)I_0(W)+I_1(W)\}  +O_{A,h,W}\!\left(\frac{X}{(\log X)^A}\right).$$

> **Proof** Put $t=r+h$ and apply the prime number theorem with its classical zero-free-region error to the Stieltjes integral against $$F_X(t)=\log(t-h)W((t-h)/X).$$ For sufficiently large $X$, the support stays in the positive $t$-axis. The continuous main term is $$\int_0^\infty\log r\,W(r/X)\,dr  =X\{(\log X)I_0(W)+I_1(W)\}.$$ The derivative of $F_X$ costs only a fixed power of $\log X$, which is absorbed by the zero-free-region saving.

###### ref-192f546371603116

###### ref-75851e5fbb3bc20a

> **Theorem: Smooth uniform Titchmarsh input** \[thm:ABL-smooth-input\] For every $A>0$, $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_{r\geq1}\tau(r)\Lambda(r+h)W(r/X)  ={}&X C_h\{(\log X)I_0(W)+I_1(W)+2\gamma I_0(W)\}  \notag\\  &+2X C_h'I_0(W)  +O_{A,h,W}\!\left(\frac{X}{(\log X)^A}\right).  \label{eq:ABL-smooth-input}\end{aligned}$$

> **Proof** Apply [`\citet[Theorem~1.1]{AssingBlomerLi2021}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-009.md#cite-8984a349ff307f96) with prime variable $t=r+h$, sign parameter $\sigma=1$, and shift $f=-h$. Their Euler product $c_s(f)$ is exactly $C_h(s)$ in [\[eq:intro-Chs\]](TPC-009.md#ref-b2c902662882970e), since it depends on the prime divisors of $f$ only. To check the endpoint hypotheses, enclose $\operatorname{supp}W$ in $[a,b]\subset(0,\infty)$. On every sharp subinterval used in partial summation, let $Y<X_1$ denote the corresponding endpoints in the prime variable $t$. Since $t=r+h\asymp X$ and $h$ is fixed, the conditions $f\ne0$, $\max(-f,0)\leq Y<X_1$, and $X_1+f\asymp X_1$ hold once $X$ is sufficiently large. Their sharp-interval main term has derivative, in the $r$ variable, $$C_h\log r+2\gamma C_h+2C_h'.$$ Stieltjes partial summation against $W(r/X)$ therefore gives [\[eq:ABL-smooth-input\]](TPC-009.md#ref-75851e5fbb3bc20a). The cited theorem supplies an arbitrary power of $\log X$ in the error, so the fixed derivatives of $W$ are harmless.

> **Remark: Provenance** The deep analytic content of [\[thm:ABL-smooth-input\]](TPC-009.md#ref-192f546371603116) belongs to Assing–Blomer–Li. We include the deduction to fix the shift convention, the derivative term $2C_h'$, and the smoothing normalization; no new Titchmarsh estimate is claimed.

## The complete residual asymptotic

###### ref-81518491a20634c6

###### ref-eae4524fdf204b69

> **Theorem: Fixed-shift zero-Mellin closure** \[thm:complete-residual-asymptotic\] Fix $h\ne0$ and $W\in C_c^1((0,\infty))$. For every $A>0$, $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal A_h(X;W)  =X\Big[&(1-C_h)\{(\log X)I_0(W)+I_1(W)\}  \notag\\  &-2(\gamma C_h+C_h')I_0(W)\Big]  +O_{A,h,W}\!\left(\frac{X}{(\log X)^A}\right).  \label{eq:complete-residual-asymptotic}\end{aligned}$$

> **Proof** Insert [\[eq:shifted-log-prime\]](TPC-009.md#ref-ab74a4f9e3b831da) and [\[eq:ABL-smooth-input\]](TPC-009.md#ref-75851e5fbb3bc20a) into the exact identity [\[eq:complete-collapse\]](TPC-009.md#ref-daa09df6fe8945d6) and subtract.

The coefficient $1-C_h$ is not introduced by a heuristic independence model. It is the exact difference between the logarithmic divisor mass and the leading Titchmarsh density in the completely aggregated coordinate.

###### ref-171949c81203f8fe

###### ref-1a571d28dd60be8f

> **Corollary: The shift two constant** \[cor:h-two-constant\] For $h=2$, $$\label{eq:C2-zeta-value}  C_2  =\frac13\frac{\zeta(2)\zeta(3)}{\zeta(6)}  =0.647865\ldots,$$ so $1-C_2>0$.

> **Proof** At $s=0$, $$C_2=\frac12\prod_{p>2}\left(1+\frac1{p(p-1)}\right).$$ The local identity $$1+\frac1{p(p-1)}  =\frac{1-p^{-6}}{(1-p^{-2})(1-p^{-3})}$$ gives $$\prod_p\left(1+\frac1{p(p-1)}\right)  =\frac{\zeta(2)\zeta(3)}{\zeta(6)}.$$ Removing the $p=2$ factor $3/2$ proves the exact formula. The numerical value is included only for orientation.

> **Remark: Energy versus aggregated influence** On $(N,2N]$ one has $\|\Lambda-1\|_2^2\sim N\log N$. Thus [\[thm:complete-residual-asymptotic\]](TPC-009.md#ref-81518491a20634c6) does not come from a small source coefficient. It shows instead that a large-energy coefficient can have a rigorously computable prime-target influence after a sufficiently nonlocal aggregation. This is compatible with, and sharper in a different norm than, the finite-conductor energy escape in TPC-7.

# The small-factor transfer

We next evaluate [\[eq:intro-small-residual\]](TPC-009.md#ref-e98799ca0107c6fd). Here $n$ is the modulus seen by the target prime. This is a one-sided Type I problem, so the classical square-root level applies.

###### ref-f738a0a021ac5a6c

###### ref-c56b90ca451767de

###### ref-8f8009bf94f47d36

> **Theorem: Coefficient-uniform fixed-product Type I estimate** \[thm:fixed-product-Type-I\] Fix $h\ne0$, $W\in C_c^1((0,\infty))$, and $A>0$. There is $B=B(A,h,W)>0$ such that, whenever $$\label{eq:fixed-product-BV-range}  1\leq D\leq\frac{X^{1/2}}{(\log X)^B},$$ one has, uniformly for arbitrary coefficients $|a_n|\leq1$, $$\label{eq:fixed-product-Type-I}  \sum_{\substack{n\leq D\\(n,h)=1}}a_n  \left\{    \sum_{m\geq1}\Lambda(nm+h)W(nm/X)    -\frac{X}{\varphi(n)}I_0(W)  \right\}  \ll_{A,h,W}\frac{X}{(\log X)^A}.$$

> **Proof** For $(n,h)=1$, the substitution $t=nm+h$ places the target in the reduced class $h\pmod n$ and changes the weight to $W((t-h)/X)$. Stieltjes partial summation bounds the row error by a constant depending on $W$ times $$\max_{y\ll_W X+|h|}  \left|   \sum_{\substack{t\leq y\\t\equiv h\pmod n}}\Lambda(t)   -\frac{y}{\varphi(n)}  \right|.$$ The continuous main term is exactly $$\frac1{\varphi(n)}\int_0^\infty W((t-h)/X)\,dt  =\frac{X}{\varphi(n)}I_0(W).$$ Sum the maximal row errors over $n\leq D$ and apply the classical maximal Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem with a slightly larger requested logarithmic saving [`\citep{Bombieri1965,Vinogradov1965,IwaniecKowalski2004}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-009.md#cite-072be3f1fcf63a7d); [\[Vinogradov1965\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-009.md#cite-91320d5c0c0e4130); [\[IwaniecKowalski2004\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-009.md#cite-49dfea87eaada156).

Rows not coprime to $h$ are supported on a finite collection of prime-power targets. We isolate this elementary point because the source coefficient is now $\Lambda(n)-1$, rather than a Möbius divisor weight.

###### ref-bb9de9d5fce88d2a

###### ref-8a1b100c594a285a

###### ref-7890a59990f1b1fb

> **Lemma: Noncoprime residual rows** \[lem:noncoprime-residual-rows\] For every $D\leq2X$ and every $\varepsilon>0$, $$\label{eq:noncoprime-residual-rows}  \sum_{\substack{n\leq D\\(n,h)>1}}  |\Lambda(n)-1|  \sum_{m\geq1}\Lambda(nm+h)|W(nm/X)|  \ll_{h,W,\varepsilon}X^\varepsilon.$$ Without an artificial upper cutoff, one also has $$\label{eq:noncoprime-residual-tail}  \sum_{\substack{m,n\geq1\\n>D\\(n,h)>1}}  |\Lambda(n)-1|\Lambda(nm+h)|W(nm/X)|  \ll_{h,W,\varepsilon}X^\varepsilon.$$

> **Proof** For either displayed sum, put $r=nm$ and enlarge the divisor range. The relevant left-hand side is at most $$\sum_{r\asymp_W X}\Lambda(r+h)|W(r/X)|  \sum_{\substack{n\mid r\\(n,h)>1}}(\Lambda(n)+1).$$ If the inner sum is nonempty, some prime $p\mid h$ divides $r$. Then $p\mid r+h$. If $\Lambda(r+h)\ne0$, it follows that $r+h=p^k$. For each of the finitely many primes dividing $h$, only $O_{h,W}(1)$ powers lie in the fixed-ratio interval forced by the support of $W$. Finally, $$\sum_{n\mid r}(\Lambda(n)+1)=\log r+\tau(r)\ll_\varepsilon X^\varepsilon,$$ after reducing $\varepsilon$ to absorb the remaining logarithms.

###### ref-1007357810219588

> **Theorem: Small-factor prime-residual transfer** \[thm:small-factor-residual\] Fix $A>0$ and choose $B=B(A,h,W)$ sufficiently large. Uniformly for $$\label{eq:small-factor-D-range}  2\leq D\leq\frac{X^{1/2}}{(\log X)^B},$$ one has $$\label{eq:small-factor-residual}  \mathcal A_{h,\leq D}(X;W)  =XI_0(W)   \sum_{\substack{n\leq D\\(n,h)=1}}   \frac{\Lambda(n)-1}{\varphi(n)}  +O_{A,h,W}\!\left(\frac{X}{(\log X)^A}\right).$$

> **Proof** The noncoprime rows are absorbed by [\[lem:noncoprime-residual-rows\]](TPC-009.md#ref-bb9de9d5fce88d2a). On the remaining rows apply [\[thm:fixed-product-Type-I\]](TPC-009.md#ref-f738a0a021ac5a6c) with $$a_n=\frac{\Lambda(n)-1}{1+\log D}.$$ These coefficients have modulus at most one. Multiply the result back by $1+\log D$ and request $A+3$ logarithmic saving in the Type I theorem. This proves [\[eq:small-factor-residual\]](TPC-009.md#ref-1007357810219588).

> **Remark: Why the coefficient is permitted here** The source factor $\Lambda(n)-1$ is unbounded and nonperiodic, but in [\[thm:small-factor-residual\]](TPC-009.md#ref-ad2a8910f75816ac) it weights the *modulus* average. After division by $1+\log D$, maximal Bombieri–Vinogradov is uniform in that row coefficient. This does not create a free second coefficient inside an individual prime progression and therefore does not constitute a Type II estimate.

# The large-factor tail and the scale-localization boundary

We now subtract the direct Type I range from the complete zero-mode asymptotic. This evaluates an aggregate lying mostly beyond the Bombieri–Vinogradov cutoff.

###### ref-e9fbc3bbed9e73c6

###### ref-18d5615d4ae98f08

###### ref-814c4eaab1819ca7

> **Theorem: Complete large-factor residual tail** \[thm:large-factor-tail\] Fix $h\ne0$, $W\in C_c^1((0,\infty))$, and $A>0$. There is $B=B(A,h,W)>0$ such that, uniformly for $$\label{eq:large-tail-D-range}  2\leq D\leq\frac{X^{1/2}}{(\log X)^B},$$ one has $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal A_{h,>D}(X;W) ={}&X\Big[(1-C_h)\{(\log X)I_0(W)+I_1(W)\}        -2(\gamma C_h+C_h')I_0(W)\Big]  \notag\\  &-XI_0(W)  \sum_{\substack{n\leq D\\(n,h)=1}}  \frac{\Lambda(n)-1}{\varphi(n)}  +O_{A,h,W}\!\left(\frac{X}{(\log X)^A}\right).  \label{eq:large-factor-tail}\end{aligned}$$

> **Proof** Use the exact split [\[eq:intro-exact-split\]](TPC-009.md#ref-b26c85a9b8f7b241). Substitute the complete evaluation [\[eq:complete-residual-asymptotic\]](TPC-009.md#ref-eae4524fdf204b69) and the small-factor evaluation [\[eq:small-factor-residual\]](TPC-009.md#ref-1007357810219588), then subtract.

The theorem keeps the finite coefficient sum visible because this gives an arbitrary logarithmic error uniformly throughout the stated range. Its asymptotic shape follows from a Dirichlet-series calculation.

###### ref-1296b1bc68550f00

###### ref-e25d974e3041e4e0

###### ref-83375f7f75a2682e

###### ref-4b8d1a46786fc08f

###### ref-82145d9582621a1f

###### ref-560d17537c2be5a4

> **Lemma: The residual coefficient average** \[lem:residual-coefficient-average\] There is a finite constant $\kappa_h$ such that $$\label{eq:residual-coefficient-average}  \sum_{\substack{n\leq D\\(n,h)=1}}  \frac{\Lambda(n)-1}{\varphi(n)}  =(1-C_h)\log D+\kappa_h+o_h(1)  \qquad(D\to\infty).$$ More precisely, $$\label{eq:reciprocal-phi-average}  \sum_{\substack{n\leq D\\(n,h)=1}}\frac1{\varphi(n)}  =C_h\log D+\gamma C_h+C_h'+o_h(1),$$ and $$\label{eq:Lambda-over-phi-average}  \sum_{\substack{n\leq D\\(n,h)=1}}  \frac{\Lambda(n)}{\varphi(n)}  =\log D+\kappa_{\Lambda,h}+o_h(1),$$ where $$\label{eq:kappa-h-definition}  \kappa_h=\kappa_{\Lambda,h}-\gamma C_h-C_h'.$$ One may take $$\label{eq:kappa-Lambda-explicit}  \kappa_{\Lambda,h}  =-\gamma   +\sum_{p\nmid h}\frac{\log p}{(p-1)^2}   -\sum_{p\mid h}\frac{\log p}{p-1},$$ whose infinite sum is absolutely convergent.

###### ref-6312434004bd92a6

###### ref-7c3f1f8e320c15d7

> **Proof** For $\Re s>0$, an Euler-factor calculation gives $$\label{eq:phi-Dirichlet-series}  \sum_{\substack{n\geq1\\(n,h)=1}}  \frac1{\varphi(n)n^s}  =\zeta(s+1)C_h(s).$$ Indeed, for $p\nmid h$ the Euler factor left after removing the $\zeta(s+1)$ factor is $$1+\frac1{(p-1)p^{s+1}},$$ whereas for $p\mid h$ it is $1-p^{-s-1}$. The product $C_h(s)$ is absolutely convergent in a neighborhood to the right of $\Re s=-1/2$. Convolving its coefficients with $1/n$, and using $\sum_{k\leq y}k^{-1}=\log y+\gamma+o(1)$, gives [\[eq:reciprocal-phi-average\]](TPC-009.md#ref-83375f7f75a2682e); the constant term is $\gamma C_h+C_h'$.
>
> For the von Mangoldt numerator, $$\label{eq:Lambda-phi-Dirichlet-series}  \sum_{\substack{n\geq1\\(n,h)=1}}  \frac{\Lambda(n)}{\varphi(n)n^s}  =\sum_{p\nmid h}  \frac{(\log p)p^{-s}}  {(p-1)(1-p^{-s-1})}.$$ An explicit prime-power comparison avoids any Tauberian issue. Namely, $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_{\substack{n\leq D\\(n,h)=1}}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\varphi(n)}  ={}&\sum_{n\leq D}\frac{\Lambda(n)}n  +\sum_{\substack{p\nmid h,\ k\geq1\\p^k\leq D}}    \frac{\log p}{p^k(p-1)}  -\sum_{\substack{p\mid h,\ k\geq1\\p^k\leq D}}    \frac{\log p}{p^k}.\end{aligned}$$ The last two sums converge absolutely as $D\to\infty$, while the standard prime number theorem consequence $\sum_{n\leq D}\Lambda(n)/n=\log D-\gamma+o(1)$ gives [\[eq:Lambda-over-phi-average\]](TPC-009.md#ref-4b8d1a46786fc08f) with [\[eq:kappa-Lambda-explicit\]](TPC-009.md#ref-560d17537c2be5a4). Subtracting [\[eq:reciprocal-phi-average\]](TPC-009.md#ref-83375f7f75a2682e) proves [\[eq:residual-coefficient-average\]](TPC-009.md#ref-e25d974e3041e4e0) and [\[eq:kappa-h-definition\]](TPC-009.md#ref-82145d9582621a1f).

###### ref-dc0de10c898be7ea

###### ref-b191633c50a09629

> **Corollary: Asymptotic shape of the tail** \[cor:large-tail-shape\] Suppose $D=D(X)\to\infty$ and that [\[eq:large-tail-D-range\]](TPC-009.md#ref-18d5615d4ae98f08) holds. Then $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal A_{h,>D}(X;W) ={}&(1-C_h)XI_0(W)\log(X/D)  \notag\\  &+X\Big[(1-C_h)I_1(W)  -\{2(\gamma C_h+C_h')+\kappa_h\}I_0(W)\Big]  +o_{h,W}(X).  \label{eq:large-tail-shape}\end{aligned}$$

> **Proof** Insert [\[eq:residual-coefficient-average\]](TPC-009.md#ref-e25d974e3041e4e0) into [\[eq:large-factor-tail\]](TPC-009.md#ref-814c4eaab1819ca7). Choose one fixed $A>1$; the displayed error is then $o(X)$.

###### ref-9a8bcc7ca3090337

###### ref-f7dab4d2da222270

> **Corollary: Half of the leading logarithmic coefficient** \[cor:half-leading-mass\] Let $$\label{eq:square-root-D}  D=\left\lfloor\frac{X^{1/2}}{(\log X)^B}\right\rfloor$$ with fixed sufficiently large $B$. If $(1-C_h)I_0(W)\ne0$, then $$\label{eq:half-leading-mass}  \mathcal A_{h,>D}(X;W)  =\frac{1-C_h}{2}XI_0(W)\log X  +O_{h,W,B}(X\log\log X).$$

> **Proof** Use $$\log(X/D)=\tfrac12\log X+B\log\log X+o(1)$$ in [\[eq:large-tail-shape\]](TPC-009.md#ref-b191633c50a09629).

> **Remark: What “beyond Bombieri–Vinogradov” means** The moduli $n>D$ in [\[thm:large-factor-tail\]](TPC-009.md#ref-e9fbc3bbed9e73c6) are not controlled one by one or in dyadic blocks by the proof. Their *complete signed sum* is known because it equals the Titchmarsh-evaluated total minus the Bombieri–Vinogradov-evaluated short part. The theorem must not be read as a new level of distribution for those moduli.

## Why the collapse is only the zero Mellin mode

###### ref-279da11c7c57b594

The factorization $r=mn$ carries a multiplicative scale coordinate. To see what is lost by complete aggregation, twist the divisor pair by $(n/m)^{it}$. For every real $t$, $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_{mn=r}(\Lambda(n)-1)(n/m)^{it}  =r^{-it}\left\{    \sum_{d\mid r}\Lambda(d)d^{2it}-\sigma_{2it}(r)  \right\}.  \label{eq:Mellin-twisted-divisor-sum}\end{aligned}$$ At $t=0$ this is $\log r-\tau(r)$. For $t\ne0$, the first divisor sum no longer collapses to $\log r$, and the second is a genuinely twisted divisor function; here $\sigma_z(r)=\sum_{d\mid r}d^z$.

###### ref-e3091324a863c376

###### ref-4739dc92e5df08f5

If $V$ is a smooth cutoff in the ratio $n/m$, Mellin inversion writes $$\label{eq:Mellin-transform-convention}  \widehat V(t)=\int_0^\infty V(y)y^{-it}\,\frac{dy}{y},$$ and, with this convention, $$\label{eq:Mellin-inversion-scale}  V(n/m)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}\widehat V(t)(n/m)^{it}\,dt$$ for a smooth compactly supported $V$. Thus localizing a factor box requires a continuum of nonzero frequencies. The value at $t=0$ does not control that integral, just as the mean of a function does not control its pointwise values.

## The fixed layer already contains the prime-pair problem

###### ref-37d77f0e0c071161

The $m=1$ layer of the complete form is $$\label{eq:m-one-layer}  \sum_{n\geq1}(\Lambda(n)-1)\Lambda(n+h)W(n/X).$$ The prime number theorem evaluates the part with coefficient $-1$. An asymptotic for the remaining product $\Lambda(n)\Lambda(n+h)$ at a fixed admissible $h$ is the Hardy–Littlewood prime-pair problem [`\citep{HardyLittlewood1923}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-009.md#cite-f4ffa381ee727373). At $h=2$ it is the twin-prime correlation. For the square-root choice of $D$, the full layer [\[eq:m-one-layer\]](TPC-009.md#ref-37d77f0e0c071161) lies inside $\mathcal A_{h,>D}$, but [\[thm:large-factor-tail\]](TPC-009.md#ref-e9fbc3bbed9e73c6) allows it to cancel against all $m\geq2$ layers. Therefore the aggregate theorem supplies no estimate for [\[eq:m-one-layer\]](TPC-009.md#ref-37d77f0e0c071161) by itself.

###### ref-bca04813c7ef5a19

Adding a prime weight in the other factor does not preserve the collapse. Indeed, $$\label{eq:two-prime-source-convolution}  \sum_{mn=r}\Lambda(m)(\Lambda(n)-1)  =(\Lambda*\Lambda)(r)-\log r.$$ The shifted target sum associated with $\Lambda*\Lambda$ is a parity-sensitive prime/semiprime correlation, not the Titchmarsh divisor sum in [\[thm:ABL-smooth-input\]](TPC-009.md#ref-192f546371603116). This marks the next genuinely new analytic interface.

> **Remark: A one-way implication ledger** The proved implications are $$\begin{gathered}  \text{complete factor aggregation}  \ +\ \text{Assing--Blomer--Li}  \Longrightarrow \text{fixed-$h$ zero-mode asymptotic},\\  \text{maximal Bombieri--Vinogradov}  \Longrightarrow \text{small-factor residual asymptotic},\\  \text{the two evaluations and exact subtraction}  \Longrightarrow \text{complete large-factor tail asymptotic}.  \end{gathered}$$ None of these arrows reverses to give a dyadic Type II estimate or a fixed-layer prime-pair asymptotic.

# Conclusion and the next analytic interface

The paper isolates one coordinate in which the full prime fluctuation can be passed through a prescribed fixed-shift prime target. Complete factor-scale aggregation turns $\Lambda-1$ into $\log-\tau$ exactly. A proven Titchmarsh theorem evaluates the total, classical Bombieri–Vinogradov evaluates the short factor range, and subtraction evaluates the complete long-factor tail. When its leading coefficient is nonzero, the $X\log X$ coefficient at a square-root cutoff is one half of the complete-sum coefficient.

This positive result is deliberately paired with its limitation. It is a statement about one scalar scale coordinate, not about the distribution of the individual layers composing it. Mellin inversion shows that a dyadic factor localization requires nonzero scale frequencies, for which the elementary divisor collapse disappears. At the endpoint $m=1$, the missing localized estimate is already the fixed-shift prime-pair problem.

The next defensible layer is therefore not to infer a localized theorem from the zero mode. It is to study the nonzero Mellin family in a region where existing dispersion or Type II estimates genuinely apply, keeping the coefficient class and averaging axes explicit. A successful result there would quantify how much scale resolution can be recovered. A negative result would still identify the exact norm or coefficient geometry that blocks recovery. Neither outcome is assumed here.
