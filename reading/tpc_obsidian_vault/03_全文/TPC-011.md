## Abstract

Put $u(n)=\Lambda(n)-1$, fix a real-valued $W\in C_c^1((0,\infty))$, and consider the large-source-factor transform $$\mathcal T_{h,D}(X;t)=  \sum_{\substack{n>D\\m\geq1}}  u(n)\Lambda(mn+h)W(mn/X)(n/m)^{it}.$$ The target-free form $\mathcal M_D$, obtained by replacing $\Lambda(mn+h)$ by $1$, is the first-moment model under a long smooth average of the shift. We study the centered transform $\mathcal R_{h,D}=\mathcal T_{h,D}-\mathcal M_D$.

Writing uniquely $n=ak$, $m=bk$, $(a,b)=1$, compresses every equal-ratio class into a primitive factor ray. The resulting frequencies $\log(a/b)$ have spacing at least $$\delta_X=2\operatorname{arsinh}\!\left(\frac{1}{2\beta X}\right)$$ when $\operatorname{supp}W\subset[\alpha,\beta]$. The generalized Hilbert inequality of Montgomery and Vaughan therefore gives, for every fixed shift and every $T\geq1$, $$\int_{-T}^{T}|\mathcal R_{h,D}(X;t)|^2dt  =2T\mathcal E_{h,D}(X)+O_W(X\mathcal E_{h,D}(X)),$$ where $\mathcal E_{h,D}$ is the exact squared ray energy. A raywise Cauchy ledger proves $\mathcal E_{h,D}\ll X(\log X)^4$. Consequently every prescribed fixed integer shift has mean-square $o(X^2)$ per Mellin frequency on bands whose length grows faster than $(\log X)^4$. This is frequency-density cancellation, not a fixed-frequency asymptotic. With a normalized Fejér kernel, distinct rays are removed exactly once $T\delta_X\geq1$.

The ray energy itself becomes arithmetically evaluable after squaring first and then averaging the shift. For nonnegative $G\in C_c^1((0,\infty))$, $X\leq H\leq X^C$, and $1\leq D\leq X^{1-\varepsilon}$, we prove $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_hG(h/H)\mathcal E_{h,D}(X)  ={}&\frac12HX I_0(G)I_2(W)\log H\\  &\times\bigl((\log X)^2-(\log D)^2\bigr)\\  &+O\!\left(HX(\log X)^2\log\log(3X)\right).\end{aligned}$$ The diagonal $\Lambda^2$ term supplies the extra logarithm. Every same-ray off-diagonal target correlation is handled only by a two-linear- forms Selberg upper sieve and an explicit $O(X\log^2X)$ source-ray ledger. This yields a genuine hard-band asymptotic for $T/X\to\infty$ and an exact Fejér-band asymptotic already at $T\asymp X$.

Finally, a determinant-layer formula identifies the unresolved mesoscopic band, and a fixed smooth endpoint selector shows that an $o(X^2)$ fixed-shift band error, together with a compatible filtered model and $o(X)$ Fourier tails, would recover the $m=1$ Hardy–Littlewood functional. Equal-ratio compression never separates the terms $\Lambda(abk^2+h)$ on one ray. Thus no fixed-shift prime-pair asymptotic, twin-prime lower bound, new level of distribution, or breach of the parity barrier is claimed.

# Introduction and claim ledger

The factor-scale coordinate introduced in TPC–9 and TPC–10 records a factorization $r=mn$ by $$u_{m,n}=\log(n/m).$$ At frequency zero, summing over all divisors gives the exact collapse $$\sum_{n\mid r}(\Lambda(n)-1)=\log r-\tau(r),$$ which leads to a fixed-shift Titchmarsh evaluation [`\citep{AssingBlomerLi2021,WangTPC9}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-011.md#cite-8984a349ff307f96); [\[WangTPC9\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-011.md#cite-7e6663b79fd9fb10). TPC–10 proved coarse two-sided factor localization and arbitrary fixed smooth localization on the Type I side. It also showed that finitely many Mellin coordinates do not stably determine a local factor-scale window, and proposed a continuum-band mean square as the next analytic target [`\citep{WangTPC10}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-011.md#cite-a4a66e4ca09481f5).

The present paper treats that target in two different quantifier regimes. Let $X\geq2$, $h\in\mathbb Z$, and $D\geq1$. Fix throughout $$u(n):=\Lambda(n)-1,  \qquad W\in C_c^1((0,\infty);\mathbb R),  \qquad \operatorname{supp}W\subset[\alpha,\beta]  \label{eq:intro-basic-data}$$ with $0<\alpha<\beta<\infty$. We extend $\Lambda(q)$ by zero for $q\leq0$. For any integrable function $F$ on $(0,\infty)$, put $$I_0(F):=\int_0^\infty F(v)\,dv,  \qquad  I_2(F):=\int_0^\infty |F(v)|^2\,dv.  \label{eq:moment-functionals}$$ All asymptotic statements are taken as $X\to\infty$; parameters declared fixed in a theorem are held fixed, while $D,H$, and $T$ may vary in the displayed ranges. Define $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal T_{h,D}(X;t)  &:={}  \sum_{\substack{n>D\\m\geq1}}  u(n)\Lambda(mn+h)W(mn/X)(n/m)^{it},  \label{eq:intro-raw-tail}\\  \mathcal M_D(X;t)  &:={}  \sum_{\substack{n>D\\m\geq1}}  u(n)W(mn/X)(n/m)^{it},  \label{eq:intro-source-model}\\  \mathcal R_{h,D}(X;t)  &:={}\mathcal T_{h,D}(X;t)-\mathcal M_D(X;t).  \label{eq:intro-centered-tail}\end{aligned}$$ Thus $\mathcal R_{h,D}$ is obtained by replacing the target factor $\Lambda(mn+h)$ by $\Lambda(mn+h)-1$. The subtraction is algebraic for every fixed $h$. It becomes a genuine first-moment model only after the long shift average proved in [\[prop:first-moment-model\]](TPC-011.md#ref-ae080051785bb583).

## What is proved

There are four main outputs.

1.  Every factor pair admits a unique representation $$n=ak,\qquad m=bk,\qquad (a,b)=1.$$ Equal ratios are thereby compressed into one primitive ray $(a,b)$. Distinct ray frequencies $\log(a/b)$ have spacing $\gg_W X^{-1}$. Applying the continuous mean-value theorem of [`\citet{MontgomeryVaughan1974}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-011.md#cite-3ce1bce980fce1d5) gives an exact fixed-shift diagonal law up to an $O(X)$ resolution error.

2.  The ray energy has the unconditional fixed-shift bound $$\mathcal E_{h,D}(X)\ll_{C,W}X(\log X)^4   \qquad (|h|\leq X^C).$$ Hence a band of length $T\gg(\log X)^4$ gives $o(X)$ cancellation in root-mean-square per frequency, for every prescribed shift. This does not evaluate any specified Mellin frequency.

3.  After the error is squared, a smooth average over shifts $h\asymp H$, $H\geq X$, permits a full arithmetic evaluation of the ray energy. The $k=\ell$ target diagonal has size $H\log H$. Same-ray terms $k\ne\ell$ require prime-pair upper bounds, but not prime-pair asymptotics: a Selberg upper sieve costs only $H\log\log X$, and a source-ray ledger sums their coefficients to $O_W(X\log^2X)$. The diagonal therefore wins by one logarithm.

4.  A compact-Fourier-support kernel resolves the factor rays exactly at the natural threshold $T\asymp X$. Below that threshold, the surviving interactions are indexed by the integer determinant $ad-bc$. At the opposite endpoint, a fixed smooth scale window can select $m=1$ exactly on every product band of multiplicative width less than four. This gives a quantitative $o(X^2)$ strength threshold for any fixed-shift continuum-band model that could imply the corresponding Hardy–Littlewood functional.

The generalized Hilbert inequality is the only input in the fixed-shift spectral theorem. The averaged-shift energy theorem additionally uses the prime number theorem and a standard two-linear-forms Selberg upper sieve. It does not import an averaged Hardy–Littlewood asymptotic. For comparison, Theorem 1.3(i) of [`\citet{MatomakiRadziwillTao2019}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-011.md#cite-03024995d598450c) evaluates two-point prime correlations for almost all shifts in the substantially shorter range $X^{8/33+\varepsilon}\leq H\leq X^{1-\varepsilon}$; TPC–7 already transferred that result to a different complete defect [`\citep{WangTPC7}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-011.md#cite-2ec3fed4bb89ce85). The later higher-uniformity theorem of [`\citet[Theorem~1.5]{MatomakiRadziwillShaoTaoTeravainen2026}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-011.md#cite-438ea47a51fd4bb1) treats higher orders while retaining the earlier two-point input. Our theorem instead evaluates a continuum of Mellin frequencies after a longer second-moment shift average.

## What is not proved

The order of averaging is essential. We first form $|\mathcal R_{h,D}(X;t)|^2$ and only then average $h$. A coherent average of $\mathcal R_{h,D}$ before squaring is much easier and says nothing about a typical shift. Likewise, an average over Mellin frequencies does not select $t=0$ or any other prescribed frequency.

On one primitive ray, the target arguments are $$abk^2+h.$$ No amount of separation between distinct values of $a/b$ decorrelates different $k$ on the same ray. In particular, the ray $(1,1)$ already contains values of $k^2+h$, while the factor endpoint $b=1$, $k=1$ contains the fixed-shift prime-pair functional. We neither estimate these objects at a prescribed $h$ nor assert that such an estimate is impossible. No twin-prime lower bound, fixed-shift Hardy–Littlewood asymptotic, new Type II estimate, new distribution level, or parity-barrier breakthrough follows.

# Primitive factor rays and logarithmic separation

The Mellin phase depends only on the reduced ratio of the two factors. This elementary observation is the structural starting point of the paper.

###### ref-658f4a37763f7519

###### ref-aa21830f9e59767f

> **Proposition: Exact factor-ray compression** \[prop:ray-compression\] For every $m,n\geq1$, write $$k=(m,n),\qquad a=n/k,\qquad b=m/k.$$ Then $(a,b)=1$, the representation $n=ak$, $m=bk$ is unique, and $$\mathcal R_{h,D}(X;t)  =\sum_{\substack{a,b\geq1\\(a,b)=1}}  c_{a,b}(h;X,D)(a/b)^{it},  \label{eq:ray-compression}$$ where $$c_{a,b}(h;X,D)  :=\sum_{\substack{k\geq1\\ak>D}}  u(ak)\bigl(\Lambda(abk^2+h)-1\bigr)  W(abk^2/X).  \label{eq:centered-ray-coefficient}$$ Only rays with $ab\leq\beta X$ occur. The raw transform $\mathcal T_{h,D}$ has the same decomposition with the target factor in [\[eq:centered-ray-coefficient\]](TPC-011.md#ref-aa21830f9e59767f) replaced by $\Lambda(abk^2+h)$.

> **Proof** The greatest-common-divisor decomposition is unique, and $$mn=abk^2,\qquad \frac{n}{m}=\frac{a}{b}.$$ Substitution in [\[eq:intro-centered-tail\]](TPC-011.md#ref-da478b9a228c35dd) gives [\[eq:ray-compression\]](TPC-011.md#ref-658f4a37763f7519). If a term is nonzero, then $abk^2/X\in\operatorname{supp}W\subset[\alpha,\beta]$, hence $ab\leq\beta X$.

###### ref-5f3ba661e5e880e6

###### ref-d4075c615b029d3c

> **Definition: Ray energy** \[def:ray-energy\] The centered factor-ray energy is $$\mathcal E_{h,D}(X)  :=\sum_{\substack{a,b\geq1\\(a,b)=1}}  |c_{a,b}(h;X,D)|^2.  \label{eq:ray-energy}$$ The analogous raw energy, with $\Lambda$ in place of $\Lambda-1$ at the target, is denoted by $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{raw}}_{h,D}(X)$.

Equal ratios have now been combined before any mean-square theorem is applied. This is indispensable: separate pairs on one ray have exactly the same Mellin frequency and cannot be made orthogonal by increasing the length of the frequency interval.

###### ref-8b3a29ddded102f2

###### ref-9831e79d7071f2d5

> **Lemma: Hyperbolic log-ratio spacing** \[lem:ratio-spacing\] Let $(a,b)=(c,d)=1$, let $ab,cd\leq\beta X$, and suppose $(a,b)\ne(c,d)$. Then $$\left|\log\frac{a}{b}-\log\frac{c}{d}\right|  \geq  \delta_X,  \qquad  \delta_X:=2\operatorname{arsinh}\!\left(\frac{1}{2\beta X}\right).  \label{eq:exact-ratio-spacing}$$ In particular, $\delta_X\asymp_W X^{-1}$.

> **Proof** Put $A=ad$ and $B=bc$. Since the two fractions are distinct, $|A-B|\geq1$. Moreover, $$AB=abcd=(ab)(cd)\leq(\beta X)^2.$$ The exact identity $$2\sinh\!\left(\frac12\left|\log\frac{A}{B}\right|\right)  =\frac{|A-B|}{\sqrt{AB}}$$ therefore gives $$2\sinh\!\left(\frac12\left|\log\frac{a/b}{c/d}\right|\right)  \geq\frac1{\beta X}.$$ Applying the increasing inverse hyperbolic sine proves [\[eq:exact-ratio-spacing\]](TPC-011.md#ref-9831e79d7071f2d5).

> **Remark: Why the scale is $X^{-1}$ rather than $X^{-2}$** The cross-products $ad$ and $bc$ could individually be large, but their product is at most $(\beta X)^2$. Near equality they are therefore both $O_W(X)$, and the integer determinant $ad-bc$ supplies a gap of order $X^{-1}$. Ignoring the hyperbolic constraints $ab,cd\ll X$ would lose this improvement.

> **Remark: Near-sharp pairs** The rays $(N,1)$ and $(N-1,1)$ have determinant one and log gap $$\log\frac{N}{N-1}=\frac1N+O(N^{-2}).$$ Taking $N\asymp X$ shows that no uniform separation scale larger than a constant multiple of $X^{-1}$ is available for arbitrary hyperbolic coefficients. Thus the $X^{-1}$ lower-bound scale is sharp up to constants; equivalently, no uniform resolution time $o(X)$ is possible. This threshold is geometric, before any prime weight is inserted.

# Fixed-shift continuum dephasing

We first keep the shift completely prescribed. The only cancellation in this section comes from integration in the Mellin frequency.

###### ref-05e733060da5a9a5

###### ref-8f91cf55511ec199

> **Theorem: Fixed-shift factor-ray mean square** \[thm:fixed-ray-mean-square\] Let $I\subset\mathbb R$ be any bounded interval. Then $$\int_I|\mathcal R_{h,D}(X;t)|^2dt  =|I|\mathcal E_{h,D}(X)+O_W\!\left(X\mathcal E_{h,D}(X)\right).  \label{eq:general-interval-ray-law}$$ In particular, $$\int_{-T}^{T}|\mathcal R_{h,D}(X;t)|^2dt  =2T\mathcal E_{h,D}(X)+O_W\!\left(X\mathcal E_{h,D}(X)\right).  \label{eq:hard-band-ray-law}$$ The same statements hold for $\mathcal T_{h,D}$ and $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{raw}}_{h,D}$.

> **Proof** By [\[prop:ray-compression\]](TPC-011.md#ref-b5e8cd5f3215b8df), the centered transform is an exponential polynomial with coefficients $c_{a,b}$ and distinct real frequencies $\lambda_{a,b}=\log(a/b)$. The frequencies are $\delta_X$-separated by [\[lem:ratio-spacing\]](TPC-011.md#ref-8b3a29ddded102f2). Corollary 2, equations (1.8)–(1.9), of the generalized Hilbert inequality of [`\citet{MontgomeryVaughan1974}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-011.md#cite-3ce1bce980fce1d5) gives $$\int_I\left|\sum_\rho c_\rho e^{it\lambda_\rho}\right|^2dt  =|I|\sum_\rho|c_\rho|^2  +O\!\left(\delta_X^{-1}\sum_\rho|c_\rho|^2\right).$$ Translation of $I$ only modulates the coefficients and does not change their squared norm. Since $\delta_X^{-1}\ll_W X$, this is [\[eq:general-interval-ray-law\]](TPC-011.md#ref-05e733060da5a9a5). The raw form has the same frequency set.

The arithmetic coefficients have a substantially better energy bound than one obtains by squaring the number of factor pairs.

###### ref-144e92939e76ee9b

> **Lemma: A source-ray square ledger** \[lem:source-ray-square-ledger\] Uniformly for $Y\geq2$, $$\sum_{ak^2\leq Y}\frac{(\Lambda(ak)-1)^2}{ak}  \ll (\log(2Y))^2.  \label{eq:source-ray-square-ledger}$$

> **Proof** Use $(\Lambda-1)^2\leq2(1+\Lambda^2)$. The contribution of $1$ is $$\sum_{k\leq\sqrt Y}\frac1k  \sum_{a\leq Y/k^2}\frac1a  \ll(\log(2Y))^2.$$ If $\Lambda(ak)\ne0$, write $ak=p^j$. Every representation has $k=p^\ell$, $a=p^{j-\ell}$, and the extra constraint is $p^{j+\ell}\leq Y$. Dropping that constraint except for $p^j\leq Y$ gives $$\sum_{p^j\leq Y}(j+1)\frac{(\log p)^2}{p^j}  \ll(\log(2Y))^2.$$ The $j=1$ part has this order by partial summation and the Chebyshev bounds; the sum over $j\geq2$ is bounded.

> **Proposition: Uniform fixed-shift energy bound** \[prop:fixed-ray-energy-bound\] Fix $C\geq1$. Uniformly for $|h|\leq X^C$ and every $D\geq1$, $$\mathcal E_{h,D}(X)+\mathcal E^{\mathrm{raw}}_{h,D}(X)  \ll_{C,W}X(\log(2X))^4.  \label{eq:fixed-ray-energy-bound}$$ Consequently, $$\int_I|\mathcal R_{h,D}(X;t)|^2dt  \ll_{C,W}(|I|+X)X(\log(2X))^4.  \label{eq:fixed-continuum-upper-bound}$$

###### ref-59563a4af54f4c06

###### ref-f7af0ded5068349f

> **Proof** For a ray $(a,b)$ let $$K_{a,b}:=\{k\geq1:ak>D,\ abk^2/X\in\operatorname{supp}W\}.$$ The support interval has fixed multiplicative width. Hence, for every $k\in K_{a,b}$, $$|K_{a,b}|\ll_W k.  \label{eq:ray-cardinality}$$ Cauchy’s inequality and $|\Lambda(q)-1|\ll_C\log(2X)$ for the target arguments in question give $$|c_{a,b}|^2  \ll_{C,W}(\log(2X))^2  \sum_{k\in K_{a,b}}k\,u(ak)^2|W(abk^2/X)|^2.$$ For fixed $a,k$, the number of $b$ in the support is $$\ll_W\frac{X}{ak^2}.  \label{eq:number-of-b-rays}$$ The possible additive $1$ is absorbed because nonemptiness implies $ak^2\ll_W X$. Summing first over $b$ therefore gives $$\mathcal E_{h,D}(X)  \ll_{C,W}X(\log(2X))^2  \sum_{ak^2\ll_W X}\frac{u(ak)^2}{ak}.$$ Apply [\[lem:source-ray-square-ledger\]](TPC-011.md#ref-b18f4201fda215ea). The raw target obeys the same pointwise bound. Finally insert [\[eq:fixed-ray-energy-bound\]](TPC-011.md#ref-28914fc8be82271b) into [\[thm:fixed-ray-mean-square\]](TPC-011.md#ref-b6cf6be345a2ba7e).

###### ref-7e4fb71cb0e4511b

> **Corollary: Density-one Mellin-frequency cancellation** \[cor:density-one-frequency\] Let $I_X$ be any intervals with lengths $L_X$ satisfying $$\frac{L_X}{(\log X)^4}\longrightarrow\infty.$$ For every fixed $h$ and every $D=D(X)$, $$\frac1{L_XX^2}\int_{I_X}|\mathcal R_{h,D}(X;t)|^2dt=o(1).  \label{eq:frequency-rms-cancellation}$$ In particular, outside a subset of relative measure $o(1)$ in $I_X$, one has $\mathcal R_{h,D}(X;t)=o(X)$.

> **Proof** Divide [\[eq:fixed-continuum-upper-bound\]](TPC-011.md#ref-f9197b76949f7664) by $L_XX^2$: $$\frac1{L_XX^2}\int_{I_X}|\mathcal R_{h,D}|^2dt  \ll_{h,W}\frac{(\log X)^4}{X}  +\frac{(\log X)^4}{L_X}.$$ Both terms tend to zero. The density statement follows from Chebyshev’s inequality, choosing an $o(X)$ threshold slowly enough.

> **Remark: What the corollary does not select** The exceptional set in [\[cor:density-one-frequency\]](TPC-011.md#ref-484dfaaf397e129a) may contain every prescribed frequency, including $t=0$, at every scale. Moreover, [\[eq:frequency-rms-cancellation\]](TPC-011.md#ref-7e4fb71cb0e4511b) is an average per unit frequency; it is not the absolute $o(X^2)$ band error needed by the endpoint recovery criterion in [7](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-011.md#src-73002b8046e1138d).

There is also an unconditional lower floor. It will be useful when interpreting the averaged theorem.

###### ref-c961f23da309c3ae

> **Proposition: Nonzero ray-energy floor** \[prop:ray-energy-floor\] Suppose $\beta<4\alpha$, let $D<\alpha X$, and fix an integer $h$. If $W\not\equiv0$, then $$\mathcal E_{h,D}(X)\gg_{h,W}X\log X.  \label{eq:ray-energy-floor}$$ The same lower bound holds for the raw ray energy.

> **Proof** On a ray $(a,1)$ with $a/X\in\operatorname{supp}W$, the support condition $\beta<4\alpha$ excludes every $k\geq2$. Hence $$c_{a,1}=u(a)(\Lambda(a+h)-1)W(a/X).$$ There is an absolute $c_*>0$ such that $u(a)^2\geq c_*$ for every $a\geq1$: off prime powers the value is $1$, and on prime powers it is $(\log p-1)^2$, whose minimum over primes is positive. The prime number theorem and partial summation give $$\sum_a(\Lambda(a+h)-1)^2|W(a/X)|^2  =X\log X\int_0^\infty|W(v)|^2dv+O_{h,W}(X).$$ Keeping only these distinct rays proves [\[eq:ray-energy-floor\]](TPC-011.md#ref-c961f23da309c3ae). Replacing $\Lambda-1$ by $\Lambda$ at the target uses the analogous second-moment formula for $\Lambda^2$.

# Arithmetic evaluation after averaging the shift

We now square first and average the shift afterwards. Let $$G\in C_c^1((0,\infty)),\qquad G\geq0,$$ with $I_0$ and $I_2$ as in [\[eq:moment-functionals\]](TPC-011.md#ref-c740dff20671a40b). The shift sum below is over all integers; the support of $G(h/H)$ makes it finite and positive for large $H$.

## The first-moment model

###### ref-ae080051785bb583

###### ref-866e5fbdd6423958

> **Proposition: Long-shift first moment** \[prop:first-moment-model\] Fix $C\geq1$. Uniformly for $X\leq H\leq X^C$, every real $t$, every $D\geq1$, and every $A>0$, $$\frac1H\sum_hG(h/H)\mathcal T_{h,D}(X;t)  =I_0(G)\mathcal M_D(X;t)+O_{A,C,W,G}\!\left(X(\log X)^{-A}\right).  \label{eq:first-moment-model}$$ Equivalently, the same normalized average of $\mathcal R_{h,D}$ is $O_A(X(\log X)^{-A})$.

###### ref-116acb6541ec45fc

> **Proof** For $r\in[\alpha X,\beta X]$, the smooth prime number theorem gives, uniformly in $r$ and $H$ in the stated range, $$\sum_hG(h/H)(\Lambda(r+h)-1)  \ll_{A,C,W,G}H(\log X)^{-A}.  \label{eq:translated-smooth-PNT}$$ Indeed, after the change of variable $q=r+h$, the weight $G((q-r)/H)$ has scale $H$ and is supported on $q\asymp_{C,G}H$. The classical zero-free-region prime number theorem is more than sufficient.
>
> The remaining source absolute mass satisfies $$\sum_{\substack{n>D\\m\geq1}}|u(n)W(mn/X)|  \ll_W X\sum_{n\ll_W X}\frac{1+\Lambda(n)}n+O_W(X)  \ll_W X\log(2X).$$ Insert [\[eq:translated-smooth-PNT\]](TPC-011.md#ref-116acb6541ec45fc), request two additional powers of $\log X$, and divide by $H$. The Mellin phase has modulus one, so the estimate is uniform in $t$.

The coherent average in [\[prop:first-moment-model\]](TPC-011.md#ref-ae080051785bb583) is not a theorem about most shifts. That requires the second moment developed next.

## The diagonal inputs

###### ref-2c5fc82a89d5ccca

###### ref-0a0a81e04f0548e3

> **Lemma: Translated target second moment** \[lem:target-second-moment\] Uniformly for $r\in[\alpha X,\beta X]$ and $X\leq H\leq X^C$, $$\sum_hG(h/H)(\Lambda(r+h)-1)^2  =H I_0(G)\log H+O_{C,W,G}(H).  \label{eq:target-second-moment}$$ The same formula, with the same leading term, holds with $(\Lambda(r+h)-1)^2$ replaced by $\Lambda(r+h)^2$.

> **Proof** Expand $(\Lambda-1)^2=\Lambda^2-2\Lambda+1$. Smooth partial summation and the prime number theorem give $$\sum_hG(h/H)\Lambda(r+h)=H I_0(G)+O_A(H(\log H)^{-A})$$ and $$\sum_hG(h/H)=H I_0(G)+O_G(1).$$ For the square, first restrict to prime target values. One von Mangoldt factor supplies the prime number theorem, while the other is $\log H+O_{C,W,G}(1)$ on the support. Thus the prime contribution is $H I_0(G)\log H+O_{C,W,G}(H)$. Proper prime powers contribute $O_{C,W,G}(H^{1/2}(\log H)^2)$, which is absorbed. Combining the three pieces proves the centered formula.

###### ref-c710cfde8ecd8d8d

###### ref-04cd59ed0cebe0e2

> **Lemma: Source diagonal** \[lem:source-diagonal\] Uniformly for $1\leq D\leq X^{1-\varepsilon}$, $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal S_D(X)  &:={}  \sum_{\substack{n>D\\m\geq1}}  u(n)^2|W(mn/X)|^2\\  &=\frac12X I_2(W)  \bigl((\log X)^2-(\log D)^2\bigr)  +O_W(X\log(2X)).  \end{aligned}  \label{eq:source-diagonal}$$

###### ref-fc1127a851c38519

###### ref-9a175b331a208f06

> **Proof** Euler summation gives, uniformly in $n$, $$\sum_{m\geq1}|W(mn/X)|^2  =\frac Xn I_2(W)+O_W(1).  \label{eq:W-square-Euler}$$ The elementary weighted second moment $$\sum_{n\leq y}\frac{(\Lambda(n)-1)^2}{n}  =\frac12(\log y)^2-\log y+O(1)  \label{eq:source-weighted-second-moment}$$ follows by expanding the square, using the classical zero-free-region prime number theorem for $\sum\Lambda(n)^2/n$ and $\sum\Lambda(n)/n$, and summing proper prime powers absolutely. Also $\sum_{n\leq y}(\Lambda(n)-1)^2\ll y\log(2y)$. Insert [\[eq:W-square-Euler\]](TPC-011.md#ref-fc1127a851c38519), subtract the ranges up to $D$, and absorb the fixed support endpoints into $O_W(X\log X)$.

## The same-ray off-diagonal ledger

###### ref-730d83c5e9ef90b5

###### ref-a111080ecb4eb34d

> **Lemma: Two-target upper sieve** \[lem:two-target-upper-sieve\] Let $r\ne r'$ lie in $[\alpha X,\beta X]$. Uniformly for $X\leq H\leq X^C$, $$\begin{aligned}  &\sum_hG(h/H)  \left|(\Lambda(r+h)-1)(\Lambda(r'+h)-1)\right|\\  &\qquad\ll_{C,W,G}  H\prod_{\substack{p\mid r-r'\\p>2}}\frac{p-1}{p-2}  \ll_{C,W,G}H\log\log(3X).  \end{aligned}  \label{eq:two-target-upper-sieve}$$ The same bound holds if either centered target factor is replaced by $\Lambda$.

> **Proof** The constant and one-prime terms cost $O_G(H)$. If both target values are prime, the Selberg upper-bound sieve for the two linear forms $h+r$ and $h+r'$ gives $$\#\{h\asymp_G H:h+r,\ h+r'\ \hbox{prime}\}  \ll_G\frac{H}{(\log H)^2}  \prod_{\substack{p\mid r-r'\\p>2}}\frac{p-1}{p-2}.$$ Restoring the two logarithmic weights proves the first bound for the prime-prime part. If either target is a proper prime power, the aggregate contribution is $O_G(H^{1/2}(\log H)^2)$.
>
> Finally, $$\prod_{\substack{p\mid q\\p>2}}\frac{p-1}{p-2}  \ll\log\log(3|q|)$$ by the standard maximal-order comparison with the product over the smallest primes; here $0<|r-r'|\ll_W X$. This proves [\[eq:two-target-upper-sieve\]](TPC-011.md#ref-a111080ecb4eb34d). See, for example, [`\citet[Chapter~6]{HalberstamRichert1974}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-011.md#cite-851286cb9018f79d) for the two-linear-forms upper sieve.

###### ref-6b5e9c2ce72f5751

###### ref-614be8406742be57

> **Lemma: Same-ray source off-diagonal** \[lem:same-ray-source-ledger\] For every $D\geq1$, $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal L_D(X):={}&  \sum_{\substack{(a,b)=1}}  \sum_{\substack{k\ne\ell\\ak,a\ell>D}}  |u(ak)u(a\ell)|\\  &\quad\times  |W(abk^2/X)W(ab\ell^2/X)|  \ll_W X(\log(2X))^2.  \end{aligned}  \label{eq:same-ray-source-ledger}$$

> **Proof** For a fixed ray use the set $K_{a,b}$ from [\[prop:fixed-ray-energy-bound\]](TPC-011.md#ref-dfe26c3cf84b5645) and put $x_k=|u(ak)W(abk^2/X)|$. Then $$\sum_{k\ne\ell}x_kx_\ell  \leq |K_{a,b}|\sum_kx_k^2  \ll_W\sum_{k\in K_{a,b}}k x_k^2$$ by [\[eq:ray-cardinality\]](TPC-011.md#ref-59563a4af54f4c06). Summing over $b$ and applying [\[eq:number-of-b-rays\]](TPC-011.md#ref-f7af0ded5068349f) yields $$\mathcal L_D(X)  \ll_W X\sum_{ak^2\ll_W X}\frac{u(ak)^2}{ak}.$$ Now use [\[lem:source-ray-square-ledger\]](TPC-011.md#ref-b18f4201fda215ea).

## The averaged ray-energy theorem

###### ref-081734935714eaae

###### ref-54a580480370c30d

> **Theorem: Averaged-shift ray energy** \[thm:averaged-ray-energy\] Fix $\varepsilon>0$ and $C\geq1$. Uniformly for $$1\leq D\leq X^{1-\varepsilon},  \qquad X\leq H\leq X^C,$$ one has $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_hG(h/H)\mathcal E_{h,D}(X)  ={}&\frac12HX I_0(G)I_2(W)\log H\\  &\times\bigl((\log X)^2-(\log D)^2\bigr)\\  &+O_{\varepsilon,C,W,G}\!\left(  HX(\log(2X))^2\log\log(3X)\right).  \end{aligned}  \label{eq:averaged-ray-energy}$$ The same asymptotic holds for $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{raw}}_{h,D}(X)$.

> **Proof** Expand the square in [\[eq:ray-energy\]](TPC-011.md#ref-d4075c615b029d3c) and interchange the finite sums. For $k=\ell$, the target argument is $r=abk^2$ and [\[lem:target-second-moment\]](TPC-011.md#ref-2c5fc82a89d5ccca) gives $$H I_0(G)\log H\sum_{(a,b)=1}\sum_k  u(ak)^2|W(abk^2/X)|^2+O_G(H\mathcal S_D(X)).$$ The double source sum is exactly $\mathcal S_D(X)$: the substitution $n=ak,m=bk$ is unique. Inserting [\[lem:source-diagonal\]](TPC-011.md#ref-c710cfde8ecd8d8d) produces the main term in [\[eq:averaged-ray-energy\]](TPC-011.md#ref-54a580480370c30d). All diagonal errors are $O_{\varepsilon,C,W,G}(HX(\log X)^2)$.
>
> For $k\ne\ell$, the two target arguments $$r=abk^2,  \qquad r'=ab\ell^2$$ are distinct. Apply [\[lem:two-target-upper-sieve\]](TPC-011.md#ref-730d83c5e9ef90b5), take absolute values of the source coefficients, and then use [\[lem:same-ray-source-ledger\]](TPC-011.md#ref-6b5e9c2ce72f5751). The total off-diagonal contribution is $$\ll_{C,W,G}H\log\log(3X)\,\mathcal L_D(X)  \ll_{C,W,G}HX(\log X)^2\log\log(3X).$$ This proves the centered theorem. For the raw energy, the target diagonal $\Lambda^2$ has the same leading term by [\[lem:target-second-moment\]](TPC-011.md#ref-2c5fc82a89d5ccca), and the same upper-sieve ledger handles the off-diagonal.

> **Remark: Where the saving comes from** No cancellation is asserted in an individual same-ray prime correlation. The target diagonal contributes $H\log H$, while the absolute off-diagonal upper sieve contributes only $H\log\log X$. The source diagonal is of order $X\log^2X$, and the entire source off-diagonal ledger has the same order. The extra logarithm on the target diagonal is the sole reason an asymptotic results.

# Exact Fejér resolution and hard-band asymptotics

The sharp interval in [\[thm:fixed-ray-mean-square\]](TPC-011.md#ref-b6cf6be345a2ba7e) leaves an $O(X\mathcal E)$ boundary error. A kernel whose Fourier transform has compact support removes that error exactly.

###### ref-5c23594133c19cb3

###### ref-cc50f16b57b5ad7f

Define the normalized Fejér kernel $$\kappa(t):=\frac2\pi\left(\frac{\sin(t/2)}t\right)^2,  \qquad \kappa(0):=\frac1{2\pi}.  \label{eq:normalized-Fejer-kernel}$$ With the convention $\widehat\kappa(\xi)=\int_\mathbb R\kappa(t)e^{-it\xi}dt$, one has $$\widehat\kappa(\xi)=(1-|\xi|)_+,  \qquad \int_\mathbb R\kappa(t)dt=1.  \label{eq:Fejer-transform}$$

###### ref-d250889d7da7fab8

###### ref-42529059bd0f63ad

> **Theorem: Exact factor-ray Parseval identity** \[thm:exact-Fejer-ray-Parseval\] If $T\delta_X\geq1$, where $\delta_X$ is defined in [\[eq:exact-ratio-spacing\]](TPC-011.md#ref-9831e79d7071f2d5), then $$\int_\mathbb R\kappa(t/T)|\mathcal R_{h,D}(X;t)|^2dt  =T\mathcal E_{h,D}(X).  \label{eq:exact-Fejer-ray-Parseval}$$ The same identity holds for the raw transform and raw ray energy.

> **Proof** Insert [\[eq:ray-compression\]](TPC-011.md#ref-658f4a37763f7519), expand the square, and use [\[eq:Fejer-transform\]](TPC-011.md#ref-cc50f16b57b5ad7f): $$\int_\mathbb R\kappa(t/T)e^{it(\lambda_\rho-\lambda_{\rho'})}dt  =T\widehat\kappa\!\left(T(\lambda_{\rho'}-\lambda_\rho)\right).$$ For distinct rays the argument has absolute value at least $T\delta_X\geq1$, so the triangle function vanishes, including at the endpoints. Every diagonal term has multiplier $T$.

Thus an effective bandwidth $T\asymp_W X$ exactly resolves all primitive factor rays. It does not resolve different $k$ inside one ray; those terms have already been combined into $c_{a,b}$.

###### ref-87b7f15e6acc9b86

###### ref-e05d4545d9a6d685

> **Corollary: Averaged-shift Fejér-band asymptotic** \[cor:averaged-Fejer-band\] Under the hypotheses of [\[thm:averaged-ray-energy\]](TPC-011.md#ref-081734935714eaae), suppose also that $T\delta_X\geq1$. Then $$\begin{aligned}  &\sum_hG(h/H)  \int_\mathbb R\kappa(t/T)|\mathcal T_{h,D}(X;t)-\mathcal M_D(X;t)|^2dt\\  &\quad=\frac12THX I_0(G)I_2(W)\log H  \bigl((\log X)^2-(\log D)^2\bigr)\\  &\qquad\quad+O_{\varepsilon,C,W,G}\!\left(  THX(\log(2X))^2\log\log(3X)\right).  \end{aligned}  \label{eq:averaged-Fejer-band}$$ If $G\not\equiv0$ and $W\not\equiv0$, this is an asymptotic throughout the stated $D,H$ range.

> **Proof** Sum [\[eq:exact-Fejer-ray-Parseval\]](TPC-011.md#ref-42529059bd0f63ad) with weight $G(h/H)$ and insert [\[eq:averaged-ray-energy\]](TPC-011.md#ref-54a580480370c30d). Since $D\leq X^{1-\varepsilon}$ and $H\geq X$, the main term is $\gg_{\varepsilon,G,W}THX(\log X)^3$ when $I_0(G)I_2(W)>0$, whereas the error is smaller by a factor $O(\log\log X/\log X)$.

###### ref-87af137c87b6cfb9

###### ref-807f62fd8be3e9a4

> **Corollary: Averaged-shift hard-band formula** \[cor:averaged-hard-band\] Under the hypotheses of [\[thm:averaged-ray-energy\]](TPC-011.md#ref-081734935714eaae), for every $T\geq1$, $$\begin{aligned}  &\sum_hG(h/H)  \int_{-T}^{T}|\mathcal T_{h,D}(X;t)-\mathcal M_D(X;t)|^2dt\\  &\quad=THX I_0(G)I_2(W)\log H  \bigl((\log X)^2-(\log D)^2\bigr)\\  &\qquad\quad+O_{\varepsilon,C,W,G}\!\left(  THX(\log(2X))^2\log\log(3X)  +HX^2(\log(2X))^3\right).  \end{aligned}  \label{eq:averaged-hard-band}$$ Consequently [\[eq:averaged-hard-band\]](TPC-011.md#ref-807f62fd8be3e9a4) is an asymptotic whenever $T/X\to\infty$.

> **Proof** Sum [\[eq:hard-band-ray-law\]](TPC-011.md#ref-8f91cf55511ec199) over $h$. Twice the leading term in [\[eq:averaged-ray-energy\]](TPC-011.md#ref-54a580480370c30d) gives the displayed main term. The first error is twice $T$ times the averaged ray-energy error. For the Montgomery–Vaughan boundary error, [\[thm:averaged-ray-energy\]](TPC-011.md#ref-081734935714eaae) and its proof give $$\sum_hG(h/H)\mathcal E_{h,D}(X)\ll_{\varepsilon,C,W,G}HX(\log X)^3.$$ Multiplication by $X$ gives the second error in [\[eq:averaged-hard-band\]](TPC-011.md#ref-807f62fd8be3e9a4). Relative to the main term, the two errors are respectively $O(\log\log X/\log X)$ and $O(X/T)$.

###### ref-61c92f5199f9e724

###### ref-70b0bc633182559c

> **Corollary: The TPC–10 truncation** \[cor:TPC10-truncation\] Let $$D=X^{1/2}(\log X)^{-B}$$ with fixed $B>0$. Then $$(\log X)^2-(\log D)^2  =\left(\frac34+o(1)\right)(\log X)^2.  \label{eq:three-quarter-source-energy}$$ Thus the leading coefficient contributed by the source cutoff is $3/8$ in [\[eq:averaged-Fejer-band\]](TPC-011.md#ref-e05d4545d9a6d685) and $3/4$ in [\[eq:averaged-hard-band\]](TPC-011.md#ref-807f62fd8be3e9a4), before the common factors $THX I_0(G)I_2(W)\log H(\log X)^2$ are restored.

> **Remark: Raw target** By the raw part of [\[thm:averaged-ray-energy\]](TPC-011.md#ref-081734935714eaae), both band corollaries remain valid with $\mathcal T_{h,D}$ in place of $\mathcal T_{h,D}-\mathcal M_D$. Centering is used because $\mathcal M_D$ is the long-shift first-moment model, not because it changes the leading second-moment scale.

## A parity-conditioned even-shift ensemble

###### ref-cc21020abfedee94

###### ref-503d087691436488

###### ref-9f53ffbe0f39bb60

###### ref-9fba7212465ac6f8

The all-shift average above is not forced by the proof. To isolate the local parity factor without selecting an individual shift, define $$\mathcal M_D^{(2)}(X;t)  :=2\sum_{\substack{n>D,\ m\geq1\\2\nmid mn}}  u(n)W(mn/X)(n/m)^{it}  \label{eq:even-shift-model}$$ For even $h$, put $\mathcal R^{(2)}_{h,D}=\mathcal T_{h,D}-\mathcal M_D^{(2)}$ and define $$\eta_r^{(2)}(h):=\Lambda(r+h)-2\mathbf 1_{2\nmid r}.  \label{eq:even-target-residual}$$ Then the ray coefficient of $\mathcal R^{(2)}_{h,D}$ is $$c^{(2)}_{a,b}(h;X,D)  =\sum_{\substack{k\geq1\\ak>D}}  u(ak)\eta_{abk^2}^{(2)}(h)W(abk^2/X),  \label{eq:even-ray-coefficient}$$ and $\mathcal E^{(2)}_{h,D}$ denotes the sum of its squared moduli. The factor $2$ in [\[eq:even-shift-model\]](TPC-011.md#ref-cc21020abfedee94) is fixed by the first-moment identity $$\frac2H\sum_{h\equiv0\ (2)}G(h/H)\mathcal T_{h,D}(X;t)  =I_0(G)\mathcal M_D^{(2)}(X;t)  +O_{A,C,W,G}\!\left(X(\log X)^{-A}\right),  \label{eq:even-first-moment}$$ uniformly for real $t$ and $X\leq H\leq X^C$.

###### ref-0f0f6a45f644e66c

###### ref-2edaeb593bb0cb21

> **Proposition: Even-shift ensemble** \[prop:even-shift-ensemble\] Under the hypotheses of [\[thm:averaged-ray-energy\]](TPC-011.md#ref-081734935714eaae), the ray energy $\mathcal E^{(2)}_{h,D}$ of $\mathcal R^{(2)}_{h,D}$ satisfies $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_{h\equiv0\ (2)}G(h/H)\mathcal E^{(2)}_{h,D}(X)  ={}&\frac14HX I_0(G)I_2(W)\log H\\  &\times\bigl((\log X)^2-(\log D)^2\bigr)\\  &+O_{\varepsilon,C,W,G}\!\left(  HX(\log X)^2\log\log(3X)\right).  \end{aligned}  \label{eq:even-shift-ray-energy}$$ Consequently the hard-band leading coefficient is one half of that in [\[eq:averaged-hard-band\]](TPC-011.md#ref-807f62fd8be3e9a4), and the Fejér-band leading coefficient is one half of that in [\[eq:averaged-Fejer-band\]](TPC-011.md#ref-e05d4545d9a6d685).

###### ref-b959ad71fe7ab6ad

###### ref-605d1b46eec268ef

###### ref-5fc2c8cd04d8b01b

###### ref-2043d60efd70e637

> **Proof** For $r\in[\alpha X,\beta X]$, the prime number theorem modulo $2$ gives $$\sum_{h\equiv0\ (2)}G(h/H)\Lambda(r+h)  =H I_0(G)\mathbf 1_{2\nmid r}+O_A(H(\log H)^{-A}).  \label{eq:even-target-first-moment}$$ Summing this estimate against the source rows proves [\[eq:even-first-moment\]](TPC-011.md#ref-9fba7212465ac6f8). The same argument for $\Lambda^2$, followed by expansion of [\[eq:even-target-residual\]](TPC-011.md#ref-503d087691436488), gives $$\sum_{h\equiv0\ (2)}G(h/H)|\eta_r^{(2)}(h)|^2  =H I_0(G)\log H\,\mathbf 1_{2\nmid r}+O_{C,W,G}(H).  \label{eq:even-target-second-moment}$$ Indeed, for odd $r$ the conditional density of primes in the odd residue class compensates for sampling half of the shifts. For even $r$, the target is even and $\Lambda(r+h)$ is supported only on powers of two, so its contribution is absorbed by the error.
>
> For distinct $r,r'\in[\alpha X,\beta X]$, one also has $$\sum_{h\equiv0\ (2)}G(h/H)  |\eta_r^{(2)}(h)\eta_{r'}^{(2)}(h)|  \ll_{C,W,G}H\log\log(3X).  \label{eq:even-two-target-upper-sieve}$$ If $r$ and $r'$ are odd, write $h=2j$ and apply the two-linear-forms Selberg upper sieve to $2j+r$ and $2j+r'$. If either is even, its target factor is supported on powers of two; the resulting polylogarithmic contribution is smaller than the displayed bound. This also covers the mixed-parity rows, which are present in [\[eq:even-ray-coefficient\]](TPC-011.md#ref-9f53ffbe0f39bb60).
>
> The odd-product source diagonal is $$\sum_{\substack{n>D,\ m\geq1\\2\nmid mn}}  u(n)^2|W(mn/X)|^2  =\frac14X I_2(W)  \bigl((\log X)^2-(\log D)^2\bigr)+O_W(X\log X).  \label{eq:even-source-diagonal}$$ Indeed, the odd $m$ sum contributes one half of the continuous density, whereas removing the prime $2$ does not change the leading $\frac12\log^2 y$ term in the weighted $u(n)^2$ sum over odd $n$.
>
> Expand the squared ray coefficients. Equations [\[eq:even-target-second-moment\]](TPC-011.md#ref-605d1b46eec268ef) and [\[eq:even-source-diagonal\]](TPC-011.md#ref-2043d60efd70e637) give the displayed main term; even target diagonal rows contribute only $O(H\mathcal S_D(X))$. For $k\ne\ell$, apply [\[eq:even-two-target-upper-sieve\]](TPC-011.md#ref-5fc2c8cd04d8b01b) and then the full source ledger [\[eq:same-ray-source-ledger\]](TPC-011.md#ref-614be8406742be57). The resulting error is $O(HX(\log X)^2\log\log(3X))$, proving [\[eq:even-shift-ray-energy\]](TPC-011.md#ref-2edaeb593bb0cb21). The band statements follow from the same factor-ray identities.

> **Remark: No individual-shift selection** The ensemble in [\[prop:even-shift-ensemble\]](TPC-011.md#ref-0f0f6a45f644e66c) contains only admissible parity shifts, but its exceptional behavior may still be concentrated at any prescribed even shift. No individual shift is selected.

# Determinant layers and the mesoscopic boundary

The large-sieve theorem becomes diagonal only after an $O(X)$ resolution loss. This section records exactly what survives below that scale.

###### ref-ce432a6c82c16181

###### ref-6ae4be435bbf2a6a

For a sharp band put $$K_T(x):=  \begin{cases}   2\sin(Tx)/x,&x\ne0,\\   2T,&x=0.  \end{cases}  \label{eq:sharp-band-kernel}$$ Direct expansion of the original factor pairs gives $$\begin{aligned}  \int_{-T}^{T}|\mathcal R_{h,D}(X;t)|^2dt  ={}&\sum_{\substack{n,m,n',m'\\n,n'>D}}  u(n)u(n')\eta(mn+h)\eta(m'n'+h)\\  &\quad\times W(mn/X)W(m'n'/X)  K_T\!\left(\log\frac{nm'}{mn'}\right),  \end{aligned}  \label{eq:fourfold-hard-band}$$ where $\eta(q)=\Lambda(q)-1$. The highest-degree von Mangoldt term in [\[eq:fourfold-hard-band\]](TPC-011.md#ref-6ae4be435bbf2a6a) contains $$\Lambda(n)\Lambda(n')\Lambda(mn+h)\Lambda(m'n'+h).$$ Thus a small fixed-shift band is a genuine fourfold arithmetic correlation, not merely a divisor mean value.

###### ref-74cfbdfa165387fc

###### ref-1a1a63bac561e294

After ray compression, the same exact identity is $$\int_{-T}^{T}|\mathcal R_{h,D}(X;t)|^2dt  =\sum_{\substack{(a,b)=1\\(c,d)=1}}  c_{a,b}\overline{c_{c,d}}  K_T\!\left(\log\frac{ad}{bc}\right).  \label{eq:ray-hard-kernel}$$ The relevant near-resonance is measured by the integer $$\Delta((a,b),(c,d)):=ad-bc.  \label{eq:ray-determinant}$$

###### ref-0494279df2747895

###### ref-0bb9491463b11819

###### ref-b7825eaf3edcab1a

> **Proposition: Compact-Fourier determinant layers** \[prop:determinant-layers\] Let $\Phi\in L^1(\mathbb R)$ and suppose, with the Fourier convention used in [\[eq:Fejer-transform\]](TPC-011.md#ref-cc50f16b57b5ad7f), that $\operatorname{supp}\widehat\Phi\subset[-L,L]$. Then $$\begin{aligned}  \frac1T\int_\mathbb R\Phi(t/T)|\mathcal R_{h,D}(X;t)|^2dt  ={}&\sum_{\substack{(a,b)=1\\(c,d)=1}}  c_{a,b}\overline{c_{c,d}}\\  &\quad\times  \widehat\Phi\!\left(T\log\frac{bc}{ad}\right).  \end{aligned}  \label{eq:compact-Fourier-layer-identity}$$ If $L/T\leq1$, every nonzero summand satisfies $$|ad-bc|  \leq2\beta X\sinh\!\left(\frac{L}{2T}\right)  \ll_L\frac XT.  \label{eq:determinant-layer-range}$$

> **Proof** Insert [\[eq:ray-compression\]](TPC-011.md#ref-658f4a37763f7519) and use Fourier inversion for the scaled weight. A term can survive only if $$\left|\log\frac{ad}{bc}\right|\leq\frac LT.$$ The exact hyperbolic identity from [\[lem:ratio-spacing\]](TPC-011.md#ref-8b3a29ddded102f2) gives $$|ad-bc|  =2\sqrt{abcd}\,  \sinh\!\left(\frac12\left|\log\frac{ad}{bc}\right|\right).$$ Since $ab,cd\leq\beta X$, this is at most the right-hand side of [\[eq:determinant-layer-range\]](TPC-011.md#ref-b7825eaf3edcab1a).

At frequency scale $T=X^\theta$, the compact-Fourier mean square therefore contains only determinant layers $$|\Delta|\ll X^{1-\theta}.$$ For $T\gg X$, only $\Delta=0$ remains; primitive fractions then force $(a,b)=(c,d)$. At $T\asymp X$, only finitely many nonzero determinant layers can remain. For $T=o(X)$, the permitted determinant range grows and the number of potentially surviving layers is allowed to grow. A diagonal law then requires new cancellation among their arithmetic coefficients.

###### ref-149c3657276b5beb

> **Proposition: Sharpness of the $X$ threshold** \[prop:X-threshold-sharp\] Let $\Phi\in L^1(\mathbb R)$ have continuous Fourier transform with $\widehat\Phi(0)\ne0$. There are hyperbolic coefficient systems supported on two primitive rays for which the $\Phi$-weighted form has a nonzero off-diagonal term whenever $1\leq T\leq cX$, with $c>0$ depending only on the kernel and support band. Consequently the order $T\asymp X$ in [\[thm:exact-Fejer-ray-Parseval\]](TPC-011.md#ref-d250889d7da7fab8) cannot be uniformly improved for arbitrary coefficients.

> **Proof** Take the two rays $(N,1)$ and $(N-1,1)$ with $N\asymp X$, and assign both coefficient one. Their determinant is $1$ and their frequency gap is $\log(N/(N-1))=N^{-1}+O(N^{-2})$. Continuity and $\widehat\Phi(0)\ne0$ give an interval about zero on which $\widehat\Phi$ is nonzero. Choose $c$ so that every scaled gap with $1\leq T\leq cX$ lies in that interval. The cross term in [\[eq:compact-Fourier-layer-identity\]](TPC-011.md#ref-0bb9491463b11819) then survives.

## The fixed-shift arithmetic left inside one ray

###### ref-29b82aea886fc5bf

###### ref-9454fe6784b05f01

The diagonal ray energy is not an elementary coefficient norm. Expanding one term gives $$\begin{aligned}  |c_{a,b}|^2  ={}&\sum_{k,\ell}  u(ak)u(a\ell)  \eta(abk^2+h)\eta(ab\ell^2+h)\\  &\quad\times W(abk^2/X)W(ab\ell^2/X),  \end{aligned}  \label{eq:intra-ray-arithmetic}$$ with the source cutoff understood. Terms $k\ne\ell$ contain two target prime weights on the quadratic sequences $$abk^2+h,\qquad ab\ell^2+h.$$ For example, the ray $(a,b)=(1,1)$ has coefficient $$c_{1,1}(h;X,D)  =\sum_{k>D}u(k)\eta(k^2+h)W(k^2/X).  \label{eq:quadratic-prime-ray}$$ No fixed-$h$ asymptotic for [\[eq:quadratic-prime-ray\]](TPC-011.md#ref-9454fe6784b05f01) or for the general off-diagonal [\[eq:intra-ray-arithmetic\]](TPC-011.md#ref-29b82aea886fc5bf) is used or obtained.

The role of the long shift average is now transparent. It does not make the same-ray correlations disappear. Rather, their total absolute contribution is upper-bounded, while the $k=\ell$ target square acquires the larger factor $\log H$. Specializing the averaged theorem back to one fixed shift would remove precisely that advantage.

> **Remark: The next analytic target** A genuine advance into the mesoscopic region $1\ll T\ll X$ would estimate the finitely many determinant-layer forms in [\[eq:compact-Fourier-layer-identity\]](TPC-011.md#ref-0bb9491463b11819) using their arithmetic coefficients, rather than their absolute values. Possible honest weakenings include averaging $h$ over shorter windows, averaging the determinant $\Delta$, or restricting $(a,b)$ to balanced subregions. The present paper supplies the exact layer identity, the permitted determinant range, and the normalization against which such an estimate should be measured.

# Endpoint recovery and the fixed-shift strength threshold

The ratio transform does not select $m=1$ by merely taking a long sharp frequency interval. A fixed scale window does select it, but that window uses a coherent superposition of all frequencies.

###### ref-a6c5036be0d60a22

###### ref-022bf29412005312

Assume in this section that $$\operatorname{supp}W\subset[\alpha,\beta],  \qquad \beta<4\alpha,  \qquad D<\alpha X.  \label{eq:endpoint-support-hypothesis}$$ Choose $V\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$ such that $$V(x)=1\quad(\log\alpha\leq x\leq\log\beta),  \qquad  V(x)=0\quad(x\leq\log(\beta/4)).  \label{eq:endpoint-window}$$

###### ref-0e5766df96ec6f5d

###### ref-704cb4ffb5eb49c6

###### ref-3bada4bbf0ea1d6f

> **Proposition: Exact one-Mellin endpoint selector** \[prop:one-Mellin-endpoint-selector\] Under [\[eq:endpoint-support-hypothesis\]](TPC-011.md#ref-a6c5036be0d60a22), $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_n u(n)\Lambda(n+h)W(n/X)  &=\frac1{2\pi}\int_\mathbb R  \widehat V(t)X^{-it}\mathcal T_{h,D}(X;t)dt,  \label{eq:raw-endpoint-selector}\\  \sum_n u(n)(\Lambda(n+h)-1)W(n/X)  &=\frac1{2\pi}\int_\mathbb R  \widehat V(t)X^{-it}\mathcal R_{h,D}(X;t)dt,  \label{eq:centered-endpoint-selector}\end{aligned}$$ where $\widehat V(t)=\int_\mathbb RV(x)e^{-itx}dx$.

###### ref-f3862f349da610ae

> **Proof** If $r=mn$ lies in the support of $W(r/X)$, then $$\log(n/m)-\log X=\log(r/X)-2\log m.  \label{eq:endpoint-coordinate}$$ For $m=1$ this lies in $[\log\alpha,\log\beta]$, where $V=1$. For $m\geq2$ it is at most $\log\beta-2\log2=\log(\beta/4)$, where $V=0$. Thus inserting $V(\log(n/m)-\log X)$ selects precisely $m=1$. The cutoff $D<\alpha X$ removes none of those rows. Fourier inversion of $V$ gives [\[eq:raw-endpoint-selector\]](TPC-011.md#ref-704cb4ffb5eb49c6); subtracting the same identity for [\[eq:intro-source-model\]](TPC-011.md#ref-cf687f79dff2f7e4) gives [\[eq:centered-endpoint-selector\]](TPC-011.md#ref-3bada4bbf0ea1d6f).

> **Remark: General product supports** Every compact subinterval of $(0,\infty)$ is a finite union of intervals of multiplicative width less than four. A smooth partition of unity in the product variable therefore reduces a general $W$ to finitely many selectors of the form above.

## Truncating the coherent selector

###### ref-9dec2fa552f188a2

The total variation of either factor-scale measure in [\[eq:raw-endpoint-selector\]](TPC-011.md#ref-704cb4ffb5eb49c6)–[\[eq:centered-endpoint-selector\]](TPC-011.md#ref-3bada4bbf0ea1d6f) satisfies $$\sum_{\substack{n>D\\m\geq1}}  |u(n)|\bigl(1+\Lambda(mn+h)\bigr)|W(mn/X)|  \ll_{h,W}X\log(2X).  \label{eq:endpoint-total-variation}$$ One way to see this is to put $r=mn$, use $$\sum_{n\mid r}|u(n)|\leq\log r+\tau(r),$$ and apply the prime number theorem to the logarithmic part and the standard shifted Titchmarsh upper bound to the divisor part [`\citep{AssingBlomerLi2021}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-011.md#cite-8984a349ff307f96).

###### ref-b7e37cf0a6cc3b48

Since $V\in C_c^\infty$, for every $A>0$, $$\left|\int_{|t|>T}\widehat V(t)X^{-it}  \mathcal R_{h,D}(X;t)dt\right|  \ll_{A,h,V,W}X\log(2X)T^{-A}.  \label{eq:endpoint-Fourier-tail}$$ The same estimate holds for the raw transform and for the source model. Thus any positive power of $\log X$ is a sufficient truncation length for an $o(X)$ approximation, after choosing $A$ large. The hard part is not the smooth Fourier tail; it is the accuracy of the arithmetic model on the retained band.

## An exact $L^2$ strength test

###### ref-5c41b7c7f04abf6e

For a function $F$ for which the integral converges, define $$\mathscr L_X(F):=\frac1{2\pi}\int_\mathbb R  \widehat V(t)X^{-it}F(t)dt.  \label{eq:endpoint-functional}$$

###### ref-e6d5ac3a70acb202

###### ref-3b470054f01f9f2e

###### ref-78e6596158e3a4f4

###### ref-8313cf74ed1fc077

> **Proposition: Endpoint discrepancy forces band energy** \[prop:endpoint-strength-test\] Let $S$ and $M$ be two transforms and let $$\operatorname{Tail}_T(S-M)  :=\frac1{2\pi}  \left|\int_{|t|>T}\widehat V(t)X^{-it}(S(t)-M(t))dt\right|.  \label{eq:model-tail}$$ Then $$\|S-M\|_{L^2[-T,T]}  \geq\frac{2\pi}{\|\widehat V\|_{L^2(\mathbb R)}}  \left(  |\mathscr L_X(S)-\mathscr L_X(M)|  -\operatorname{Tail}_T(S-M)  \right)_+.  \label{eq:endpoint-strength-lower-bound}$$ Consequently, if the endpoint discrepancy is $\gg X$ and the Fourier tail is $o(X)$, then $$\int_{-T}^{T}|S(t)-M(t)|^2dt\gg X^2.  \label{eq:endpoint-X2-floor}$$ Conversely, an absolute band error $o(X^2)$ together with an $o(X)$ tail forces the two endpoint functionals to agree up to $o(X)$.

> **Proof** Split [\[eq:endpoint-functional\]](TPC-011.md#ref-5c41b7c7f04abf6e) at $|t|=T$, apply the triangle inequality, and then apply Cauchy–Schwarz on $[-T,T]$: $$|\mathscr L_X(S)-\mathscr L_X(M)|  \leq\frac{\|\widehat V\|_2}{2\pi}  \|S-M\|_{L^2[-T,T]}  +\operatorname{Tail}_T(S-M).$$ Rearrangement proves [\[eq:endpoint-strength-lower-bound\]](TPC-011.md#ref-78e6596158e3a4f4). The two consequences are immediate.

The normalization is important. A statement $$\int_{-T}^{T}|S-M|^2dt=o(TX^2)$$ is only a root-mean-square statement. When $T\to\infty$, Cauchy–Schwarz allows an $o(X\sqrt T)$ coherent error and does not recover the endpoint. The absolute $o(X^2)$ threshold in [\[prop:endpoint-strength-test\]](TPC-011.md#ref-e6d5ac3a70acb202) is strictly stronger.

## The Hardy–Littlewood boundary

###### ref-fd4520aeea2c392b

For a nonzero integer shift $h$, write the usual prime-pair singular series as $$\mathfrak S(h):=  \begin{cases}   2C_2\displaystyle\prod_{\substack{p\mid h\\p>2}}   \dfrac{p-1}{p-2},&2\mid h,\\[0.8em]   0,&2\nmid h,  \end{cases}  \qquad  C_2:=\prod_{p>2}\left(1-\frac1{(p-1)^2}\right).  \label{eq:prime-pair-singular-series}$$ This is the constant in the corresponding Hardy–Littlewood prediction [`\citep{HardyLittlewood1923}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-011.md#cite-f4ffa381ee727373); no such prediction is assumed below.

###### ref-59740d5ae6e1c7b7

For the raw prime-target transform, the selected endpoint is $$\begin{aligned}  \mathscr L_X(\mathcal T_{h,D})  &=\sum_n(\Lambda(n)-1)\Lambda(n+h)W(n/X)\\  &=\sum_n\Lambda(n)\Lambda(n+h)W(n/X)  -X I_0(W)+o_{h,W}(X),  \end{aligned}  \label{eq:endpoint-HL-reduction}$$ by the shifted prime number theorem. Hence, for one fixed admissible even shift and one fixed $W$, evaluating [\[eq:endpoint-HL-reduction\]](TPC-011.md#ref-59740d5ae6e1c7b7) with the predicted main term is equivalent to the corresponding single-weight Hardy–Littlewood assertion. The centered endpoint has the same predicted leading term because $\sum_n(\Lambda(n)-1)W(n/X)=o_W(X)$.

It follows from [\[prop:endpoint-strength-test\]](TPC-011.md#ref-e6d5ac3a70acb202) that a fixed-shift band model with absolute $o(X^2)$ error, a compatible filtered model value, and $o(X)$ tails would already contain that Hardy–Littlewood endpoint. The implication is one-way: the endpoint controls one filtered functional, not the entire band.

There is an unconditional parity check on the natural source model.

###### ref-33be12f1ab7478f0

###### ref-d9763705141c6c53

> **Corollary: Odd-shift model floor** \[cor:odd-shift-model-floor\] Assume $I_0(W)\ne0$ and fix an odd integer $h$. Let $S=\mathcal T_{h,D}$ and $M=\mathcal M_D$. If $T\to\infty$ so that the tail in [\[eq:model-tail\]](TPC-011.md#ref-3b470054f01f9f2e) is $o(X)$—for example any fixed positive power of $\log X$—then $$\int_{-T}^{T}|\mathcal T_{h,D}(X;t)-\mathcal M_D(X;t)|^2dt  \gg_{h,V,W}X^2.  \label{eq:odd-shift-band-floor}$$

> **Proof** If $h$ is odd, two prime-power-supported values $n$ and $n+h$ can both occur only when one is a power of two. There are $O(\log X)$ such possibilities, so $$\sum_n\Lambda(n)\Lambda(n+h)W(n/X)=o_{h,W}(X).$$ The prime number theorem applied to the two linear terms in the centered endpoint then gives $$\mathscr L_X(\mathcal T_{h,D}-\mathcal M_D)  =-X I_0(W)+o_{h,W}(X).$$ Apply [\[prop:endpoint-strength-test\]](TPC-011.md#ref-e6d5ac3a70acb202) and [\[eq:endpoint-Fourier-tail\]](TPC-011.md#ref-b7e37cf0a6cc3b48).

> **Remark: Why this is not a twin-prime lower bound** The odd-shift corollary is a local-parity sanity check: the target-free model does not encode the singular local factor. For admissible even shifts, replacing the explicit odd-shift value by $(\mathfrak S(h)-1)XI_0(W)$ is exactly the unproved prime-pair step. No such replacement is made here.

# Finite certificate and reproducibility

The proofs above are analytic. A deterministic finite certificate is included only to test the exact algebra that precedes the estimates. It does not test a prime-pair asymptotic. The script `experiments/factor_ray_certificate.py` performs the following checks for a documented finite hyperbolic support.

1.  It enumerates every supported factor pair $(n,m)$ and groups it by the reduced fraction $(a,b)=(n/(n,m),m/(n,m))$. At several real frequencies it compares the original factor-pair sum with the compressed ray sum.

2.  It computes all adjacent gaps between the ordered ray frequencies (and hence their global minimum), verifies the exact hyperbolic lower bound in [\[eq:exact-ratio-spacing\]](TPC-011.md#ref-9831e79d7071f2d5), and records the minimizing rays.

3.  It evaluates the hard-band quadratic form in two ways: directly from the factor pairs and from the compressed ray coefficients. It also verifies that the Fejér multiplier is diagonal once the finite frequency bandwidth exceeds the reciprocal of the actual minimum ray gap.

4.  It counts the determinant layers permitted by the compact-Fourier support condition at a sequence of resolutions and verifies the endpoint classification $m=1$ versus $m\geq2$ under [\[eq:endpoint-support-hypothesis\]](TPC-011.md#ref-a6c5036be0d60a22).

The companion unit test repeats the certificate at smaller parameter values and uses exact rational arithmetic for the ray labels and determinants. Floating-point comparisons occur only in exponential and logarithmic evaluations and are guarded by reported tolerances. The machine-readable output is `experiments/data/factor-ray-certificate.json`. Reproduction requires only the Python standard library:

```text
python experiments/factor_ray_certificate.py
python -m unittest experiments.test_factor_ray_certificate
```

The saved output is a regression certificate for the identities, not evidence for the fixed-$h$ statements explicitly excluded in [1](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-011.md#src-8b5f2aa1fc5a860d).

# Conclusion and next boundary

This paper closes one well-defined step in the factor-scale program. A complete large-factor prime-target residual is converted into a separated exponential polynomial by grouping factor pairs along primitive ratio rays. At fixed shift this yields a hyperbolic Mellin large-sieve law, an $X\log^4X$ ray-energy upper bound, density-one cancellation in long frequency intervals, and exact Fejér diagonalization at the geometric resolution $T\asymp X$. None of these conclusions evaluates a prescribed frequency.

Squaring first and averaging $h$ over a window of length at least $X$ adds arithmetic content. The source diagonal admits an explicit asymptotic evaluation; the target square contributes $\log H$; and a two-linear-forms upper sieve is sufficient for every same-ray off-diagonal. The result is an unconditional averaged-shift ray-energy asymptotic and its hard- and smooth-band consequences. The even-shift version records the parity-conditioned local factor without selecting any individual even shift.

The remaining fixed-shift obstruction is now explicit rather than hidden in a generic appeal to the parity problem. Spectral separation removes different reduced ratios but leaves all radial indices $k$ coherent. Their coefficients contain quadratic target correlations $\Lambda(abk^2+h)\Lambda(ab\ell^2+h)$. In the mesoscopic band, additional interactions are organized by the determinant $ad-bc$. At the factor endpoint, a single smooth coherent functional recovers the weighted Hardy–Littlewood quantity of [`\citet{HardyLittlewood1923}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-011.md#cite-f4ffa381ee727373); hence an absolute $o(X^2)$ fixed-shift band approximation, together with a compatible filtered model and $o(X)$ tails, would already have substantial prime-pair content.

The next honest target is therefore not to assert a fixed-$h$ diagonal formula. It is to find cancellation in one controlled weakening of the determinant-layer or intra-ray forms: a shorter shift average, a balanced ray sector, or an average over a bounded set of determinant layers. Any such theorem must be compared with the endpoint strength test before it is interpreted arithmetically. The classical sieve framework described by [`\citet{IwaniecKowalski2004}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-011.md#cite-49dfea87eaada156) remains the relevant boundary: no twin-prime lower bound, Hardy–Littlewood asymptotic at $h=2$, or violation of sieve parity is obtained here.
