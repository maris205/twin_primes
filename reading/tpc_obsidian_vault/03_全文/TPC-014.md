## Abstract

Let $u(n)=\Lambda(n)-1$, let $W\in C_c^1((0,\infty);\mathbb R)$ and $G\in C_c^1(\mathbb R;\mathbb R)$ be nonzero, and write every factor pair uniquely as $n=ak$, $m=bk$, $(a,b)=1$. TPC-13 separated the joint labels $(\log(a/b),\log k)$ and proved diagonal-energy laws together with character-phase first moments. Here we evaluate one fixed-shift aggregate and close the short-shift first moment from character phases to the full bounded-mask ball.

The first result is at a prescribed fixed nonzero shift. The exact radial shell $k=1$, with all primitive orientations retained, is $$\mathcal K_h(X)=  \sum_{\substack{a>1,\ b\geq1\\(a,b)=1}}  u(a)(\Lambda(ab+h)-1)W(ab/X).$$ If $$\beta_h=\prod_{p\mid h}  \left(1-\frac{p}{p^2-p+1}\right),$$ then the unitary-divisor Titchmarsh theorem of Biao Wang implies $$\mathcal K_h(X)=  \left(\frac1{\zeta(2)}-\beta_h\right)  XI_0(W)\log X+O_{h,W}(X).$$ Thus a nontrivial signed GCD–Mellin radial shell is evaluated for every fixed $h$, including $h=2$. It still sums all primitive directions $b$; it does not isolate the endpoint $b=1$ and gives no twin-prime conclusion.

The second result controls the full coefficient-space dual ball after a growing short-shift average. For $X^{2/15+\varepsilon}\leq H\leq X$ and every $A>0$, the Guth–Maynard short-interval inputs give, provided all translated targets remain at height comparable with $X$, $$\left\|  \frac1H\sum_qG(q/H)(\Lambda(\,\cdot+h_0+q)-1)  \right\|_{\ell^2(r\asymp X)}  \ll_{A,\varepsilon,G}X^{1/2}(\log X)^{-A}.$$ Consequently, uniformly for every $D\geq1$, every bounded, shift-independent mask $|\omega(a,b,k)|\leq1$ satisfies $$\begin{aligned}  &\frac1H\sum_qG(q/H)  \sum_{\substack{a,b,k\geq1,\ (a,b)=1\\ak>D}}  \omega(a,b,k)u(ak)\\  &\qquad{}\times  (\Lambda(abk^2+h_0+q)-1)W(abk^2/X)  \ll_A X(\log X)^{-A}.  \end{aligned}$$ uniformly in the mask. Taking $D=1$, this includes the exact endpoint $b=k=1$, individual radial layers, primitive rays, and arbitrary signed unions. It yields an averaged endpoint prime-pair asymptotic at the two-fifteenths scale, but no selected shift.

After recentering on the factorized target variable, the same theorem controls every bounded mask of Vaughan’s hard packet under the short-shift average. Finally, an exact local-data minimax formula and a nearly sharp square-root divisor-signature dichotomy exhibit a prime/semiprime null direction. A balanced bilinear factor row detects that direction exactly; detecting it is an information test, not an estimate for the arithmetic packet. No fixed-shift prime-pair asymptotic, twin-prime lower bound, new level of distribution, or breach of sieve parity is claimed.

# Introduction and scope

The sequence represented here by TPC-7, TPC-12, and TPC-13 separated three questions that are often compressed into one. A complete multiplicative aggregate can be evaluated at a fixed shift, but may hide the factor scale. A Mellin coordinate can resolve primitive ratio rays, but a ratio alone collapses every radial multiple. Adding the GCD coordinate restores the full factor label, but exact inversion merely reveals the coefficient: it does not estimate the fixed-shift prime pair sitting at the endpoint. See [`\citet{WangTPC7,WangTPC12,WangTPC13}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-014.md#cite-2ec3fed4bb89ce85); [\[WangTPC12\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-014.md#cite-3050715960fc46d3); [\[WangTPC13\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-014.md#cite-d889f650293167a1).

This paper takes the next step in two different quantifier regimes. First, we keep one exact radial shell and retain a prescribed fixed shift. Second, we keep an arbitrary signed label mask but average the shift over a growing window. The distinction is essential.

All asymptotics are as $X\to\infty$. The functions $W,G$ and the fixed shift $h$ in the coprime-shell theorem are held fixed; uniform parameters are identified explicitly.

## The fixed-shift radial shell

###### ref-3d44f4d0d8991cf3

###### ref-b31c71762a17c6f2

###### ref-b59202442800d470

###### ref-f5fcd4f5bfb97d8f

The shell $k=1$ consists of the coprime factor pairs $(a,b)$. At source cutoff $D=1$, its signed residual is $$\mathcal K_h(X)=  \sum_{\substack{a>1,\ b\ge1\\(a,b)=1}}  u(a)\eta(ab+h)W(ab/X).  \label{eq:intro-k-shell}$$ For a fixed nonzero integer $h$, define $$\beta_h=  \prod_{p\mid h}  \left(1-\frac{p}{p^2-p+1}\right),  \label{eq:beta-h}$$ where the product is over the prime divisors of $|h|$ and the empty product is $1$. Our first main theorem is $$\mathcal K_h(X)=  \left(\frac1{\zeta(2)}-\beta_h\right)  XI_0(W)\log X+O_{h,W}(X).  \label{eq:intro-fixed-shell-law}$$ The proof is not a prime-pair estimate. Grouping by $r=ab$ turns the source coefficient into $$\log\operatorname{rad}(r)-2^{\omega(r)}+1.  \label{eq:intro-unitary-coefficient}$$ The term $2^{\omega(r)}$ is the unitary divisor function. The fixed-shift prime target against this function is evaluated by the published unitary-divisor Titchmarsh theorem of [`\citet{BiaoWang2026}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-014.md#cite-f7c938b6730b68e7). The remaining terms are elementary at the required precision.

###### ref-c2fe81d237a3011d

For $h=2$, [\[eq:beta-h\]](TPC-014.md#ref-b31c71762a17c6f2) gives $\beta_2=1/3$ and the coefficient in [\[eq:intro-fixed-shell-law\]](TPC-014.md#ref-b59202442800d470) is $$\frac6{\pi^2}-\frac13>0.  \label{eq:h-two-shell-coefficient}$$ This is a fixed-$2$ theorem, but not a twin-prime theorem. The shell retains $k=1$ and sums every primitive orientation $b$; the twin-prime endpoint is the single orientation $b=1$.

## The complete dual mask under a short-shift mean

###### ref-14e96a8ef0319116

In the short-shift part we impose the geometric condition that all translated targets occurring on [\[eq:W-support\]](TPC-014.md#ref-fb88641c876fcc12) remain in a fixed positive dyadic multiple of $X$. For example, it is enough that $$|h_0|+H\max_{v\in\operatorname{supp}G}|v|\leq\frac{\alpha X}{2}.  \label{eq:translation-geometric-range}$$ The one-sided version with $G$ supported in $(0,\infty)$ allows the same upper range as TPC-12 after changing the fixed dyadic constants.

###### ref-bd254729359297da

###### ref-b210369519c59e08

For an arbitrary shift-independent mask $$|\omega_{X,D}(a,b,k)|\leq1,  \label{eq:bounded-mask}$$ define $$\mathcal Z_{h,D}^{\omega}(X)=  \sum_{\substack{a,b,k\geq1,\ (a,b)=1\\ak>D}}  \omega_{X,D}(a,b,k)u(ak)  \eta(abk^2+h)W(abk^2/X).  \label{eq:masked-residual}$$ The mask may depend on $X,D,H$ and may be discontinuous in every label, but it may not depend on the sampled shift $h$.

###### ref-153e744333265af6

###### ref-71bb43ba75867b3b

For fixed $0<\varepsilon<13/15$, uniformly in $$X^{2/15+\varepsilon}\leq H\leq X  \label{eq:short-shift-range}$$ subject to [\[eq:translation-geometric-range\]](TPC-014.md#ref-14e96a8ef0319116), we prove that, for every $A>0$, $$\sup_{\|\omega\|_\infty\leq1}  \left|  \frac1H\sum_{q\in\mathbb Z}G(q/H)  \mathcal Z_{h_0+q,D}^{\omega}(X)  \right|  \ll_{A,\varepsilon,W,G}X(\log X)^{-A}.  \label{eq:intro-mask-theorem}$$ This is stronger than uniformity in the two character phases $(a/b)^{it}k^{is}$. It closes that phase family to the entire bounded $\ell^\infty$ coefficient-mask ball. In particular it includes the exact endpoint mask $\mathbf 1_{b=k=1}$, a single radial layer $\mathbf 1_{k=k_0}$, one primitive ray, a dyadic source layer, and arbitrary signed unions of these sets.

The analytic input is Corollary 1.4 of [`\citet{GuthMaynard2026}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-014.md#cite-47cea3521d045af7), which gives the prime number theorem in almost all intervals of length $X^{2/15+\varepsilon}$. Our contribution is an $\ell^2$ dual transfer. The translated residual vector has arbitrarily small logarithmic norm, while every bounded GCD–Mellin mask has a divisor-controlled $\ell^2$ norm. This is not a new short-interval theorem.

## Endpoint, hard packets, and the information gate

###### ref-da42adedea4b09e2

The endpoint mask yields $$\frac1H\sum_qG(q/H)  \sum_n u(n)\eta(n+h_0+q)W(n/X)  \ll_A X(\log X)^{-A}.  \label{eq:intro-endpoint-covariance}$$ After adding back the one-prime terms, its raw form has main term $XI_0(W)I_0(G)$. This reaches the endpoint before taking the mean, but the mean still samples $\asymp H$ shifts. It gives no information about the coordinate $h=2$.

There is also a legal target-side Vaughan formulation. One first uses the factorized target $t=\ell k$ as the outer center; only then may one apply an arbitrary mask $\omega_{\ell,k}$. The coefficient is independent of the subsequent translation, so [\[eq:intro-mask-theorem\]](TPC-014.md#ref-71bb43ba75867b3b)’s abstract $\ell^2$ mechanism gives a short-shift averaged model for every masked hard packet. Masking a factorization of $r+h$ while retaining $r$ as center would instead make the mask $h$-dependent and is not covered.

Finally we give two exact boundary theorems. First, nonlinear recovery from a specified local pushforward has minimax radius equal to an $\ell^\infty$ row-space distance. Second, the complete divisor signature up to $R$ distinguishes primes from composites in $(X,2X]$ once $R\geq\sqrt{2X}$, whereas below $(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{2X}$ a prime and a squarefree semiprime have identical zero signatures. A balanced bilinear factor row separates this null pair exactly. That is a test that the row contains new information, not a proof that its arithmetic sum is small.

# Exact signed selectors in the joint label space

We record the bilinear form of the tensor-Fejér identity from TPC-13. It is the precise sense in which a signed label is retained before any shift average.

###### ref-46fdf08ef982d343

###### ref-1a828b8d65c02424

Let $J$ be a finite index set with pairwise distinct joint frequencies $$\xi_j=(\lambda_j,\rho_j)\in\mathbb R^2,  \qquad j\in J,  \label{eq:abstract-joint-frequencies}$$ and define $$\mathcal O_x(t,s)=\sum_{j\in J}x_j e^{i(t\lambda_j+s\rho_j)}.  \label{eq:abstract-observation}$$ Use the normalized Fejér kernel $$\kappa(t)=\frac2\pi\left(\frac{\sin(t/2)}t\right)^2,  \qquad  \widehat\kappa(\xi)=(1-|\xi|)_+.  \label{eq:fejer-kernel}$$

###### ref-6d22a77c275b6fad

###### ref-e14b10793ce06a5a

> **Proposition: Exact dual-mask identity** \[prop:dual-mask-identity\] Let $T,S>0$ and suppose that, for every $i\ne j$, $$T|\lambda_i-\lambda_j|\geq1  \quad\text{or}\quad  S|\rho_i-\rho_j|\geq1.  \label{eq:pairwise-mask-separation}$$ For arbitrary coefficient and mask vectors $x,\omega\in\mathbb C^J$, $$\frac1{TS}\iint_{\mathbb R^2}  \kappa(t/T)\kappa(s/S)  \mathcal O_x(t,s)\overline{\mathcal O_\omega(t,s)}\,dt\,ds  =\sum_{j\in J}x_j\overline{\omega_j}.  \label{eq:dual-mask-identity}$$ Conversely, if [\[eq:pairwise-mask-separation\]](TPC-014.md#ref-6d22a77c275b6fad) fails for one pair, [\[eq:dual-mask-identity\]](TPC-014.md#ref-e14b10793ce06a5a) fails for some $x,\omega$ supported on that pair.

> **Proof** Expanding the integral, the $(i,j)$ term is multiplied by $$\widehat\kappa(T(\lambda_i-\lambda_j))  \widehat\kappa(S(\rho_i-\rho_j)).$$ The diagonal multiplier is $1$, and every off-diagonal multiplier vanishes under [\[eq:pairwise-mask-separation\]](TPC-014.md#ref-6d22a77c275b6fad). If that condition fails for one pair, both multipliers are strictly positive; choosing $x$ and $\omega$ on the pair leaves a nonzero cross term.

###### ref-528ebf5ea09de8f5

For the factor coordinates [\[eq:gcd-coordinates\]](TPC-014.md#ref-b3f84754e50573dd), use $$\lambda_{a,b,k}=\log(a/b),  \qquad  \rho_{a,b,k}=\log k.  \label{eq:gcd-mellin-frequencies}$$ The global gap bounds in TPC-13 permit $T\asymp X$ and $S\asymp\sqrt X$. Sectorwise filters use the smaller anisotropic scales $T_R\asymp R$ and $S_R\asymp\sqrt{X/R}$ after each sector is isolated. We import those gap estimates; no new large-sieve theorem is asserted here.

###### ref-d1f35fd9e68cddd6

###### ref-6c09f80496b5e9cd

###### ref-e3336273c2c164fd

###### ref-123c0b7dcea18c09

For a fixed shift $h$, set $$x_{a,b,k}(h;X,D)=  \mathbf 1_{ak>D}u(ak)\eta(abk^2+h)W(abk^2/X).  \label{eq:arithmetic-joint-coefficient}$$ For an arithmetic mask $\omega$, apply [\[eq:dual-mask-identity\]](TPC-014.md#ref-e14b10793ce06a5a) with dual vector $\overline\omega$. It recovers $$\sum_{a,b,k\geq1}x_{a,b,k}(h;X,D)  \omega_{a,b,k}  \label{eq:masked-linear-functional}$$ without first replacing $x$ by $|x|^2$. In particular, $$\begin{aligned}  \omega^{\rm end}_{a,b,k}&=\mathbf 1_{b=k=1},  \label{eq:endpoint-mask}\\  \omega^{(k_0)}_{a,b,k}&=\mathbf 1_{k=k_0},  \label{eq:radial-layer-mask}\\  \omega^{(a_0,b_0)}_{a,b,k}&=\mathbf 1_{(a,b)=(a_0,b_0)}  \label{eq:ray-mask}\end{aligned}$$ select the endpoint, a radial layer, and a primitive ray, respectively.

###### ref-b76f7653467a7ed3

> **Proposition: Commutation with translation averaging** \[prop:selector-translation-commutation\] Let the mask be independent of $h$. Under [\[eq:pairwise-mask-separation\]](TPC-014.md#ref-6d22a77c275b6fad), $$\begin{aligned}  &\frac1H\sum_qG(q/H)  \sum_jx_j(h_0+q)\overline{\omega_j}  \notag\\  &\quad={}  \frac1{HTS}\sum_qG(q/H)  \iint_{\mathbb R^2}\kappa(t/T)\kappa(s/S)  \mathcal O_{x(h_0+q)}(t,s)  \overline{\mathcal O_\omega(t,s)}\,dt\,ds.  \label{eq:translation-mask-commutation}\end{aligned}$$

> **Proof** All sums are finite and the Fejér kernels are integrable, so the assertion is [\[prop:dual-mask-identity\]](TPC-014.md#ref-2de122ded98bc796) followed by Fubini’s theorem.

The proposition is an exact commuting-filter statement. It does not make a shift-dependent target mask legal. If $\omega$ is allowed to inspect $h$ and then retain only factorisations of $r+h$ favorable to primality, the coefficient vector itself changes with the translated von Mangoldt function and the short-interval transfer below does not apply.

The mask symbol $\omega_{a,b,k}$ is unrelated to the standard arithmetic function $\omega(r)$ counting distinct prime divisors. The context will always distinguish them.

# A fixed-shift law on the exact coprime shell

###### ref-6d5f050d100a6c72

We now prove [\[eq:intro-fixed-shell-law\]](TPC-014.md#ref-b59202442800d470). The arithmetic function $$d^*(r)=2^{\omega(r)}  =\#\{(a,b)\in\mathbb N^2:ab=r,\ (a,b)=1\}  \label{eq:unitary-divisor-function}$$ counts unitary divisors. We use $a\parallel r$ to mean $a\mid r$ and $(a,r/a)=1$.

###### ref-e03a6880e9f0607c

###### ref-5cd26509da279da3

###### ref-ca188777e9d5caa4

> **Lemma: Exact shell coefficient** \[lem:exact-shell-coefficient\] For every $r\geq1$, $$\sum_{\substack{a\parallel r\\a>1}}u(a)  =\log\operatorname{rad}(r)-d^*(r)+1.  \label{eq:exact-shell-coefficient}$$ Consequently $$\mathcal K_h(X)=  \sum_{r\geq1}  \bigl(\log\operatorname{rad}(r)-d^*(r)+1\bigr)  \eta(r+h)W(r/X).  \label{eq:shell-product-ledger}$$

> **Proof** The unitary divisors of $r=\prod_pp^{\nu_p}$ are obtained by choosing, for each $p\mid r$, either $1$ or the complete prime-power block $p^{\nu_p}$. Hence there are $2^{\omega(r)}$ of them. The von Mangoldt weight is nonzero on a unitary divisor precisely when that divisor is one prime-power block, and therefore $$\sum_{a\parallel r}\Lambda(a)  =\sum_{p\mid r}\log p=\log\operatorname{rad}(r).$$ Since $u(1)=-1$, deleting $a=1$ adds $1$ and proves [\[eq:exact-shell-coefficient\]](TPC-014.md#ref-5cd26509da279da3). Grouping [\[eq:intro-k-shell\]](TPC-014.md#ref-3d44f4d0d8991cf3) by $r=ab$ proves [\[eq:shell-product-ledger\]](TPC-014.md#ref-ca188777e9d5caa4).

The leading term comes entirely from the mismatch between the ordinary mean of $d^*$ and its mean at a shifted prime.

###### ref-9be2d6024dfb4d73

###### ref-98c7acd2e77e53c1

> **Theorem: Unitary-divisor Titchmarsh input** \[thm:unitary-titchmarsh-input\] For every fixed nonzero integer $h$, $$\sum_{p\leq y}d^*(p-h)  =\beta_hy+O_h\left(\frac y{\log y}\right),  \label{eq:unitary-titchmarsh-input}$$ where $d^*(n)=0$ for $n\leq0$ and $\beta_h$ is given by [\[eq:beta-h\]](TPC-014.md#ref-b31c71762a17c6f2).

This is the $k=2$ case of Corollary 1.2 of [`\citet{BiaoWang2026}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-014.md#cite-f7c938b6730b68e7). In the notation of that paper, the displayed constant initially contains $$\frac{\zeta(2)\zeta(3)}{\zeta(6)}  \prod_p\left(1-\frac1{p^2-p+1}\right).$$ The local identity $$\frac{1-p^{-6}}{(1-p^{-2})(1-p^{-3})}  \left(1-\frac1{p^2-p+1}\right)=1$$ reduces it to [\[eq:beta-h\]](TPC-014.md#ref-b31c71762a17c6f2).

###### ref-e85507cac3a361a5

###### ref-d69e84c523480298

###### ref-7443ecad06dc59ba

> **Lemma: The two unitary-divisor means** \[lem:two-unitary-means\] For fixed $h\ne0$, $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_{r\geq1}d^*(r)W(r/X)  &=\frac{XI_0(W)}{\zeta(2)}\log X+O_W(X),  \label{eq:ordinary-unitary-mean}\\  \sum_{r\geq1}d^*(r)\Lambda(r+h)W(r/X)  &=\beta_hXI_0(W)\log X+O_{h,W}(X).  \label{eq:prime-unitary-mean}\end{aligned}$$

###### ref-0f1fe0cd9655ee54

> **Proof** The Dirichlet series identity $$\sum_{r\geq1}\frac{d^*(r)}{r^s}  =\frac{\zeta(s)^2}{\zeta(2s)}  \qquad(\Re s>1)  \label{eq:unitary-dirichlet-series}$$ has a double pole at $s=1$ with leading coefficient $1/\zeta(2)$. Standard Selberg–Delange summation, or the convolution identity obtained from [\[eq:unitary-dirichlet-series\]](TPC-014.md#ref-0f1fe0cd9655ee54), gives $$\sum_{r\leq y}d^*(r)  =\frac y{\zeta(2)}\log y+O(y).$$ Partial summation against $W(r/X)$ proves [\[eq:ordinary-unitary-mean\]](TPC-014.md#ref-d69e84c523480298). Only its $X\log X$ coefficient is used; all secondary $X$ terms are absorbed in the remainder.
>
> For [\[eq:prime-unitary-mean\]](TPC-014.md#ref-7443ecad06dc59ba), first restrict $r+h$ to primes and put $p=r+h$. Partial summation in [\[eq:unitary-titchmarsh-input\]](TPC-014.md#ref-98c7acd2e77e53c1), with the test function $$y\longmapsto \log y\,W((y-h)/X),$$ gives $$\sum_p d^*(p-h)\log p\,W((p-h)/X)  =\beta_hXI_0(W)\log X+O_{h,W}(X).$$ Here the main integral is $$\int_0^\infty \log y\,W((y-h)/X)\,dy  =XI_0(W)\log X+O_{h,W}(X).$$ The targets $r+h=p^j$, $j\geq2$, contribute $O_{h,W}(X^{3/4})$, say: there are $O(X^{1/2})$ such targets, and $d^*(p^j-h)\ll_\delta X^\delta$. Taking $\delta=1/8$ and absorbing the remaining logarithm proves the displayed bound and hence the full von Mangoldt form.

It remains to show that the logarithmic-radical part has no $X\log X$ term.

###### ref-a5a648d7057fa4ac

###### ref-81954b70b1349663

###### ref-2e697ab38813e68a

> **Lemma: Radical residual** \[lem:radical-residual\] For every fixed nonzero $h$, $$\sum_{r\geq1}\log\operatorname{rad}(r)\eta(r+h)W(r/X)  =O_{h,W}(X).  \label{eq:radical-residual}$$ Also $$\sum_{r\geq1}\eta(r+h)W(r/X)=O_{h,W}(X/\log X).  \label{eq:constant-residual-bound}$$

###### ref-a16837a078f8fbb1

###### ref-1c57154d0514d936

###### ref-86ec17fb9208b703

> **Proof** Write $$L(r)=\log r-\log\operatorname{rad}(r)  =\sum_{\substack{p^j\mid r\\j\geq2}}\log p.  \label{eq:squarefull-log-correction}$$ The classical zero-free-region prime number theorem, after translating the variable by fixed $h$ and applying smooth partial summation, gives $$\sum_r\log r\,\eta(r+h)W(r/X)=O_{h,W}(X).  \label{eq:log-residual-bound}$$ The weaker error shown here is more than sufficient.
>
> We claim $$\sum_{r\asymp_WX}L(r)(1+\Lambda(r+h))\ll_{h,W}X.  \label{eq:squarefull-correction-bound}$$ For the unweighted part, insert [\[eq:squarefull-log-correction\]](TPC-014.md#ref-a16837a078f8fbb1); the convergent sum $\sum_{p,j\geq2}(\log p)/p^j$ gives $O(X)$, and the endpoint cost is $O(X^{1/2}\log X)$.
>
> For the von Mangoldt part, first remove proper prime-power targets. Since $L(r)\ll_W\log X$ on the support, $$\sum_{\substack{\ell^\nu\asymp_W X\\\nu\geq2}}  (\log\ell)L(\ell^\nu-h)  \ll_{h,W}X^{1/2}\log X.$$ It remains to consider prime targets. First take $p^j\leq X^{1/2}$. If $p\nmid h$, the Brun–Titchmarsh inequality in the reduced class $h\pmod{p^j}$ gives [`\citep{IwaniecKowalski2004}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-014.md#cite-49dfea87eaada156) $$\sum_{\substack{r\asymp_WX\\p^j\mid r}}  \mathbf 1_{r+h\ {\rm prime}}\Lambda(r+h)  \ll_{h,W}\frac X{\varphi(p^j)}.$$ Indeed, $$\sum_p\sum_{j\geq2}\frac{\log p}{\varphi(p^j)}  =\sum_p\frac{\log p}{(p-1)^2}<\infty.$$ If $p\mid h$, a prime target divisible by $p$ would have to equal $p$ and is absent from the support for large $X$. For $p^j>X^{1/2}$, use $\Lambda\leq\log(3X)$ and count the $O(X/p^j+1)$ multiples. Explicitly, $$\log X  \sum_{\substack{j\geq2,\ p^j>X^{1/2}\\p^j\ll_W X}}  (\log p)\left(\frac X{p^j}+1\right)  \ll_W X^{3/4}\log X;$$ the squares dominate, while $j\geq3$ is smaller. This proves [\[eq:squarefull-correction-bound\]](TPC-014.md#ref-86ec17fb9208b703). Combining it with [\[eq:log-residual-bound\]](TPC-014.md#ref-1c57154d0514d936) proves [\[eq:radical-residual\]](TPC-014.md#ref-81954b70b1349663).
>
> Finally, [\[eq:constant-residual-bound\]](TPC-014.md#ref-2e697ab38813e68a) is the ordinary smooth prime number theorem, again after translating by fixed $h$.

###### ref-3be1ca9d6c3e19e5

###### ref-f327a24a58e5f265

> **Theorem: Fixed-shift coprime-shell law** \[thm:fixed-shell-law\] Let $h\ne0$ be fixed. Then $$\mathcal K_h(X)=  \left(\frac1{\zeta(2)}-\beta_h\right)  XI_0(W)\log X+O_{h,W}(X).  \label{eq:fixed-shell-law}$$ For $h=2$, the leading coefficient is $6/\pi^2-1/3$.

> **Proof** Insert [\[eq:shell-product-ledger\]](TPC-014.md#ref-ca188777e9d5caa4). The contribution of $-d^*(r)\eta(r+h)$ is the difference of [\[eq:ordinary-unitary-mean\]](TPC-014.md#ref-d69e84c523480298) and [\[eq:prime-unitary-mean\]](TPC-014.md#ref-7443ecad06dc59ba). The radical and constant terms are $O(X)$ by [\[lem:radical-residual\]](TPC-014.md#ref-a5a648d7057fa4ac).

> **Remark: Where the fixed shift was bought** The theorem does not estimate each orientation $b$ separately. The exact sum over all $b$ produces $d^*(r)$, and the unitary-divisor Titchmarsh theorem evaluates that aggregate at shifted primes. If one retains only $b=1$, the sum becomes $$\sum_a(\Lambda(a)-1)(\Lambda(a+h)-1)W(a/X),$$ which is the fixed-shift prime-pair covariance. Thus [\[thm:fixed-shell-law\]](TPC-014.md#ref-3be1ca9d6c3e19e5) is genuine fixed-$h$ progress at the radial-shell level, not an endpoint estimate in disguise.

# An $\ell^2$ short-shift transfer for the full dual ball

We now change the shift quantifier. The underlying prime theorem is the almost-all short-interval result of [`\citet{GuthMaynard2026}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-014.md#cite-47cea3521d045af7); the point here is that its exceptional set yields a small vector in the full dual coefficient space.

###### ref-4a27a12260245056

For integer $h_0$ and parameters satisfying [\[eq:short-shift-range\]](TPC-014.md#ref-153e744333265af6) and [\[eq:translation-geometric-range\]](TPC-014.md#ref-14e96a8ef0319116), define, on the product-center interval in [\[eq:W-support\]](TPC-014.md#ref-fb88641c876fcc12), $$\Delta_{X,H,h_0}(r)=  \frac1H\sum_{q\in\mathbb Z}G(q/H)\eta(r+h_0+q).  \label{eq:translated-residual-vector}$$

###### ref-ffcaecb07e0cb8a5

###### ref-d65496ca8368b962

> **Theorem: Translated prime residual in $\ell^2$** \[thm:translated-residual-l2\] Fix $0<\varepsilon<13/15$. For every $A>0$, uniformly in the stated geometric range, $$\left(  \sum_{\alpha X\leq r\leq\beta X}  |\Delta_{X,H,h_0}(r)|^2  \right)^{1/2}  \ll_{A,\varepsilon,W,G}X^{1/2}(\log X)^{-A}.  \label{eq:translated-residual-l2}$$

###### ref-eb1587a20cf0076e

###### ref-4283daf891d1c12a

###### ref-b6b7818ff4bb061c

> **Proof** We give the smoothing argument because $G$ may have two-sided support. First suppose $H\leq X^{0.98}$ and put $$L=\left\lceil\exp\{(\log X)^{1/8}\}\right\rceil.$$ Partition a fixed interval containing $\operatorname{supp}G$ into $L$ equal pieces. The corresponding integer shift blocks have length $H/L+O(1)\geq X^{2/15+\varepsilon/2}$ for large $X$, and at most $O(X^{0.98})$. Apply Corollary 1.4 of [`\citet{GuthMaynard2026}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-014.md#cite-47cea3521d045af7) to each forward block. Negative shifts merely change the starting point, which remains comparable with $X$ by [\[eq:translation-geometric-range\]](TPC-014.md#ref-14e96a8ef0319116). Taking the union over $O(L)$ blocks gives a set $\mathfrak E\subset[\alpha X,\beta X]\cap\mathbb Z$ with $$|\mathfrak E|\ll_{\varepsilon,W,G}  X\exp\{-c_{\varepsilon,G}(\log X)^{1/4}\}.  \label{eq:l2-exception-size}$$ Outside this set, the prime number theorem holds on every block with a stretched-exponential relative error. Freeze $G$ on each block. Its $C^1$ variation contributes $O_G(H\log X/L)$ by the elementary bound $\Lambda\leq\log(3X)$, which is $O_{B,G}(H(\log X)^{-B})$ for every fixed $B$. Partial summation and the negligible proper prime powers therefore give, outside $\mathfrak E$, $$|\Delta_{X,H,h_0}(r)|\ll_{B,\varepsilon,G}(\log X)^{-B}  \label{eq:l2-regular-pointwise}$$ for every $B>0$.
>
> If $H>X^{0.98}$, partition the shift range into consecutive blocks of length between $X^{0.97}$ and $2X^{0.97}$. Corollary 1.3 of [`\citet{GuthMaynard2026}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-014.md#cite-47cea3521d045af7), whose all-interval range begins below this length, applies to every block. Freezing $G$ now costs $O_G(X^{0.97}\log X)=O_{B,G}(H(\log X)^{-B})$. Thus [\[eq:l2-regular-pointwise\]](TPC-014.md#ref-4283daf891d1c12a) holds at every center in the long-window range, and we take $\mathfrak E=\varnothing$. This also explains why the claimed range extends beyond the $X^{0.99}$ upper endpoint of the almost-all corollary.
>
> At an exceptional center, the elementary upper-bound sieve, or simply the pointwise bound for $\Lambda$, gives $$|\Delta_{X,H,h_0}(r)|\ll_G\log(3X).  \label{eq:l2-exception-pointwise}$$ Choose $B>A+2$. Squaring [\[eq:l2-regular-pointwise\]](TPC-014.md#ref-4283daf891d1c12a) and summing over $O(X)$ regular centers gives $O(X(\log X)^{-2A})$. Equations [\[eq:l2-exception-size\]](TPC-014.md#ref-eb1587a20cf0076e)–[\[eq:l2-exception-pointwise\]](TPC-014.md#ref-b6b7818ff4bb061c) give a smaller bound on the exceptional set, because the stretched exponential absorbs every fixed logarithmic power. Taking square roots proves the theorem. The $O_G(H^{-1})$ Riemann-sum error in the constant part of $\eta=\Lambda-1$ is absorbed since $H$ is a fixed positive power of $X$.

###### ref-367a734bc43f9501

###### ref-a036e3c979e634ee

> **Corollary: Abstract coefficient-space transfer** \[cor:abstract-coefficient-transfer\] Suppose $c_X(r)$ is supported on $[\alpha X,\beta X]$ and, for some fixed $B\geq0$, $$\sum_r|c_X(r)|^2\ll X(\log X)^B. \label{eq:coefficient-l2-hypothesis}$$ Assume that the implied constant is independent of $X,H,h_0$. Then, for every $A>0$, $$\frac1H\sum_qG(q/H)  \sum_rc_X(r)\eta(r+h_0+q)  \ll_{A,B,\varepsilon,W,G,c}X(\log X)^{-A}.  \label{eq:abstract-coefficient-transfer}$$

> **Proof** Cauchy–Schwarz and [\[thm:translated-residual-l2\]](TPC-014.md#ref-ffcaecb07e0cb8a5), requested with $A+B/2+2$ in place of $A$, give the result.

###### ref-3349ca020361455c

###### ref-94c3ea01f0b9260d

We now verify that every bounded full-coordinate mask belongs to the same coefficient ball. Group [\[eq:masked-residual\]](TPC-014.md#ref-b210369519c59e08) by $r=abk^2$: $$\mathcal Z_{h,D}^{\omega}(X)  =\sum_rC_{\omega,D}(r)\eta(r+h),  \label{eq:masked-product-grouping}$$ where $$C_{\omega,D}(r)=W(r/X)  \sum_{\substack{abk^2=r,\ (a,b)=1\\ak>D}}  \omega_{X,D}(a,b,k)u(ak).  \label{eq:masked-product-coefficient}$$

###### ref-908611bc65280f1f

###### ref-88df5f83b522df1a

###### ref-23a1b121d18dbd38

> **Lemma: Uniform bounded-mask ledger** \[lem:bounded-mask-ledger\] Uniformly in $D\geq1$ and every mask satisfying [\[eq:bounded-mask\]](TPC-014.md#ref-bd254729359297da), $$\begin{aligned}  |C_{\omega,D}(r)|  &\leq\|W\|_\infty\bigl(\log r+\tau(r)\bigr),  \label{eq:mask-pointwise-ledger}\\  \sum_r|C_{\omega,D}(r)|^2  &\ll_WX(\log X)^3.  \label{eq:mask-l2-ledger}\end{aligned}$$

> **Proof** The bijection [\[eq:gcd-coordinates\]](TPC-014.md#ref-b3f84754e50573dd) sends the triples with $abk^2=r$ to the factor pairs $(n,m)$ with $nm=r$, and $ak=n$. Therefore $$|C_{\omega,D}(r)|  \leq\|W\|_\infty\sum_{n\mid r}|\Lambda(n)-1|  \leq\|W\|_\infty  \left(\sum_{n\mid r}\Lambda(n)+\tau(r)\right).$$ The divisor identity $\sum_{n\mid r}\Lambda(n)=\log r$ proves [\[eq:mask-pointwise-ledger\]](TPC-014.md#ref-88df5f83b522df1a). The classical second moment [`\citep{MontgomeryVaughan2007}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-014.md#cite-3c91c7e57f203d4b) $$\sum_{r\leq y}\tau(r)^2\ll y(\log(2y))^3$$ then proves [\[eq:mask-l2-ledger\]](TPC-014.md#ref-23a1b121d18dbd38).

###### ref-57bd1a3062e45d90

###### ref-9f350efc8ffbd748

> **Theorem: Uniform full-label mask transfer** \[thm:uniform-mask-transfer\] Fix $0<\varepsilon<13/15$. For every $A>0$, uniformly in $D\geq1$, every mask [\[eq:bounded-mask\]](TPC-014.md#ref-bd254729359297da), and the range of [\[thm:translated-residual-l2\]](TPC-014.md#ref-ffcaecb07e0cb8a5), $$\frac1H\sum_qG(q/H)  \mathcal Z_{h_0+q,D}^{\omega}(X)  \ll_{A,\varepsilon,W,G}X(\log X)^{-A}.  \label{eq:uniform-mask-transfer}$$ The implied constant is independent of the mask.

> **Proof** Apply [\[cor:abstract-coefficient-transfer\]](TPC-014.md#ref-4e8e837b6ed1d776) to [\[eq:masked-product-coefficient\]](TPC-014.md#ref-94c3ea01f0b9260d), using [\[eq:mask-l2-ledger\]](TPC-014.md#ref-23a1b121d18dbd38).

###### ref-2b071728baa7e834

###### ref-327d44af9bf5b5c4

There is an equivalent raw-target formulation. Put $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal T_{h,D}^{\omega}(X)  &=\sum_{\substack{a,b,k\geq1,\ (a,b)=1\\ak>D}}  \omega_{X,D}(a,b,k)u(ak)  \Lambda(abk^2+h)W(abk^2/X),  \label{eq:raw-masked-transform}\\  \mathcal M_D^{\omega}(X)  &=\sum_{\substack{a,b,k\geq1,\ (a,b)=1\\ak>D}}  \omega_{X,D}(a,b,k)u(ak)W(abk^2/X).  \label{eq:raw-mask-model}\end{aligned}$$

###### ref-b604d5b80d98eb04

###### ref-381574c364ca376b

> **Corollary: Uniform raw mask model** \[cor:uniform-raw-mask-model\] Under the hypotheses of [\[thm:uniform-mask-transfer\]](TPC-014.md#ref-57bd1a3062e45d90), $$\frac1H\sum_qG(q/H)\mathcal T_{h_0+q,D}^{\omega}(X)  =I_0(G)\mathcal M_D^{\omega}(X)  +O_{A,\varepsilon,W,G}\bigl(X(\log X)^{-A}\bigr).  \label{eq:uniform-raw-mask-model}$$

> **Proof** Write $\Lambda=1+\eta$ and use [\[thm:uniform-mask-transfer\]](TPC-014.md#ref-57bd1a3062e45d90). Euler summation gives $H^{-1}\sum_qG(q/H)=I_0(G)+O_G(H^{-1})$. The pointwise ledger and the mean order of $\tau$ give $|\mathcal M_D^\omega|\ll_WX\log(2X)$, so the Riemann-sum error is absorbed in the displayed remainder.

> **Remark: Uniform masks are not uniform target adaptation** The theorem permits a different mask for every $X,D,H$, including masks chosen after inspecting the source labels. It does not permit $\omega=\omega_h$ to inspect each translated target. Such a family could retain precisely the prime targets and would make the assertion circular.

# Endpoint consequences and parity-conditioned shifts

###### ref-28a7f79325a605f7

The endpoint is selected before the shift average by [\[eq:endpoint-mask\]](TPC-014.md#ref-6c09f80496b5e9cd). If $D\leq X^{1-\eta_0}$ for a fixed $\eta_0>0$, then $a>D$ automatically on the support of $W(a/X)$ for large $X$, and $$\mathcal Z_h^{\rm end}(X)=  \sum_{n\geq1}u(n)\eta(n+h)W(n/X).  \label{eq:endpoint-centered-correlation}$$

###### ref-fa5dd2b407f5fbb3

###### ref-ad85b67b1488e735

> **Corollary: Signed endpoint mean at the two-fifteenths scale** \[cor:endpoint-centered-mean\] Under the hypotheses of [\[thm:uniform-mask-transfer\]](TPC-014.md#ref-57bd1a3062e45d90), for every $A>0$, $$\frac1H\sum_qG(q/H)  \sum_n u(n)\eta(n+h_0+q)W(n/X)  \ll_{A,\varepsilon,W,G}X(\log X)^{-A}.  \label{eq:endpoint-centered-mean}$$

> **Proof** Take $D=1$ and $\omega=\omega^{\rm end}$ in [\[thm:uniform-mask-transfer\]](TPC-014.md#ref-57bd1a3062e45d90).

The raw form is a smooth average of prime-pair correlations.

###### ref-cb840b261d771e7a

###### ref-288d67862526709b

> **Corollary: Raw endpoint prime-pair mean** \[cor:raw-endpoint-mean\] Under the same hypotheses, $$\begin{aligned}  &\frac1H\sum_qG(q/H)  \sum_n\Lambda(n)\Lambda(n+h_0+q)W(n/X)  \notag\\  &\qquad={}  XI_0(W)I_0(G)+O_{A,\varepsilon,W,G}  \bigl(X(\log X)^{-A}\bigr).  \label{eq:raw-endpoint-mean}\end{aligned}$$

> **Proof** Apply [\[cor:abstract-coefficient-transfer\]](TPC-014.md#ref-4e8e837b6ed1d776) to $c_X(n)=\Lambda(n)W(n/X)$. Its hypothesis follows from the classical estimate $$\sum_n\Lambda(n)^2|W(n/X)|^2\ll_WX\log X.$$ This controls the centered target. Now use $$\frac1H\sum_qG(q/H)=I_0(G)+O_G(H^{-1})$$ and the smooth prime number theorem $\sum_n\Lambda(n)W(n/X)=XI_0(W)+O_A(X(\log X)^{-A})$. The polynomial lower bound on $H$ absorbs the Riemann-sum error.

This corollary has a different quantifier from the almost-all-shift theorem of [`\citet{MatomakiRadziwillTao2019}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-014.md#cite-03024995d598450c). That theorem gives the singular-series asymptotic at almost every individual shift in its long window once $H\geq X^{8/33+\varepsilon}$. On the overlapping unmasked range it is stronger and yields such a mean. Equation [\[eq:raw-endpoint-mean\]](TPC-014.md#ref-288d67862526709b) reaches the shorter two-fifteenths scale and the ambient theorem permits arbitrary source masks, but it gives no individual-shift asymptotic.

## Even shifts and the correct local center

###### ref-61c9142ba4ca7a1a

###### ref-8aa2aafc8b73ef2b

###### ref-c9f821afb9236d0c

Conditioning the shift on parity changes the local mean. Suppose $h_0$ is even and sum only over even $q$. For a product center $r$, define $$\eta_r^{(2)}(h)=  \Lambda(r+h)-2\mathbf 1_{2\nmid r},  \qquad h\equiv0\pmod2.  \label{eq:parity-conditioned-residual}$$ If $r$ is odd, the target runs over odd integers and has conditional von Mangoldt density $2$; if $r$ is even, the target is even and only powers of $2$ contribute. $$\begin{aligned}  C_H&=\frac1H\sum_qG(q/H),  &  C_H^{(2)}&=\frac2H\sum_{q\equiv0\ (2)}G(q/H),  \label{eq:parity-lattice-masses}\\  \Delta^{(2)}(r)  &=\frac2H\sum_{q\equiv0\ (2)}  G(q/H)\eta_r^{(2)}(h_0+q).  \label{eq:parity-translated-residual}\end{aligned}$$

###### ref-b87f4e3813cd3d4a

###### ref-bd7c43caa99ccb68

> **Theorem: Uniform even-shift mask transfer** \[thm:even-mask-transfer\] Under the hypotheses of [\[thm:uniform-mask-transfer\]](TPC-014.md#ref-57bd1a3062e45d90), with $h_0$ even, for every $A>0$, $$\begin{aligned}  &\sup_{\|\omega\|_\infty\leq1}  \left|\frac2H  \sum_{\substack{q\in\mathbb Z\\q\equiv0\ (2)}}G(q/H)  \sum_{\substack{a,b,k\geq1,\ (a,b)=1\\ak>D}}  \omega_{X,D}(a,b,k)u(ak)  \eta_{abk^2}^{(2)}(h_0+q)W(abk^2/X)  \right|  \notag\\  &\qquad\ll_{A,\varepsilon,W,G}X(\log X)^{-A}.  \label{eq:even-mask-transfer}\end{aligned}$$

> **Proof** Euler summation gives $$C_H=I_0(G)+O_G(H^{-1}),  \qquad  C_H^{(2)}=I_0(G)+O_G(H^{-1}).$$ If $r$ is odd, then odd $q$ make $r+h_0+q$ even. At height comparable with $X$, the von Mangoldt support on that lattice contains only $O_G(1)$ powers of $2$. Consequently $$\Delta^{(2)}(r)  =2\Delta_{X,H,h_0}(r)  +O_G\!\left(\frac{\log X}{H}\right).$$ If $r$ is even, the even-$q$ target itself is even and $$\Delta^{(2)}(r)  =O_G\!\left(\frac{\log X}{H}\right).$$ The polynomial lower bound for $H$ therefore gives $\|\Delta^{(2)}\|_2\ll_A X^{1/2}(\log X)^{-A}$. Apply the mask ledger [\[eq:mask-l2-ledger\]](TPC-014.md#ref-23a1b121d18dbd38) and Cauchy–Schwarz.

###### ref-eb60e8fa8df64b4e

###### ref-b060f29ea577837f

> **Corollary: Conditional even prime-pair mean** \[cor:even-prime-pair-mean\] Under the hypotheses of [\[thm:even-mask-transfer\]](TPC-014.md#ref-b87f4e3813cd3d4a), one has $$\begin{aligned}  &\frac2H\sum_{\substack{q\in\mathbb Z\\q\equiv0\ (2)}}G(q/H)  \sum_n\Lambda(n)\Lambda(n+h_0+q)W(n/X)  \notag\\  &\qquad={}  2XI_0(W)I_0(G)+O_{A,\varepsilon,W,G}  \bigl(X(\log X)^{-A}\bigr).  \label{eq:even-prime-pair-mean}\end{aligned}$$

> **Proof** Let $$P^{(2)}(n)=  \frac2H\sum_{q\equiv0\ (2)}  G(q/H)\Lambda(n+h_0+q).$$ The preceding proof gives $P^{(2)}(n)=\Delta^{(2)}(n)+2C_H^{(2)}\mathbf 1_{2\nmid n}$. By Cauchy–Schwarz, the contribution of $\Delta^{(2)}$ against $\Lambda(n)W(n/X)$ is $O_A(X(\log X)^{-A})$ after requesting a larger value of $A$. The smooth prime number theorem, with the powers of $2$ removed, gives $$\sum_{2\nmid n}\Lambda(n)W(n/X)  =XI_0(W)+O_A(X(\log X)^{-A}).$$ Since $C_H^{(2)}=I_0(G)+O_G(H^{-1})$, the asserted main term follows.

The factor $2$ is the conditional mean of the prime-pair singular series on even shifts. Without the normalization $2/H$, the leading coefficient is $1$, the same as in [\[eq:raw-endpoint-mean\]](TPC-014.md#ref-288d67862526709b). Neither normalization selects the particular even shift $2$.

# Masked Vaughan packets after target recentering

The full-label theorem controls factorisations of the source center $r=mn$. A target-side Vaughan packet requires a different ordering of operations: first make the factorized target itself the center, and only then average its translated prime partner.

###### ref-f80e65b7b68a2bcd

###### ref-b29bf209e0b8a7d4

###### ref-12f9ba543c63a621

For a finitely supported function $F$ and parameters $U,V\geq1$, put $$\beta_V(k)=\sum_{\substack{d\mid k\\d\leq V}}\mu(d).  \label{eq:vaughan-beta}$$ The truncated Möbius coefficient $\beta_V(k)$ is unrelated to the fixed-shift Euler factor $\beta_h$ in [\[eq:beta-h\]](TPC-014.md#ref-b31c71762a17c6f2). Vaughan’s identity in the form used in TPC-6 [`\citep{WangTPC6}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-014.md#cite-6473d4ef04e4cd49) is $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_t\Lambda(t)F(t) ={}&\sum_{t\leq U}\Lambda(t)F(t)  -\sum_{\substack{\ell\leq U\\d\leq V}}  \Lambda(\ell)\mu(d)\sum_{j\geq1}F(\ell dj)  \notag\\  &+\sum_{d\leq V}\mu(d)  \sum_{j\geq1}(\log j)F(dj)  -\sum_{\substack{\ell>U\\k>1}}  \Lambda(\ell)\beta_V(k)F(\ell k).  \label{eq:vaughan-identity}\end{aligned}$$ Moreover, $$\beta_V(k)=0\qquad(1<k\leq V),  \label{eq:vaughan-beta-vanishing}$$ so the last sum is supported on $\ell>U$, $k>V$. The identity itself is classical [`\citep{Vaughan1977,HeathBrown1982}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-014.md#cite-5d701c205feb1c96); [\[HeathBrown1982\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-014.md#cite-4c4858d3c0e33012); we include it to fix signs and the factor labels.

###### ref-ff134dca4f90f095

Consider the target-weighted prime-pair form $$\mathcal C_h^{\rm tar}(X)=  \sum_t\Lambda(t)\Lambda(t+h)W(t/X).  \label{eq:target-weighted-pair-form}$$ Taking $F(t)=\Lambda(t+h)W(t/X)$ in [\[eq:vaughan-identity\]](TPC-014.md#ref-b29bf209e0b8a7d4), the last term is the hard packet. We permit an arbitrary bounded mask on its now fixed factorisation.

###### ref-593bd1a1fe32dff8

###### ref-337582e5bb5d1d96

###### ref-26024a0396452c0c

###### ref-f00aa0c8bb2831c6

> **Definition: Masked hard-packet coefficient** \[def:masked-hard-packet\] Let $|\omega_{\ell,k}|\leq1$ be independent of $h$. Define $$\begin{aligned}  c_{U,V}^{\omega}(t)  &=-W(t/X)  \sum_{\substack{\ell k=t\\\ell>U,\ k>V}}  \omega_{\ell,k}\Lambda(\ell)\beta_V(k),  \label{eq:hard-packet-coefficient}\\  \mathcal H_{h;U,V}^{\omega}(X)  &=\sum_tc_{U,V}^{\omega}(t)\Lambda(t+h),  \label{eq:raw-hard-packet}\\  \mathcal H_{h;U,V}^{\omega,\circ}(X)  &=\sum_tc_{U,V}^{\omega}(t)\eta(t+h).  \label{eq:centered-hard-packet}\end{aligned}$$

The unmasked case $\omega\equiv1$ is exactly the final term of Vaughan’s identity for [\[eq:target-weighted-pair-form\]](TPC-014.md#ref-ff134dca4f90f095). A mask extracts a specified dyadic or signed subpacket; the extraction is made before the shift is sampled.

###### ref-32a6b00f3108abb4

###### ref-75499e835a285ad1

###### ref-a9ce0bbc7135751b

> **Lemma: Uniform hard-packet ledger** \[lem:hard-packet-ledger\] Uniformly in $U,V\geq1$ and $\|\omega\|_\infty\leq1$, $$\begin{aligned}  |c_{U,V}^{\omega}(t)|  &\leq\|W\|_\infty\tau(t)\log t,  \label{eq:hard-packet-pointwise}\\  \sum_t|c_{U,V}^{\omega}(t)|^2  &\ll_WX(\log X)^5.  \label{eq:hard-packet-l2}\end{aligned}$$

> **Proof** Equation [\[eq:vaughan-beta\]](TPC-014.md#ref-f80e65b7b68a2bcd) gives $|\beta_V(k)|\leq\tau(k)\leq\tau(t)$ when $k\mid t$. Hence $$|c_{U,V}^{\omega}(t)|  \leq\|W\|_\infty\tau(t)  \sum_{\ell\mid t}\Lambda(\ell)  =\|W\|_\infty\tau(t)\log t.$$ The second assertion follows from the classical second moment of $\tau$.

###### ref-1507ae75dfd5a3f7

###### ref-d6877a9e96be14aa

###### ref-c89860cd96e6c019

> **Theorem: Short-shift averaged closure of every masked hard packet** \[thm:masked-hard-packet-transfer\] Fix $0<\varepsilon<13/15$. Under the range of [\[thm:translated-residual-l2\]](TPC-014.md#ref-ffcaecb07e0cb8a5), uniformly in $U,V$ and every bounded shift-independent mask, $$\frac1H\sum_qG(q/H)  \mathcal H_{h_0+q;U,V}^{\omega,\circ}(X)  \ll_{A,\varepsilon,W,G}X(\log X)^{-A}  \label{eq:centered-hard-packet-transfer}$$ for every $A>0$. Equivalently, $$\begin{aligned}  \frac1H\sum_qG(q/H)  \mathcal H_{h_0+q;U,V}^{\omega}(X)  ={}&I_0(G)\sum_tc_{U,V}^{\omega}(t)  \notag\\  &+O_{A,\varepsilon,W,G}\bigl(X(\log X)^{-A}\bigr).  \label{eq:raw-hard-packet-transfer}\end{aligned}$$

> **Proof** Apply [\[cor:abstract-coefficient-transfer\]](TPC-014.md#ref-4e8e837b6ed1d776) with $c_X=c_{U,V}^{\omega}$ and use [\[eq:hard-packet-l2\]](TPC-014.md#ref-a9ce0bbc7135751b). The raw form follows from $\Lambda=1+\eta$ and Euler summation. Indeed, $|\sum_t c_{U,V}^{\omega}(t)|\ll_WX(\log X)^{5/2}$ by Cauchy–Schwarz, so the $O_G(H^{-1})$ lattice error is absorbed by the polynomial lower bound on $H$.

> **Remark: What has and has not become Type II** The coefficient in [\[eq:hard-packet-coefficient\]](TPC-014.md#ref-337582e5bb5d1d96) is the bilinear hard packet left by Vaughan’s identity, and the theorem controls every one of its fixed masks. A balanced dyadic Type-II packet is obtained only after placing both factors in comparable prescribed ranges. The cancellation in [\[eq:centered-hard-packet-transfer\]](TPC-014.md#ref-d6877a9e96be14aa), however, comes from averaging the second prime variable and importing the Guth–Maynard short-interval theorem. We do not prove a new fixed-$h$ Type-II estimate.

> **Remark: Why target recentering is mandatory** If $r$ remains the outer center and one masks a factorisation of $r+h$, the mask varies with $h$ and can align with the target primes. Such a packet is not covered by [\[thm:translated-residual-l2\]](TPC-014.md#ref-ffcaecb07e0cb8a5). In [\[eq:hard-packet-coefficient\]](TPC-014.md#ref-337582e5bb5d1d96), $t=\ell k$ is fixed first; only the partner $t+h$ is translated. This is the legal order of operations.

# The local-information null space and the Type-II gate

The preceding theorems preserve arbitrary labels that are already present in a coefficient array. They do not say whether a proposed label family contains enough information to distinguish primes from balanced semiprimes. We make that question exact.

## An exact minimax formula for a local pushforward

###### ref-283abd7b55f69f1d

Let $I$ and $\Sigma$ be finite sets, let $\sigma:I\to\Sigma$ be a signature map, and let $w=(w_i)_{i\in I}$ be real weights. The associated pushforward is $$(\mathcal N_{\sigma,w}x)(s)  =\sum_{\substack{i\in I\\\sigma(i)=s}}w_i x_i,  \qquad s\in\Sigma.  \label{eq:local-pushforward}$$ For $v\in\mathbb R^I$, write $L_v(x)=\sum_i v_ix_i$.

###### ref-548936a39899fed2

###### ref-bdab8e24777fbf8c

###### ref-758f4ac3bb140fb8

###### ref-a34aeacfbab9ba38

> **Theorem: Exact local-data minimax radius** \[thm:local-minimax\] For $B>0$, even allowing an arbitrary nonlinear decoder $\Phi:\operatorname{ran}\mathcal N_{\sigma,w}\to\mathbb R$, one has $$\begin{aligned}  &\inf_{\Phi}  \sup_{\|x\|_1\leq B}  \left|L_v(x)-\Phi(\mathcal N_{\sigma,w}x)\right|  \notag\\  &\qquad={}  B\inf_{g:\Sigma\to\mathbb R}  \max_{i\in I}|v_i-w_i g(\sigma(i))|.  \label{eq:local-minimax}\end{aligned}$$ Consequently exact recovery on the $\ell^1$ ball is possible if and only if $$v_i=w_i g(\sigma(i))  \quad(i\in I)  \label{eq:local-exact-recovery}$$ for some $g$. In the unweighted case $w_i=1$, $$e_B(v;\sigma)  =\frac B2\max_{s\in\Sigma}  \left(  \max_{\sigma(i)=s}v_i-\min_{\sigma(i)=s}v_i  \right).  \label{eq:unweighted-fiber-oscillation}$$ Empty fibers are omitted from the maximum.

> **Proof** Let $N=\mathcal N_{\sigma,w}$. Two points of the $\ell^1$ ball with the same observation differ by an element of $\ker N$ of norm at most $2B$. Conversely, if $z\in\ker N$ and $\|z\|_1\leq2B$, then $z/2$ and $-z/2$ are such a pair. The smallest worst-case radius of all observation fibers is therefore $$B\sup_{\substack{z\in\ker N\\\|z\|_1\leq1}}|L_v(z)|.$$ Finite-dimensional quotient duality identifies this norm with $$B\,\operatorname{dist}_{\ell^\infty}  \bigl(v,\operatorname{ran}N^*\bigr).$$ Since $(N^*g)_i=w_i g(\sigma(i))$, this is [\[eq:local-minimax\]](TPC-014.md#ref-bdab8e24777fbf8c). The zero-distance criterion gives [\[eq:local-exact-recovery\]](TPC-014.md#ref-758f4ac3bb140fb8). When $w_i=1$, the best constant on each fiber is the midpoint of the extreme $v_i$ values, proving [\[eq:unweighted-fiber-oscillation\]](TPC-014.md#ref-a34aeacfbab9ba38).

Thus adding nonlinear post-processing does not repair a missing row. The obstruction is an exact null-space statement, not a limitation of a particular learning rule or inversion algorithm.

## The square-root divisor-information dichotomy

###### ref-9e9e5074fa0bb5f3

For $t\in I_X=(X,2X]\cap\mathbb N$ and $R\geq2$, define the complete local divisor signature $$\sigma_R(t)=  \bigl(\mathbf 1_{d\mid t,\ d<t}\bigr)_{2\leq d\leq R}.  \label{eq:divisor-signature}$$ Thus only proper divisors are recorded, even if $R\geq t$.

###### ref-ddf3cb2aef8b161a

###### ref-0c11aec24b2db4c5

> **Proposition: Square-root information gate** \[prop:square-root-information-gate\] The following statements hold.
>
> 1.  If $R\geq\sqrt{2X}$, then $\sigma_R(t)=0$ for $t\in I_X$ if and only if $t$ is prime.
>
> 2.  Fix $0<\delta<1$. For all sufficiently large $X$, uniformly for $R\leq(1-\delta)\sqrt{2X}$, there are a prime $p\in I_X$ and distinct primes $q_1,q_2>R$ such that $$s=q_1q_2\in I_X,   \qquad   \sigma_R(p)=\sigma_R(s)=0.   \label{eq:prime-semiprime-null-pair}$$

> **Proof** If $t\leq2X$ is composite, it has a divisor $d$ with $2\leq d\leq\sqrt t\leq\sqrt{2X}$, proving (i).
>
> For (ii), put $$c_0=\max\{1,(1-\delta)\sqrt2\}<\sqrt2$$ and choose a closed interval $[c_1,c_2]$ strictly inside $(c_0,\sqrt2)$. The prime number theorem supplies two distinct primes in $[c_1\sqrt X,c_2\sqrt X]$ for all sufficiently large $X$; after shrinking the interval if necessary, every product of two points in it lies in $(X,2X]$. Call the primes $q_1,q_2$. They exceed $R$, so their product has zero signature. A prime $p\in(X,2X]$, again supplied by the prime number theorem, has the same signature.

This is an information-resolution threshold. It is not an analytic level of distribution and does not assert that sieving to $\sqrt{2X}$ can be carried out with a sufficiently accurate remainder. It is a theorem about the specified data interface $\sigma_R$: an algorithm supplied with the integer $t$ itself or with additional arithmetic rows has more information. Its relation to the classical parity limitation is therefore diagnostic, not an alternative proof of that limitation [`\citep{Selberg1952Parity}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-014.md#cite-aa901a19a21f6091).

###### ref-616f53f9df8b0773

###### ref-eb6b8205d7cc0e2e

###### ref-17aa66bf50588afb

Take the unweighted pushforward by $\sigma_R$ and view an unknown $x\in\ell^1(I_X)$ as a signed mass distribution. On the null pair in [\[eq:prime-semiprime-null-pair\]](TPC-014.md#ref-0c11aec24b2db4c5), the prime indicator takes values $1$ and $0$, the Liouville function takes values $-1$ and $1$, and $\Lambda$ takes values $\log p$ and $0$. Hence [\[thm:local-minimax\]](TPC-014.md#ref-548936a39899fed2) gives, on the unit ball, $$\begin{aligned}  e_1(\mathbf 1_{\mathbb P};\sigma_R)&=\frac12,  \label{eq:prime-indicator-minimax}\\  e_1(\lambda;\sigma_R)&=1,  \label{eq:liouville-minimax}\\  e_1(\Lambda;\sigma_R)&\geq\frac12\log p.  \label{eq:lambda-minimax}\end{aligned}$$ The first two equalities also use the global ranges $\mathbf 1_{\mathbb P}\in\{0,1\}$ and $\lambda\in\{-1,1\}$.

## A balanced factor row sees the null direction

###### ref-9820607f4850d3d3

###### ref-a8daef388e93849f

For coefficient sequences $\alpha,\beta$, define the bilinear observation on a response vector $x$ by $$\mathcal B_{\alpha,\beta}(x)  =\sum_{m,n}\alpha_m\beta_n x(mn).  \label{eq:typeii-row}$$ For the pair in [\[eq:prime-semiprime-null-pair\]](TPC-014.md#ref-0c11aec24b2db4c5), $$\mathcal B_{\delta_{q_1},\delta_{q_2}}(\delta_p)=0,  \qquad  \mathcal B_{\delta_{q_1},\delta_{q_2}}(\delta_s)=1.  \label{eq:typeii-separates-null-pair}$$ Thus a single balanced factor row strictly enlarges this specified divisor-signature data model: it separates two points that every test $d\leq R$ identifies.

###### ref-e296caadfd0dddcd

###### ref-074c466be1c33c7b

The same distinction is visible in the hard coefficient from [\[eq:vaughan-identity\]](TPC-014.md#ref-b29bf209e0b8a7d4). Remove the smooth weight and put $$H_{U,V}(N)=  -\sum_{\substack{\ell k=N\\\ell>U,\ k>V}}  \Lambda(\ell)\beta_V(k).  \label{eq:finite-vaughan-hard-row}$$ If $p,q_1,q_2>\max\{U,V\}$ and $s=q_1q_2$, then $$H_{U,V}(p)=0,  \qquad  H_{U,V}(s)=-\log s.  \label{eq:vaughan-hard-null-witness}$$ Indeed $\beta_V(q_i)=1$. In Vaughan’s identity the $d=1$ logarithmic packet contributes $+\log N$ to both inputs; the hard packet cancels it exactly for the semiprime and leaves it untouched for the prime.

Equations [\[eq:typeii-separates-null-pair\]](TPC-014.md#ref-a8daef388e93849f) and [\[eq:vaughan-hard-null-witness\]](TPC-014.md#ref-074c466be1c33c7b) certify that this balanced bilinear row contains information absent from the specified local signature. They provide no cancellation estimate for a long Type-II sum. That analytic estimate is the gate that remains.

# Why the positive theorems do not select a fixed prime pair

There are two different losses in the preceding results. The fixed-shift law retains the shell $k=1$ but aggregates its orientations. The full-mask law retains every orientation but averages the shift. Neither loss can be reversed by an algebraic change of coordinates alone.

## One shift average does not recover one shift coordinate

The following Hilbert-space calculation isolates the quantifier loss. Let $Q$ be a finite shift set, let $w\in\mathbb C^Q\setminus\{0\}$, and observe only $A_w(z)=\langle z,w\rangle=\sum_{q\in Q}z_q\overline{w_q}$.

###### ref-51001aeac50364a6

###### ref-2c5deebac762d839

> **Proposition: Exact coordinate-recovery minimax** \[prop:shift-coordinate-minimax\] For $q_\ast\in Q$ and $B>0$, $$\begin{aligned}  &\inf_{\Phi:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C}  \sup_{\|z\|_2\leq B}  |z_{q_\ast}-\Phi(A_w(z))|  \notag\\  &\qquad={}  B\left(  1-\frac{|w_{q_\ast}|^2}{\|w\|_2^2}  \right)^{1/2}.  \label{eq:shift-coordinate-minimax}\end{aligned}$$

> **Proof** As in [\[thm:local-minimax\]](TPC-014.md#ref-548936a39899fed2), the exact radius is $B$ times the norm of the target functional on $\ker A_w$. This is the distance from the coordinate vector $e_{q_\ast}$ to $\operatorname{span}\{w\}$, which is the right side of [\[eq:shift-coordinate-minimax\]](TPC-014.md#ref-2c5deebac762d839). The orthogonal projection also gives the optimal linear decoder, so allowing nonlinear decoders does not reduce the radius.

###### ref-e92f2ad269e06d19

###### ref-fb62710f76bf818c

For the smooth weights in this paper, $$w_q=H^{-1}G(q/H),  \qquad  \|w\|_2^2\asymp_G H^{-1}.  \label{eq:smooth-shift-vector}$$ If the selected coordinate lies in the support, then $|w_{q_\ast}|^2\ll_GH^{-2}$, and therefore the minimax radius is $$B\bigl(1-O_G(H^{-1})\bigr).  \label{eq:smooth-shift-minimax-limit}$$ If the coordinate lies outside the sampled window, then $w_{q_\ast}=0$ and the radius is exactly $B$. On an unrestricted response ball, a growing smooth mean retains asymptotically none of the stable information needed to recover one coordinate. Arithmetic prime-pair vectors are not arbitrary vectors, so this proposition is not an impossibility theorem for twin primes. It says precisely why the averaged theorem, by itself, cannot be inverted at $h=2$.

## Why the fixed shell does not isolate the endpoint

###### ref-8ac91dd2d8a7c3de

###### ref-ac5abcc01748d86f

At fixed $h$, the $k=1$ shell has the exact orientation decomposition $$\mathcal K_h(X)=  \sum_{\substack{a>1,\ b\geq1\\(a,b)=1}}  u(a)\eta(ab+h)W(ab/X).  \label{eq:fixed-shell-orientation-decomposition}$$ The proof of [\[thm:fixed-shell-law\]](TPC-014.md#ref-3be1ca9d6c3e19e5) first sums all orientations with $ab=r$, producing the unitary-divisor coefficient $\log\operatorname{rad}(r)-d^*(r)+1$. Biao Wang’s theorem estimates that aggregate. It supplies no estimate uniform in a dual orientation mask and therefore cannot be combined with the exact Fejér selector to retain only $b=1$. For that mask the target is exactly $$\sum_a(\Lambda(a)-1)(\Lambda(a+h)-1)W(a/X),  \label{eq:fixed-endpoint-boundary}$$ the unresolved fixed-shift covariance.

###### ref-1ffc4b292b7ef2e2

The Hardy–Littlewood prime-pair conjecture predicts the local main-term coefficient $$\sum_a(\Lambda(a)-1)(\Lambda(a+h)-1)W(a/X)  \sim\bigl(\mathfrak S(h)-1\bigr)XI_0(W),  \label{eq:expected-fixed-covariance}$$ where $\mathfrak S(h)$ is the prime-pair singular series [`\citep{HardyLittlewood1923}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-014.md#cite-f4ffa381ee727373). In particular, $\mathfrak S(2)=2C_2$, with $$C_2=\prod_{p>2}\left(1-\frac1{(p-1)^2}\right).$$ Thus the expected $h=2$ covariance is $(2C_2-1)XI_0(W)$, not zero.

This distinction is easy to lose: exact coordinate recovery is an identity for a finite coefficient array, while an arithmetic asymptotic is a norm estimate uniform over the selector needed for inversion. The former does not manufacture the latter.

## Sparse rays fall outside the natural-scale transfer

###### ref-2f432e4fa3ae885c

###### ref-e6f17bca8368e426

###### ref-e9056a777a9ead2c

The $\ell^2$ transfer is uniform over masks, but its absolute bound need not be sharp at the natural size of every sparse mask. Fix a primitive orientation $(a_0,b_0)$. Its product centers have the form $$r=a_0b_0k^2\asymp X,  \label{eq:quadratic-ray-centers}$$ so only $O_{a_0,b_0}(X^{1/2})$ centers occur. The source coefficient has $$\|c_{a_0,b_0}\|_2  \ll_{a_0,b_0,W}X^{1/4}\log X.  \label{eq:quadratic-ray-coefficient-norm}$$ Combining this with the global residual norm [\[eq:translated-residual-l2\]](TPC-014.md#ref-d65496ca8368b962) yields only $$\left|  \sum_{r=a_0b_0k^2}c_{a_0,b_0}(r)  \Delta_{X,H,h_0}(r)  \right|  \ll_A X^{3/4}(\log X)^{-A},  \label{eq:sparse-ray-global-l2-bound}$$ after renaming $A$. For every fixed $A$, this is not $o(X^{1/2})$. Thus the uniform theorem controls the full mask ball on the ambient $X$-scale, but it does not give natural-scale cancellation on an individual quadratic ray. Such a result would require a restriction estimate for the translated residual on sparse polynomial centers.

## The remaining fixed-shift gate

The results can now be placed in a strict logical order:

1.  the joint GCD–Mellin coordinate preserves every finite factor label exactly;

2.  the fixed $k=1$ aggregate is evaluable at every prescribed $h\ne0$;

3.  all shift-independent signed labels are controllable after a growing short-shift mean;

4.  local divisor labels below the square-root gate possess an exact prime/semiprime null direction;

5.  a balanced bilinear row detects that direction, but estimating the corresponding fixed-$h$ arithmetic packet remains open.

Accordingly, the next useful target is not another algebraic inversion. It is a fixed-shift asymptotic, or cancellation after subtracting the correct local main term, for a sufficiently localized Type-II packet with enough uniformity to preserve the endpoint label. Proving such an estimate would be genuine analytic progress. The present paper neither assumes nor claims it.

# Finite certificate and conclusion

The accompanying script `experiments/signed_label_certificate.py` is a deterministic finite certificate. It checks the following identities independently of the asymptotic proofs:

###### ref-8a83e233c3af64d0

1.  the unitary-divisor shell coefficient $\sum_{a\parallel r,a>1}(\Lambda(a)-1) =\log\operatorname{rad}(r)-2^{\omega(r)}+1$ on a finite range;

2.  the bijection between factor pairs $(n,m)$ and coordinates $(a,b,k)$, including endpoint and radial masks;

3.  Vaughan’s identity and the hard-packet values on the finite prime/semiprime witness;

4.  the identical divisor signatures of $$p=101,\qquad s=11\cdot13=143,\qquad R=10,   \label{eq:finite-null-witness}$$ together with their separation by a balanced factor row;

5.  the exact finite-fiber minimax radius and the Hilbert-space shift-coordinate radius for sample weights.

The script writes a JSON record with all parameters and residuals, and the unit test recomputes every assertion. The certificate does not test the Guth–Maynard or unitary-divisor Titchmarsh inputs.

The two quantifier regimes may be summarized without implication arrows:

|                     | All orientations $b$                                                                  | Endpoint orientation $b=1$                                                                                                                         |
|:--------------------|:--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|:---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Fixed $h$           | Fixed coprime-shell law in [\[thm:fixed-shell-law\]](TPC-014.md#ref-3be1ca9d6c3e19e5) | Unresolved fixed prime-pair covariance                                                                                                             |
| Growing mean in $h$ | Full bounded-mask transfer                                                            | Endpoint corollaries in \[[cor:endpoint-centered-mean](TPC-014.md#ref-fa5dd2b407f5fbb3),[cor:raw-endpoint-mean](TPC-014.md#ref-cb840b261d771e7a)\] |

The main conclusions are therefore separated cleanly by quantifier. At every fixed nonzero shift, one nontrivial exact radial shell has an explicit asymptotic; at $h=2$ its leading coefficient is $6/\pi^2-1/3$. Under a growing short-shift average of length $H\geq X^{2/15+\varepsilon}$, every bounded shift-independent label mask is controlled simultaneously, including the prime-pair endpoint and every masked target-recentered Vaughan packet. An exact minimax theorem then locates the information missing from local divisor signatures, and a balanced bilinear row detects the resulting prime/semiprime null direction.

What remains is analytic rather than representational: obtain a fixed-shift asymptotic, or cancellation after subtracting its correct local main term, for a sharply localized Type-II packet while retaining the endpoint label. The fixed-shell law, the full-mask short-shift theorem, and the null-space witness provide three independently checkable footholds for that next step. None is a fixed-shift prime-pair asymptotic or a breach of the parity barrier.
