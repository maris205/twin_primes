## Abstract

Fix $h\ne0$, let $W\in C_c^\infty((0,\infty);\mathbb R)$, and write $$\Lambda_R(n)=\sum_{q\le R}\frac{\mu(q)}{\varphi(q)}c_q(n),  \qquad r_R=\Lambda-\Lambda_R,  \qquad R=X^{1/2-\delta}.$$ TPC-16 isolated a three-scale fixed-shift Type-II packet with source scale $L$, quotient scale $K$, and divisor label $D$, and proved cancellation for one $D$-resolved packet near the $11/21$ factorable boundary. We replace that isolated slice by an assembled divisor prefix and then sharpen the spectral description of what remains.

The published core uses Maynard’s 2025 fixed-residue distribution theorem. For a dyadic source block, all divisor labels $d\le D_0$ are summed at once through $$\beta_{\le D_0}(k)=\sum_{\substack{d\mid k\\d\le D_0}}\mu(d).$$ Under the three Maynard monomial conditions, the corresponding residual packet has arbitrary logarithmic saving. In particular, the whole prefix $d\le X^{1/21-o(1)}$ is packaged directly near $L=X^{10/21-o(1)}$, yielding an exact tail normal form.

An explicitly segregated extension imports Theorem 1.1 of Li’s currently unrefereed arXiv:2602.20917v6, revised 27 May 2026. Its signed bilinear estimate deletes the third Maynard monomial condition. The resulting exponent polytope has vertex $$(\log_XL,\log_XD_0)=\left(\frac8{17},\frac1{17}\right),  \qquad \log_X(LD_0)=\frac9{17}.$$ Thus, within the TPC-16 interface and using that version-locked imported theorem, an entire prefix $d\le X^{1/17+o(1)}$ is removed near $L=X^{8/17-o(1)}$. The proof records why the target weight depends on the source factor but not on $d$; arbitrary pair-dependent masks are not covered. The published Maynard layer remains intact under any later revision of the preprint.

For the surviving dyadic block $$\mathcal H_{L,K}(\alpha,\beta)  =\sum_{\ell,k}c_{\ell,k}\mathrm e(\alpha\ell+\beta k),  \qquad LK\asymp X,$$ we prove the explicit energy bound $$\sum_{\ell,k}|c_{\ell,k}|^2\ll X(\log X)^3.$$ Exact Nyquist Parseval makes every natural-size exceptional phase a finite translation-spectral atom. A flat-top reproducing formula shows that failure at the distinguished zero phase forces quantitative energy inside a polylogarithmically enlarged uncertainty cell. Finally, $TT^*$ reduces such failure to a positive off-diagonal correlation of the residuals $r_R(\ell_1k+h)$ and $r_R(\ell_2k+h)$ of size $\gg XL/(\log X)^2$ at a dyadic witness scale. A sharp abstract countermodel shows that band limitation, total residual energy, and phase mean squares alone cannot exclude the zero mode.

No fixed-shift prime-pair asymptotic, twin-prime lower bound, new distribution theorem, or breach of sieve parity is claimed.

## The phase and dispersion layer

###### ref-c07b00d6e03daf85

###### ref-6e51369653118756

The square-root normal form of TPC-16 reduces the remaining total gate to dyadic blocks $$\mathcal H_{L,K}(\alpha,\beta)  =\sum_{\ell,k}c_{\ell,k}\mathrm e(\alpha\ell+\beta k),  \qquad LK\asymp_W X,  \label{eq:phase-block-intro}$$ whose arithmetic value is $\mathcal H_{L,K}(0,0)$. We first replace an $X^{1+\varepsilon}$ energy ledger by the explicit estimate $$E_{L,K}:=\sum_{\ell,k}|c_{\ell,k}|^2  \ll_{h,W,\delta}X(\log X)^3.  \label{eq:explicit-energy-intro}$$ Exact finite Fourier sampling then implies that, at natural height $\eta X$, at most $O_\eta((\log X)^3)$ Nyquist phases can be large. This does not exclude the constant character $(0,0)$.

###### ref-54c7e578023c4856

A flat-top reproducing kernel makes the obstruction local. If $|\mathcal H_{L,K}(0,0)|\ge\eta X$, then a phase cell of dimensions $$\frac{(\log X)^B}{L}\times\frac{(\log X)^B}{K}  \label{eq:planck-cell-intro}$$ around the origin carries energy $\gg_\eta X$, provided both radii are smaller than the circle scale. If one factor length is at most polylogarithmic, that coordinate is the whole circle and the statement is one-coordinate localization. Thus a large zero mode is not an isolated measure-zero accident; it is an uncertainty-scale concentration.

###### ref-1b233811d01271fe

Finally, $TT^*$ gives an arithmetic normal form. A large block forces a positive off-diagonal correlation $$\sum_{\ell_1\ne\ell_2}\Lambda(\ell_1)\Lambda(\ell_2)  \sum_k r_R(\ell_1k+h)r_R(\ell_2k+h)\,\mathcal W_{\ell_1,\ell_2,k}  \label{eq:ttstar-correlation-intro}$$ of size $\gg XL$ at natural block scale, or $\gg XL/(\log X)^2$ at the dyadic witness scale coming from a failed total estimate. This is weaker than demanding a small operator norm for the whole residual matrix and identifies the next specific arithmetic correlation.

For every modulus $m\ge1$, define the prime progression density and the finite-model complete-period mean by $$\rho_h(m)=\mathbf 1_{(m,h)=1}\frac{m}{\varphi(m)},  \qquad  \rho_{h,R}(m)=  \sum_{\substack{q\mid m\\q\le R}}  \frac{\mu(q)c_q(h)}{\varphi(q)}.  \label{eq:row-densities}$$ TPC-16 proves the exact drift identity $$\Delta_{h,R}(m)  :=\rho_h(m)-\rho_{h,R}(m)  =\sum_{\substack{q\mid m\\q>R}}  \frac{\mu(q)c_q(h)}{\varphi(q)}.  \label{eq:row-drift}$$ For a uniformly bounded compactly supported $C^1$ family $F$, its finite-row decomposition is $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_{j\ge1}r_R(mj+h)F(mj/X)  ={}&\Delta_{h,R}(m)\frac Xm I_0(F)      +\mathcal E_{m,h}(X;F)      +O_F(R(\log(2R))^2),  \label{eq:row-decomposition}\\  \mathcal E_{m,h}(X;F)  :={}&  \sum_{j\ge1}\Lambda(mj+h)F(mj/X)  -\rho_h(m)\frac Xm I_0(F).  \label{eq:prime-row-error}\end{aligned}$$ Thus the residual row has three distinct pieces: prime-progression error, explicit cutoff drift, and finite-model lattice leakage.

# Bilinear distribution inputs and a one-factor smooth transfer

###### ref-edf619f4db758be8

Write $$E_\pi(t;q,h)=\pi(t;q,h)-\frac{\pi(t)}{\varphi(q)}  \qquad ((q,h)=1).  \label{eq:pi-error}$$ The two imported theorems used below have different logical strengths. Maynard controls the absolute double sum in a three-inequality region; Li controls a signed bilinear sum in a larger two-inequality region. We first formulate the common consequence actually needed by the assembled packet.

###### ref-85b6c1527cfbd151

> **Definition: Signed bilinear distribution profile** Fix $\eta>0$. A set $\mathfrak R_\eta$ of pairs $(L,D)$ has property $\mathrm{BD}_h$ if, for every fixed $A,C>0$, every pair of sequences $a_\ell,b_d$ satisfying $$|a_\ell|\le(\log X)^C,  \qquad |b_d|\le(\log X)^C,$$ and every $t\asymp X$, one has $$\sum_{\substack{\ell\le2L,\ d\le D\\(\ell d,h)=1}}  a_\ell b_d E_\pi(t;\ell d,h)  \ll_{A,C,h,\eta}t(\log t)^{-A}  \label{eq:bd-profile}$$ uniformly for $(L,D)\in\mathfrak R_\eta$. The profile is understood with a fixed power margin, so replacing $X$ by $t\asymp X$ is harmless.

The log-bounded formulation is deliberate. It is the common consequence of the two imported inputs needed by this paper. Li’s signed theorem permits the stronger class of divisor-bounded coefficients, whereas Maynard’s absolute unweighted estimate does not by itself permit arbitrary pointwise divisor weights.

## The published and version-locked profiles

###### ref-0406c94bbfe29acb

Maynard’s theorem [`\citep[Theorem~1.1]{Maynard2025I}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-017.md#cite-158e56c67e354b21) implies $\mathrm{BD}_h$ on $$\mathfrak R^{\mathrm M}_\eta  =\left\{(L,D):  DL^2\le X^{1-\eta},\quad  D^{12}L^7\le X^{4-\eta},\quad  D^{20}L^{19}\le X^{10-\eta}  \right\}.  \label{eq:maynard-profile}$$ Here the orientation in Maynard’s notation is $(Q_1,Q_2)=(D,2L)$; its three conditions are asymmetric. For Li the orientation is instead $(Q_1,Q_2)=(2L,D)$. Absorbing fixed factors into the power margin gives $\mathrm{BD}_h$ on $$\mathfrak R^{\mathrm L}_\eta  =\left\{(L,D):  DL^2\le X^{1-\eta},\quad  D^{12}L^7\le X^{4-\eta}  \right\}.  \label{eq:li-profile}$$ The superscript $\mathrm L$ always denotes the version-locked Li input, not a theorem proved in this paper.

> **Remark: Why other large-modulus theorems are not inserted** Maynard’s $3/5$ well-factorable theorem [`\citep{Maynard2025II}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-017.md#cite-4e699b9f634601fc) applies to collapsed modulus weights possessing triple well-factorability. The specific coefficient $\Lambda(\ell)\mu(d)$ with an independently resolved source factor has no such property in general. Replacing it by a factorable sieve weight would change the exact Vaughan packet. Likewise, splitting a general $d$ into several independent factors fails for a divisor label that is itself a large prime. We therefore do not promote a well-factorable or trilinear theorem to the present packet.

## Smooth von Mangoldt transfer

The signed nature of [\[eq:li-v6-theorem\]](TPC-017.md#ref-a2d91828928b36c2) requires the test weight to preserve separated coefficients. The following lemma records the exact allowable dependence.

###### ref-f95be70bffd9db08

###### ref-c63fc44f9e87f1db

> **Lemma: One-factor-dependent smooth transfer** \[lem:one-factor-smooth-transfer\] Let $(L,D)$ belong to a $\mathrm{BD}_h$ profile. For every $\ell\le2L$, let $F_{\ell,X}\in C_c^1((0,\infty))$ belong to a family with a common compact support and a common $C^1$ bound. Let $|a_\ell|,|b_d|\le(\log X)^C$. Then $$\begin{aligned}  &\sum_{\substack{\ell\le2L,\ d\le D\\(\ell d,h)=1}}  a_\ell b_d  \left[  \sum_{j\ge1}\Lambda(\ell dj+h)  F_{\ell,X}(\ell dj/X)  -\frac{X}{\varphi(\ell d)}I_0(F_{\ell,X})  \right]  \notag\\  &\hspace{8em}\ll_{A,C,h,\eta,\mathcal F}X(\log X)^{-A}  \label{eq:smooth-lambda-transfer}\end{aligned}$$ for every fixed $A>0$, where $\mathcal F$ denotes the common support and $C^1$ bounds of the weight family.

###### ref-48037f01f87b18aa

###### ref-4ccb2f4aa490f9a0

###### ref-1ababa0e82096aef

###### ref-e633c6e158a136fd

###### ref-0f870c9c8d9db428

###### ref-3804cfb53f30eb38

###### ref-5e2beb97fdd57b10

> **Proof** We give the transfer in detail because the one-factor restriction is essential. First retain only target primes. Put $$\omega_{\ell,X}(t)  = (\log t)F_{\ell,X}((t-h)/X).  \label{eq:stieltjes-weight}$$ The support lies in one fixed interval $t\asymp X$, and $$|\omega_{\ell,X}'(t)|\ll\frac{\log X}{X}  \label{eq:weight-derivative}$$ uniformly in $\ell$. Stieltjes partial summation makes the difference between the prime progression sum and its global-prime counterpart equal to $$-\int  E_\pi(t;\ell d,h)\omega_{\ell,X}'(t)\,dt.  \label{eq:stieltjes-error}$$ There are no endpoint terms after extending the compactly supported weight by zero.
>
> After summing [\[eq:stieltjes-error\]](TPC-017.md#ref-1ababa0e82096aef) over $\ell,d$, fix $t$ inside the integral. The coefficient $$a_{\ell,t}=a_\ell\omega_{\ell,X}'(t)  \label{eq:t-dependent-source-coefficient}$$ still depends only on $\ell$, while $b_d$ depends only on $d$. Write $$a_{\ell,t}=\frac{\log X}{X}\widetilde a_{\ell,t},  \qquad |\widetilde a_{\ell,t}|\le(\log X)^{C'}$$ using [\[eq:weight-derivative\]](TPC-017.md#ref-4ccb2f4aa490f9a0). The $\mathrm{BD}_h$ estimate applies pointwise to $\widetilde a_{\ell,t}$ and $b_d$, uniformly in $t$. Its $t\asymp X$ scale is multiplied by $(\log X)/X$; integrating over an interval of length $O(X)$ restores the final $X$ scale and loses only one fixed logarithmic power. Increasing the requested saving absorbs that loss.
>
> The global prime sum satisfies, uniformly over the same family, $$\sum_p(\log p)F_{\ell,X}((p-h)/X)  =XI_0(F_{\ell,X})+O(X\exp(-c\sqrt{\log X})).  \label{eq:global-pnt-family}$$ The accumulated error is negligible because standard divisor estimates give $$\sum_{\ell\le2L,\ d\le D}  \frac{|a_\ell b_d|}{\varphi(\ell d)}  \ll_C(\log X)^{C'}.  \label{eq:inverse-totient-weight}$$
>
> It remains to restore target prime powers. If $p^\nu\asymp X$, $\nu\ge2$, and $p^\nu\equiv h\pmod{\ell d}$, then $\ell d\mid p^\nu-h$. Hence the total number of ordered pairs is bounded by a fixed divisor function, and $$\sum_{\substack{p^\nu\asymp X\\\nu\ge2}}  (\log p)\tau_3(|p^\nu-h|)^{C'}  \ll_{C',\varepsilon}X^{1/2+\varepsilon}.  \label{eq:target-prime-powers}$$ This is absorbed by any prescribed logarithmic saving. Combining the prime, global-main-term, and prime-power estimates proves [\[eq:smooth-lambda-transfer\]](TPC-017.md#ref-c63fc44f9e87f1db).

###### ref-62561a27e020b4de

> **Remark: Forbidden pair dependence** The proof does not allow an arbitrary family $F_{\ell,d,X}$. In the signed Li layer, the Stieltjes derivative of such a family would create a genuinely pair-dependent coefficient, outside [\[eq:li-v6-theorem\]](TPC-017.md#ref-a2d91828928b36c2). The packet used below is admissible because its weight has the exact form $$F_{\ell,X}(u)  =W(u)\psi\!\left(\frac{Xu}{\ell K}\right),  \qquad  F_{\ell,X}(\ell dj/X)  =W(\ell dj/X)\psi(dj/K),  \label{eq:one-factor-weight-identity}$$ which is independent of $d$ once $\ell$ is fixed. The product ledger $LK\asymp_WX$ supplies a common compact support and common $C^1$ bound.

# The assembled-prefix theorem

We now pass from one dyadic divisor label to a complete initial segment. We give a direct sharp-prefix proof: the coefficient $\mathbf 1_{d\le D_0}$ is inserted into the bilinear distribution theorem itself. This packages the cumulative statement without endpoint loss or dependence on a choice of dyadic partition.

###### ref-7a0c143ef5baf932

###### ref-2408555c729c9c63

###### ref-615816000e06386d

###### ref-72da0de90ef540cf

> **Theorem: Cumulative divisor-prefix cancellation** \[thm:assembled-prefix\] Fix $h\ne0$, $0<\delta<1/2$, and $\eta>0$. Let $L,K,D_0$ satisfy $$\begin{aligned}  &LK\asymp_W X,  \qquad L>2R,  \qquad 1\le D_0<R,  \qquad D_0\le V,  \qquad K>2V,  \label{eq:assembly-geometry}\\  &(L,D_0)\in\mathfrak R_\eta,  \qquad LD_0R\le X^{1-\eta},  \label{eq:assembly-analysis}\end{aligned}$$ where $\mathfrak R_\eta$ is any signed bilinear distribution profile with property $\mathrm{BD}_h$. Then, for every $A>0$, $$\mathcal C_{L,K}(D_0)  \ll_{A,h,W,\psi,\delta,\eta}X(\log X)^{-A}.  \label{eq:assembled-prefix-bound}$$

###### ref-5bddab97fff94b99

###### ref-49c81a2c2bcea7d4

###### ref-f6e0d18f1d200c4a

###### ref-d73564ffa18599d9

###### ref-04561310a109fda5

###### ref-14d2d1be94c3158e

###### ref-105f1769630ed294

> **Proof** Start from the opened expression [\[eq:opened-prefix-block\]](TPC-017.md#ref-1755ebf038858100). We first remove source prime powers $\ell=p^a$, $a\ge2$. On the support, $j\asymp_W K/d$, and [\[eq:pointwise-residual\]](TPC-017.md#ref-58d2b1a6b49bdbd6) gives, for every fixed $\varepsilon>0$, $$\begin{aligned}  &\sum_{\substack{p^a\asymp L\\a\ge2}}  \Lambda(p^a)  \sum_{d\le D_0}\sum_{j\asymp K/d}  |r_R(p^adj+h)|  \notag\\  &\hspace{6em}\ll_{W,\varepsilon}  L^{1/2+\varepsilon}K(\log(2D_0))X^\varepsilon  \ll_{W,\varepsilon}X^{1+2\varepsilon}L^{-1/2}\log X.  \label{eq:assembled-source-powers}\end{aligned}$$ Put $\rho_0=1/2-\delta>0$. Since $L>2R=X^{\rho_0+o(1)}$, choosing $\varepsilon<\rho_0/8$ makes the last quantity $O(X^{1-\rho_0/4})$. Thus it has a fixed power saving. We may therefore restrict $\ell$ to primes. In particular, $\ell>R>|h|$ for all sufficiently large $X$.
>
> For fixed $\ell,d$, apply the row decomposition [\[eq:row-decomposition\]](TPC-017.md#ref-2a81f80881d020ca) with $$F_{\ell,X}(u)  =W(u)\psi\!\left(\frac{Xu}{\ell K}\right).  \label{eq:assembly-row-weight}$$ The family is uniformly supported and uniformly $C^1$ by $LK\asymp_WX$, and $$F_{\ell,X}(\ell dj/X)  =W(\ell dj/X)\psi(dj/K).$$
>
> On the reduced rows $(\ell d,h)=1$, the aggregate prime-progression error is $O_A(X(\log X)^{-A})$ by [\[lem:one-factor-smooth-transfer\]](TPC-017.md#ref-f95be70bffd9db08), with $$a_\ell=\Lambda(\ell)\psi(\ell/L)\mathbf 1_{\ell\,\mathrm{prime}},  \qquad  b_d=\mu(d)\mathbf 1_{d\le D_0}.  \label{eq:assembly-separated-coefficients}$$ The logarithm in $a_\ell$ is absorbed by requesting one additional logarithmic power.
>
> It remains to check the nonreduced rows. Since $\ell>|h|$ is prime, $(\ell d,h)>1$ implies $(d,h)>1$. If $p\mid(d,h)$, every integer $\ell dj+h$ is divisible by $p$; a nonzero von Mangoldt value therefore forces $$\ell dj+h=p^\nu.  \label{eq:nonreduced-prime-power-equation}$$ For each of the finitely many $p\mid h$, only $O_{h,W}(1)$ exponents occur at scale $X$, while the number of ordered factorizations $\ell d\mid p^\nu-h$ is $O_{h,\varepsilon}(X^\varepsilon)$. Hence the total prime part of the nonreduced rows is $O_{h,W,\varepsilon}(X^\varepsilon)$. Their continuous prime mean and complete finite-model mean both vanish. Indeed, $\mu(d)\ne0$ makes $d$ squarefree, every divisor of $d$ lies below $R$, and the local factor at $p\mid(d,h)$ is $1-1=0$.
>
> The finite-row lattice leakage in [\[eq:row-decomposition\]](TPC-017.md#ref-2a81f80881d020ca) is $O(R(\log(2R))^2)$ per ordered pair $(\ell,d)$. Summing the outer weights gives $$\ll LD_0R(\log X)^3  \ll X^{1-\eta}(\log X)^3.  \label{eq:assembled-leakage}$$
>
> Finally consider the cutoff drift. For prime $\ell>R$, squarefree $d<R$, and $\ell\nmid d$, all divisors of $\ell d$ below $R$ divide $d$. Consequently $$\Delta_{h,R}(\ell d)=  \begin{cases}  \displaystyle\frac{d}{\varphi(d)(\ell-1)},&(d,h)=1,\\[0.7em]  0,&(d,h)>1.  \end{cases}  \label{eq:assembled-drift-formula}$$ After insertion into the continuous term of [\[eq:row-decomposition\]](TPC-017.md#ref-2a81f80881d020ca), its total absolute value is $$\begin{aligned}  &\ll_W X  \sum_{\ell\asymp L}  \frac{\Lambda(\ell)}{\ell(\ell-1)}  \sum_{d\le D_0}\frac{\mu(d)^2}{\varphi(d)}  \notag\\  &\hspace{6em}\ll_W \frac XL(\log(2D_0))^2.  \label{eq:assembled-drift-total}\end{aligned}$$ This again has a fixed power saving because $L>2R$. The source powers, reduced prime errors, nonreduced rows, leakage, and drift are therefore all $O_A(X(\log X)^{-A})$, after shrinking fixed power margins if necessary.

The conclusion has an exact and useful tail formulation.

###### ref-af38d5d1057e6cff

> **Corollary: Exact tail normal form** \[cor:tail-normal-form\] Under the hypotheses of [\[thm:assembled-prefix\]](TPC-017.md#ref-7a0c143ef5baf932), $$\mathcal H^{\mathrm{HB}}_{L,K}  =\mathcal T_{L,K}(D_0,V)  +O_{A,h,W,\psi,\delta,\eta}    \bigl(X(\log X)^{-A}\bigr)  \label{eq:tail-normal-form}$$ for every $A>0$.

> **Proof** Combine the exact coefficient split [\[eq:exact-prefix-tail-split\]](TPC-017.md#ref-f3e3548dc5d699b0) with [\[eq:assembled-prefix-bound\]](TPC-017.md#ref-72da0de90ef540cf).

> **Remark: What the normal form does and does not do** The tail coefficient $$\beta_{(D_0,V]}(k)  =\sum_{\substack{d\mid k\\D_0<d\le V}}\mu(d)$$ is signed. Equation [\[eq:tail-normal-form\]](TPC-017.md#ref-af38d5d1057e6cff) compresses the unknown part to a shorter divisor interval, but supplies neither positivity nor cancellation inside that interval. In particular, it is not a prime-pair lower bound and does not evade the parity obstruction.

> **Theorem: Explicit block-energy ledger** \[thm:explicit-block-energy\] For every relevant pair $(L,K)$, $$E_{L,K}\ll_{h,W,\psi,\delta}X(\log X)^3.  \label{eq:explicit-block-energy}$$ Moreover, $$\begin{aligned}  \int_\mathbb T|\mathcal H_{L,K}(\alpha,0)|^2\,d\alpha  &\ll K E_{L,K}  \ll \frac{X^2(\log X)^3}{L},  \label{eq:alpha-phase-energy}\\  \int_\mathbb T|\mathcal H_{L,K}(0,\beta)|^2\,d\beta  &\ll L E_{L,K}  \ll \frac{X^2(\log X)^3}{K},  \label{eq:beta-phase-energy}\\  \int_{\mathbb T^2}|\mathcal H_{L,K}(\alpha,\beta)|^2  \,d\alpha\,d\beta  &=E_{L,K}.  \label{eq:two-phase-energy}\end{aligned}$$

###### ref-4fde5962b42fbfed

###### ref-7d9e05a76ce7dc1c

> **Proof** Group [\[eq:block-energy\]](TPC-017.md#ref-2f8e6d21bae25fc5) by $n=\ell k$. Since a divisor $d$ of $n$ has $\Lambda(d)\ne0$ only when $d=p^a$, one has $$\sum_{d\mid n}\Lambda(d)^2  =\sum_{p^a\parallel n}a(\log p)^2  \le(\log n)^2.  \label{eq:divisor-lambda-square}$$ It follows that $$\begin{aligned}  E_{L,K}  &\ll_\psi  \sum_n r_R(n+h)^2|W(n/X)|^2  \sum_{\ell\mid n}\Lambda(\ell)^2  \notag\\  &\ll X(\log X)^3  \label{eq:block-energy-proof}\end{aligned}$$ by [\[eq:shifted-residual-energy\]](TPC-017.md#ref-cd2313e558fed078).
>
> For the first one-phase identity, Parseval in $\ell$ and Cauchy–Schwarz in the $O(K)$ possible values of $k$ give $$\int_\mathbb T|\mathcal H(\alpha,0)|^2d\alpha  =\sum_\ell\left|\sum_kc_{\ell k}\right|^2  \ll K\sum_{\ell,k}|c_{\ell k}|^2.$$ Interchanging the roles of $\ell,k$ proves [\[eq:beta-phase-energy\]](TPC-017.md#ref-c80b3452ad34208d); two-dimensional Parseval proves [\[eq:two-phase-energy\]](TPC-017.md#ref-b7c473d47acfeb83). Finally use $LK\asymp_WX$.

The continuum identity has an exact finite counterpart. Choose integers $$N_L>4L,  \qquad N_K>4K,  \qquad N_L\asymp L,  \qquad N_K\asymp K.  \label{eq:nyquist-grid-sizes}$$

###### ref-8678a7497cddb985

###### ref-8d59546a6eb868bf

> **Theorem: Exact Nyquist Parseval and exceptional-phase count** \[thm:nyquist-parseval\] With [\[eq:nyquist-grid-sizes\]](TPC-017.md#ref-602ad883e783760e), $$\frac1{N_LN_K}  \sum_{a=0}^{N_L-1}\sum_{b=0}^{N_K-1}  \left|\mathcal H_{L,K}\!\left(\frac a{N_L},\frac b{N_K}\right)\right|^2  =E_{L,K}.  \label{eq:exact-nyquist-parseval}$$ Consequently, for every $A_0>0$, $$\#\left\{(a,b):  \left|\mathcal H_{L,K}\!\left(\frac a{N_L},\frac b{N_K}\right)\right|  \ge A_0\right\}  \le\frac{N_LN_KE_{L,K}}{A_0^2}.  \label{eq:exceptional-phase-count}$$ In particular, there are at most $$O_\eta((\log X)^3)  \quad\hbox{phases of height at least $\eta X$},  \label{eq:natural-phase-count}$$ and at most $O_\eta((\log X)^5)$ phases of height at least $\eta X/\log X$.

> **Proof** Discrete character orthogonality gives $$\frac1{N_L}\sum_{a=0}^{N_L-1}  \mathrm e(a(\ell_1-\ell_2)/N_L)=\mathbf 1_{\ell_1=\ell_2}$$ on the frequency support, because $|\ell_1-\ell_2|<N_L$; the analogous identity holds in $k$. This proves [\[eq:exact-nyquist-parseval\]](TPC-017.md#ref-8678a7497cddb985). Markov’s inequality gives [\[eq:exceptional-phase-count\]](TPC-017.md#ref-8d59546a6eb868bf). Now use $N_LN_K\asymp LK\asymp_WX$ and [\[eq:explicit-block-energy\]](TPC-017.md#ref-9b5041da0036f830).

###### ref-59159aa88760c6db

###### ref-974163a816159ef1

This sampling identity has a literal finite spectral interpretation. Embed $(c_{\ell k})$ into $\ell^2(\mathbb Z/N_L\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z/N_K\mathbb Z)$ and set $$\Phi_{a,b}(\ell,k)  =\frac1{\sqrt{N_LN_K}}  \mathrm e\!\left(\frac{a\ell}{N_L}+\frac{bk}{N_K}\right).  \label{eq:finite-translation-characters}$$ These are the joint eigencharacters of the two coordinate translations. With the convention $\langle f,g\rangle=\sum f\overline g$, one has $$|\langle c,\Phi_{a,b}\rangle|^2  =\frac1{N_LN_K}  \left|\mathcal H_{L,K}\!\left(-\frac a{N_L},-\frac b{N_K}\right)\right|^2.  \label{eq:phase-as-spectral-coefficient}$$ Negating $(a,b)$ merely permutes the Nyquist grid. Removing a spectral projection of height at least $A_0$ therefore decreases Frobenius energy by at least $A_0^2/(N_LN_K)$. At natural height only $O((\log X)^3)$ such increments are possible.

> **Remark: No distinguished-phase conclusion from Parseval** The arithmetic point $(\alpha,\beta)=(0,0)$ is the constant translation character. Equations [\[eq:exact-nyquist-parseval\]](TPC-017.md#ref-8678a7497cddb985) and [\[eq:exceptional-phase-count\]](TPC-017.md#ref-8d59546a6eb868bf) prove that large characters are sparse, but do not say that the constant one is absent. No zeta-zero or prime “eigenvalue” is involved in this finite spectrum.

# Uncertainty-cell concentration and a $TT^*$ normal form

The preceding energy theorem makes large phases sparse. We next show that a large zero phase must occupy energy at the reciprocal resolution of the two factor coordinates.

###### ref-27cd7a97a438507f

###### ref-4b8d7d684c007759

###### ref-3270c8986c588064

Choose $\vartheta\in C_c^\infty((0,\infty))$ equal to $1$ on $\operatorname{supp}\psi$, and define the periodic flat-top kernels $$V_L(\alpha)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}\vartheta(n/L)\mathrm e(n\alpha),  \qquad  V_K(\beta)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}\vartheta(n/K)\mathrm e(n\beta).  \label{eq:flat-top-kernels}$$ Poisson summation gives, for every $B_0>0$, $$|V_L(\alpha)|\ll_{B_0,\vartheta}  L(1+L\|\alpha\|)^{-B_0},  \label{eq:flat-top-decay}$$ and similarly for $V_K$. For $T\ge2$, put $$\Omega_T=\left\{(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb T^2:  \|\alpha\|\le T/L,\quad \|\beta\|\le T/K\right\}.  \label{eq:uncertainty-cell}$$ If one of the displayed radii exceeds $1/2$, the corresponding condition is interpreted as the whole circle.

###### ref-12b3de689a2134df

> **Theorem: Flat-top localization** \[thm:flat-top-localization\] For every $B_0>0$, $$|\mathcal H_{L,K}(0,0)|  \le C_{B_0}\sqrt{LK}  \left[  \left(\int_{\Omega_T}|\mathcal H_{L,K}(\alpha,\beta)|^2  \,d\alpha\,d\beta\right)^{1/2}  +T^{-B_0}E_{L,K}^{1/2}  \right].  \label{eq:flat-top-localization}$$ Consequently, for each fixed $\eta>0$, one may choose a fixed $B>0$ such that, with $T=(\log X)^B$, $$\begin{aligned}  |\mathcal H_{L,K}(0,0)|\ge\eta X  &\quad\Longrightarrow\quad  \int_{\Omega_T}|\mathcal H_{L,K}|^2  \gg_\eta X,  \label{eq:natural-planck-concentration}\\  |\mathcal H_{L,K}(0,0)|\ge\frac{\eta X}{\log X}  &\quad\Longrightarrow\quad  \int_{\Omega_T}|\mathcal H_{L,K}|^2  \gg_\eta\frac{X}{(\log X)^2}.  \label{eq:witness-planck-concentration}\end{aligned}$$

###### ref-243420575c40ce9e

> **Proof** Fourier reproduction gives the exact identity $$\mathcal H_{L,K}(0,0)  =\int_{\mathbb T^2}\mathcal H_{L,K}(\alpha,\beta)  V_L(-\alpha)V_K(-\beta)\,d\alpha\,d\beta.  \label{eq:flat-top-reproduction}$$ Indeed, integration selects the frequencies $n=\ell$ and $m=k$, on which both flat-top multipliers equal $1$.
>
> On $\Omega_T$, Cauchy–Schwarz and Parseval for the kernels give $$\left|\int_{\Omega_T}\mathcal HV_LV_K\right|  \ll\sqrt{LK}  \left(\int_{\Omega_T}|\mathcal H|^2\right)^{1/2}.$$ On the complement, apply Cauchy–Schwarz again, use [\[eq:two-phase-energy\]](TPC-017.md#ref-b7c473d47acfeb83), and integrate the rapid decay [\[eq:flat-top-decay\]](TPC-017.md#ref-4b8d7d684c007759). Taking the decay exponent larger than the requested $B_0$ yields $$\left|\int_{\mathbb T^2\setminus\Omega_T}\mathcal HV_LV_K\right|  \ll_{B_0}\sqrt{LK}\,T^{-B_0}E_{L,K}^{1/2}.$$ This proves [\[eq:flat-top-localization\]](TPC-017.md#ref-12b3de689a2134df).
>
> Now $LK\asymp_WX$ and $E_{L,K}^{1/2}\ll\sqrt X(\log X)^{3/2}$. Choose $B$ and then $B_0$ so that the tail in [\[eq:flat-top-localization\]](TPC-017.md#ref-12b3de689a2134df) is smaller than one half of the relevant threshold. Rearranging proves [\[eq:natural-planck-concentration\]](TPC-017.md#ref-56983dcc36ff7b79) and [\[eq:witness-planck-concentration\]](TPC-017.md#ref-cd860604e171872a).

###### ref-720afb6a230b6c16

The same obstruction has a more arithmetic formulation. Put $$S_k=\sum_\ell c_{\ell k}  \label{eq:row-compressed-coefficient}$$ and define $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal C^{\mathrm{off}}_{L,K}  :={}&\sum_{\ell_1\ne\ell_2}  \Lambda(\ell_1)\Lambda(\ell_2)  \psi(\ell_1/L)\psi(\ell_2/L)  \notag\\[-0.2em]  &\quad\times\sum_k  r_R(\ell_1k+h)r_R(\ell_2k+h)  W(\ell_1k/X)W(\ell_2k/X)  \notag\\[-0.2em]  &\hspace{12em}\times\psi(k/K)^2  \mathbf 1_{\ell_1,\ell_2>M}\mathbf 1_{k>1}.  \label{eq:offdiagonal-correlation}\end{aligned}$$ All quantities are real because the weights are real.

> **Theorem: $TT^*$ residual-pair reduction** \[thm:ttstar-reduction\] One has the exact identity $$\sum_k|S_k|^2=E_{L,K}+\mathcal C^{\mathrm{off}}_{L,K}.  \label{eq:ttstar-identity}$$ In particular, $$\mathcal C^{\mathrm{off}}_{L,K}=o(XL)  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad  \mathcal H_{L,K}(0,0)=o(X).  \label{eq:ttstar-sufficient-condition}$$ Conversely, for every fixed $\eta>0$, $$\begin{aligned}  |\mathcal H_{L,K}(0,0)|\ge\eta X  &\quad\Longrightarrow\quad  \mathcal C^{\mathrm{off}}_{L,K}  \ge(c_W\eta^2+o(1))XL,  \label{eq:ttstar-natural-obstruction}\\  |\mathcal H_{L,K}(0,0)|\ge\frac{\eta X}{\log X}  &\quad\Longrightarrow\quad  \mathcal C^{\mathrm{off}}_{L,K}  \gg_{W,\eta}\frac{XL}{(\log X)^2}.  \label{eq:ttstar-witness-obstruction}\end{aligned}$$

###### ref-df21b55b119e36d3

###### ref-d21a9d901fcd4835

> **Proof** Expanding $\sum_k|S_k|^2$ separates the diagonal $\ell_1=\ell_2$, which is exactly $E_{L,K}$, from [\[eq:offdiagonal-correlation\]](TPC-017.md#ref-720afb6a230b6c16). This proves [\[eq:ttstar-identity\]](TPC-017.md#ref-7720c64d44008c1d). Since only $O(K)$ values of $k$ occur, $$|\mathcal H_{L,K}(0,0)|^2  =\left|\sum_kS_k\right|^2  \ll K\bigl(E_{L,K}+\mathcal C^{\mathrm{off}}_{L,K}\bigr).  \label{eq:ttstar-cauchy}$$ Now $K\asymp_WX/L$ and $$E_{L,K}\ll X(\log X)^3=o(XL),  \label{eq:diagonal-negligible-for-ttstar}$$ because every nonzero square-root block has $L\gg M=X^{1/2-o(1)}$. The sufficient condition and both converse estimates follow from [\[eq:ttstar-cauchy\]](TPC-017.md#ref-df21b55b119e36d3).

> **Corollary: A failed total gate produces a two-form witness** \[cor:failed-gate-witness\] Suppose that along a sequence of $X$, $$|\mathcal H^\sharp_{h,R;M}(X)|\ge\eta X  \label{eq:failed-total-gate}$$ for some fixed $\eta>0$. Then for one of the $O_W(\log X)$ relevant dyadic pairs $(L,K)$, $$|\mathcal H_{L,K}(0,0)|\gg_{W,\eta}\frac X{\log X}  \label{eq:dyadic-zero-witness}$$ and $$\mathcal C^{\mathrm{off}}_{L,K}  \gg_{W,\eta}\frac{XL}{(\log X)^2}.  \label{eq:dyadic-ttstar-witness}$$ After a further dyadic decomposition of $|\ell_1-\ell_2|$, one separation scale $\Delta$ carries a positive contribution $$\gg_{W,\eta}\frac{XL}{(\log X)^3}.  \label{eq:separation-scale-witness}$$ The witness may be restricted to distinct prime $\ell_1,\ell_2\asymp L$: if $\mathcal C^{\mathrm{off},\mathbb P}_{L,K}$ denotes [\[eq:offdiagonal-correlation\]](TPC-017.md#ref-720afb6a230b6c16) with both source variables restricted to primes, then $$\mathcal C^{\mathrm{off},\mathbb P}_{L,K}  \gg_{W,\eta}\frac{XL}{(\log X)^2},  \label{eq:prime-restricted-ttstar-witness}$$ and one prime separation scale contributes $\gg_{W,\eta}XL/(\log X)^3$. The determinant of its two affine forms is $$\det\begin{pmatrix}\ell_1&h\\\ell_2&h\end{pmatrix}  =h(\ell_1-\ell_2).  \label{eq:two-form-determinant}$$

###### ref-e6a59551908059fa

> **Proof** Insert the dyadic partition in the two factor coordinates. The product constraint leaves $O_W(\log X)$ nonzero pairs, so the triangle inequality gives [\[eq:dyadic-zero-witness\]](TPC-017.md#ref-f3e9b2261cf5e8d1); then apply [\[eq:ttstar-witness-obstruction\]](TPC-017.md#ref-e1f7f06b5b485d11). >

The total zero-phase contribution of source prime powers $\ell=p^a$, $a\ge2$, is $$\ll_{W,\varepsilon}X L^{-1/2+\varepsilon}  =o(X/\log X),  \label{eq:source-powers-below-witness}$$ by [\[eq:pointwise-residual\]](TPC-017.md#ref-58d2b1a6b49bdbd6) and $L\gg M$. Removing it before the $TT^*$ expansion gives $$|\mathcal H^{\mathbb P}_{L,K}(0,0)|  \gg_{W,\eta}X/\log X.$$ Now repeat the proof of [\[thm:ttstar-reduction\]](TPC-017.md#ref-2688a1aa65b1504a) for $c^{\mathbb P}$; its diagonal energy is at most $E_{L,K}$. This proves [\[eq:prime-restricted-ttstar-witness\]](TPC-017.md#ref-18427131b5d64d9d). Decomposing this prime-restricted correlation by $|\ell_1-\ell_2|$ proves the stated prime separation-scale bound. Formula [\[eq:two-form-determinant\]](TPC-017.md#ref-fb21ba50b1c034d4) is immediate.

> **Remark: The next arithmetic gate** The target in [\[eq:offdiagonal-correlation\]](TPC-017.md#ref-720afb6a230b6c16) is weaker than a small operator norm for the whole residual matrix: only the source-prime direction and its $TT^*$ self-correlation matter. A future theorem showing $o(XL)$ for this averaged two-form correlation would close the corresponding zero-phase block. No such estimate is proved here.

# Random masks and a sharp information boundary

The zero-phase obstruction is deterministic. Independent signs erase it uniformly over all phases at a much smaller scale, which separates a generic entropy statement from the arithmetic alignment that remains.

###### ref-4d98988eafcc65a1

###### ref-23ee16c82deee373

###### ref-3aa1ffa448b6465e

###### ref-cf1bae388613c794

> **Theorem: Uniform random-entry cancellation** \[thm:random-entry-cancellation\] Let $(\varepsilon_{\ell k})$ be independent Rademacher variables and put $$\mathcal H^\varepsilon_{L,K}(\alpha,\beta)  =\sum_{\ell,k}\varepsilon_{\ell k}c_{\ell k}  \mathrm e(\alpha\ell+\beta k).  \label{eq:random-entry-polynomial}$$ There is an absolute $C>0$ such that, for $u\ge0$, $$\mathbb P\left(  \sup_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb T}|\mathcal H^\varepsilon_{L,K}(\alpha,\beta)|  >C\sqrt{E_{L,K}(\log(2LK)+u)}  \right)\le 2e^{-u}.  \label{eq:random-entry-tail}$$ In particular, with probability $1-O(X^{-10})$, $$\sup_{\alpha,\beta}|\mathcal H^\varepsilon_{L,K}(\alpha,\beta)|  \ll\sqrt X(\log X)^2=o(X).  \label{eq:random-entry-uniform-bound}$$

###### ref-c1a0f3c3dc6a11ee

> **Proof** For each fixed phase, the Rademacher exponential-moment inequality gives $$\mathbb P(|\mathcal H^\varepsilon(\alpha,\beta)|>t)  \le2\exp(-c t^2/E_{L,K}).  \label{eq:fixed-phase-subgaussian}$$ A rectangular grid with $O(LK)$ points and fixed oversampling controls the supremum of a trigonometric polynomial with degrees $O(L)$ and $O(K)$ up to an absolute constant; this follows, for example, from the Bernstein derivative inequality in each coordinate. Apply [\[eq:fixed-phase-subgaussian\]](TPC-017.md#ref-c1a0f3c3dc6a11ee) on that grid and take a union bound. This proves [\[eq:random-entry-tail\]](TPC-017.md#ref-3aa1ffa448b6465e). Choosing $u=12\log X$ and using [\[eq:explicit-block-energy\]](TPC-017.md#ref-9b5041da0036f830) proves [\[eq:random-entry-uniform-bound\]](TPC-017.md#ref-cf1bae388613c794). This is the standard Salem–Zygmund net argument; see [`\citet{Kahane1985,Zygmund2002}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-017.md#cite-e956276134bf568c); [\[Zygmund2002\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-017.md#cite-771eb9181a159084).

###### ref-21918d045b798aae

Even one sign per source row is enough on the zero-$\beta$ slice, and hence at the distinguished zero phase. Let $$A_\ell=\sum_kc_{\ell k}.  \label{eq:row-sums-for-randomization}$$

###### ref-b356978d8bfdde3e

> **Corollary: Uniform random-row cancellation** \[cor:random-row-cancellation\] For independent Rademacher signs $(\varepsilon_\ell)$, with probability $1-O(X^{-10})$, $$\sup_{\alpha\in\mathbb T}  \left|\sum_\ell\varepsilon_\ell A_\ell\mathrm e(\alpha\ell)\right|  \ll \frac{X(\log X)^2}{\sqrt L}  \le X^{3/4+o(1)}=o(X).  \label{eq:random-row-uniform-bound}$$

###### ref-d5e8e257763ab092

> **Proof** By [\[eq:alpha-phase-energy\]](TPC-017.md#ref-131fef0569b5d5b4), $$\sum_\ell|A_\ell|^2  =\int_\mathbb T|\mathcal H_{L,K}(\alpha,0)|^2d\alpha  \ll\frac{X^2(\log X)^3}{L}.  \label{eq:row-sum-energy}$$ The one-dimensional version of the preceding net argument contributes one further factor $\sqrt{\log X}$. Finally, every nonzero square-root block has $L\gg M=X^{1/2-o(1)}$.

Randomization is not available in the arithmetic packet. The next proposition shows that this distinction is logically necessary: the phase-energy ledgers proved so far are compatible with a macroscopic deterministic zero mode.

###### ref-60085cb9f79b2fa4

###### ref-bbe7dade230e7cb9

> **Proposition: Sharp abstract countermodel** \[prop:abstract-countermodel\] Fix a sequence of product blocks with $LK\asymp X$ and $\min(L,K)\to\infty$. Suppose there are intervals of lengths $\asymp L$ and $\asymp K$ whose product rectangle lies in a region on which $|W|$ and both dyadic weights are uniformly bounded below. There exists a real sequence $r^\star_X(m)$ such that
>
> 1.  $|r^\star_X(m)|\ll\sqrt{\log X}\ll_\varepsilon X^\varepsilon$;
>
> 2.  its ambient energy on a fixed interval of length $\asymp X$ is $\asymp X\log X$;
>
> 3.  the corresponding block satisfies all upper bounds in \[[thm:explicit-block-energy](TPC-017.md#ref-8a41bae2e8563a4f),[thm:nyquist-parseval](TPC-017.md#ref-a091e71aa70f5ccc)\];
>
> 4.  nevertheless, $$|\mathcal H^\star_{L,K}(0,0)|\asymp X.   \label{eq:countermodel-large-zero}$$
>
> This is an information-theoretic countermodel, not a statement about the arithmetic residual $r_R$.

###### ref-7b44245b3c86fdd6

###### ref-2cc5607adfe73877

###### ref-c55096e6245cf173

###### ref-0f9f308cfaba3f74

> **Proof** Let $\mathcal A$ be the set of target coordinates $m=\ell k+h$ occurring in the block with $\Lambda(\ell)\ne0$. On these coordinates define $$r^\star_X(m)=\operatorname{sgn}W((m-h)/X),  \label{eq:countermodel-on-block}$$ with an arbitrary choice at zeros of $W$. Then $$\mathcal H^\star_{L,K}(0,0)  =\sum_{\ell,k}\Lambda(\ell)|W(\ell k/X)|  \psi(\ell/L)\psi(k/K).  \label{eq:countermodel-positive-sum}$$ After restricting to fixed subintervals on which the weights do not vanish, the prime number theorem in the $\ell$ coordinate gives [\[eq:countermodel-large-zero\]](TPC-017.md#ref-bbe7dade230e7cb9).
>
> On the block, $|r^\star_X|=1$, so $$E^\star_{L,K}  \ll K\sum_{\ell\asymp L}\Lambda(\ell)^2  \ll X\log X.  \label{eq:countermodel-block-energy}$$ All one- and two-phase upper bounds then follow from the same Parseval and Cauchy–Schwarz arguments as before; exact Nyquist Parseval is purely algebraic.
>
> The number of distinct block coordinates is at most the number of ordered pairs, hence $$|\mathcal A|  \ll K\#\{\ell\asymp L:\Lambda(\ell)\ne0\}  \le K\bigl(\pi(2L)+O(\sqrt L\log L)\bigr)  \ll\frac X{\log L}=o(X).  \label{eq:countermodel-used-coordinates}$$ There are therefore $\asymp X$ unused target coordinates in a slightly larger fixed ambient interval. Set $r^\star_X(m)=B_X$ on those coordinates, where $B_X\asymp\sqrt{\log X}$ is chosen to give any prescribed positive constant multiple of $X\log X$ ambient energy. This does not change the block and proves all four assertions.

The proposition proves only that $$\text{band limitation + pointwise size + total energy  + phase mean squares}  \label{eq:no-go-information-list}$$ cannot, by themselves, force cancellation at the distinguished zero phase. # Finite certificate, maze exits, and conclusion

The companion script

`experiments/tpc17_certificate.py`

performs four deterministic checks:

1.  it solves the Maynard and Li boundary intersections over exact rational arithmetic and verifies the gap $9/17-11/21=2/357$;

2.  it verifies the explicit monomial and $\delta$ ledgers at a sample interior point;

3.  it checks $\beta_V=\beta_{\le D_0}+\beta_{(D_0,V]}$ on a finite range; and

4.  it tests exact Nyquist Parseval and the $TT^*$ diagonal/off-diagonal identity on a deterministic finite matrix.

The generated JSON file records all inputs and residual errors. These checks certify algebra and implementation only. They are not evidence for an unproved prime-pair asymptotic and are not used in any proof.

The resulting maze has three mathematically distinct exits.

| Route            | Target                                                                                                                | Honest stopping result                                                                                                         |
|:-----------------|:----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|:-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Tail route       | Estimate a nontrivial subrange of $\beta_{(D_0,V]}$ by additional factor shape or cancellation                        | A larger exact prefix, or a theorem identifying which tail shapes are inaccessible to current distribution inputs              |
| Dispersion route | Prove $o(XL)$ for the averaged two-form correlation [\[eq:offdiagonal-correlation\]](TPC-017.md#ref-720afb6a230b6c16) | A zero-phase cancellation theorem on a restricted corridor, or a sharp lower-bound obstruction for existing $TT^*$ information |
| Hybrid route     | Use divisor-tail structure before the $TT^*$ expansion                                                                | A determinant-resolved normal form combining $d>D_0$ with $h(\ell_1-\ell_2)$, even if the final cancellation remains open      |

The paper therefore advances on two fronts. Analytically, it packages the factorable divisor slices as a complete initial segment and compresses the unresolved Vaughan coefficient to an exact tail. Spectrally, it turns a possible distinguished zero-mode failure into a sparse uncertainty-cell concentration and then into one explicit off-diagonal two-form correlation. The countermodel proves that the phase mean-square ledgers available here, even together with pointwise and total-energy bounds, do not close that gate.

The next useful theorem must consequently exploit arithmetic structure in either the Möbius tail or the determinant-resolved residual pair. This is a narrower and more falsifiable target than the original fixed-shift correlation, but it remains well short of a twin-prime or general Hardy–Littlewood theorem.
