## Abstract

Fix $h\ne0$. TPC-17 reduces a family of symmetric fixed-shift Type-II blocks to the sharp Möbius tail $$\beta_I(k)=\sum_{\substack{d\mid k\\D_0<d\le V}}\mu(d),  \qquad I=(D_0,V],$$ but its separate $TT^*$ theorem concerns an asymmetric packet and cannot be spliced into this tail. We construct two quadraticizations inside the symmetric tail block itself.

The first is an exact least-common-multiple transform $$\beta_I(k)^2=\sum_{q\mid k}\Gamma_I(q),  \qquad  \Gamma_I(q)=\sum_{[d_1,d_2]=q}\mu(d_1)\mu(d_2).$$ Because $V^2\le R$, every squared-tail row returns to the original Ramanujan cutoff $q\le R$. If $D_0<V/2$, Selberg’s classical divisor Gram factorization gives a positive terminal-octave Gram floor and, when $K/V^2\to\infty$ as in the explicit TPC-17 profiles, natural $k$-space $L^2$ mass; coefficient smallness cannot close it. The second quadraticization first fixes $d\asymp D$, opens $k=dj$, and applies $TT^*$ in $j$. With $m=\ell d$, $Q=LD$, and $J=K/D$, the determinant is $h(m_1-m_2)$. We prove by elementary counting that the same-source, small-determinant, and large-$(d_1,d_2)$ channels are all negligible. Thus failure of a tail slice forces a quantitatively large correlation with distinct prime sources, large determinant, and low divisor gcd.

There is a further exact split by $s=(k,h)$. In every nonprimitive sector $s>1$, writing $H=\operatorname{rad}(s)$ gives $$\beta_I(k)=\sum_{\substack{e\mid k\\(e,H)=1}}\mu(e)  \sum_{f\mid H}\mu(f)\mathbf 1_{D_0<ef\le V},$$ and the inner coefficient is supported only on $(D_0/H,D_0]\cup(V/H,V]$. Hence the whole interior tail cancels pointwise. Moreover, up to a negligible prime-power term, the nonprimitive residual correlation is a finite-model–finite-model correlation governed by three explicit Chinese-remainder conditions.

These results identify a sharper stopping point; they do not estimate the remaining primitive generic correlation. We state a conditional determinant-dispersion input that would close the tail and explain why, under an explicit all-block cover hypothesis for the previous normal form, it reaches the fixed-shift Hardy–Littlewood problem. No twin-prime theorem, new level of distribution, or breach of sieve parity is claimed.

# Introduction and claim ledger

The parity problem is not merely a shortage of numerical cancellation. It is a structural failure of ordinary sieve information to distinguish integers with an odd or even number of prime factors; see Selberg’s classical discussion and modern sieve treatments [`\citep{Selberg1952Parity,FI2010}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-018.md#cite-aa901a19a21f6091); [\[FI2010\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-018.md#cite-8f51c21436d9670a). The fixed-shift prime-pair problem therefore provides a useful stress test for any proposed spectral or dynamical reformulation: an attractive change of variables is valuable only if every discarded term and every imported distribution theorem is made explicit.

TPC-15–TPC-17 follow this discipline for the finite Ramanujan model [`\citep{WangTPC15,WangTPC16,WangTPC17}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-018.md#cite-b0805195de3a7444); [\[WangTPC16\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-018.md#cite-706dced581e13590); [\[WangTPC17\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-018.md#cite-48c7e4d945aa60c7) $$\Lambda_R(n)=\sum_{q\le R}\frac{\mu(q)}{\varphi(q)}c_q(n),  \qquad r_R(n)=\Lambda(n)-\Lambda_R(n).$$ TPC-17 proves cancellation for an assembled initial divisor segment and leaves, on certain symmetric Heath–Brown blocks, the exact coefficient $$\beta_I(k)=\sum_{\substack{d\mid k\\d\in I}}\mu(d),  \qquad I=(D_0,V].$$ It also proves a $TT^*$ stopping theorem for a different, asymmetric square-root packet. The two statements are not interchangeable: the asymmetric packet has no factor $\beta_I(k)$, while squaring the latter changes both its row geometry and its coefficient class. The first purpose of this paper is to construct the correct quadratic forms directly from the symmetric tail.

## What is proved

There are four unconditional structural statements within the TPC interface.

1.  The square of the sharp divisor tail has an exact lcm expansion. Since $V^2\le R$, its new row label lies back inside the finite-model cutoff. We call this *lcm cutoff return*.

2.  If $D_0<V/2$, the associated divisor Gram form has an explicit positive lower bound coming from $r\in(V/2,V]$. When $K/V^2\to\infty$, as in the two explicit TPC-17 profiles, this yields natural $k$-space $L^2$ mass. Thus neither Cauchy–Schwarz nor a claim that the tail coefficient is $L^2$-small can remove those remaining packets. The Gram identity and terminal-octave mechanism are classical; our contribution is to place them exactly at the TPC-17 stopping gate.

3.  A different, $d$-first $TT^*$ expansion produces row moduli $m=\ell d$ and determinant $h(m_1-m_2)$. We remove three degenerate families without any distribution theorem. A failed slice must therefore concentrate in the generic channel $$\ell_1\ne\ell_2,\qquad  |m_1-m_2|>QX^{-\kappa},\qquad  (d_1,d_2)\le X^\kappa.$$

4.  Splitting by $(k,h)$ reveals an exact asymmetry. In the primitive sector the two target values have gcd dividing $\ell_1-\ell_2$. In every nonprimitive sector the interior Möbius interval cancels pointwise and only fixed-ratio endpoint bands remain. Terms containing a target von Mangoldt factor reduce to prime powers, so the surviving correlation is, up to a power-saving error at the relevant scale, a finite-model correlation with an exact CRT kernel.

The individual lcm, Gram, and inclusion–exclusion identities are classical or elementary. The contribution claimed here is their exact coupling to the TPC-17 tail and the resulting stopping decomposition. The combined conclusion is a stopping theorem, not an asymptotic formula. It converts a failed symmetric tail block into either a primitive large-determinant/low-gcd residual witness or a nonprimitive endpoint finite-model witness.

## What is not proved

We do not prove any of the following:

-   a fixed-$h$ Hardy–Littlewood asymptotic or infinitely many twin primes;

-   cancellation of the primitive generic determinant correlation;

-   a new level of distribution for primes;

-   that the sharp Möbius interval coefficient is well-factorable;

-   that an exponent appearing in an existing one-prime progression theorem can be transplanted to the two-residual correlation here.

Indeed, the last sections explain that a uniform estimate strong enough to remove every relevant generic slice, combined with TPC-16 and TPC-17, would deliver the fixed-shift Hardy–Littlewood conclusion. This is why the remaining statement is recorded as a conditional input rather than advertised as a routine consequence of current dispersion technology.

## Relation to classical and current work

The lcm Gram form belongs to the Selberg-sieve tradition. Closely related Möbius double sums were studied by [`\citet{DressIwaniecTenenbaum1983}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-018.md#cite-1d54a96a050797fa) and [`\citet{deLaBretecheDressTenenbaum2020}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-018.md#cite-f4a9ef82a2d5adb1); smoothing and factorization issues are developed systematically by [`\citet{GranvilleKoukoulopoulosMaynard2021}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-018.md#cite-2a4f7d6993078cd5). A recent preprint of [`\citet{RamareZunigaAlterman2026v3}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-018.md#cite-56b46edb8f2905a1) makes the same terminal-octave obstruction especially transparent. We therefore make no novelty claim for the bare Gram identity or its endpoint floor.

For distribution beyond the square-root barrier, the relevant lesson from Maynard’s published fixed-residue and well-factorable theorems [`\citep{Maynard2025I,Maynard2025II}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-018.md#cite-158e56c67e354b21); [\[Maynard2025II\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-018.md#cite-4e699b9f634601fc), and from the more recent preprints of [`\citet{Lichtman2023}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-018.md#cite-6d2b96a41798f2f3) and [`\citet{Li2026v6}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-018.md#cite-ccea3250102f24c5), is that coefficient shape matters as much as the numerical exponent. The sharp interval $\mu(d)\mathbf 1_I(d)$ contains isolated large primes and is not covered by their balanced factorization hypotheses. Moreover, their conclusions concern one prime in progressions; the object left here is a correlation of two residual affine forms. Kloosterman-fraction estimates such as [`\citet{BettinChandee2018}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-018.md#cite-20c5dcaa0c8d6228) are important ingredients in dispersion arguments but do not, without a new reduction, estimate our variable-determinant form.

> **Theorem: Exact lcm transform** \[thm:lcm-transform\] For every $k\ge1$, $$\boxed{\ \beta_I(k)^2=\sum_{q\mid k}\Gamma_I(q).\ }  \label{eq:lcm-transform}$$ Consequently, $$\Gamma_I(q)=\sum_{a\mid q}\mu(q/a)\beta_I(a)^2.  \label{eq:Gamma-inversion}$$ Moreover, $$\operatorname{supp}\Gamma_I\subset\{q\le V^2:q\ \text{squarefree}\},  \qquad  |\Gamma_I(q)|\le3^{\omega(q)},  \label{eq:Gamma-support-bound}$$ and $$\sum_q|\Gamma_I(q)|\le V^2.  \label{eq:Gamma-l1}$$

> **Proof** Expand the square in [\[eq:beta-I\]](TPC-018.md#ref-e6a11398644267bc). The conditions $d_1\mid k$ and $d_2\mid k$ are equivalent to $[d_1,d_2]\mid k$; grouping by that least common multiple proves [\[eq:lcm-transform\]](TPC-018.md#ref-c6bd77834c2b91c3). Equation [\[eq:Gamma-inversion\]](TPC-018.md#ref-587dae2b9b976c46) is ordinary Möbius inversion.
>
> Only squarefree $d_i$ contribute. Hence their lcm is squarefree and at most $V^2$. For a squarefree $q$, each prime divisor can lie in $d_1$ only, $d_2$ only, or both, giving at most $3^{\omega(q)}$ ordered pairs. Finally, summing absolute values before grouping by the lcm gives at most the number of ordered pairs $d_1,d_2\in I$, which is at most $V^2$.

The sign structure becomes more transparent after separating the gcd.

> **Proposition: Pairwise-coprime lcm coordinates** \[prop:gab\] One has the exact formula $$\Gamma_I(q)=  \sum_{\substack{gab=q\\  g,a,b\ \mathrm{squarefree}\\  (g,a)=(g,b)=(a,b)=1\\  D_0<ga\le V,\ D_0<gb\le V}}  \mu(a)\mu(b),  \label{eq:Gamma-gab}$$ The squarefree condition is part of the summation range.

> **Theorem: Terminal-octave rigidity** \[thm:terminal-octave\] If $D_0<V/2$, then $$\begin{aligned}  Q_I  &\ge\sum_{V/2<r\le V}\frac{\mu(r)^2\varphi(r)}{r^2}  \label{eq:terminal-octave-finite}\\  &=C_{\mathrm{sf}}\log2+o(1),  \qquad  C_{\mathrm{sf}}=\prod_p  \left(1-\frac2{p^2}+\frac1{p^3}\right)>0.  \label{eq:terminal-octave-asymptotic}\end{aligned}$$

Take $y=V/2$, $z=V$.

> **Proposition: Weighted second moment** \[prop:weighted-second-moment\] Let $w\in C_c^1((0,\infty))$. Then $$\sum_{k\ge1}\beta_I(k)^2w(k/K)  =K I_0(w)Q_I+O_w(V^2),  \qquad I_0(w)=\int_0^\infty w(t)\,dt.  \label{eq:weighted-beta-second-moment}$$ In particular, if $w\ge0$, $I_0(w)>0$, $D_0<V/2$, and $K/V^2\to\infty$, then $$\sum_k\beta_I(k)^2w(k/K)\gg_w K.  \label{eq:beta-natural-L2}$$

> **Proof** Insert [\[eq:lcm-transform\]](TPC-018.md#ref-c6bd77834c2b91c3) and use $$\sum_jw(qj/K)=\frac Kq I_0(w)+O_w(1).$$ The main coefficient is $$\sum_q\frac{\Gamma_I(q)}q  =\sum_{d,e\in I}\frac{\mu(d)\mu(e)}{[d,e]}=Q_I,$$ while [\[eq:Gamma-l1\]](TPC-018.md#ref-4b737a369a4d0316) bounds the total error by $O_w(V^2)$. The final assertion follows from [\[thm:terminal-octave\]](TPC-018.md#ref-c1806937e7b2067b).

> **Remark: A rigorous coefficient-shape obstruction** Suppose $D_0<\sqrt V$ and let $p\in(\sqrt V,V]$ be prime. Then $\mu(p)\mathbf 1_I(p)=-1$. On the other hand, if $a$ and $b$ are both supported on $[1,\sqrt V]$, then $(a*b)(p)=0$. Hence the exact sharp coefficient $\mu(d)\mathbf 1_I(d)$ cannot be a finite linear combination of such balanced convolutions. This elementary observation does not rule out every notion of factorability, but it does rule out the balanced shape needed for a direct invocation of the current well-factorable distribution theorems. Together with [\[eq:beta-natural-L2\]](TPC-018.md#ref-edda1043d8be1427), it shows why neither a formal factor split nor coefficient $L^2$ smallness closes the tail.

The identities in this section are classical Selberg diagonalization. The endpoint floor has close precedents in [`\citet{DressIwaniecTenenbaum1983,deLaBretecheDressTenenbaum2020,RamareZunigaAlterman2026v3}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-018.md#cite-1d54a96a050797fa); [\[deLaBretecheDressTenenbaum2020\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-018.md#cite-f4a9ef82a2d5adb1); [\[RamareZunigaAlterman2026v3\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-018.md#cite-56b46edb8f2905a1). The new role here is diagnostic: it identifies which tempting route at the TPC-17 tail gate is unavailable before any prime-correlation estimate is attempted.

> **Theorem: Tail-weighted $TT^*$ stopping theorem** \[thm:same-k-ttstar\] Assume [\[eq:block-geometry\]](TPC-018.md#ref-547a3dbe2659fd43) and $L\ge X^{\lambda_0}$ for a fixed $\lambda_0>0$. Then the diagonal is smaller than the right-hand side by a fixed power of $X$, proving the claim.

> **Theorem: Elementary removal of the degenerate channels** \[thm:degenerate-removal\] For every fixed $\varepsilon>0$, $$\begin{aligned}  |\mathcal C_D^{\mathrm{same}}|  &\ll_\varepsilon XQ\,L^{-1}X^\varepsilon,  \label{eq:same-bound}\\  |\mathcal C_D^{\mathrm{near}}|  &\ll_\varepsilon XQ\bigl(X^{-\kappa}+L^{-1}\bigr)X^\varepsilon,  \label{eq:near-bound}\\  |\mathcal C_D^{\mathrm{gcd}}|  &\ll_\varepsilon XQ\,X^{-\kappa+\varepsilon}.  \label{eq:gcd-bound}\end{aligned}$$ If $L\ge X^{\lambda_0}$ for fixed $\lambda_0>0$, all three have a fixed power saving after choosing $\varepsilon$ sufficiently small. > **Proof** By [\[eq:soft-pointwise\]](TPC-018.md#ref-1c0bd6993c0d5139), each summand is $O_\varepsilon(X^\varepsilon)$ after absorbing logarithms. It therefore suffices to count tuples.
>

# Primitive content and nonprimitive endpoint collapse

###### ref-13a5c4021dce0336

The determinant is only part of the common-divisor geometry. For a given $k$, put $$s=(k,h),\qquad k=sk',\qquad h=sh',\qquad(k',h')=1.  \label{eq:content-decomposition}$$

###### ref-118db4b2192ea07e

###### ref-3cabd82f50a26e8b

###### ref-d3fa4c5c4222d83f

> **Proposition: Target gcd geometry** \[prop:target-gcd\] For distinct source primes $\ell_1,\ell_2$ there exists $t\mid(\ell_1-\ell_2)$ such that $$\gcd(\ell_1k+h,\ell_2k+h)=s\,t.  \label{eq:target-gcd}$$ In particular, on the primitive sector $(k,h)=1$, $$\gcd(\ell_1k+h,\ell_2k+h)\mid|\ell_1-\ell_2|.  \label{eq:primitive-target-gcd}$$

> **Proof** After removing $s$, the two target values are $\ell_i k'+h'$. Their gcd divides their difference $(\ell_1-\ell_2)k'$. It is coprime to $k'$, because $\gcd(\ell_i k'+h',k')=(h',k')=1$. Hence it divides $\ell_1-\ell_2$. Restoring $s$ proves the formula.

###### ref-15c3dbec0fc5a9fa

For the lcm row $k=qj$, knowing $(q,h)$ alone does not determine the content. Indeed, with $s_0=(q,h)$, $$(qj,h)=s_0\gcd\!\left(j,\frac h{s_0}\right).  \label{eq:qj-content}$$ Thus a complete content split must also separate the finitely many divisors of $h/s_0$. Since $h$ is fixed, this costs no power of $\log X$.

The Möbius coefficient undergoes an exact cancellation on every nonprimitive sector.

###### ref-448d2acaeecff07f

###### ref-b6bb33a0b1771994

> **Theorem: Nonprimitive endpoint collapse** \[thm:endpoint-collapse\] Let $s=(k,h)>1$ and $H=\operatorname{rad}(s)$. Then $$\boxed{\quad  \beta_I(k)=  \sum_{\substack{e\mid k\\(e,H)=1}}\mu(e)\Omega_H(e),  \qquad  \Omega_H(e)=\sum_{f\mid H}\mu(f)  \mathbf 1_{D_0<ef\le V}.  \quad}  \label{eq:endpoint-collapse}$$ Moreover, $$\boxed{\quad  \operatorname{supp}\Omega_H\subset  (D_0/H,D_0]\cup(V/H,V].  \quad}  \label{eq:endpoint-support}$$ Thus the full interior $D_0<e\le V/H$ cancels pointwise.

> **Proof** Since $H\mid k$, every squarefree divisor $d\mid k$ has a unique factorization $d=ef$ with $f\mid H$ and $(e,H)=1$. The two factors are coprime, so $\mu(d)=\mu(e)\mu(f)$. Inserting the interval condition on $d$ proves [\[eq:endpoint-collapse\]](TPC-018.md#ref-448d2acaeecff07f).
>
> If $e\le D_0/H$, then $ef\le D_0$ for every $f\mid H$, so $\Omega_H(e)=0$. If $D_0<e\le V/H$, every $f\mid H$ satisfies $D_0<ef\le V$, and $$\Omega_H(e)=\sum_{f\mid H}\mu(f)=0$$ because $H>1$. Finally $e>V$ gives no admissible $f$. The only remaining possibilities lie in the two intervals in [\[eq:endpoint-support\]](TPC-018.md#ref-b6bb33a0b1771994). The intervals may overlap, which causes no problem.

Because $s\mid h$, one has $H\le|h|$. Hence each surviving interval has a fixed multiplicative width depending only on $h$. This is a strict pointwise identity, not an average cancellation statement. After squaring [\[eq:endpoint-collapse\]](TPC-018.md#ref-448d2acaeecff07f), every nonprimitive $\beta_I(k)^2$ is assembled from two endpoint divisor labels $e_1,e_2$ in the same fixed union.

###### ref-945ce5bfed0aeddd

We now show that a nonprimitive residual pair contains no genuinely new target-prime correlation. For $s\mid h$, define $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal C_{I,s}^{\mathrm{off}}  :={}&\sum_{\ell_1\ne\ell_2}  \lambda_{\ell_1}\lambda_{\ell_2}  \sum_{\substack{k\\(k,h)=s}}  \beta_I(k)^2r_R(n_1)r_R(n_2)  \mathcal W_{\ell_1,\ell_2}(k),  \label{eq:sector-residual-correlation}\\  n_i:={}&\ell_i k+h,  \notag\end{aligned}$$ and let $\mathcal C_{I,s}^{MM,\mathrm{off}}$ be the same expression with $r_R(n_1)r_R(n_2)$ replaced by $\Lambda_R(n_1)\Lambda_R(n_2)$.

###### ref-ad624b909383b44b

###### ref-6c2b34be89252b20

> **Theorem: Nonprimitive finite-model reduction** \[thm:nonprimitive-MM\] For each fixed divisor $s>1$ of $h$ and every $\varepsilon>0$, $$\boxed{\quad  \mathcal C_{I,s}^{\mathrm{off}}  =\mathcal C_{I,s}^{MM,\mathrm{off}}  +O_{\varepsilon,h,W,\psi}(LX^\varepsilon).  \quad}  \label{eq:nonprimitive-MM}$$ The coefficient in the finite-model correlation is endpoint-localized by [\[eq:endpoint-collapse\]](TPC-018.md#ref-448d2acaeecff07f)–[\[eq:endpoint-support\]](TPC-018.md#ref-b6bb33a0b1771994).

> **Proof** Expand $$\begin{aligned}  r_R(n_1)r_R(n_2)  ={}&\Lambda(n_1)\Lambda(n_2)  -\Lambda(n_1)\Lambda_R(n_2)  -\Lambda_R(n_1)\Lambda(n_2)\\  &+\Lambda_R(n_1)\Lambda_R(n_2).\end{aligned}$$ Choose a prime $p\mid s$. Since $p\mid k$ and $p\mid h$, both $n_i$ are divisible by $p$. If $\Lambda(n_i)\ne0$, then $n_i=p^\nu$. On the fixed multiplicative support $n_i\asymp_WX$, there are $O_{h,W}(1)$ possible exponents $\nu$. The equation $$\ell_i k=p^\nu-h$$ has $O_\varepsilon(X^\varepsilon)$ factor pairs. Once one target von Mangoldt factor is fixed in this way, the other source variable has $O(L)$ choices. The soft bounds for $\beta_I$ and $\Lambda_R$, and all logarithmic weights, are absorbed into another $X^\varepsilon$. Thus every term containing at least one target $\Lambda$ is $O_{\varepsilon,h,W,\psi}(LX^\varepsilon)$. Only the last finite-model product remains.

> **Remark** Endpoint localization does not imply that the endpoint correlation is small. It changes its algebraic type and removes the interior interval; an estimate for the remaining finite-model lattice sum is still needed.

# The exact finite-model CRT kernel

###### ref-fbbbb8e3634969f3

The Ramanujan identity $$c_q(n)=\sum_{u\mid(q,n)}u\mu(q/u)$$ gives the finite divisor expansion $$\Lambda_R(n)=\sum_{\substack{u\mid n\\u\le R}}\lambda_R'(u),  \qquad  \lambda_R'(u)=u\sum_{m\le R/u}  \frac{\mu(um)\mu(m)}{\varphi(um)}.  \label{eq:lambda-prime-R}$$ We derive the exact compatibility conditions for the small lcm row $q\mid k$ in [\[eq:lcm-residual-correlation\]](TPC-018.md#ref-3c3626cbb078ee7b).

###### ref-c5ec021c081830e6

###### ref-b0e9157989d69abd

> **Lemma: Three-modulus compatibility** \[lem:CRT-compatibility\] Let $q,u,v\le R$, and let $\ell_1,\ell_2>R$ be primes. The system $$q\mid k,\qquad  u\mid\ell_1k+h,\qquad  v\mid\ell_2k+h  \label{eq:CRT-system}$$ is soluble if and only if $$\boxed{\quad  (q,u)\mid h,\qquad  (q,v)\mid h,\qquad  (u,v)\mid h(\ell_1-\ell_2).  \quad}  \label{eq:CRT-conditions}$$ When soluble, $k$ occupies one residue class modulo $[q,u,v]$.

> **Proof** The sources are invertible modulo $u$ and $v$. Thus [\[eq:CRT-system\]](TPC-018.md#ref-c5ec021c081830e6) is the system $$k\equiv0\pmod q,\qquad  k\equiv-h\overline\ell_1\pmod u,\qquad  k\equiv-h\overline\ell_2\pmod v.$$ The generalized Chinese remainder theorem says that pairwise compatibility is necessary and sufficient. Compatibility of the first congruence with the second and third gives the first two divisibilities in [\[eq:CRT-conditions\]](TPC-018.md#ref-b0e9157989d69abd). If $g=(u,v)$, compatibility of the last two is $h(\overline\ell_1-\overline\ell_2)\equiv0\pmod g$. Multiplication by the unit $\ell_1\ell_2$ turns this into $g\mid h(\ell_1-\ell_2)$. The final statement is the uniqueness part of the generalized CRT.

Let $F\in C_c^1((0,\infty))$ and define the finite-model row $$\mathcal M_R(q;\ell_1,\ell_2;F)  =\sum_{\substack{k\ge1\\q\mid k}}  \Lambda_R(\ell_1k+h)\Lambda_R(\ell_2k+h)F(k/K).  \label{eq:finite-model-row}$$

> **Theorem: Explicit two-target density** \[thm:finite-model-density\] One has the exact expansion $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal M_R(q;\ell_1,\ell_2;F)  ={}&\sum_{u,v\le R}\lambda_R'(u)\lambda_R'(v)  \mathbf 1_{(q,u)\mid h}\mathbf 1_{(q,v)\mid h}  \mathbf 1_{(u,v)\mid h(\ell_1-\ell_2)}  \notag\\[-0.2em]  &\quad\times  \sum_{\substack{k\equiv\kappa(q,u,v)\!   \pmod{[q,u,v]}}}F(k/K),  \label{eq:finite-model-exact-CRT}\end{aligned}$$ where $\kappa(q,u,v)$ is the compatible class and an incompatible summand is zero. Its complete-period density, normalized relative to the $q$-row, is $$\boxed{\quad  \rho^{(2)}_{h,R}(q;\ell_1,\ell_2)  =q\sum_{u,v\le R}\lambda_R'(u)\lambda_R'(v)  \frac{  \mathbf 1_{(q,u)\mid h}\mathbf 1_{(q,v)\mid h}  \mathbf 1_{(u,v)\mid h(\ell_1-\ell_2)}}{[q,u,v]}.  \quad}  \label{eq:two-target-density}$$ Consequently, $$\mathcal M_R(q;\ell_1,\ell_2;F)  =\frac Kq\rho^{(2)}_{h,R}(q;\ell_1,\ell_2)I_0(F)  +\mathcal E_R(q;\ell_1,\ell_2;F),  \label{eq:finite-model-main-error}$$ with the elementary bound $$|\mathcal E_R(q;\ell_1,\ell_2;F)|  \ll_F R^2(\log(2R))^4.  \label{eq:finite-model-crude-error}$$

> **Proof** Insert [\[eq:lambda-prime-R\]](TPC-018.md#ref-fbbbb8e3634969f3) twice in [\[eq:finite-model-row\]](TPC-018.md#ref-644227f50c26f6c1) and apply [\[lem:CRT-compatibility\]](TPC-018.md#ref-37181c6286da419a). This proves the exact formula. On a compatible residue class modulo $M=[q,u,v]$, Euler summation gives $$\sum_{k\equiv\kappa\pmod M}F(k/K)  =\frac KM I_0(F)+O_F(1).$$ The main terms assemble into [\[eq:two-target-density\]](TPC-018.md#ref-0d48a62e87c600c0).
>
> The formula [\[eq:lambda-prime-R\]](TPC-018.md#ref-fbbbb8e3634969f3) and the standard bound $n/\varphi(n)\ll\log\log(3n)$ give $|\lambda_R'(u)|\ll(\log(2R))^2$ uniformly. Summing the $O_F(1)$ errors over $u,v\le R$ proves [\[eq:finite-model-crude-error\]](TPC-018.md#ref-6efe7c426b55fcbe).

The third condition in [\[eq:CRT-conditions\]](TPC-018.md#ref-b0e9157989d69abd) contains the transverse determinant $h(\ell_1-\ell_2)$, whereas the first two retain the lcm row $q\mid k$. This is why replacing the geometry by the single product $hq(\ell_1-\ell_2)$ loses information.

The exact kernel is useful, but the crude error in [\[eq:finite-model-crude-error\]](TPC-018.md#ref-6efe7c426b55fcbe) is far too large after summing the two source variables. Controlling incomplete periods requires genuine cancellation in the $u,v$ coefficients or a spectral/dispersion transformation. We claim no asymptotic evaluation of this kernel here.

# The stopping dichotomy and a conditional closure gate

###### ref-417d0f04e3931da1

We assemble the two quadraticizations. For each positive divisor $s\mid h$, put $$\mathcal T_s^{\mathbb P}  =\sum_{\substack{k\\(k,h)=s}}\beta_I(k)S_k.  \label{eq:sector-tail}$$ Write $\mathcal C_{D,\mathrm{prim}}^{\mathrm{gen}}$ for the generic subsum $\mathcal C_D^{\mathrm{gen}}$ of \[[eq:d-off](TPC-018.md#ref-98a2419b3802e793),[eq:generic-set](TPC-018.md#ref-ed16f091bee991ff)\] with the fixed mask $\xi(d,j)=\mathbf 1_{(dj,h)=1}$.

> **Theorem: Primitive/nonprimitive stopping dichotomy** \[thm:stopping-dichotomy\] Assume [\[eq:block-geometry\]](TPC-018.md#ref-547a3dbe2659fd43), $D_0<V/2$, and $L\ge X^{\lambda_0}$ for a fixed $\lambda_0>0$. Suppose that, for fixed $a\ge0$ and $\eta>0$, $$|\mathcal T_{L,K}(I)|\ge\frac{\eta X}{(\log X)^a}.  \label{eq:tail-failure}$$ Then, for every fixed $\kappa>0$ and all sufficiently large $X$, at least one of the following alternatives holds.
>
> 1.  **Primitive generic determinant witness.** There is a dyadic $D\le V$, with $Q=LD$, such that the primitive $d$-slice has a generic correlation satisfying $$\mathcal C_{D,\mathrm{prim}}^{\mathrm{gen}}  \gg_{\eta,h,W}  \frac{XQ}{(\log X)^{2a+2}},  \label{eq:primitive-generic-dichotomy}$$ supported on $$\ell_1\ne\ell_2,\qquad  |\ell_1d_1-\ell_2d_2|>QX^{-\kappa},\qquad  (d_1,d_2)\le X^\kappa,\qquad  (d_1j,h)=(d_2j,h)=1.$$
>
> 2.  **Nonprimitive endpoint finite-model witness.** There is a divisor $s>1$ of $h$ for which $$\mathcal C_{I,s}^{MM,\mathrm{off}}  \gg_{\eta,h,W}\frac{XL}{(\log X)^{2a}}.  \label{eq:nonprimitive-dichotomy}$$ Its Möbius coefficients are supported only on the two endpoint bands in [\[eq:endpoint-support\]](TPC-018.md#ref-b6bb33a0b1771994). After a dyadic split of $|\ell_1-\ell_2|$, one signed separation cell contributes $$\gg_{\eta,h,W}\frac{XL}{(\log X)^{2a+1}}.  \label{eq:nonprimitive-Delta-cell}$$

> **Proof** By [\[lem:source-primes\]](TPC-018.md#ref-cd7f467f865cfa9a), the source prime-power error is smaller than [\[eq:tail-failure\]](TPC-018.md#ref-51a07f0366866a2a) by a fixed power. Since $(k,h)$ can take only $O_h(1)$ values, one sector in [\[eq:sector-tail\]](TPC-018.md#ref-417d0f04e3931da1) has magnitude $\gg_{\eta,h}X(\log X)^{-a}$.
>
> If the large sector is $s=1$, open $\beta_I(k)$ and use a smooth dyadic partition on $d\in I$. There are $O(\log X)$ slices, so one primitive slice [\[eq:TD\]](TPC-018.md#ref-969278f498d96119), with $\xi(d,j)=\mathbf 1_{(dj,h)=1}$, has magnitude $\gg X(\log X)^{-a-1}$. Apply [\[thm:generic-stopping\]](TPC-018.md#ref-722f51216f463a57) with $a$ replaced by $a+1$ to obtain [\[eq:primitive-generic-dichotomy\]](TPC-018.md#ref-deeb1ea7fe7c006f).
>
> If the large sector has $s>1$, repeat the same-$k$ Cauchy argument of [\[thm:same-k-ttstar\]](TPC-018.md#ref-903578a75bf61e9d) with the restriction $(k,h)=s$. Its diagonal is still $O_\varepsilon(X^{1+\varepsilon})$, so $$\mathcal C_{I,s}^{\mathrm{off}}  \gg_{\eta,h,W}XL(\log X)^{-2a}.$$ The error in [\[eq:nonprimitive-MM\]](TPC-018.md#ref-6c2b34be89252b20) is negligible at this scale, which proves [\[eq:nonprimitive-dichotomy\]](TPC-018.md#ref-f5247630298991bd). Endpoint localization follows by squaring [\[eq:endpoint-collapse\]](TPC-018.md#ref-448d2acaeecff07f). There are $O(\log X)$ nonempty dyadic source-separation ranges; since their signed sum is positive and has the displayed size, at least one proves [\[eq:nonprimitive-Delta-cell\]](TPC-018.md#ref-13112a676b0c639f).

> **Remark: Block scope** is attached to a symmetric tail block for which the TPC-17 prefix theorem applies. It does not assert that every possible failure of a fixed-shift prime-pair asymptotic must occur in this particular family of blocks. That global statement requires the full preceding normal-form interface.

We now isolate the exact analytic input that would remove alternative (A). This input is deliberately stated rather than assumed true.

## How strong is the conditional input?

###### ref-4d3cafbba7ab176d

Write $$L=X^{\lambda+o(1)},\qquad  V=X^{v+o(1)},\qquad  v=\frac14-\frac\delta2.$$ The largest opened row has $Q=LV=X^{\lambda+v+o(1)}$. Define the boundary exponent $$\theta_{\mathrm{tail}}=\lambda+\frac14-\frac\delta2.  \label{eq:theta-tail}$$ Because [\[input:DD\]](TPC-018.md#ref-2c435f3a0bd2ec4c) retains a fixed $\varepsilon_0$ margin, full-tail closure requires $\theta>\theta_{\mathrm{tail}}$ by a fixed amount; equality is only the boundary value. For the two explicit TPC-17 profiles, let $\sigma>0$ denote the fixed power-saving slack, subject to $\sigma<1/100$ in the published row and $\sigma<1/1000$ in the version-locked Li row. The boundary ledger is:

|:------------------------|:------------------------------------------|:--------------------------------------|

Maynard’s published well-factorable benchmark reaches level $3/5$ for its specified coefficient class [`\citep{Maynard2025II}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-018.md#cite-4e699b9f634601fc). > **Corollary: Why uniform closure reaches the fixed-shift problem** \[cor:DD-HL-boundary\] Assume the symmetric normal form of TPC-16 [`\citep{WangTPC16}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-018.md#cite-706dced581e13590) and, as an additional global cover hypothesis, that its hard packet admits $$\mathcal H^{\mathrm{HB}}_{h,R;V}(X)  =\sum_{b\in\mathcal B_X}(\mathcal C_b+\mathcal T_b)+o(X),  \qquad |\mathcal B_X|=O_W(\log X),  \label{eq:all-block-cover}$$ where every block satisfies [\[eq:block-geometry\]](TPC-018.md#ref-547a3dbe2659fd43), the TPC-17 assembled-prefix estimate for $\mathcal C_b$, and the opened-$d$ geometry for $\mathcal T_b$. Assume also $\mathrm{DD}_h(\theta)$ with a fixed strict margin above every boundary in [\[eq:theta-tail\]](TPC-018.md#ref-4d3cafbba7ab176d). Then the resulting chain gives the weighted fixed-shift Hardy–Littlewood asymptotic $$\sum_n\Lambda(n)\Lambda(n+h)W(n/X)  =\mathfrak S(h)X I_0(W)+o(X).  \label{eq:conditional-HL}$$ For $h=2$ and nonnegative nonzero $W$, this would imply infinitely many twin primes after the standard removal of prime powers.

> **Proof** The assumed all-block prefix estimates remove the $\mathcal C_b$ terms. Proposition [\[prop:conditional-closure\]](TPC-018.md#ref-c4dfbce7a1e9de5f) removes each complementary $\mathcal T_b$. The global cover [\[eq:all-block-cover\]](TPC-018.md#ref-19ad03d62e5ae7f4) and the TPC-16 normal form identify the sum of those blocks with the difference between the left side of [\[eq:conditional-HL\]](TPC-018.md#ref-cbd17bf10379585c) and its calibrated singular-series main term. There are only $O_W(\log X)$ blocks, so arbitrary logarithmic saving survives their sum. The $h=2$ consequence follows from positivity and the fact that source or target prime powers contribute $o(X)$.

This corollary is a logical strength test, not a proof of its hypotheses. It explains why the primitive generic channel should not be dismissed as a routine Kloosterman estimate. By contrast, [`\citet{MatomakiRadziwillTao2019}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-018.md#cite-03024995d598450c) establish the expected prime correlations for almost all shifts in sufficiently long ranges. That is a rigorous averaged-$h$ benchmark, but it supplies no direct fixed-$h$, lcm-row-resolved estimate for [\[eq:DD\]](TPC-018.md#ref-487bb8004aa1f4fa).

# Finite certificate and conclusion

The accompanying script `experiments/tpc18_certificate.py` performs exact finite checks of the algebraic layer. It verifies:

1.  the lcm transform [\[eq:lcm-transform\]](TPC-018.md#ref-c6bd77834c2b91c3), its Möbius inversion, and the $g,a,b$ parameterization;

2.  the Gram identity [\[eq:Gram-factorization\]](TPC-018.md#ref-5d6d76a1bd38b87e) using rational arithmetic and the exact finite-box identity $$\sum_{k\le K}\beta_I(k)^2  =\sum_q\Gamma_I(q)\left\lfloor\frac Kq\right\rfloor;$$

3.  the terminal-octave lower bound and a numerical partial Euler product for $C_{\mathrm{sf}}$;

4.  endpoint collapse for several nonprimitive contents;

5.  the three CRT compatibility conditions by exhaustive residue enumeration;

6.  a finite matrix $TT^*$ identity and elementary tuple counts for the same-source, near-determinant, and large-gcd channels.

The generated JSON file records all parameters and results. These checks are deterministic regression tests for exact identities. They are not numerical evidence for a prime-pair asymptotic, for [\[eq:DD\]](TPC-018.md#ref-487bb8004aa1f4fa), or for the twin-prime conjecture.

## What moved forward

###### ref-7ff5bd17005ede4e

TPC-17 ended with a sharp Möbius tail but no quadratic form adapted to that coefficient. The present paper supplies two such forms and proves that they reveal different, compatible information: $$\begin{array}{ccl}  \text{same-$k$ square}  &\Longrightarrow& q=[d_1,d_2]\le V^2\le R,  \quad\text{explicit lcm/CRT cells},\\[0.3em]  \text{$d$-first square}  &\Longrightarrow& m_i=\ell_i d_i>R,  \quad h(m_1-m_2),\ \text{generic determinant channel}.  \end{array}  \label{eq:two-quadraticizations-summary}$$ The first route also proves a coefficient no-go result: the terminal octave carries nonvanishing Gram mass. The second route removes three large but elementary degeneracies. The content split then shows that nonprimitive sectors are not simply smaller copies of the primitive problem: their interior tail cancels exactly and their target-prime terms collapse to sparse prime powers.

The remaining primitive large-determinant/low-gcd correlation is a sharper and more testable gate than the undifferentiated tail. It is also honestly hard: a uniform bound at all necessary row exponents, combined with the preceding papers, reaches the fixed-shift Hardy–Littlewood asymptotic. Future progress can therefore proceed in three falsifiable directions:

-   exploit additional factor shapes inside the generic channel without claiming coverage of the sharp tail as a whole;

-   transform the variable determinant through dispersion, reciprocity, Poisson summation, and a Kuznetsov-type spectral step, with every incomplete-period error retained;

-   first prove an averaged-shift or restricted-determinant theorem, then state precisely what extra uniformity is missing at fixed $h$.

Any one of these may yield a partial positive theorem or a rigorous no-go result. What has been gained here is a stable junction at which such alternatives no longer conceal one another. No parity barrier has been broken, but a broad unresolved packet has been reduced to two explicit and structurally different obstructions.
