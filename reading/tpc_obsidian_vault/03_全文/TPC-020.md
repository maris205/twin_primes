## Abstract

TPC-19 shows that the primitive residual correlation left by TPC-18 cannot be estimated by centering the prime–prime, prime–model, model–prime, and model–model channels separately. We give the missing common algebraic reduction and then sharpen its long-period spectral gate.

Let $P_m$ and $M_m$ be the locally calibrated prime and finite Ramanujan-model signals on a primitive affine row $mj+h$, put $H=\operatorname{rad}|h|$, and set $Z_m=P_m-M_m$. For a finite target horizon $U$, we prove the pointwise identity $$Z_m(j)=\varepsilon_U(m)+  \sum_{\substack{u\le U\\(u,mH)=1}}  b_R(u)\left(\mathbf 1_{u\mid mj+h}-\frac1u\right),  \qquad b_R(u)=-\mu(u)w_R(u),$$ where $w_R(u)>0$ is explicit. A uniform Möbius calibration theorem gives $\varepsilon_U(m)\ll_A \rho_{P,H}(m)(\log U)^{-A}$ throughout every fixed polynomial row range. Thus the full $PP-PM-MP+MM$ product has one coefficient and one centered divisor kernel, up to an arbitrarily logarithmically small scalar defect. Its primitive-conditional centered-kernel $(u,v)$-zero mode is $$\frac{b_R(u)b_R(v)}{uv}  \bigl((u,v)\mathbf 1_{(u,v)\mid m_1-m_2}-1\bigr),$$ so the zero mode of every coprime divisor pair vanishes termwise in the matched channel.

For $g=(u,v)$, $u=ga$, $v=gb$, and $l=[u,v]=gab$, we compute every Fourier coefficient of the primitive centered kernel on its true period $Hl$. When the two divisor congruences are compatible, all four phases align and the coefficient contains the exact multiplier $$B_{g,a,b}(r)=g-\mathbf 1_{b\mid r}-\mathbf 1_{a\mid r}+\mathbf 1_{l\mid r}.$$ This yields a two-scale Poisson theorem. If $H\max(u,v)\le JX^{-\eta}$, the two single-indicator spectra and the constant spectrum complete with arbitrary power accuracy even when the joint period $Hl$ is long. For all $u,v\le R=X^{1/2-\delta}$, the strict-margin range in which every single and constant spectrum completes is $Q\le X^{1/2+\delta-\eta_0}$, substantially wider than the $Q\le X^{2\delta-\eta_0}$ range obtained by completing the whole joint period at once. In the genuinely remaining low-frequency joint packet, however, $B_{g,a,b}(r)=g$; centering supplies no further nonzero-frequency cancellation.

Finally, compatible row pairs are aggregated by their joint CRT class $\kappa\bmod l$. The three inverse phases of TPC-19 compress exactly to the ordinary finite Fourier transform of a single fiber vector $Z_l(\kappa)$. Splitting that vector into its unit-class mean and discrepancy produces an explicit Ramanujan bias and an exact Parseval identity for the fiber variance. For this chosen compatible short–short packet, the remaining inputs are a bound for the explicit Ramanujan mean and a quantitative estimate for this modulus-dependent fiber discrepancy, or a norm-controlled low-rank substitute. The cited large-sieve and Kloosterman-fraction theorems do not automatically provide those inputs for the sharp Möbius row weights, and the large-divisor legs remain outside this reduction. No residual-dispersion estimate, fixed-shift Hardy–Littlewood asymptotic, twin-prime theorem, or breach of sieve parity is claimed.

# Introduction

TPC-18 reduces a failed symmetric fixed-shift tail block to a primitive, generic two-row correlation of the residual $r_R=\Lambda-\Lambda_R$, where $$\Lambda_R(n)=\sum_{q\le R}\frac{\mu(q)}{\varphi(q)}c_q(n)$$ and the row determinant is $h(m_1-m_2)$ [`\citep{WangTPC18}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-020.md#cite-4abf939102bd1649). TPC-19 then gives exact divisor and Poisson normal forms for this correlation [`\citep{WangTPC19}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-020.md#cite-09db42d5184e63ba). It also records a crucial negative fact: the centered model–model channel can have a nonzero complete-period mean on a primitive, distinct-source row pair. A proof cannot therefore estimate $$PP,\qquad PM,\qquad MP,\qquad MM$$ independently after taking absolute values. Either additional oscillation over the whole row family or a genuinely matched spectral argument is required.

The first purpose of this paper is to carry out that matching at the algebraic level. Let $P_m$ and $M_m$ denote the locally calibrated prime and finite-model signals on the row $mj+h$, and put $Z_m=P_m-M_m$. The four products above are exactly the expansion of $Z_{m_1}Z_{m_2}$. We prove that, on every finite target horizon, this difference has one common centered-divisor representation $$Z_m(j)=\varepsilon_U(m)+  \sum_u b_R(u)C_{m,u}(j),  \qquad  C_{m,u}(j)=\mathbf 1_{u\mid mj+h}-\frac1u.  \label{eq:tpc20-intro-common-kernel}$$ Here $b_R(u)=-\mu(u)w_R(u)$ is the actual residual coefficient found in TPC-19, not the coefficient of only one of the four channels. The scalar $\varepsilon_U(m)$ is not discarded formally: we prove a uniform Möbius calibration theorem which makes it arbitrarily logarithmically small for every polynomial row range. Consequently the $(u,v)$-term of the matched centered kernel has the primitive-conditional connected zero mode $$\frac{b_R(u)b_R(v)}{uv}  \left((u,v)\mathbf 1_{(u,v)\mid m_1-m_2}-1\right).  \label{eq:tpc20-intro-connected-mode}$$ The zero mode of every $(u,v)=1$ term vanishes before any estimate is made.

The second purpose is to resolve the apparent three-phase complexity of the long-lcm Poisson spectrum. Write $$g=(u,v),\qquad u=ga,\qquad v=gb,\qquad q=[u,v]=gab.$$ For compatible divisor congruences, all four centered Fourier terms align on the common period $Hq$, where $H=\operatorname{rad}|h|$. Their exact multiplier is $$B_{g,a,b}(r)  =g-\mathbf 1_{b\mid r}-\mathbf 1_{a\mid r}+\mathbf 1_{q\mid r}.  \label{eq:tpc20-intro-B}$$ This alignment has two consequences. First, the joint, two single, and constant terms should not all be completed at the longest period. The single terms have periods $Hu$ and $Hv$. Completing those shorter periods yields a strict-margin range $$Q\le X^{1/2+\delta-\eta_0}  \label{eq:tpc20-intro-new-wedge}$$ for completing all single and constant terms across $u,v\le R=X^{1/2-\delta}$, compared with $Q\le X^{2\delta-\eta_0}$ when the full joint period is completed at once. The compatible joint packet is retained. Second, on its genuinely remaining low nonzero frequencies, the multiplier in [\[eq:tpc20-intro-B\]](TPC-020.md#ref-6cd6f259dfdca582) is exactly $g$. The matched algebra has removed the spurious single spectra, but it has not created cancellation in the surviving joint packet.

The third purpose is to identify that packet without inverse-phase notation obscuring its coefficient. Compatible row residue pairs are parametrized by one unit class $\kappa\bmod q$. After aggregating the actual row weights on these fibers, the joint phase is the ordinary DFT $$S_q(r)=\sum_{\kappa\in(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^\times}  Z_q(\kappa)e_q(r\overline H\kappa).  \label{eq:tpc20-intro-fiber-dft}$$ The three inverse phases displayed in TPC-19 are precisely the CRT factorization of this one character. Splitting $Z_q$ into its unit-class mean and discrepancy gives $$S_q(r)=\overline Z_qc_q(r)+\widehat D_q(r)$$ and the exact identity $$\sum_{r\bmod q}|\widehat D_q(r)|^2  =q\sum_{\kappa\in(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^\times}|D_q(\kappa)|^2.  \label{eq:tpc20-intro-fiber-parseval}$$ Thus, for this chosen compatible short–short packet, the remaining gates are a quantitative theorem for the actual prime–Möbius row weights across CRT fibers and a separate bound for the explicit Ramanujan mean packet. The large-divisor legs $u>R$ or $v>R$ remain outside this reduction. The fiber gate is not an unspecified need for cancellation among three unrelated inverse phases.

These reductions are rigorous progress, but they do not supply the required estimates for this packet. A coefficient concentrated on one CRT fiber saturates Parseval. At the opposite extreme, a uniform fiber vector produces an explicit Ramanujan bias, and on coprime frequencies its transverse Möbius sign migrates to the determinant content. The cited bilinear and trilinear Kloosterman-fraction estimates apply only after a separable coefficient reduction, while the current fiber vector is a modulus-dependent joint array [`\citep{DukeFriedlanderIwaniec1997,BettinChandee2018,Wright2026}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-020.md#cite-63bff44e59766643); [\[BettinChandee2018\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-020.md#cite-20c5dcaa0c8d6228); [\[Wright2026\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-020.md#cite-f8666ba79358b969). Pascadi’s frequency-concentration theorem gives a powerful abstract interface once a representing measure satisfying its size and two-frequency conditions has been constructed; it does not prove those conditions for the sharp coefficient here [`\citep{Pascadi2026LargeSieve}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-020.md#cite-362d37d3b467d698).

The exact primitive conditional mean of the finite model is $$\rho_{M,H,R}(m)  =\frac{H}{\varphi(H)}\frac d{\varphi(d)},  \label{eq:tpc20-model-density}$$ while the calibrated prime local density is $$\rho_{P,H}(m)  =\frac{H}{\varphi(H)}\frac m{\varphi(m)}  =\frac{mH}{\varphi(mH)}.  \label{eq:tpc20-prime-density}$$ The second formula is a local calibration, not a prime number theorem for the growing modulus $mH$. Define $$P_m(j)=\Lambda(mj+h)-\rho_{P,H}(m),  \qquad  M_m(j)=\Lambda_R(mj+h)-\rho_{M,H,R}(m),  \label{eq:tpc20-PM-signals}$$ and $$Z_m(j)=P_m(j)-M_m(j).  \label{eq:tpc20-Z-signal}$$ TPC-19 proves the exact drift identity $$r_R(mj+h)=Z_m(j)+\delta_H(m),  \qquad  \delta_H(\ell d)  =\frac{H}{\varphi(H)}    \frac{d}{\varphi(d)(\ell-1)}.  \label{eq:tpc20-row-drift}$$ The contribution of one drift to a full primitive row-pair correlation is $O_{h,\varepsilon}(XDX^\varepsilon)$, and the two-drift contribution is $O_{h,\varepsilon}(XD L^{-1}X^\varepsilon)$. Both have a fixed power saving relative to the natural scale $$Q^2J\asymp XQ  \label{eq:tpc20-natural-scale}$$ whenever $L\ge X^{\lambda_0}$ for fixed $\lambda_0>0$.

Throughout, $F$ belongs to a uniformly bounded subset of $C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$, and its support is chosen so that the target values are positive and $n_m(j)\ll_F X$. The actual TPC-18 weights also depend smoothly on $mj/X$ and $dj/K$. Fourier inversion in logarithmic coordinates writes them as an absolutely convergent superposition of separated row and orbit weights; truncating the dual variables at a fixed power of $X$ has arbitrary power accuracy. It therefore suffices to prove the spectral identities for one separated packet with a common factor $F(j/J)$, then restore that superposition.

> **Theorem: One coefficient for all four channels** \[thm:tpc20-common-recombination\] Under the setup of [2](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-020.md#src-7b4f8c3df6f7dadc), in particular $dH\le R$, and under [\[eq:tpc20-target-horizon\]](TPC-020.md#ref-fbe0c58e0dbf27a5), one has pointwise $$\boxed{  Z_m(j)=\varepsilon_U(m)+  \sum_{\substack{u\le U\\(u,mH)=1}}  b_R(u)C_{m,u}(j).}  \label{eq:tpc20-common-recombination}$$ Consequently, with $\varepsilon_i=\varepsilon_U(m_i)$, $$\begin{aligned}  Z_{m_1}(j)Z_{m_2}(j)  ={}&\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_2  +\varepsilon_1\sum_v b_R(v)C_{m_2,v}(j)  +\varepsilon_2\sum_u b_R(u)C_{m_1,u}(j)  \notag\\  &+\sum_{u,v}b_R(u)b_R(v)  C_{m_1,u}(j)C_{m_2,v}(j),  \label{eq:tpc20-four-channel-recombined}\end{aligned}$$ where the last three sums carry the horizon and coprimality restrictions from [\[eq:tpc20-common-recombination\]](TPC-020.md#ref-3010d32dd546fe8f). > **Proposition: Connected determinant zero mode** \[prop:tpc20-common-zero-mode\] Let $(u,m_1H)=(v,m_2H)=1$, put $$g=(u,v),\qquad \Delta=m_1-m_2.$$ Then $$\boxed{  \mathbb E_H\!  \left[C_{m_1,u}C_{m_2,v}\right]  =\frac1{uv}\left(g\mathbf 1_{g\mid\Delta}-1\right).}  \label{eq:tpc20-common-zero-mode}$$ In particular, every $g=1$ zero mode in the recombined $PP-PM-MP+MM$ channel vanishes termwise. Moreover, $$g\mathbf 1_{g\mid\Delta}-1  =\sum_{r=1}^{g-1}e_g(r\Delta).  \label{eq:tpc20-determinant-spectrum}$$

> **Proof** The two congruences for $j$ are individually soluble and have conditional densities $1/u$ and $1/v$. Their joint density is $g/(uv)$ precisely when their reductions modulo $g$ agree. Primitivity makes this compatibility equivalent to $g\mid\Delta$; otherwise the joint density is zero. Expanding the two centered indicators proves [\[eq:tpc20-common-zero-mode\]](TPC-020.md#ref-289611c638f91993). The complete additive-character sum modulo $g$, with its zero frequency removed, gives [\[eq:tpc20-determinant-spectrum\]](TPC-020.md#ref-80f0db1e7a6600eb).

On squarefree support write $$u=ga,\qquad v=gb,  \qquad g,a,b\text{ pairwise coprime}.  \label{eq:tpc20-squarefree-gab}$$ Then $$b_R(ga)b_R(gb)  =\mu(a)\mu(b)w_R(ga)w_R(gb).  \label{eq:tpc20-common-sign-cancellation}$$ The determinant content $g$ is unsigned. If the TPC-18 row signs $\mu(d_1)\mu(d_2)$ are also included, the admissibility conditions imply $(a,d_1)=(b,d_2)=1$, and hence $$\mu(d_1)\mu(d_2)\mu(a)\mu(b)  =\mu(d_1a)\mu(d_2b).  \label{eq:tpc20-row-frequency-sign-fusion}$$ No coprimality between $d_1a$ and $d_2b$ is asserted: $d_1,d_2$ may intersect, as may $a,d_2$ and $b,d_1$.

# Uniform removal of the finite calibration defect

The formal identity $$\sum_{\substack{n\ge1\\(n,M)=1}}  \frac{-\mu(n)\log n}{n}  =\frac{M}{\varphi(M)}$$ is a conditionally convergent statement at $s=1$. Since the row modulus grows with $X$, a fixed-$M$ limit is not enough for [\[eq:tpc20-calibration-defect\]](TPC-020.md#ref-acdcbbf401fccad9). We prove the required uniform form.

> **Theorem: Uniform Möbius calibration** \[thm:tpc20-uniform-calibration\] For every fixed $A>0$ and $C>0$, uniformly for $$U\ge3,\qquad 1\le M\le U^C,  \label{eq:tpc20-calibration-range}$$ one has $$\boxed{  \sum_{\substack{n\le U\\(n,M)=1}}  \frac{-\mu(n)\log n}{n}  =\frac{M}{\varphi(M)}  +O_{A,C}\!\left(  \frac{M}{\varphi(M)}(\log U)^{-A}\right).}  \label{eq:tpc20-uniform-calibration}$$ Squarefreeness of $M$ is not required.

> **Proof** Let $$\mathscr S(M)=\{k\ge1:p\mid k\Longrightarrow p\mid M\},  \qquad  g_M(k)=\mathbf 1_{k\in\mathscr S(M)}.$$ Its Dirichlet series is $$G_M(s)=\sum_{k\in\mathscr S(M)}k^{-s}  =\prod_{p\mid M}(1-p^{-s})^{-1},  \qquad  G_M(1)=\frac{M}{\varphi(M)}.  \label{eq:tpc20-GM}$$ Euler factors give the exact convolution $$\mu(n)\mathbf 1_{(n,M)=1}=(\mu*g_M)(n).  \label{eq:tpc20-coprime-mobius-convolution}$$ Put $$\mathscr A(x)=\sum_{n\le x}\frac{-\mu(n)\log n}{n},  \qquad  \mathscr B(x)=\sum_{n\le x}\frac{\mu(n)}n.$$ Opening [\[eq:tpc20-coprime-mobius-convolution\]](TPC-020.md#ref-11487e8a3259faba) and using $-\log(dk)=-\log d-\log k$ gives $$\begin{aligned}  S_M(U)  :={}&\sum_{\substack{n\le U\\(n,M)=1}}  \frac{-\mu(n)\log n}{n}  \notag\\  ={}&\sum_{\substack{k\le U\\k\in\mathscr S(M)}}  \frac{\mathscr A(U/k)}k  -\sum_{\substack{k\le U\\k\in\mathscr S(M)}}  \frac{\log k}{k}\mathscr B(U/k).  \label{eq:tpc20-calibration-convolution}\end{aligned}$$ Therefore $$\begin{aligned}  S_M(U)-G_M(1)  ={}&\sum_{\substack{k\le U\\k\in\mathscr S(M)}}  \frac{\mathscr A(U/k)-1}{k}  \notag\\  &-\sum_{\substack{k\le U\\k\in\mathscr S(M)}}  \frac{\log k}{k}\mathscr B(U/k)  -\sum_{\substack{k>U\\k\in\mathscr S(M)}}\frac1k.  \label{eq:tpc20-calibration-error-split}\end{aligned}$$
>
> The classical zero-free region for $\zeta(s)$, followed by partial summation, implies that for every $B>0$ $$|\mathscr A(x)-1|+|\mathscr B(x)|  \ll_B(\log x)^{-B}\qquad(x\ge3).  \label{eq:tpc20-classical-mobius-input}$$ See, for example, [`\citet[Chapter~5]{IwaniecKowalski2004}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-020.md#cite-49dfea87eaada156). The two limiting constants follow respectively from $(1/\zeta)'(1)=1$ and $1/\zeta(1)=0$. For $x\ge1$, the crude bounds $$|\mathscr A(x)-1|\ll1+\log^2(2x),  \qquad  |\mathscr B(x)|\ll\log(2x)  \label{eq:tpc20-crude-mobius}$$ will suffice.
>
> Write $\mathscr L=\log U$ and choose $$\eta=\frac{C_0\log\mathscr L}{\mathscr L},  \qquad  P_0=\exp\!\left(\frac{\mathscr L}  {4C_0\log\mathscr L}\right),  \label{eq:tpc20-rankin-parameters}$$ where $C_0$ will be fixed at the end. We claim $$G_M(1-\eta)\ll_{C,C_0}G_M(1).  \label{eq:tpc20-Euler-ratio}$$ Indeed, $$\begin{aligned}  \log\frac{G_M(1-\eta)}{G_M(1)}  &=  \int_{1-\eta}^{1}  \sum_{p\mid M}\frac{\log p}{p^\sigma-1}\,d\sigma.\end{aligned}$$ For $p\le P_0$, the definition of $P_0$ gives $\eta\log p\le1/4$, and the contribution is $$\ll\eta\sum_{p\le P_0}\frac{\log p}{p}\ll1.$$ There are at most $\log M/\log P_0\ll_{C,C_0}\log\mathscr L$ prime divisors $p>P_0$, and their total contribution is $$\ll_{C,C_0}(\log\mathscr L)P_0^{-1+\eta}=o(1).$$ This proves [\[eq:tpc20-Euler-ratio\]](TPC-020.md#ref-bbbb0ae08c50efd3). The same split gives $$\mathcal L_M(1-\eta)  :=\sum_{p\mid M}\frac{\log p}{p^{1-\eta}-1}  \ll_{C,C_0}\mathscr L.  \label{eq:tpc20-log-Euler-factor}$$
>
> Rankin’s trick, [\[eq:tpc20-Euler-ratio\]](TPC-020.md#ref-bbbb0ae08c50efd3), and [\[eq:tpc20-log-Euler-factor\]](TPC-020.md#ref-3829a81e444f3443) now give, for $Y\ge1$, $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_{\substack{k>Y\\k\in\mathscr S(M)}}\frac1k  &\ll Y^{-\eta}G_M(1),  \label{eq:tpc20-rankin-unweighted}\\  \sum_{\substack{k>Y\\k\in\mathscr S(M)}}\frac{\log k}{k}  &\ll Y^{-\eta}G_M(1)\mathscr L.  \label{eq:tpc20-rankin-weighted}\end{aligned}$$
>
> Set $$Z=\exp(\sqrt{\mathscr L}),\qquad Y_0=U/Z.$$ For $k\le Y_0$, one has $\log(U/k)\ge\sqrt{\mathscr L}$. Applying [\[eq:tpc20-classical-mobius-input\]](TPC-020.md#ref-6162edae0369e89a) with exponent $B_0$, and using $$\sum_{k\in\mathscr S(M)}\frac{\log k}{k}  =-G_M'(1)  \ll_C G_M(1)\mathscr L,$$ bounds the two central pieces of [\[eq:tpc20-calibration-error-split\]](TPC-020.md#ref-0d7e8148f8030b78) by $$O\!\left(G_M(1)\mathscr L^{-B_0/2}\right)  +O\!\left(G_M(1)\mathscr L^{1-B_0/2}\right).  \label{eq:tpc20-central-calibration-error}$$ Moreover, $$\eta\log Y_0\ge\frac{C_0}{2}\log\mathscr L$$ for sufficiently large $U$. Equations [\[eq:tpc20-rankin-unweighted\]](TPC-020.md#ref-de9366a632bd73e9) and [\[eq:tpc20-rankin-weighted\]](TPC-020.md#ref-a49f738fd7adc48a), combined with the crude bounds [\[eq:tpc20-crude-mobius\]](TPC-020.md#ref-238c94d066201817) on $Y_0<k\le U$, bound all three tail pieces by $$O\!\left(G_M(1)\mathscr L^{3/2-C_0/2}\right).  \label{eq:tpc20-tail-calibration-error}$$ Taking $B_0=C_0=2A+8$, and enlarging the constant for bounded $U$, proves [\[eq:tpc20-uniform-calibration\]](TPC-020.md#ref-f9cb31dd4082ad25).

# The exact four-channel Fourier kernel

We first keep all four terms created by centering. The calculation is coefficient-independent: it applies both to the finite-model coefficient and to every finite short–short block of the common residual coefficient in [\[thm:tpc20-common-recombination\]](TPC-020.md#ref-61f912786f7d0000). It does not yet estimate the sum over rows or the complementary large-divisor legs.

Put $$g=(u,v),\qquad a=\frac{u}{g},\qquad b=\frac{v}{g},\qquad  q=[u,v]=gab,  \label{eq:tpc20-gabq}$$ so that $(a,b)=1$. The finite-model coefficients are squarefree supported, in which case $g,a,b$ are pairwise coprime, but the first Fourier identities below do not require squarefreeness. We assume $$(u,m_1H)=(v,m_2H)=1.  \label{eq:tpc20-primitive-moduli}$$ Since $H=\operatorname{rad}|h|$, this also makes $h$ invertible modulo $u$ and $v$. Define $$\kappa_1\equiv-h\overline{m_1}\pmod u,  \qquad  \kappa_2\equiv-h\overline{m_2}\pmod v.  \label{eq:tpc20-kappa12}$$ Every inverse below is taken in the modulus of the character in which it occurs. We use $e_s(x)=\exp(2\pi i x/s)$, with $e_1(x)=1$, and write $$c_H(r)=\sum_{c\bmod H}^{*}e_H(rc).  \label{eq:tpc20-cH}$$

> **Lemma: Primitive compatibility** \[lem:tpc20-primitive-compatibility\] The classes in [\[eq:tpc20-kappa12\]](TPC-020.md#ref-3b02f218fdf3b4d5) are compatible modulo $g$ if and only if $$g\mid m_1-m_2.  \label{eq:tpc20-compatible-determinant}$$ When this holds, they determine one class $\kappa\bmod q$, and $(\kappa,q)=1$.

> **Proof** Modulo $g$, compatibility is equivalent to $h(\overline{m_1}-\overline{m_2})\equiv0$. Since $h,m_1,m_2$ are units modulo $g$, this is equivalent to $m_2-m_1\equiv0\pmod g$. The generalized Chinese remainder theorem gives the class modulo $[u,v]=q$. Its reductions are a unit times $h$, so it is a unit modulo both $u$ and $v$, hence modulo $q$.

> **Theorem: Exact joint, single, and constant Fourier coefficients** \[thm:tpc20-four-channel-coefficients\] Let $\chi=\mathbf 1_{\{g\mid m_1-m_2\}}$. If $\chi=1$, let $\kappa\bmod q$ be the class in [\[lem:tpc20-primitive-compatibility\]](TPC-020.md#ref-f5b79d9b758c236e); when $\chi=0$, the first term below is omitted. For every $r\bmod Hq$, $$\begin{aligned}  \widehat K(r)  =\frac{c_H(r)}{Huv}\Bigg[{}  &g\chi e_q(-r\overline H\kappa)  -\mathbf 1_{\{b\mid r\}}    e_u\!\left(-\frac rb\overline H\kappa_1\right)  \notag\\  &-\mathbf 1_{\{a\mid r\}}    e_v\!\left(-\frac ra\overline H\kappa_2\right)  +\mathbf 1_{\{q\mid r\}}\Bigg].  \label{eq:tpc20-four-channel-Khat}\end{aligned}$$ In particular, $$\widehat K(0)  =\frac{\varphi(H)}{Huv}(g\chi-1).  \label{eq:tpc20-zero-mode}$$ If $\chi=1$, define $$B_{g,a,b}(r)  =g-\mathbf 1_{\{b\mid r\}}-\mathbf 1_{\{a\mid r\}}    +\mathbf 1_{\{q\mid r\}}.  \label{eq:tpc20-common-multiplier}$$ Then the four terms have a common phase and $$\boxed{  \widehat K(r)  =\frac{c_H(r)}{Huv}e_q(-r\overline H\kappa)  B_{g,a,b}(r).}  \label{eq:tpc20-compatible-Khat}$$

> **Proof** Identify a class modulo $Hq$ with $c\bmod H$ and $y\bmod q$. The additive character factors as $$e_{Hq}(-rx)  =e_H(-rc\overline q)e_q(-ry\overline H).$$ Summation over $(c,H)=1$ gives $c_H(r\overline q)=c_H(r)$, because $q$ is a unit modulo $H$. The compatible joint class therefore contributes $$\chi\frac{c_H(r)}{Hq}e_q(-r\overline H\kappa)  =\frac{c_H(r)}{Huv}g\chi e_q(-r\overline H\kappa).$$
>
> The class $y\equiv\kappa_1\pmod u$ has $b=q/u$ lifts modulo $q$. Their character sum is zero unless $b\mid r$; when $r=bs$, it is $b e_u(-s\overline H\kappa_1)$. After multiplying by the centering coefficient $-1/v$, this is the second term of [\[eq:tpc20-four-channel-Khat\]](TPC-020.md#ref-dd7dc0dfb3acf416). The other single term is identical with $u,v$ interchanged. The constant term is supported on $q\mid r$. This proves the first formula and its zero mode.
>
> Suppose now that $\chi=1$. If $b\mid r$, reduction of the joint phase modulo $u$ gives $$e_q(-r\overline H\kappa)  =e_u\!\left(-\frac rb\overline H\kappa_1\right).$$ The analogous identity holds when $a\mid r$, and the joint phase is one when $q\mid r$. Factoring it out gives [\[eq:tpc20-compatible-Khat\]](TPC-020.md#ref-159c563c0e3d98bb).

> **Proposition: Exact multiplier and kernel energy** \[prop:tpc20-multiplier-energy\] In the compatible case, $$0\le B_{g,a,b}(r)\le g  \label{eq:tpc20-B-range}$$ and $$\sum_{r\bmod q}B_{g,a,b}(r)^2  =qg^2+(1-2g)(u+v)+4g-3.  \label{eq:tpc20-B-energy}$$ Exactly $$q-u-v+g=g(a-1)(b-1)  \label{eq:tpc20-full-multiplier-count}$$ frequencies satisfy $B_{g,a,b}(r)=g$. More generally, with $\chi=\mathbf 1_{\{g\mid m_1-m_2\}}$, $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_{r\bmod Hq}|\widehat K(r)|^2  =\frac{\varphi(H)}H\Bigg[  &\frac{(u-1)(v-1)}{u^2v^2}  \notag\\  &+\frac{g\chi-1}{uv}  \left(1-\frac2u\right)\left(1-\frac2v\right)  \Bigg].  \label{eq:tpc20-kernel-energy}\end{aligned}$$

> **Proof** For $g=1$, coprimality of $a,b$ gives $$B_{1,a,b}(r)  =(1-\mathbf 1_{\{a\mid r\}})   (1-\mathbf 1_{\{b\mid r\}}).$$ For $g\ge2$, the possible values are $g,g-1,g-2$, proving [\[eq:tpc20-B-range\]](TPC-020.md#ref-501810997e08b7ce). The counts of $b\mid r$, $a\mid r$, their intersection, and $q\mid r$ modulo $q$ are respectively $u,v,g,1$. Expanding the square proves [\[eq:tpc20-B-energy\]](TPC-020.md#ref-11b4188a212c868a); inclusion-exclusion proves [\[eq:tpc20-full-multiplier-count\]](TPC-020.md#ref-45a276fe9fc309fe).
>
> For the last identity use Plancherel on $\mathbb Z/(Hq)\mathbb Z$. Conditional on $(j,H)=1$, the two divisibility events have probabilities $1/u,1/v$ and joint probability $g\chi/(uv)$. Writing a Bernoulli square as $(I-p)^2=(1-2p)I+p^2$ gives the bracket in [\[eq:tpc20-kernel-energy\]](TPC-020.md#ref-e8988d16d9cc1ee5); the primitive mask contributes the density $\varphi(H)/H$.

# Single-leg completion and the intermediate exponent wedge

The four natural lattices in [\[eq:tpc20-four-poisson-lattices\]](TPC-020.md#ref-eb895c40fdc8fb81) have different periods. This allows both single-indicator channels to be completed even when the joint period $H[u,v]$ is long.

> **Theorem: Two-scale completion** \[thm:tpc20-two-scale-completion\] Fix $\eta>0$, and let $F$ range over a uniformly bounded subset of $C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$. Suppose $$H\max(u,v)\le JX^{-\eta}.  \label{eq:tpc20-single-short-condition}$$ Then, for every $A>0$, $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}K_{u,v}^{m_1,m_2}(j)F(j/J)  ={}&J\widehat F(0)\frac{\varphi(H)}{Huv}(g\chi-1)  \notag\\  &+\chi\frac{J}{Hq}\sum_{n\ne0}  c_H(n)e_q(n\overline H\kappa)  \widehat F\!\left(\frac{nJ}{Hq}\right)  +O_{A,\eta,F}(X^{-A}),  \label{eq:tpc20-two-scale-completed}\end{aligned}$$ where $q=[u,v]$, $\chi=\mathbf 1_{\{g\mid m_1-m_2\}}$, and the second line is absent when $\chi=0$. The error remains arbitrarily power-saving after any fixed polynomial number of row and finite-model divisor sums carrying polynomially bounded weights.

> **Proof** In [\[eq:tpc20-four-poisson-lattices\]](TPC-020.md#ref-eb895c40fdc8fb81), every nonzero frequency of the $u$-single channel has Fourier argument of size at least $J/(Hu)\ge X^\eta$, and similarly for the $v$-single channel. The constant channel has the still shorter period $H$. Repeated integration by parts removes all their nonzero frequencies with arbitrary power saving. Their three zero modes add to $-J\widehat F(0)\varphi(H)/(Huv)$. The joint zero mode is $J\widehat F(0)\varphi(H)g\chi/(Huv)$, and its nonzero frequencies are retained. Choosing the number of integrations after the fixed polynomial multiplicity proves the last assertion.

> **Corollary: Low-frequency joint packet** \[cor:tpc20-low-frequency-joint\] Under the hypotheses of [\[thm:tpc20-two-scale-completion\]](TPC-020.md#ref-32a49c1bad3627f2), fix $0<\epsilon<\eta$. The joint remainder in [\[eq:tpc20-two-scale-completed\]](TPC-020.md#ref-8838d29b9c5886f9) may be truncated to $$0<|n|\le N_{q,\epsilon},  \qquad  N_{q,\epsilon}=X^\epsilon\frac{Hq}{J},  \label{eq:tpc20-dual-length}$$ with arbitrary power error. Whenever this range is nonempty, $$|n|<a,\qquad |n|<b,  \label{eq:tpc20-low-frequency-transverse}$$ for all sufficiently large $X$. Consequently the compatible common-lattice multiplier satisfies $$B_{g,a,b}(n)=g  \qquad(0<|n|\le N_{q,\epsilon}).  \label{eq:tpc20-low-frequency-Bg}$$

> **Proof** For $|n|>N_{q,\epsilon}$, the Fourier argument in the joint sum has size greater than $X^\epsilon$, so rapid decay of $\widehat F$ gives the truncation. Since $q/b=u$ and $q/a=v$, $$\frac{N_{q,\epsilon}}b  =X^\epsilon\frac{Hu}{J}\le X^{\epsilon-\eta},  \qquad  \frac{N_{q,\epsilon}}a  =X^\epsilon\frac{Hv}{J}\le X^{\epsilon-\eta}.$$ Thus a nonzero retained integer is divisible by neither $a$ nor $b$. > **Proposition: Sharp boundary of smooth single-leg completion** \[prop:tpc20-single-boundary-nogo\] Polynomial separation $Hu\le JX^{-\eta}$ is the sufficient condition used above. # CRT-fiber compression and the exact remaining gate

We now compress the compatible joint inverse phases. Throughout this section the row and orbit weights have first been separated as in the smooth-separation step of the TPC interface, so that the same function $F(j/J)$ occurs for every row pair in one packet. The separated row weights are absorbed into coefficients $z_{m_1,m_2}$. The argument is exact for each separated packet; the usual rapidly convergent superposition may be restored afterwards.

Fix $u,v$ with $(uv,H)=1$, put $q=[u,v]$, and let $\mathscr R_{u,v}$ be any finite set of row pairs such that $(u,m_1)=(v,m_2)=1$ for every $(m_1,m_2)\in\mathscr R_{u,v}$, including any generic pruning independent of $j$. Retain only the compatible pairs $g\mid m_1-m_2$.

> **Lemma: Compatible row residues are CRT fibers** \[lem:tpc20-row-fiber-bijection\] The map which assigns to a compatible row pair its joint class $\kappa\bmod q$ takes values in $$\mathcal U_q=(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^{\times}.  \label{eq:tpc20-unit-fibers}$$ Conversely, a class $\kappa\in\mathcal U_q$ determines the unique compatible pair of row residue classes $$m_1\equiv-h\overline\kappa\pmod u,  \qquad  m_2\equiv-h\overline\kappa\pmod v.  \label{eq:tpc20-row-residue-from-kappa}$$ Thus compatible pairs of row residue classes are in bijection with $\mathcal U_q$.

> **Proof** The forward statement is [\[lem:tpc20-primitive-compatibility\]](TPC-020.md#ref-f5b79d9b758c236e). Conversely, [\[eq:tpc20-row-residue-from-kappa\]](TPC-020.md#ref-8437fdf17137e9ed) makes $m_i\kappa\equiv-h$ in the required modulus. The two resulting classes are units and are compatible by construction. Inversion modulo $u$ and $v$ shows uniqueness.

> **Theorem: Triple inverse phases compress to one DFT** \[thm:tpc20-fiber-DFT\] The sum over compatible row pairs of the joint Poisson phase is exactly $S_{u,v}(r)$. If $u$ and $v$ are squarefree, as they are on both the $\lambda'_R$- and $b_R$-supports, then in the coordinates $u=ga$, $v=gb$ the integers $g,a,b$ are pairwise coprime and each summand may equivalently be written as $$\begin{aligned}  e_q(r\overline H\kappa)  ={}&e_g\!\left(-hr\overline H\,\overline{m_1}\,                  \overline{ab}\right)  e_a\!\left(-hr\overline H\,\overline{m_1}\,                  \overline{gb}\right)  \notag\\  &\times  e_b\!\left(-hr\overline H\,\overline{m_2}\,                  \overline{ga}\right).  \label{eq:tpc20-three-phase-factorization}\end{aligned}$$ with a modulus-one factor interpreted as $1$. Thus the three displayed inverse phases are the CRT factorization of one ordinary Fourier character, not three independent spectra. Moreover, $$\boxed{  \sum_{r\bmod q}|S_{u,v}(r)|^2  =q\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal U_q}|Z_{u,v}(\kappa)|^2.}  \label{eq:tpc20-fiber-parseval}$$

> **Proof** The first assertion follows by grouping the row pairs according to the bijection in [\[lem:tpc20-row-fiber-bijection\]](TPC-020.md#ref-f66a7a5bb42fcf5f). Under the stated squarefree hypothesis, the factorization in [\[eq:tpc20-three-phase-factorization\]](TPC-020.md#ref-17bf25346477f57b) is CRT factorization of the character modulo the pairwise coprime moduli $g,a,b$, followed by $m_i\kappa\equiv-h$ in the appropriate factors. Finally, $r\mapsto r\overline H$ permutes the frequencies modulo $q$, so ordinary finite-group Parseval proves [\[eq:tpc20-fiber-parseval\]](TPC-020.md#ref-1abb5b6627cb8ecb).

> **Theorem: Ramanujan mean and fiber discrepancy** \[thm:tpc20-mean-discrepancy\] Put $$\overline Z_q  =\frac1{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal U_q}Z_q(\kappa),  \qquad  D_q(\kappa)=Z_q(\kappa)-\overline Z_q.  \label{eq:tpc20-fiber-mean-discrepancy}$$ Then $$S_q(r)  =\overline Z_q c_q(r)  +\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal U_q}  D_q(\kappa)e_q(r\overline H\kappa),  \label{eq:tpc20-DFT-mean-split}$$ and $$\boxed{  \sum_{r\bmod q}  |S_q(r)-\overline Z_qc_q(r)|^2  =q\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal U_q}|D_q(\kappa)|^2.}  \label{eq:tpc20-discrepancy-parseval}$$ Since $(H,q)=1$, the deterministic part of [\[eq:tpc20-long-q-packet\]](TPC-020.md#ref-2995e68e56e8e714) is the one-modulus Ramanujan packet $$\mathcal L_q^{\mathrm{mean}}  =\overline Z_q\frac{J}{Hq}\sum_{r\ne0}  c_{Hq}(r)\widehat F\!\left(\frac{rJ}{Hq}\right).  \label{eq:tpc20-Ramanujan-mean-packet}$$

> **Proposition: Restricted-frequency fiber bound** \[prop:tpc20-restricted-fiber-bound\] Fix $\epsilon>0$ and suppose $$J\ge 4HX^\epsilon.  \label{eq:tpc20-no-frequency-wrap}$$ Write $\mathcal L_{q,\le N}^{\mathrm{disc}}$ for the discrepancy part of [\[eq:tpc20-long-q-packet\]](TPC-020.md#ref-2995e68e56e8e714) truncated at $$0<|r|\le N_{q,\epsilon}=X^\epsilon\frac{Hq}{J},  \label{eq:tpc20-fiber-window}$$ Then $$\boxed{  |\mathcal L_{q,\le N}^{\mathrm{disc}}|  \ll_{H,F,\epsilon}  X^{\epsilon/2}\sqrt{\frac JH}  \left(\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal U_q}|D_q(\kappa)|^2\right)^{1/2}}  \label{eq:tpc20-restricted-fiber-bound}$$ and, for every $A>0$, $$\mathcal L_q^{\mathrm{disc}}  =\mathcal L_{q,\le N}^{\mathrm{disc}}  +O_{A,F,H,\epsilon}\!\left(  X^{-A}\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal U_q}|D_q(\kappa)|\right).  \label{eq:tpc20-fiber-tail-error}$$ Let $\mathcal Q$ be a family of long denominators and assume, for some fixed $C_0$, the explicit polynomial mass bound $$\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q}\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal U_q}  |D_q(\kappa)|\ll X^{C_0}.  \label{eq:tpc20-fiber-tail-mass}$$ Then a sufficient discrepancy input is $$\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q}  \left(\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal U_q}|D_q(\kappa)|^2\right)^{1/2}  \ll X^{-\epsilon}\frac{XQ}{\sqrt J}(\log X)^{-B},  \label{eq:tpc20-fiber-gate}$$ with the harmless power of $X^\epsilon$ adjusted as needed. ###### ref-a7d5ab410abe3f0b

> **Proposition: Sharp algebraic no-go for arbitrary fibers** \[prop:tpc20-fiber-nogo\] CRT compression alone implies neither uniform pointwise decay of the restricted discrepancy transform nor a restricted-frequency saving for arbitrary fiber vectors. Indeed, if an admissible coefficient vector is supported on one class $\kappa_0\in\mathcal U_q$, say $Z_q=A\mathbf 1_{\{\kappa_0\}}$, then $$|S_q(r)|=|Z_q(\kappa_0)|  \qquad\text{for every }r.  \label{eq:tpc20-one-fiber-saturation}$$ Moreover its mean-zero discrepancy transform is $$\widehat D_q(r)  =A\left(  e_q(r\overline H\kappa_0)-\frac{c_q(r)}{\varphi(q)}  \right).  \label{eq:tpc20-one-fiber-discrepancy}$$ If $\varphi(q)>1$ and $(r,q)=1$, then $$|\widehat D_q(r)|  \ge |A|\left(1-\frac1{\varphi(q)}\right).  \label{eq:tpc20-one-fiber-discrepancy-lower}$$ Thus a nonempty retained window, which contains the coprime frequency $r=1$, can still carry order-$|A|$ discrepancy. Its uncentered Fourier energy is exactly $q|A|^2$, distributed uniformly over all $q$ frequencies. A useful improvement of [\[eq:tpc20-restricted-fiber-bound\]](TPC-020.md#ref-4121697f3f146af5) requires an arithmetic bound for the actual row-fiber discrepancy, or a separable low-rank decomposition whose coefficient cost is controlled.

> **Proof** For a one-fiber vector, $S_q(r)=Z_q(\kappa_0)e_q(r\overline H\kappa_0)$. This proves [\[eq:tpc20-one-fiber-saturation\]](TPC-020.md#ref-5f67cbb8758c7fa0) and equality in the corresponding Parseval identity. Subtracting the unit-class mean gives [\[eq:tpc20-one-fiber-discrepancy\]](TPC-020.md#ref-604feadd3a6c02c7). When $(r,q)=1$, $c_q(r)=\mu(q)$, so the reverse triangle inequality proves [\[eq:tpc20-one-fiber-discrepancy-lower\]](TPC-020.md#ref-a7d5ab410abe3f0b).

###### ref-1affd8084ca4c868

> **Remark: Why a generic additive large sieve does not close the gate** \[rem:tpc20-generic-large-sieve-nogo\] For denominators $q\asymp Q_0$, the relevant dual length is $N\asymp HQ_0/J$, whereas generic Farey spacing costs $Q_0^2$. Thus the standard large-sieve constant $N+Q_0^2$ is dominated by $Q_0^2$ in the present low-frequency regime. Likewise, existing bilinear or trilinear Kloosterman bounds require a separated coefficient array and do not accept an arbitrary modulus-dependent vector $Z_q(\kappa)$. The exact new gate is therefore a quantitative equidistribution or controlled-rank theorem for the actual prime-Möbius row fibers, together with control or matching of the explicit Ramanujan mean packet.

###### ref-d948de6452b65671

> **Remark: Scope of the compression** \[rem:tpc20-fiber-scope\] The compression theorem applies to a separated compatible joint packet. In the intermediate range of [\[cor:tpc20-whole-single-range\]](TPC-020.md#ref-cb5ecfc4aac8ea5d), it describes the only nonzero spectrum left in the chosen short–short block after single-leg completion. It does not by itself estimate that packet, control the large-divisor legs of the common residual coefficient, or prove the all-modulus primitive residual-dispersion target.

# Technology interfaces and the coefficient gate

TPC-18 produces the primitive two-row residual correlation, and TPC-19 separates its direct determinant-frequency and long-lcm Poisson interfaces [`\citep{WangTPC18,WangTPC19}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-020.md#cite-4abf939102bd1649); [\[WangTPC19\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-020.md#cite-09db42d5184e63ba). The results above improve the second interface in two exact ways: the four centered channels are placed on one Fourier lattice before estimation, and the compatible triple inverse phase is compressed to the single fiber transform [\[eq:tpc20-aggregated-DFT\]](TPC-020.md#ref-edfc919fcf1c1421). This section records how several cited estimates interface with those reductions, and which hypothesis on the chosen prime–Möbius fiber vector is still missing.

## The frequency-concentration interface

###### ref-6491c26b7f035ecb

###### ref-99635f902315c0d3

###### ref-3c62117af43480f8

###### ref-f0331485f04b3d38

A particularly flexible input relevant here is the exceptional-spectrum large sieve of [`\citet{Pascadi2026LargeSieve}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-020.md#cite-362d37d3b467d698). Put $\mathbb T=\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$. In its notation, for $\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb T$, put $$T_N(\alpha,\beta)  =\min_{t\in\mathbb Z_{\ge1}}\bigl(t+N\lVert t\alpha\rVert_{\mathbb T}                          +N\lVert t\beta\rVert_{\mathbb T}\bigr),  \qquad  \mathcal I_N(\nu)  =\iint T_N(\alpha,\beta)\,        d|\nu|(\alpha)d|\nu|(\beta).  \label{eq:tpc20-Pascadi-functional}$$ Theorem 5.2 of that paper treats a coefficient $a_n=f(n/N)\check\nu(n)$. Suppressing its standard smooth-cutoff, parameter, and cusp-normalization hypotheses, the theorem gives $$\sum_{\lambda_j<1/4}X^{2\theta_j}  \left|\sum_{n\asymp N}a_n\rho_{j\mathfrak a}(a_0n)\right|^2  \ll_\epsilon  (qa_0NX)^{2\epsilon}  \left(1+\frac{a_0N}{q}\right)\mathcal A^2,  \label{eq:tpc20-Pascadi-bound}$$ provided, in particular, $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal A  &\gg \lVert a\rVert_2  +\frac{\sqrt{(a_0,q)}N}{\sqrt{q+a_0N}}|\nu|(\mathbb T),  \label{eq:tpc20-Pascadi-size-gate}\\  X  &\ll \max\!\left(1,\frac q{a_0N}\right)        \max\!\left(1,\frac{\mathcal A^2}                            {\mathcal I_N(\nu)}\right).  \label{eq:tpc20-Pascadi-concentration-gate}\end{aligned}$$ Thus this result is an interface theorem, not a black-box estimate for an arbitrary determinant sequence. Before it can be used here one must derive one common spectral coefficient from the matched four-channel kernel, identify its spectral level $q$, length $N$, scalar $a_0$, and exceptional parameter $X$, construct a representing measure $\nu$, and verify both [\[eq:tpc20-Pascadi-size-gate\]](TPC-020.md#ref-3c62117af43480f8) and [\[eq:tpc20-Pascadi-concentration-gate\]](TPC-020.md#ref-f0331485f04b3d38). In particular, the spectral level called $q$ in [\[eq:tpc20-Pascadi-bound\]](TPC-020.md#ref-99635f902315c0d3) must not be identified with either the row scale or the fiber modulus merely because the same letter is traditionally used. Applying the theorem separately to the joint, two single, and constant channels is not a substitute: the intervening triangle inequalities would discard the matching encoded by [\[eq:tpc20-compatible-Khat\]](TPC-020.md#ref-159c563c0e3d98bb). The coefficient presented to the large sieve must be the combined coefficient whenever more than one channel survives on the block under consideration.

###### ref-b190d23e6c0ebf7b

###### ref-b1ed4f73c8f9c71a

There is a sharp positive benchmark. Theorem 1.7 of [`\citet{Pascadi2026LargeSieve}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-020.md#cite-362d37d3b467d698) applies to the smooth difference coefficient $$\sum_{h_1\ell_1-h_2\ell_2=n}  \Phi_1(h_1/H_0)\Phi_2(h_2/H_0)  e(h_1\alpha_1+h_2\alpha_2),$$ subject to its stated spectral-level and parameter conditions, including the regime in which the level is at least of order $L^2$. It does not cover the sharp coefficient isolated in TPC-19, where $\omega_D$ is a $1$-bounded smooth cutoff supported on $d\asymp D$: $$A_{\ell_1,\ell_2}(n)  =\sum_{\ell_1d_1-\ell_2d_2=n}  \mu(d_1)\mu(d_2)\omega_D(d_1)\omega_D(d_2).  \label{eq:tpc20-sharp-determinant-coefficient}$$ Indeed, if $$M_\ell(\alpha)  =\sum_d\mu(d)\omega_D(d)e(-\ell d\alpha),$$ then $\widehat A_{\ell_1,\ell_2} =M_{\ell_1}\overline{M_{\ell_2}}$. The natural representing measure has density $\widehat A_{\ell_1,\ell_2}$, and hence total variation $$V_{\ell_1,\ell_2}  =\int_{\mathbb T}|M_{\ell_1}(\alpha)M_{\ell_2}(\alpha)|\,d\alpha  \ll D  \label{eq:tpc20-natural-measure-variation}$$ by Parseval and Cauchy–Schwarz. Indeed, $\lVert M_\ell\rVert_2^2\le\sum_{d\asymp D}|\omega_D(d)|^2\ll D$. In the formal smooth-benchmark scaling $N\asymp LD$, spectral level $q\asymp L^2$, $a_0=1$, and $L>2D$, retaining $\mathcal A=D^{1+o(1)}$ would already require $V_{\ell_1,\ell_2}=D^{o(1)}$, together with the independent two-frequency condition [\[eq:tpc20-Pascadi-concentration-gate\]](TPC-020.md#ref-f0331485f04b3d38). Neither estimate follows from [\[eq:tpc20-natural-measure-variation\]](TPC-020.md#ref-b1ed4f73c8f9c71a).

Davenport’s uniform Möbius exponential-sum estimate, in the form recalled by [`\citet{GreenTao2008}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-020.md#cite-9da5e354dbf34af1), gives arbitrary logarithmic saving in $\sup_\alpha|\sum_{d\leq D}\mu(d)e(d\alpha)|$. After partial summation it also handles a standard smooth dyadic cutoff. This is a one-frequency $L^\infty$ statement. It does not bound the product variation in [\[eq:tpc20-natural-measure-variation\]](TPC-020.md#ref-b1ed4f73c8f9c71a) at the scale required above and does not control the two-frequency functional $\mathcal I_N$. Consequently it does not verify Pascadi’s two gates for [\[eq:tpc20-sharp-determinant-coefficient\]](TPC-020.md#ref-b190d23e6c0ebf7b).

## What one-fraction estimates do and do not import

The classical bilinear estimate of [`\citet{DukeFriedlanderIwaniec1997}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-020.md#cite-63bff44e59766643) controls a single Kloosterman fraction $e(a\overline m/n)$ with a separated coefficient $\alpha_m\beta_n$. The trilinear estimate of [`\citet{BettinChandee2018}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-020.md#cite-20c5dcaa0c8d6228) admits a further separated coefficient $\nu_a$, and the recent preprint [`\citet{Wright2026}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-020.md#cite-f8666ba79358b969) treats a useful partially fixed denominator while retaining the same essential one-fraction and separability requirements. The latter is presently a version-sensitive, non-peer-reviewed preprint, so only its explicitly stated current hypotheses may be used. These results can therefore be invoked on a dyadic slice only after the slice has been reduced to their precise phase and coefficient forms and all of their size hypotheses have been checked. They are not estimates for an arbitrary two-dimensional array indexed by a modulus and a residue class.

The compression in [\[thm:tpc20-fiber-DFT\]](TPC-020.md#ref-add0f8b77c628424) supplies the needed single phase, but not the needed coefficient separation. After aggregation, the relevant vector is $$(q,\kappa)\longmapsto D_q(\kappa),  \qquad \kappa\in\mathcal U_q.$$ Replacing $\kappa$ by its inverse may display the phase as one Kloosterman fraction, but it simultaneously replaces the coefficient by $D_q(\overline m)$; the joint dependence on $q$ and $m$ remains. Likewise, Theorem 1 of [`\citet{BlomerMilicevic2015}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-020.md#cite-4d5493ad647c9b5a) averages complete Kloosterman sums over their modulus $c$, with a residue weight modulo $q_0$, a smooth $c/X$ weight, and a cost measured by the $\ell^1$-norm of the $q_0$-Mellin transform of that residue weight. It is not a direct estimate for the one-modulus fiber transform $S_q(r)$. The identity [\[eq:tpc20-aggregated-DFT\]](TPC-020.md#ref-edfc919fcf1c1421) alone does not convert the fiber vector into that weighted-modulus average. None of the cited results is a separate *long-lcm Poisson spectrum* theorem: Poisson summation gives the exact normal form, while the arithmetic estimate of its coefficient array is an additional step.

###### ref-f22a417411fbc132

The obstruction may be quantified. On a dyadic modulus block $q\asymp Q_0$, choose representatives $0\leq\kappa<2Q_0$, extend $D_q(\kappa)$ by zero outside $\kappa\in\mathcal U_q$, and define the separable projective cost $$\mathfrak R_\pi(D;Q_0)  =\inf_{D_q(\kappa)=\sum_\nu x_\nu(q)y_\nu(\kappa)}    \sum_\nu\lVert x_\nu\rVert_2\lVert y_\nu\rVert_2.  \label{eq:tpc20-projective-rank-cost}$$ A direct termwise use of a separated bilinear or trilinear theorem pays the decomposition cost in [\[eq:tpc20-projective-rank-cost\]](TPC-020.md#ref-f22a417411fbc132). Small Hilbert-space norm or Parseval energy does not by itself imply a sufficiently small projective cost. Thus “one phase” is a necessary structural reduction, whereas controlled projective rank is an additional arithmetic input.

## A defensible next gate

###### ref-dbe72042340aaec5

For a family $\mathcal Q$ of long denominators, set $$\mathfrak V(\mathcal Q)  =\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q}q    \sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal U_q}|D_q(\kappa)|^2.  \label{eq:tpc20-total-fiber-variance}$$ By [\[eq:tpc20-discrepancy-parseval\]](TPC-020.md#ref-e6523aeed8226587), this is exactly the complete frequency energy of the discrepancy transforms. It is not yet a restricted-frequency saving: the one-fiber example in [\[prop:tpc20-fiber-nogo\]](TPC-020.md#ref-3cd90564fcd9f718) retains an order-one coprime-frequency discrepancy after its mean is removed. The direct next proof obligation is therefore the weighted $\ell^1_q\ell^2_\kappa$ estimate stated in [\[eq:tpc20-fiber-gate\]](TPC-020.md#ref-d32747beccf002ef), or a stronger variance estimate that implies it with a fixed margin. This route uses the exact restricted-frequency bound in [\[prop:tpc20-restricted-fiber-bound\]](TPC-020.md#ref-0c8a121b9b67703e) and requires no claim that any cited Kloosterman theorem accepts the fiber array.

An alternative low-rank route must do all three of the following on each dyadic block: exhibit an explicit decomposition in [\[eq:tpc20-projective-rank-cost\]](TPC-020.md#ref-f22a417411fbc132); substitute the actual block parameters into one specified theorem of [`\citet{DukeFriedlanderIwaniec1997}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-020.md#cite-63bff44e59766643), [`\citet{BettinChandee2018}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-020.md#cite-20c5dcaa0c8d6228), or [`\citet{Wright2026}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-020.md#cite-f8666ba79358b969); and prove that the imported bound multiplied by the full decomposition cost is below the TPC-18 obstruction scale with a fixed margin. Low rank without this norm and parameter ledger is not a valid transfer theorem. In either route the explicit Ramanujan mean packet [\[eq:tpc20-Ramanujan-mean-packet\]](TPC-020.md#ref-73e71b9b6b118d98) remains a separate term; fiber discrepancy estimates do not cancel it.

Finally, no estimate in this paper is taken from the January 2026 withdrawn preprint entitled *Bilinear forms with Kloosterman fractions and applications*. Its withdrawal notice reports a missing factor in the claimed bound, and it is intentionally given no bibliographic entry here. The unconditional boundary is therefore as follows: the results audited above supply rigorous smooth and separated benchmarks, while neither the sharp frequency-concentration gates nor the actual fiber-variance/projective-rank gate is proved here or supplied by those results. In particular, the results above do not establish cancellation of the full TPC-18 residual, a fixed-shift prime-pair asymptotic, a twin-prime lower bound, or a breach of the sieve parity barrier.

# Finite dynamics, exact certificate, and conclusion

###### ref-a7bf559bf804b967

The word *spectrum* in this paper has a finite and literal dynamical meaning. For each divisor pair, translation $$\tau:j\longmapsto j+1  \qquad\text{on }\mathbb Z/(H[u,v])\mathbb Z  \label{eq:tpc20-cyclic-shift}$$ is a finite measure-preserving dynamical system. Its Koopman characters are $e_{H[u,v]}(rj)$, and the coefficients computed in [\[thm:tpc20-four-channel-coefficients\]](TPC-020.md#ref-81c245b52b107a9b) are exactly the spectral coordinates of the centered local divisor observable. The Poisson step couples these finite cyclic spectra to the smooth orbit window, while [\[thm:tpc20-fiber-DFT\]](TPC-020.md#ref-add0f8b77c628424) reorganizes the compatible row weights on a single CRT fiber. This terminology does not assert a spectral realization of the zeros of the Riemann zeta function.

## Conclusion

TPC-20 supplies the matched reduction that TPC-19 left open. The prime–prime, prime–model, model–prime, and model–model channels combine pointwise into one centered divisor kernel with one residual coefficient. A uniform Möbius theorem makes the finite calibration defect arbitrarily logarithmically small throughout polynomial row ranges, and [\[cor:tpc20-calibration-negligible\]](TPC-020.md#ref-5eb6a022ab6e6820) makes it negligible at packet scale under its explicit packet hypotheses, which hold for the separated packets in the single-leg range. The resulting primitive-conditional zero mode is connected, and the zero mode of every coprime divisor pair vanishes before any triangle inequality is applied.

The exact Fourier calculation then separates genuinely different period scales. Completing the two single legs at periods $Hu$ and $Hv$, rather than artificially at $H[u,v]$, identifies uniform single-leg completion up to $Q\le X^{1/2+\delta-\eta_0}$, beyond the older whole-joint-period completion range $Q\le X^{2\delta-\eta_0}$. This removes two spurious long spectra. It does not cancel the surviving joint packet: on its low nonzero frequencies the aligned multiplier equals $g=(u,v)$.

Finally, the apparent triple inverse phase of TPC-19 is one ordinary DFT after compatible row pairs are grouped by their joint CRT class. The transform splits exactly into a Ramanujan mean packet and a fiber discrepancy, with Parseval identifying the full discrepancy energy. For this chosen compatible short–short packet, the remaining obligations are concrete: prove the weighted restricted-frequency fiber gate (or an explicitly normed low-rank substitute) for the actual sharp Möbius row weights, and separately control the Ramanujan mean. The cited separated Kloosterman-fraction and frequency-concentration results do not automatically provide either input. The large-divisor legs remain outside this reduction.

Thus this paper advances the route by an exact common-channel compression, a wider unconditional single-leg completion range, and a sharply formulated coefficient gate. It does not estimate the full TPC-18 residual, prove a fixed-shift Hardy–Littlewood asymptotic or a twin-prime lower bound, breach the sieve parity barrier, or realize zeta zeros as dynamical eigenvalues.
