## Abstract

TPC-20 compresses the compatible short–short part of a primitive Möbius residual correlation into one finite Fourier transform on each unit fiber modulo $q$. Its exact mean–discrepancy split leaves two apparently separate analytic obligations. We close the mean obligation and replace the remaining arbitrary-fiber formulation by two exact normal forms that retain the row provenance.

Put $H=\operatorname{rad}|h|$, $QJ\asymp X$, and let $F\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$. The Ramanujan mean packet at modulus $q$ is exactly $$\overline Z_q\left\{  \sum_{(j,Hq)=1}F(j/J)  -J\frac{\varphi(Hq)}{Hq}\widehat F(0)\right\}.$$ The expression in braces is $O_F(\tau(Hq))$, uniformly even when $Hq\asymp J$. Under the standard separated-packet bound $\mathcal Z_1\ll Q^2X^\varepsilon$, absolute summation over every short–short divisor pair gives $O_\varepsilon(Q^2X^\varepsilon)$; this scale is $\asymp XQJ^{-1}X^\varepsilon$. Hence throughout the strict single-leg range of TPC-20 the complete Ramanujan mean has a fixed power saving and is negligible with arbitrary logarithmic saving.

The actual row geometry also gives the unconditional occupancy bound $$\lVert D_q\rVert_{2,\mathcal U_q}\ll_\varepsilon Q^2q^{-1/2}X^\varepsilon.$$ On $q\asymp Y$, however, its insertion into the restricted-frequency estimate saves only when $Y<J$ by a fixed power. Thus occupancy alone recovers the already-completable joint-period range and does not enter the genuinely long regime.

For a rank-one row packet, lcm aggregation is a divisor-lattice Möbius difference of cumulative row-fiber products. For the actual generic Schur mask, multiplicative Fourier inversion gives a sharper normal form: the nonprincipal fiber energy is exactly a shared-$g$ convolution of masked two-row Dirichlet-character sums, where $g=(u,v)$. In the unmasked case these sums factor into one-row Dirichlet polynomials; in the generic case the determinant and gcd mask remains inside a connected fourth moment.

We finally prove that scalar degenerate removal cannot be moved through the nonlinear fiber $L^2$ norm merely by citing its scalar saving. A single row diagonal produces exactly the one-fiber spike of TPC-20 and saturates every nonprincipal multiplicative frequency relative to its coefficient amplitude, although its complete scalar correlation is power-saving. The correct next input is therefore a masked shared-factor character fourth-moment estimate, not an unpruned Gram norm. We do not prove the short–short discrepancy estimate, control the large-divisor legs, breach sieve parity, or obtain a fixed-shift prime-pair asymptotic.

# Introduction

The symmetric Möbius tail isolated in TPC-17 is reduced in TPC-18 to a primitive two-row residual correlation. After diagonal, same-source, near-determinant, and large-gcd pairs are removed by elementary counting, a failed block forces a generic correlation at the natural scale $XQ$ [`\citep{WangTPC18}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-021.md#cite-4abf939102bd1649). TPC-19 gives exact divisor and Poisson normal forms for that correlation, but its four locally centered channels cannot be estimated independently without losing their matching [`\citep{WangTPC19}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-021.md#cite-09db42d5184e63ba). TPC-20 performs the matching first. In the strict single-leg range it completes the two one-divisor spectra and compresses the remaining compatible short–short joint spectrum into one ordinary finite Fourier transform on $\mathcal U_q=(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^\times$ [`\citep{WangTPC20}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-021.md#cite-7525f88be3fcfa5f).

###### ref-f9fafd4613f5bb88

The last compression has the exact split $$S_q(r)=\overline Z_q\,c_q(r)+\widehat D_q(r),  \qquad  \sum_{r\bmod q}|\widehat D_q(r)|^2  =q\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal U_q}|D_q(\kappa)|^2.  \label{eq:tpc21-intro-mean-discrepancy}$$ TPC-20 deliberately leaves both terms on the right as analytic inputs. The purpose of the present paper is to decide which part is elementary, which part has additional row structure, and which part is still a new arithmetic estimate.

## The mean packet closes absolutely

###### ref-eef3dc442ccf5caf

###### ref-4abc7ad8893b430e

###### ref-26ac382832d33cc8

Our first result removes one of the two inputs. For $M\ge1$, define $$\Delta_M(F;J)  =\frac JM\sum_{r\ne0}c_M(r)    \widehat F\!\left(\frac{rJ}{M}\right).  \label{eq:tpc21-intro-delta}$$ Reduced-class Poisson summation and ordinary Möbius inversion give $$\Delta_M(F;J)  =\sum_{(j,M)=1}F(j/J)   -J\frac{\varphi(M)}M\widehat F(0),  \qquad  |\Delta_M(F;J)|\ll_F\tau(M).  \label{eq:tpc21-intro-delta-bound}$$ The estimate is uniform through the transition $M\asymp J$; it is a bounded smooth lattice discrepancy, not a long-modulus prime theorem. The TPC-20 mean packet is exactly $\overline Z_q\Delta_{Hq}(F;J)$. Under the standard separated-packet bound $\mathcal Z_1\ll Q^2X^\varepsilon$, summing it absolutely over all short–short lcm layers gives $$\sum_q|\mathcal L_q^{\rm mean}|  \ll_\varepsilon Q^2X^\varepsilon,  \qquad  Q^2X^\varepsilon\asymp\frac{XQ}{J}X^\varepsilon.  \label{eq:tpc21-intro-mean-closure}$$ Thus the mean is power-saving throughout the strict single-leg range. No cancellation between moduli is used.

## What row occupancy proves, and where it stops

###### ref-95f6e6f59fda2936

###### ref-5e1cd4ce730d67e2

The fiber vector is not completely arbitrary. Because $u\le R<L/2$, fixing $d$ and a row class modulo $u$ confines the prime source $\ell$ to one progression containing $O(L/u)$ integers. Consequently each fiber contains at most $O(Q/u)$ rows on the $u$-side. This gives the unconditional estimate $$\lVert D_q\rVert_{2,\mathcal U_q}  \ll_\varepsilon\frac{Q^2}{\sqrt q}X^\varepsilon.  \label{eq:tpc21-intro-occupancy}$$ For $q\asymp Y$, insertion into the TPC-20 restricted-frequency inequality produces $$\sum_{q\asymp Y}|\mathcal L_q^{\rm disc}|  \ll_\varepsilon Q^2\sqrt{JY}\,X^\varepsilon.  \label{eq:tpc21-intro-occupancy-scalar}$$ Relative to $XQ=Q^2J$, this saves a fixed power only when $Y<J$ by a fixed power. That is precisely the region where the whole joint period is already short. Occupancy improves the abstract one-fiber bound, but it does not cross the long-lcm boundary.

## Two provenance-preserving normal forms

###### ref-c89202d344b233a0

###### ref-e6bd913aa1f6cc30

The original $TT^*$ row weights have rank-one provenance before the generic Schur mask is imposed. For a separated bilinear packet, let $A_u^x(\kappa)$ and $A_u^y(\kappa)$ be the two one-row sums in the class $m\kappa\equiv-h\pmod u$, and put $$B_s^x(\kappa)=\sum_{u\mid s}\vartheta_R(u)A_u^x(\kappa),  \qquad  B_s^y(\kappa)=\sum_{u\mid s}\vartheta_R(u)A_u^y(\kappa).  \label{eq:tpc21-intro-prefixes}$$ The unmasked lcm layer has the exact divisor-lattice formula $$Z_q^{x,y}(\kappa)  =\sum_{s\mid q}\mu(q/s)  B_s^x(\kappa\bmod s)B_s^y(\kappa\bmod s).  \label{eq:tpc21-intro-divisor-reassembly}$$ In the Hermitian case this is a Möbius difference of cumulative nonnegative energies. CRT lifting then shows that on frequencies coprime to $q$, the outer lcm Möbius signs all disappear. This is structure, but not automatic cancellation.

###### ref-db1533c6cd5385d7

For the actual generic mask, multiplicative Fourier analysis is more faithful. Write $q=gab$, $u=ga$, $v=gb$, with $g,a,b$ pairwise coprime. If $\chi=\chi_g\chi_a\chi_b$ is a character of $\mathcal U_q$, the fiber transform is exactly a convolution over characters $\psi$ of the shared factor $g$: $$\widehat Z_{u,v}^{\,\mathfrak m}(\chi)  =\frac{\overline{\chi(-h)}}{\varphi(g)}  \sum_{\psi\bmod g}  \mathcal B_{\mathfrak m}  (\chi_g\chi_a\psi,\chi_b\overline\psi).  \label{eq:tpc21-intro-character-convolution}$$ Here $\mathfrak m(m_1,m_2)$ is the generic Schur mask and $\mathcal B_{\mathfrak m}$ is its masked two-row Dirichlet-character sum. When $\mathfrak m\equiv1$, the latter factors into two one-row Dirichlet polynomials. For the generic mask it does not. Multiplicative Parseval therefore identifies the exact surviving input as a masked shared-factor character fourth moment.

## Why scalar unpruning cannot be put inside the norm

The isolated short–short signal is $O_\varepsilon(X^\varepsilon)$ pointwise. Hence the TPC-18 tuple counts allow the degenerate row pairs to be restored and removed at the level of the complete scalar correlation, with fixed-power error. It is tempting to restore the full Gram packet, take its fiber $L^2$ norm, and then subtract the degenerate scalar terms. This is invalid because the norm is nonlinear.

###### ref-064fe421c5295560

We make the failure exact. A single row diagonal contributes to one class $\kappa_q$ only: $$Z_{q,m}^{\rm rowdiag}(\kappa)  =|x_m|^2\Gamma_\vartheta(q)   \mathbf 1_{\{\kappa=\kappa_q\}},  \label{eq:tpc21-intro-diagonal-spike}$$ and its multiplicative Fourier magnitude is constant in every nonprincipal frequency. Thus a row diagonal can saturate the one-fiber obstruction of TPC-20 even though its summed physical correlation is already negligible. The generic mask must remain inside the connected character moment, or the degenerate pieces must be removed before a nonlinear norm is applied.

The paper therefore makes a positive reduction without claiming a parity breakthrough: the Ramanujan mean is closed, the arbitrary fiber is replaced by an exact masked character object, and two insufficient shortcuts are ruled out quantitatively. The masked fourth moment and the large-divisor legs remain open.

# The inherited primitive fiber interface

###### ref-2726b98f54ba48aa

###### ref-f1f85471985316f7

###### ref-1cb7825ecfaa537f

Fix $h\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}$, put $H=\operatorname{rad}|h|$, and let $$e(t)=\exp(2\pi i t),\qquad e_s(t)=e(t/s).$$ $$R=\lfloor X^{1/2-\delta}\rfloor,  \qquad  V=\lfloor R^{1/2}\rfloor,  \qquad 0<\delta<\frac12.  \label{eq:tpc21-RV}$$ Take $X$ large enough that $R\ge H^2$. Then the inherited restriction $d\le V$ implies $dH\le R$, as required in the TPC-20 recombination. The row parameters satisfy $$Q=LD,\qquad QJ\asymp X,\qquad  L>\max(2R,2|h|),  \label{eq:tpc21-row-scales}$$ and $$\ell\in[L/2,2L]\text{ is prime},  \qquad  d\in[D,2D]\cap[1,V],  \qquad  m=\ell d.  \label{eq:tpc21-row-support}$$ The integer $d$ is squarefree, $(d,H)=1$, and the orbit is restricted to $(j,H)=1$. In particular $(m,H)=1$. All implied constants may depend on $h,\delta$, and fixed smooth weights.

###### ref-6e75cf956fbaf4b3

###### ref-f9bd124dfca26dc9

TPC-20 supplies two short–short coefficient choices, $$\vartheta_R(u)=\lambda'_R(u)  \quad\text{or}\quad  \vartheta_R(u)=b_R(u)\mathbf 1_{u\le R},  \label{eq:tpc21-theta-choices}$$ where both are supported on squarefree $u\le R$ and, for every fixed $\varepsilon>0$, $$|\vartheta_R(u)|\ll_\varepsilon X^\varepsilon.  \label{eq:tpc21-theta-soft}$$ Both coefficient choices are real-valued. This fact is used whenever a Hermitian packet is written as an absolute square. The first choice is the centered finite Ramanujan model. The second is the matched residual coefficient restricted to its two short divisor legs. No statement below controls the complementary terms with $u>R$ or $v>R$.

## Rows, masks, and fiber vectors

###### ref-59ff120bbd13d822

Let $\mathcal A$ be one finite separated row packet. Its labels $\alpha=(\ell,d)$ satisfy [\[eq:tpc21-row-support\]](TPC-021.md#ref-1cb7825ecfaa537f), and its weighted cardinality obeys $$\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal A}|x_\alpha|  +\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal A}|y_\alpha|  \ll QX^\varepsilon  \label{eq:tpc21-row-mass}$$ for the bounded row weights used below. The original smooth factors in $mj/X$ and $dj/K$ admit the standard rapidly convergent Mellin–Fourier separation. Each separated term has a common $F(j/J)$ and rank-one row weights $x_{\alpha_1}y_{\alpha_2}$; the total projective mass of the truncated superposition is $X^\varepsilon$, with arbitrary power error.

###### ref-12d047bf3263076d

The generic TPC-18 row-pair mask is $$\mathfrak m(\alpha_1,\alpha_2)  =\mathbf 1_{\{\ell_1\ne\ell_2\}}   \mathbf 1_{\{|m_1-m_2|>QX^{-\kappa}\}}   \mathbf 1_{\{(d_1,d_2)\le X^\kappa\}},  \label{eq:tpc21-generic-mask}$$ where $\kappa>0$ is fixed. We also allow an arbitrary fixed row-pair restriction independent of $j$, denoted by the same letter, when proving the exact fiber identities.

###### ref-fb3de54278bcfca7

###### ref-2a485a9f428b57e4

###### ref-b22efdd454163ab8

###### ref-7d13c1fc13279339

###### ref-9c9a0cb6fa433d46

For squarefree $u,v\le R$, put $$g=(u,v),\qquad q=[u,v].  \label{eq:tpc21-gq}$$ All aggregated layers used below are restricted to $(q,H)=1$. Layers with $(q,H)>1$ have empty admissible fibers and are omitted; in particular $\overline H\pmod q$ is always defined. If $$(u,m_1H)=(v,m_2H)=1,$$ then the pair $(m_1,m_2)$ is compatible precisely when $g\mid m_1-m_2$. It then determines the unique unit class $$\kappa\in\mathcal U_q=(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^\times,  \qquad  m_1\kappa\equiv-h\pmod u,  \quad  m_2\kappa\equiv-h\pmod v.  \label{eq:tpc21-joint-kappa}$$ If either row–modulus admissibility condition fails, the corresponding fiber is zero. Define $$\begin{aligned}  Z_{u,v}^{\mathfrak m}(\kappa)  ={}&\sum_{\alpha_1,\alpha_2\in\mathcal A}  x_{\alpha_1}y_{\alpha_2}  \mathfrak m(\alpha_1,\alpha_2)  \mathbf 1_{\{m_1\equiv-h\overline\kappa\ (u)\}}  \mathbf 1_{\{m_2\equiv-h\overline\kappa\ (v)\}},  \label{eq:tpc21-uv-fiber}\\  Z_q^{\mathfrak m}(\kappa)  ={}&\sum_{\substack{u,v\le R\\{}[u,v]=q}}  \vartheta_R(u)\vartheta_R(v)  Z_{u,v}^{\mathfrak m}(\kappa).  \label{eq:tpc21-q-fiber}\end{aligned}$$ Congruences modulo $1$ are vacuous. An inadmissible divisor–row combination contributes zero. In the retained low nonzero joint frequencies of TPC-20, the common multiplier is $g$, and the factor $g/(uv)=1/q$ gives the scalar packet $$\mathcal L_q  =\frac{J}{Hq}\sum_{r\ne0}c_H(r)  \widehat F\!\left(\frac{rJ}{Hq}\right)  S_q(r),  \qquad  S_q(r)=\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal U_q}  Z_q^{\mathfrak m}(\kappa)e_q(r\overline H\kappa).  \label{eq:tpc21-long-packet}$$

###### ref-9c578d555dd54fbf

Put $$\overline Z_q^{\mathfrak m}  =\frac1{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal U_q}  Z_q^{\mathfrak m}(\kappa),  \qquad  D_q^{\mathfrak m}(\kappa)  =Z_q^{\mathfrak m}(\kappa)-\overline Z_q^{\mathfrak m}.  \label{eq:tpc21-mean-discrepancy}$$ Then $$S_q(r)=\overline Z_q^{\mathfrak m}c_q(r)  +\widehat D_q^{\mathfrak m}(r),  \qquad  \sum_{r\bmod q}|\widehat D_q^{\mathfrak m}(r)|^2  =q\lVert D_q^{\mathfrak m}\rVert_{2,\mathcal U_q}^2.  \label{eq:tpc21-additive-parseval}$$ The mean term is closed in [3](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-021.md#src-6ebc1fa4b938c5ea); the discrepancy is the subject of the remaining sections.

## The inherited restricted-frequency gate

###### ref-75c50c89f498413c

###### ref-cf50b0c9eeb89fff

For a long-modulus family $\mathcal Q$, TPC-20 proves, after truncating the rapidly decaying Fourier tail, that $$\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q}|\mathcal L_q^{\rm disc}|  \ll_{H,F,\varepsilon}X^{\varepsilon/2}\sqrt{\frac JH}  \sum_{q\in\mathcal Q}\lVert D_q^{\mathfrak m}\rVert_{2,\mathcal U_q}  +O_A(X^{-A}\mathcal Z_1),  \label{eq:tpc21-inherited-gate}$$ under its no-wrap and polynomial-mass hypotheses. Here $\mathcal Z_1$ denotes the relevant total coefficient mass. The natural row-pair–orbit scale is $$Q^2J\asymp XQ.  \label{eq:tpc21-natural-scale}$$ Every use of [\[eq:tpc21-inherited-gate\]](TPC-021.md#ref-75c50c89f498413c) below is compared with this scale. The estimate is sufficient, not known to be necessary; in particular, scalar cancellation may be invisible to the nonlinear fiber norm.

# The Ramanujan mean as a coprime-lattice discrepancy

###### ref-91d5991c0d9ee329

###### ref-103e1091416ed533

We first dispose of the deterministic mean packet left by the CRT-fiber decomposition of TPC-20. The argument applies to one separated compatible short–short packet. Thus the same function $F(j/J)$, with $F$ in a uniformly bounded subset of $C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$, occurs for every row pair, and all separated row weights are absorbed into coefficients $z_{m_1,m_2}$. We use the Fourier convention $$\widehat F(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb R}F(x)e(-x\xi)\,dx.  \label{eq:tpc21-Fourier-convention}$$ The divisor coefficient $\vartheta_R$ denotes either $$\vartheta_R(u)=\lambda'_R(u)  \qquad\text{or}\qquad  \vartheta_R(u)=b_R(u)\mathbf 1_{\{u\le R\}},  \label{eq:tpc21-two-theta-choices}$$ and is extended by zero outside its squarefree support.

###### ref-b046be71c5b20ea4

###### ref-5aee00b850511de8

###### ref-77db2a82202beefd

For admissible $u,v\le R$, define the compatible row-pair mass $$\begin{aligned}  \mathfrak R_{u,v}  :={}&\sum_{(m_1,m_2)}z_{m_1,m_2}  \mathbf 1_{\{(u,m_1H)=1\}}  \mathbf 1_{\{(v,m_2H)=1\}}  \mathbf 1_{\{(u,v)\mid m_1-m_2\}}.  \label{eq:tpc21-compatible-row-mass}\end{aligned}$$ Any row-pair restriction independent of the orbit variable may be included in this sum. The compatible-fiber bijection implies $$\sum_{\kappa\in(\mathbb Z/[u,v]\mathbb Z)^\times}  Z_{u,v}(\kappa)=\mathfrak R_{u,v}.  \label{eq:tpc21-fiber-total-mass}$$ Consequently, after aggregating all pairs with the same least common multiple, $$\boxed{  \overline Z_q  =\frac1{\varphi(q)}  \sum_{\substack{u,v\le R\\{}[u,v]=q}}  \vartheta_R(u)\vartheta_R(v)\mathfrak R_{u,v}.}  \label{eq:tpc21-aggregated-fiber-mean}$$ Every nonzero term has $(q,H)=1$. No equidistribution of the row weights among the unit fibers is used in [\[eq:tpc21-aggregated-fiber-mean\]](TPC-021.md#ref-77db2a82202beefd).

## Exact reduced-class Poisson inversion

###### ref-e9735d879a0623d5

For an integer $M\ge1$, put $$\Delta_M(F;J)  =\frac JM\sum_{r\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}}  c_M(r)\widehat F\!\left(\frac{rJ}{M}\right).  \label{eq:tpc21-Delta-definition}$$

###### ref-1472b4a7abb66505

###### ref-15b4b1ea5acbaa6c

###### ref-ce44992cfad948b5

> **Proposition: The Ramanujan spectrum is a coprime-lattice error** \[prop:tpc21-coprime-lattice-identity\] For every $M\ge1$ and $J>0$, one has the exact identity $$\boxed{  \Delta_M(F;J)  =\sum_{\substack{n\in\mathbb Z\\(n,M)=1}}F(n/J)  -\frac{\varphi(M)}M J\widehat F(0).}  \label{eq:tpc21-coprime-lattice-identity}$$ Moreover, uniformly in $M$ and $J$, $$|\Delta_M(F;J)|\ll_F\tau(M).  \label{eq:tpc21-Delta-tau-bound}$$ The implied constant is uniform when $F$ ranges over a uniformly bounded subset of $C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$.

###### ref-64b1a8dc1bd12f6f

> **Proof** For each reduced class $a\bmod M$, Poisson summation on the progression $a+M\mathbb Z$ gives $$\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}F\!\left(\frac{a+kM}{J}\right)  =\frac JM\sum_{r\in\mathbb Z}  e_M(ra)\widehat F\!\left(\frac{rJ}{M}\right).$$ Summing over $a\bmod M$ with $(a,M)=1$ produces $c_M(r)$. The term $r=0$ is $J\varphi(M)\widehat F(0)/M$; removing it proves [\[eq:tpc21-coprime-lattice-identity\]](TPC-021.md#ref-15b4b1ea5acbaa6c).
>
> For the uniform bound, insert $\mathbf 1_{\{(n,M)=1\}}=\sum_{d\mid(n,M)}\mu(d)$ in the physical-space side. This gives $$\Delta_M(F;J)  =\sum_{d\mid M}\mu(d)  \left\{   \sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}F(dk/J)-\frac Jd\widehat F(0)  \right\}.  \label{eq:tpc21-Delta-Mobius-expansion}$$ The expression in braces is $O_F(1)$, uniformly for $d,J>0$. Indeed, when $J/d\ge1$, the standard bounded-variation estimate for a Riemann sum gives this bound. When $J/d<1$, compact support shows that the discrete sum contains $O_F(1)$ points, while $(J/d)\widehat F(0)=O_F(1)$. Summing over $d\mid M$ proves [\[eq:tpc21-Delta-tau-bound\]](TPC-021.md#ref-ce44992cfad948b5).

###### ref-04d9c23db6f8d5f8

The deterministic part of the TPC-20 long packet was $$\mathcal L_q^{\mathrm{mean}}  =\overline Z_q\frac{J}{Hq}  \sum_{r\ne0}c_{Hq}(r)  \widehat F\!\left(\frac{rJ}{Hq}\right).$$ Thus [\[prop:tpc21-coprime-lattice-identity\]](TPC-021.md#ref-1472b4a7abb66505) gives the exact physical-space form $$\boxed{  \mathcal L_q^{\mathrm{mean}}  =\overline Z_q\,\Delta_{Hq}(F;J).}  \label{eq:tpc21-mean-packet-physical-form}$$ This identity concerns only the chosen compatible short–short packet; it does not include either large-divisor leg of the full residual.

## The arithmetic cost of all least common multiples

###### ref-5d997f983f3d428c

###### ref-37d83859fd2e7431

> **Lemma: Summable lcm cost** \[lem:tpc21-lcm-arithmetic-cost\] Assume $R\le X$ and let $\vartheta_R$ be either coefficient in [\[eq:tpc21-two-theta-choices\]](TPC-021.md#ref-103e1091416ed533). For every $\varepsilon>0$, $$\boxed{  \sum_{u,v\le R}  \frac{|\vartheta_R(u)\vartheta_R(v)|        \tau(H[u,v])}{\varphi([u,v])}  \ll_{\varepsilon,H}X^\varepsilon.}  \label{eq:tpc21-lcm-arithmetic-cost}$$

###### ref-0e2a18ed922c2b22

> **Proof** The divisor formula for the finite model gives, on squarefree support, $$|\lambda'_R(u)|  \le \frac u{\varphi(u)}  \sum_{b\le R/u}\frac{\mu(b)^2}{\varphi(b)}  \ll_\eta R^\eta$$ for every $\eta>0$. Here one may use $n/\varphi(n)\ll_\eta n^\eta$ and $1/\varphi(b)\ll_\eta b^{-1+\eta}$. Since $b_R(u)=-\mu(u)\log u-\lambda'_R(u)$, the same bound, after adjusting $\eta$, holds for both choices of $\vartheta_R$.
>
> Put $q=[u,v]$. The standard divisor estimates imply $$\frac{\tau(Hq)}{\varphi(q)}  =\frac1q\tau(Hq)\frac q{\varphi(q)}  \ll_{\eta,H}\frac{X^\eta}{q},$$ because $q\le R^2\le X^2$. It remains to note that $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_{u,v\le R}\frac1{[u,v]}  &\le  \sum_{g\le R}\frac1g  \left(\sum_{a\le R/g}\frac1a\right)  \left(\sum_{b\le R/g}\frac1b\right)  \notag\\  &\ll \log^3(2R).  \label{eq:tpc21-reciprocal-lcm-sum}\end{aligned}$$ Indeed, write $u=ga$, $v=gb$ with $g=(u,v)$, and then drop the restriction $(a,b)=1$. Combining these estimates and choosing $\eta$ as a sufficiently small fixed fraction of $\varepsilon$ proves [\[eq:tpc21-lcm-arithmetic-cost\]](TPC-021.md#ref-37d83859fd2e7431).

# Deterministic row occupancy and the long-lcm frontier

This section uses one feature which is absent from an arbitrary fiber vector: the fibers come from rows $m=\ell d$, with $\ell\asymp L$, $d\asymp D$, and $L>2R$. It gives a genuine anti-concentration estimate, but the resulting discrepancy bound reaches only the already-completable side of the joint-period boundary. The Ramanujan mean is handled separately and more strongly in [3](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-021.md#src-6ebc1fa4b938c5ea).

## Safe restoration of the Cartesian row packet

###### ref-49d65a6924915dbd

Put $$Z_m^{\mathrm{sh}}(j)  =\sum_{\substack{u\le R\\(u,mH)=1}}  b_R(u)\left(\mathbf 1_{u\mid mj+h}-\frac1u\right).  \label{eq:tpc21-short-signal}$$ Only the short coefficient $b_R(u)\mathbf 1_{u\le R}$ is used here.

###### ref-223fe32b77879fb8

###### ref-cd9160f79ebdeeda

> **Lemma: Pointwise size of the isolated short signal** \[lem:tpc21-short-pointwise\] Uniformly on the TPC row and orbit support, for every fixed $\epsilon>0$, $$|Z_m^{\mathrm{sh}}(j)|\ll_{\epsilon,h}X^\epsilon.  \label{eq:tpc21-short-pointwise}$$

###### ref-cd2204b0c03c5425

> **Proof** The coefficient is squarefree supported and $$\max_{u\le R}|b_R(u)|\ll (\log(2R))^2,  \qquad  \sum_{u\le R}\frac{|b_R(u)|}{u}\ll(\log(2R))^2.  \label{eq:tpc21-b-short-bounds}$$ Indeed, the first estimate follows from the positive formula for $\lambda'_R$, while the second follows from $b_R(u)=-\mu(u)\log u-\lambda'_R(u)$ and the Euler-product estimate $\sum_{u\le R}|\lambda'_R(u)|/u\ll(\log(2R))^2$. Consequently $$|Z_m^{\mathrm{sh}}(j)|  \le \tau(mj+h)\max_{u\le R}|b_R(u)|      +\sum_{u\le R}\frac{|b_R(u)|}{u}.$$ The targets are $O(X)$, so the standard divisor bound proves [\[eq:tpc21-short-pointwise\]](TPC-021.md#ref-cd9160f79ebdeeda).

###### ref-8a5d6488cbaaf861

###### ref-d24281aa9be0837d

> **Proposition: Scalar-safe unpruning** \[prop:tpc21-scalar-unpruning\] Fix $\kappa,\lambda_0>0$, assume $L\ge X^{\lambda_0}$, and let the row weights be polylogarithmically bounded. In a scalar short–short correlation, the diagonal, same-source, near-determinant, and large row-gcd pairs may be restored at total cost $$\begin{aligned}  O_\epsilon\!\left(  X^{1+\epsilon}  +XQ\{L^{-1}+X^{-\kappa}\}X^\epsilon  \right).  \label{eq:tpc21-unpruning-error}\end{aligned}$$ In particular, after taking $\epsilon>0$ sufficiently small, this is $O_A(XQ(\log X)^{-A})$ for every fixed $A>0$.

> **Proof** Apply [\[lem:tpc21-short-pointwise\]](TPC-021.md#ref-223fe32b77879fb8) to both short signals and repeat the tuple counts in the TPC-18 degenerate-channel removal [`\citep{WangTPC18}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-021.md#cite-4abf939102bd1649). The diagonal has $O(LDJ)=O(X)$ tuples. The same-source channel has $O(LD^2J)=O(XQ/L)$ tuples, the near channel has $O(XQ(X^{-\kappa}+L^{-1}))$, and the large-gcd channel has $O(XQX^{-\kappa})$. Source logarithms and bounded smooth weights are absorbed into $X^\epsilon$. Since $Q\ge L\ge X^{\lambda_0}$, the diagonal also has a fixed power saving relative to $XQ$.

###### ref-b8a6adb16fbbf846

> **Remark: The order of operations matters** \[rem:tpc21-unpruning-caveat\] permits restoration of the full Cartesian row set in the *total scalar short–short packet*. It does not say that every individual $q$-fiber, Poisson frequency, or mean/discrepancy piece of the excluded rows is small. Thus one must unprune before the fiber decomposition and keep [\[eq:tpc21-unpruning-error\]](TPC-021.md#ref-d24281aa9be0837d) outside it; coefficientwise unpruning would be an unjustified stronger assertion.

## A row-fiber occupancy theorem

###### ref-8d599e6f31614c74

###### ref-bdf9976b1c3212a2

###### ref-a247633f30bb247e

Let $\mathscr A$ be the full row family $$\alpha=(\ell,d),\qquad  \ell\in[L/2,2L]\text{ prime},\qquad  d\in[D,2D]\cap[1,V],\qquad m_\alpha=\ell d,  \label{eq:tpc21-row-family}$$ with any rowwise coprimality masks inserted into the coefficients. A smooth separation of the original two-row weight gives bilinear packets $x_\alpha y_\beta F(j/J)$. We assume $$|x_\alpha|\le W_x,\qquad |y_\alpha|\le W_y,  \label{eq:tpc21-row-weight-size}$$ where $W_x,W_y$ are polylogarithmic in the application. For squarefree $u\le R$, $(u,H)=1$, define $$A_u^x(\kappa)  =\sum_{\substack{\alpha\in\mathscr A\\  m_\alpha\kappa\equiv-h\ (\bmod u)}}x_\alpha,  \qquad \kappa\in\mathcal U_u,  \label{eq:tpc21-one-row-fiber}$$ and define $A_u^y$ similarly.

###### ref-f2d507a73caa06fa

###### ref-19bd82c445c6035c

###### ref-21255184dbaef67e

###### ref-50e756063c4fc7da

> **Lemma: Deterministic row anti-concentration** \[lem:tpc21-row-occupancy\] Uniformly for $u\le R$, $$\begin{aligned}  \|A_u^x\|_\infty  &\ll W_x\frac Qu,  \label{eq:tpc21-row-linfty}\\  \sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal U_u}|A_u^x(\kappa)|  &\ll W_xQ,  \label{eq:tpc21-row-lone}\\  \|A_u^x\|_2  &\ll W_x\frac Q{\sqrt u}.  \label{eq:tpc21-row-ltwo}\end{aligned}$$ The same estimates hold with $x$ replaced by $y$.

> **Proof** Fix $d$ and $\kappa$. The congruence in [\[eq:tpc21-one-row-fiber\]](TPC-021.md#ref-a247633f30bb247e) confines $\ell$ to one class modulo $u$. An interval of length $O(L)$ contains $O(L/u+1)=O(L/u)$ integers in that class because $L>2R\ge2u$. There are $O(D)$ choices of $d$, proving [\[eq:tpc21-row-linfty\]](TPC-021.md#ref-19bd82c445c6035c). Every admissible row belongs to exactly one class $\kappa$, so summing absolute values before grouping proves [\[eq:tpc21-row-lone\]](TPC-021.md#ref-21255184dbaef67e). Finally, $\|A\|_2^2\le\|A\|_\infty\|A\|_1$, which gives [\[eq:tpc21-row-ltwo\]](TPC-021.md#ref-50e756063c4fc7da).

###### ref-7e350ef5cd63ee5e

###### ref-34ff7b681fd4e04c

Let $\vartheta$ be supported on squarefree $u\le R$, and suppose $$|\vartheta(u)|\le\Theta_R.  \label{eq:tpc21-theta-size}$$ Both $\lambda'_R(u)$ and $b_R(u)\mathbf 1_{u\le R}$ satisfy this with $\Theta_R\ll(\log(2R))^2$. For squarefree $q$ with $(q,H)=1$, define the full bilinear lcm fiber $$Z_q^{x,y}(\kappa)  =\sum_{\substack{u,v\le R\\{}[u,v]=q}}  \vartheta(u)\vartheta(v)  A_u^x(\kappa\bmod u)A_v^y(\kappa\bmod v),  \qquad \kappa\in\mathcal U_q.  \label{eq:tpc21-full-lcm-fiber}$$ No complex conjugation is implicit in [\[eq:tpc21-full-lcm-fiber\]](TPC-021.md#ref-34ff7b681fd4e04c); after Mellin separation the packet is bilinear. A Hermitian square is the special case in which the second factor is conjugated.

###### ref-4ac839ae94a607d8

###### ref-0b5ff076bfa3a118

###### ref-41ff1baa738f1715

###### ref-27286f4ddde5f5bb

###### ref-d831951fe0a56b9f

> **Theorem: Actual-fiber sup norm and variance** \[thm:tpc21-occupancy-variance\] Put $$\mathfrak g(q)=\prod_{p\mid q}\left(2+\frac1p\right).  \label{eq:tpc21-occupancy-gq}$$ Then $$\begin{aligned}  \|Z_q^{x,y}\|_\infty  &\ll W_xW_y\Theta_R^2Q^2\frac{\mathfrak g(q)}q,  \label{eq:tpc21-fiber-linfty}\\  |\overline Z_q^{x,y}|  &\ll W_xW_y\Theta_R^2Q^2\frac{\mathfrak g(q)}q,  \label{eq:tpc21-fiber-mean-bound}\\  \|D_q^{x,y}\|_2  &\ll W_xW_y\Theta_R^2Q^2        \frac{\mathfrak g(q)}{\sqrt q},  \label{eq:tpc21-fiber-discrepancy-bound}\end{aligned}$$ where $D_q^{x,y}=Z_q^{x,y}-\overline Z_q^{x,y}$ and the mean is over $\mathcal U_q$. The same three estimates hold after multiplying each row pair in [\[eq:tpc21-full-lcm-fiber\]](TPC-021.md#ref-34ff7b681fd4e04c) by any Schur mask of modulus at most $1$, in particular by the generic mask [\[eq:tpc21-generic-mask\]](TPC-021.md#ref-12d047bf3263076d).

###### ref-919793a7eb3a93dc

> **Proof** For squarefree $q$, independent assignment of every prime divisor to $u$ only, $v$ only, or both gives the exact identity $$\sum_{\substack{u,v\mid q\\{}[u,v]=q}}\frac1{uv}  =\prod_{p\mid q}\left(\frac2p+\frac1{p^2}\right)  =\frac1q\prod_{p\mid q}\left(2+\frac1p\right).  \label{eq:tpc21-exact-lcm-product}$$ The restrictions $u,v\le R$ can only decrease the positive sum. Insert [\[eq:tpc21-row-linfty\]](TPC-021.md#ref-19bd82c445c6035c) in [\[eq:tpc21-full-lcm-fiber\]](TPC-021.md#ref-34ff7b681fd4e04c) and use [\[eq:tpc21-exact-lcm-product\]](TPC-021.md#ref-919793a7eb3a93dc) to obtain [\[eq:tpc21-fiber-linfty\]](TPC-021.md#ref-41ff1baa738f1715). If a Schur mask $\mathfrak m(\alpha,\beta)$ with $|\mathfrak m|\le1$ is inserted, the absolute value of its $(u,v,\kappa)$-fiber is at most $$\left(\sum_{\alpha\ {\rm in\ the}\ u{\rm\mbox{-}fiber}}|x_\alpha|\right)  \left(\sum_{\beta\ {\rm in\ the}\ v{\rm\mbox{-}fiber}}|y_\beta|\right),$$ so exactly the same majorant applies. The mean bound is immediate. Finally, subtraction of the mean is orthogonal in $\ell^2(\mathcal U_q)$, and therefore $$\|D_q^{x,y}\|_2\le\|Z_q^{x,y}\|_2  \le\sqrt{\varphi(q)}\,\|Z_q^{x,y}\|_\infty.$$ This proves [\[eq:tpc21-fiber-discrepancy-bound\]](TPC-021.md#ref-d831951fe0a56b9f).

## What the occupancy bound buys

Let $q\asymp Y$ be a dyadic denominator block and retain the low-frequency window of TPC-20. Assume $J\ge4HX^{\epsilon_0}$, so that the positive and negative retained frequencies do not wrap modulo $q$, and assume the polynomial fiber-mass condition needed to remove the rapidly decaying Fourier tail.

###### ref-d4a2d313f52b692c

###### ref-d7057e93f70cf989

###### ref-131bdf3b38bc66b6

> **Corollary: Dyadic discrepancy consequence** \[cor:tpc21-dyadic-occupancy\] For fixed $H$, with a summed Fourier tail smaller than every power of $X$, $$\sum_{q\asymp Y}|\mathcal L_q^{\mathrm{disc}}|  \ll_{F,H,\epsilon_0}  X^{\epsilon_0/2}W_xW_y\Theta_R^2  Q^2\sqrt{JY}\log(2Y),  \label{eq:tpc21-dyadic-discrepancy}$$ Relative to the natural scale $XQ=Q^2J$, the right-hand side is $$\ll X^{\epsilon_0/2}W_xW_y\Theta_R^2\log(2Y)  \sqrt{\frac YJ}.  \label{eq:tpc21-occupancy-ratio}$$ Consequently this method gives a fixed power saving when $Y\le JX^{-\eta}$, after taking $0<\epsilon_0<\eta$, but gives no fixed power saving in a genuinely long block $Y\ge JX^\eta$.

###### ref-f676b20cdd8b0c11

> **Proof** The restricted-frequency Parseval estimate of TPC-20 gives $$|\mathcal L_q^{\mathrm{disc}}|  \ll X^{\epsilon_0/2}\sqrt J\,\|D_q^{x,y}\|_2.$$ Moreover, for $\Re s>1$, $$\sum_{q\ge1}\frac{\mu^2(q)\mathfrak g(q)}{q^s}  =\zeta(s)^2\mathcal H(s),$$ where the local factor of $\mathcal H$ is $$\left(1+\left(2+\frac1p\right)p^{-s}\right)(1-p^{-s})^2.$$ After the linear terms $2p^{-s}$ cancel, the remaining Euler product is absolutely convergent for $\Re s>1/2$. If $\mathcal H(s)=\sum_n h(n)n^{-s}$, then in particular $\sum_n|h(n)|/n<\infty$. Dirichlet convolution with the divisor function, followed by $\sum_{n\le T}\tau(n)\ll T\log(2T)$, gives $$\sum_{q\le T}\mu^2(q)\mathfrak g(q)\ll T\log(2T),  \qquad  \sum_{q\asymp Y}\frac{\mu^2(q)\mathfrak g(q)}{\sqrt q}  \ll\sqrt Y\log(2Y).  \label{eq:tpc21-gq-average}$$ Sum [\[eq:tpc21-fiber-discrepancy-bound\]](TPC-021.md#ref-d831951fe0a56b9f) and use [\[eq:tpc21-gq-average\]](TPC-021.md#ref-f676b20cdd8b0c11). Division by $Q^2J$ proves [\[eq:tpc21-occupancy-ratio\]](TPC-021.md#ref-131bdf3b38bc66b6).

###### ref-8d8dbb1566fc9700

> **Remark: A normalization gain, not a long-lcm estimate** \[rem:tpc21-occupancy-frontier\] The factor $q^{-1/2}$ in [\[eq:tpc21-fiber-discrepancy-bound\]](TPC-021.md#ref-d831951fe0a56b9f) is a genuine improvement over treating $Z_q(\kappa)$ as an arbitrary fiber vector. Nevertheless, [\[eq:tpc21-occupancy-ratio\]](TPC-021.md#ref-131bdf3b38bc66b6) crosses the natural scale precisely at $Y\asymp J$, which is also the joint-period completion boundary up to fixed powers of $X$ and the fixed factor $H$. Thus deterministic row occupancy does not penetrate the long-lcm gate. This is a limit of the stated estimate, not a lower bound for the arithmetic packet.

The mean term behaves better: [\[thm:tpc21-summed-mean-closure\]](TPC-021.md#ref-89e4aaaeeab44196) closes it absolutely for the original masked packet, without restoring the Cartesian row family or invoking the occupancy estimate.

# Divisor-lattice reassembly of rank-one fibers

This section records the exact extra structure present before the generic row-pair mask is imposed. It applies to one separated rank-one packet; the two row weights need not be complex conjugates. The distinction is important because smooth separation preserves bilinear rank one, but it does not make every individual separated packet positive semidefinite. All divisor layers in this section are admissible and hence coprime to $H$; identically zero inadmissible layers are omitted.

###### ref-f1040229d9eca03b

###### ref-380c633bb10e7d1b

For $u\le R$ and $\xi\in\mathcal U_u$, define $$A_u^x(\xi)  =\sum_{\substack{\alpha\in\mathcal A\\         m_\alpha\equiv-h\overline\xi\ (u)}}x_\alpha,  \qquad  A_u^y(\xi)  =\sum_{\substack{\alpha\in\mathcal A\\         m_\alpha\equiv-h\overline\xi\ (u)}}y_\alpha.  \label{eq:tpc21-one-row-fibers}$$ The congruence forces $(m_\alpha,u)=1$. If $u\mid s$, we lift a function on $\mathcal U_u$ to $\mathcal U_s$ through reduction modulo $u$, without changing its notation. Put $$B_s^x(\xi)=\sum_{u\mid s}\vartheta_R(u)A_u^x(\xi\bmod u),  \qquad  B_s^y(\xi)=\sum_{u\mid s}\vartheta_R(u)A_u^y(\xi\bmod u).  \label{eq:tpc21-divisor-prefixes}$$ The coefficient $\vartheta_R$ is extended by zero, so divisors outside the chosen short support make no contribution.

###### ref-55aa30040f86096b

###### ref-467ff51624c59349

###### ref-9c5b6af228392b1b

> **Theorem: Lcm layers are divisor Möbius differences** \[thm:tpc21-lcm-mobius-reassembly\] For the unmasked packet $\mathfrak m\equiv1$, one has pointwise on $\mathcal U_q$ $$\boxed{  Z_q^{1}(\kappa)  =\sum_{s\mid q}\mu(q/s)  B_s^x(\kappa\bmod s)B_s^y(\kappa\bmod s).}  \label{eq:tpc21-lcm-mobius-reassembly}$$ Equivalently, $$\sum_{q\mid s}Z_q^{1}(\xi\bmod q)  =B_s^x(\xi)B_s^y(\xi).  \label{eq:tpc21-cumulative-rank-one}$$ In the Hermitian case $y_\alpha=\overline{x_\alpha}$, the right side of [\[eq:tpc21-cumulative-rank-one\]](TPC-021.md#ref-9c5b6af228392b1b) is $|B_s^x(\xi)|^2\ge0$.

> **Proof** For fixed $u,v$, the two row sums separate: $$Z_{u,v}^{1}(\kappa)  =A_u^x(\kappa\bmod u)A_v^y(\kappa\bmod v).$$ Therefore $$\begin{aligned}  B_s^x(\xi)B_s^y(\xi)  &=\sum_{u,v\mid s}\vartheta_R(u)\vartheta_R(v)  A_u^x(\xi)A_v^y(\xi)\\  &=\sum_{q\mid s}Z_q^{1}(\xi\bmod q),\end{aligned}$$ because $u,v\mid s$ is equivalent to $[u,v]\mid s$. Ordinary Möbius inversion on the divisor lattice proves [\[eq:tpc21-lcm-mobius-reassembly\]](TPC-021.md#ref-467ff51624c59349).

## Means, centered prefix products, and covariance

Taking means and then centering [\[eq:tpc21-lcm-mobius-reassembly\]](TPC-021.md#ref-467ff51624c59349) gives the following exact consequence.

###### ref-b641908a9c68f71e

###### ref-efae017c311357ed

###### ref-de28961bd22921e2

> **Corollary: Divisor lift of the unmasked discrepancy** \[cor:tpc21-divisor-lift-discrepancy\] For every squarefree $q$, $$\begin{aligned}  \overline Z_q^{1}  &=\sum_{s\mid q}\mu(q/s)E_s^{x,y},  \label{eq:tpc21-divisor-lift-mean}\\  D_q^{1}(\kappa)  &=\sum_{s\mid q}\mu(q/s)  d_s^{x,y}(\kappa\bmod s).  \label{eq:tpc21-divisor-lift-D}\end{aligned}$$ In the Hermitian case $E_s^{x,\overline x}\ge0$, and $$\sum_{q\mid s}\overline Z_q^{1}=E_s^{x,\overline x}\ge0.  \label{eq:tpc21-cumulative-mean-positive}$$ No positivity is asserted for an individual lcm layer.

###### ref-05cfc7110f82f54c

###### ref-61204feb0025ea40

For $s_1,s_2\mid q$, put $w=[s_1,s_2]$ and define the normalized prefix covariance $$\Gamma_{s_1,s_2}  =\frac1{\varphi(w)}\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal U_w}  d_{s_1}^{x,y}(\xi\bmod s_1)  \overline{d_{s_2}^{x,y}(\xi\bmod s_2)}.  \label{eq:tpc21-prefix-covariance}$$ Expanding the square in [\[eq:tpc21-divisor-lift-D\]](TPC-021.md#ref-efae017c311357ed) gives the exact variance formula $$\boxed{  \lVert D_q^{1}\rVert_{2,\mathcal U_q}^{2}  =\varphi(q)\sum_{s_1,s_2\mid q}  \mu(q/s_1)\mu(q/s_2)\Gamma_{s_1,s_2}.}  \label{eq:tpc21-exact-divisor-covariance}$$ Thus even the unmasked variance is a connected divisor covariance, not the sum of independent prefix variances.

## Additive Fourier lifting and primitive sign loss

###### ref-72f526c9161780cf

Use the additive transform $$\widehat d_s^{x,y}(a)  =\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal U_s}d_s^{x,y}(\xi)  e_s(a\overline H\xi).  \label{eq:tpc21-ds-additive-transform}$$ When $s=1$, the centered function is zero. For $s\mid q$, put $t=q/s$; because $q$ is squarefree, $(s,t)=1$. Let $\overline t$ denote the inverse of $t\pmod s$.

###### ref-423dcfd525eee414

###### ref-3c3f359af656672a

###### ref-6d2e56b664ec469e

###### ref-2423dddc9e637840

> **Theorem: CRT lift of the divisor discrepancy** \[thm:tpc21-additive-divisor-lift\] For every $r\in\mathbb Z$, $$\boxed{  \widehat D_q^{1}(r)  =\sum_{s\mid q}\mu(t)c_t(r)  \widehat d_s^{x,y}(r\overline t\bmod s),  \qquad t=q/s.}  \label{eq:tpc21-additive-divisor-lift}$$ Since $t$ is squarefree, $$\mu(t)c_t(r)  =\mu((t,r))\varphi((t,r)).  \label{eq:tpc21-mobius-Ramanujan-product}$$ In particular, if $(r,q)=1$, then $$\boxed{  \widehat D_q^{1}(r)  =\sum_{s\mid q}  \widehat d_s^{x,y}(r\overline{q/s}\bmod s).}  \label{eq:tpc21-primitive-frequency-sign-loss}$$ All outer lcm Möbius signs have disappeared. The summands may still cancel through their internal phases; no positivity of the Fourier coefficients is claimed.

> **Proof** Lift one function $d_s^{x,y}$ from $\mathcal U_s$ to $\mathcal U_q$. CRT factorization with $q=st$ gives $$\begin{aligned}  &\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal U_q}d_s^{x,y}(\kappa\bmod s)  e_q(r\overline H\kappa)\\  &\qquad=  \widehat d_s^{x,y}(r\overline t\bmod s)  \sum_{\eta\in\mathcal U_t}e_t(r\overline H\overline s\eta)\\  &\qquad=  c_t(r)\widehat d_s^{x,y}(r\overline t\bmod s).\end{aligned}$$ Insert [\[eq:tpc21-divisor-lift-D\]](TPC-021.md#ref-efae017c311357ed). For squarefree $t$, the standard formula $c_t(r)=\mu(t/(t,r))\varphi((t,r))$ proves [\[eq:tpc21-mobius-Ramanujan-product\]](TPC-021.md#ref-6d2e56b664ec469e) and the primitive-frequency specialization.

###### ref-40ef86d6c1ccfa46

The same lifting gives a useful but generally lossy sufficient bound: $$\boxed{  \lVert D_q^{1}\rVert_{2,\mathcal U_q}  \le\sum_{s\mid q}  \sqrt{\frac{\varphi(q)}{\varphi(s)}}  \lVert d_s^{x,y}\rVert_{2,\mathcal U_s}.}  \label{eq:tpc21-prefix-triangle-bound}$$ It follows by applying the triangle inequality to [\[eq:tpc21-divisor-lift-D\]](TPC-021.md#ref-efae017c311357ed); the norm of a lifted function is multiplied by $\sqrt{\varphi(q)/\varphi(s)}$.

## Reduction to one-row second and fourth moments

###### ref-66dbc49c49559fa9

###### ref-71863991514e0e87

Let $$C_s^x=\frac1{\varphi(s)}\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal U_s}B_s^x(\xi),  \qquad  \mathcal E_s^x(\xi)=B_s^x(\xi)-C_s^x,  \label{eq:tpc21-one-row-centered-prefix}$$ and define $C_s^y,\mathcal E_s^y$ analogously. Direct expansion gives $$\begin{aligned}  d_s^{x,y}  ={}&C_s^x\mathcal E_s^y+C_s^y\mathcal E_s^x  +\mathcal E_s^x\mathcal E_s^y  -\frac1{\varphi(s)}\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal U_s}    \mathcal E_s^x(\xi)\mathcal E_s^y(\xi).  \label{eq:tpc21-prefix-error-expansion}\end{aligned}$$

###### ref-5cda82274c0b087c

###### ref-4d4cdfaecb1efa3a

###### ref-dd2603b44cc4ab19

> **Proposition: One-row moment transfer** \[prop:tpc21-one-row-moment-transfer\] With unnormalized $L^p(\mathcal U_s)$ norms, $$\boxed{  \lVert d_s^{x,y}\rVert_2  \le |C_s^x|\lVert\mathcal E_s^y\rVert_2     +|C_s^y|\lVert\mathcal E_s^x\rVert_2     +2\lVert\mathcal E_s^x\rVert_4       \lVert\mathcal E_s^y\rVert_4.}  \label{eq:tpc21-one-row-moment-transfer}$$ In the Hermitian case this becomes $$\lVert |B_s^x|^2-E_s^{x,\overline x}\rVert_2  \le2|C_s^x|\lVert\mathcal E_s^x\rVert_2  +2\lVert\mathcal E_s^x\rVert_4^2.  \label{eq:tpc21-hermitian-fourth-moment-gate}$$

> **Proof** Only the centered product in [\[eq:tpc21-prefix-error-expansion\]](TPC-021.md#ref-71863991514e0e87) needs attention. Hölder gives $\lVert\mathcal E_s^x\mathcal E_s^y\rVert_2 \le\lVert\mathcal E_s^x\rVert_4 \lVert\mathcal E_s^y\rVert_4$. Moreover, $$\sqrt{\varphi(s)}  \left|\frac1{\varphi(s)}\sum_\xi  \mathcal E_s^x(\xi)\mathcal E_s^y(\xi)\right|  \le \lVert\mathcal E_s^x\rVert_4       \lVert\mathcal E_s^y\rVert_4.$$ The triangle inequality proves the claim.

###### ref-2af52665018c3db0

###### ref-bbf072e185fc134b

The row means and errors in this proposition have a direct arithmetic interpretation. Averaging [\[eq:tpc21-one-row-fibers\]](TPC-021.md#ref-f1040229d9eca03b) over $\xi\in\mathcal U_u$ yields $$\frac1{\varphi(u)}\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal U_u}A_u^x(\xi)  =\frac1{\varphi(u)}   \sum_{\substack{\alpha\in\mathcal A\\(m_\alpha,u)=1}}x_\alpha.  \label{eq:tpc21-Au-exact-mean}$$ For the TPC rows, fixing $d$ turns $A_u^x(\xi)-\operatorname{avg}A_u^x$ into a prime-progression error for $$\ell\equiv-h\overline\xi\,\overline d\pmod u.  \label{eq:tpc21-row-prime-progression}$$ Thus [\[eq:tpc21-one-row-moment-transfer\]](TPC-021.md#ref-4d4cdfaecb1efa3a) is a precise point of entry for progression second and fourth moments. It is not itself such an estimate.

###### ref-25b457ff61d70782

> **Remark: Mask boundary** \[rem:tpc21-divisor-mask-boundary\] The divisor-lattice formulas in this section require a rank-one unmasked row packet. A Schur mask depending jointly on $(m_1,m_2)$ does not factor through [\[eq:tpc21-one-row-fibers\]](TPC-021.md#ref-f1040229d9eca03b). The generic mask is treated without deletion in [6](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-021.md#src-139a1a6727279e7c). Scalar degenerate removal does not justify inserting $\mathfrak m\equiv1$ into the nonlinear norm [\[eq:tpc21-inherited-gate\]](TPC-021.md#ref-75c50c89f498413c); [7](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-021.md#src-5d71ccdd3635f521) gives the exact counterexample.

# The shared-factor character gate

The additive transform in [\[eq:tpc21-long-packet\]](TPC-021.md#ref-9c9a0cb6fa433d46) is naturally defined on the full cyclic group modulo $q$. The coefficient itself, however, is supported on the unit group. Multiplicative Fourier analysis on that unit group retains the provenance of both rows and, in particular, keeps the generic Schur mask inside the transform. This section gives the exact change of basis. It is an identity, not an estimate for the resulting character moment.

For a positive integer $s$, write

$$G_s=(\mathbb Z/s\mathbb Z)^\times,  \qquad  \mathfrak X(s)=\widehat G_s,$$

###### ref-231c774b7b4a9fe7

###### ref-edfae10c6ef464da

###### ref-04edb6e28cab7d50

###### ref-41e94f9f8cf84e4d

with $G_1$ and $\mathfrak X(1)$ interpreted as trivial groups. A character is extended by zero away from the units whenever it is evaluated on an integer. For $f:G_s\to\mathbb C$, use the convention $$\widehat f^{\,\times}(\chi)  =\sum_{k\in G_s}f(k)\overline{\chi(k)}.  \label{eq:tpc21-multiplicative-transform}$$ Then $$\begin{aligned}  f(k)  &=\frac1{\varphi(s)}    \sum_{\chi\in\mathfrak X(s)}    \widehat f^{\,\times}(\chi)\chi(k),  \label{eq:tpc21-multiplicative-inversion}\\  \sum_{k\in G_s}|f(k)|^2  &=\frac1{\varphi(s)}    \sum_{\chi\in\mathfrak X(s)}    |\widehat f^{\,\times}(\chi)|^2.  \label{eq:tpc21-multiplicative-parseval}\end{aligned}$$ In particular, if $f^\circ=f-|G_s|^{-1}\sum_{k\in G_s}f(k)$, then $$\boxed{  \|f^\circ\|_{2,G_s}^2  =\frac1{\varphi(s)}   \sum_{\substack{\chi\in\mathfrak X(s)\\\chi\ne\mathbf1_s}}   |\widehat f^{\,\times}(\chi)|^2.}  \label{eq:tpc21-nonprincipal-parseval}$$ Thus “connected” below means that the principal unit-group character, equivalently the fiber mean, has been removed.

## The masked shared-$g$ convolution

###### ref-577eb1c5959bcb63

Fix squarefree divisor moduli $u,v\leq R$ with $(uv,H)=1$. Modulus pairs failing this coprimality condition have empty admissible fibers and are omitted. Compatibility of the two row labels is imposed inside the fiber. Write $$g=(u,v),\qquad u=ga,\qquad v=gb,\qquad q=[u,v]=gab.  \label{eq:tpc21-character-gab}$$ The integers $g,a,b$ are pairwise coprime. CRT therefore identifies $G_q=G_g\times G_a\times G_b$. Write a character of $G_q$ as

$$\chi=\chi_g\boxtimes\chi_a\boxtimes\chi_b,  \qquad  \chi_c\in\mathfrak X(c).$$

###### ref-a31f24b46a2daad4

For characters $\alpha\in\mathfrak X(u)$ and $\beta\in\mathfrak X(v)$, define the masked two-row Dirichlet-character sum $$\mathcal B_{\mathfrak m}^{u,v}(\alpha,\beta)  =\sum_{\omega_1,\omega_2\in\mathcal A}  x_{\omega_1}y_{\omega_2}  \mathfrak m(\omega_1,\omega_2)  \alpha(m_{\omega_1})\beta(m_{\omega_2}).  \label{eq:tpc21-masked-B}$$ Here $\omega_i=(\ell_i,d_i)$ is a row label. The zero extension of the characters inserts the row–modulus coprimality conditions. There is no compatibility indicator in [\[eq:tpc21-masked-B\]](TPC-021.md#ref-a31f24b46a2daad4); it will be created exactly by averaging a character of the shared factor $g$.

###### ref-ceb040349e50c42e

###### ref-1bc5b02278152688

> **Theorem: Exact masked shared-factor transform** \[thm:tpc21-masked-shared-character\] With the conventions above, $$\boxed{  \widehat Z_{u,v}^{\,\mathfrak m,\times}(\chi)  =\frac{\overline{\chi(-h)}}{\varphi(g)}   \sum_{\psi\in\mathfrak X(g)}   \mathcal B_{\mathfrak m}^{u,v}   \left(    (\chi_g\psi)\boxtimes\chi_a,    \overline\psi\boxtimes\chi_b   \right).}  \label{eq:tpc21-masked-shared-character}$$ The factor $1/\varphi(g)$ is essential. It is the normalized orthogonality projector onto compatible row pairs.

###### ref-e84051051614d8e6

###### ref-d1c6c6bc27fe65e9

> **Proof** Suppose first that a pair of rows contributes to $Z_{u,v}^{\mathfrak m}(\kappa)$. In the CRT coordinates of [\[eq:tpc21-character-gab\]](TPC-021.md#ref-577eb1c5959bcb63), $$\kappa_g\equiv-h\overline{m_1}              \equiv-h\overline{m_2}\pmod g,  \quad  \kappa_a\equiv-h\overline{m_1}\pmod a,  \quad  \kappa_b\equiv-h\overline{m_2}\pmod b.$$ Consequently $$\overline{\chi(\kappa)}  =\overline{\chi(-h)}   \chi_g(m_1)\chi_a(m_1)\chi_b(m_2).  \label{eq:tpc21-row-character-phase}$$ For unit rows, character orthogonality gives $$\mathbf 1_{\{m_1\equiv m_2\ (g)\}}  =\frac1{\varphi(g)}   \sum_{\psi\in\mathfrak X(g)}   \psi(m_1)\overline{\psi(m_2)}.  \label{eq:tpc21-shared-character-projector}$$ Insert [\[eq:tpc21-row-character-phase\]](TPC-021.md#ref-e84051051614d8e6) and [\[eq:tpc21-shared-character-projector\]](TPC-021.md#ref-d1c6c6bc27fe65e9) in the definition of the multiplicative transform, then interchange the finite sums. The two row variables carry respectively $(\chi_g\psi)\boxtimes\chi_a$ and $\overline\psi\boxtimes\chi_b$, proving [\[eq:tpc21-masked-shared-character\]](TPC-021.md#ref-1bc5b02278152688).

###### ref-55590589e87a8341

###### ref-5da8400f2b31233a

The unmasked identity has an additional factorization. Define the one-row Dirichlet polynomials $$\mathcal A_x^{(s)}(\rho)  =\sum_{\omega\in\mathcal A}x_\omega\rho(m_\omega),  \qquad  \mathcal A_y^{(s)}(\rho)  =\sum_{\omega\in\mathcal A}y_\omega\rho(m_\omega),  \qquad \rho\in\mathfrak X(s).  \label{eq:tpc21-row-Dirichlet-polynomials}$$ When no ambiguity is possible, the superscript $s$ is suppressed. The relation $k\equiv-h\overline m\pmod s$ also gives the useful one-row formula $$\sum_{k\in G_s}A_s^x(k)\overline{\rho(k)}  =\overline{\rho(-h)}\,\mathcal A_x^{(s)}(\rho),  \label{eq:tpc21-row-fiber-character}$$ and likewise for $y$. Indeed, $\overline{\rho(-h\overline m)} =\overline{\rho(-h)}\rho(m)$.

###### ref-c2a773e6475bce0f

###### ref-889f59e14c2529cf

###### ref-51bb090358dee4ca

> **Corollary: Unmasked row-polynomial factorization** \[cor:tpc21-unmasked-character-factorization\] If $\mathfrak m\equiv1$, then $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal B_1^{u,v}(\alpha,\beta)  &=\mathcal A_x^{(u)}(\alpha)    \mathcal A_y^{(v)}(\beta),  \label{eq:tpc21-unmasked-B-factorization}\\  \widehat Z_{u,v}^{\,1,\times}(\chi)  &=\frac{\overline{\chi(-h)}}{\varphi(g)}    \sum_{\psi\in\mathfrak X(g)}    \mathcal A_x^{(u)}     ((\chi_g\psi)\boxtimes\chi_a)    \mathcal A_y^{(v)}     (\overline\psi\boxtimes\chi_b).  \label{eq:tpc21-unmasked-shared-character}\end{aligned}$$ No conjugation of the $y$-polynomial is implicit. In a Hermitian packet it is already contained in the choice $y_\omega=\overline{x_\omega}$.

For the generic mask [\[eq:tpc21-generic-mask\]](TPC-021.md#ref-12d047bf3263076d), the factorization [\[eq:tpc21-unmasked-B-factorization\]](TPC-021.md#ref-889f59e14c2529cf) is unavailable: the determinant, same-source, and divisor-gcd conditions jointly depend on both row labels. Formula [\[eq:tpc21-masked-shared-character\]](TPC-021.md#ref-1bc5b02278152688) remains exact because it never removes that mask.

## Lcm aggregation and the exact fourth-moment gate

###### ref-796d3ef8a8b347d2

###### ref-842942c6d30369c5

Fix an admissible squarefree $q$, so that $(q,H)=1$. For every ordered pair $u,v\leq R$ with $[u,v]=q$, use the decomposition [\[eq:tpc21-character-gab\]](TPC-021.md#ref-577eb1c5959bcb63) and restrict a given $\chi\in\mathfrak X(q)$ to the corresponding CRT factors. Define $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal T_q^{\mathfrak m}(\chi)  &:=\sum_{\substack{u,v\leq R\\{}[u,v]=q}}  \frac{\vartheta_R(u)\vartheta_R(v)}{\varphi((u,v))}  \sum_{\psi\in\mathfrak X((u,v))}  \mathcal B_{\mathfrak m}^{u,v}  \left(   (\chi_g\psi)\boxtimes\chi_a,   \overline\psi\boxtimes\chi_b  \right).  \label{eq:tpc21-aggregated-character-coefficient}\end{aligned}$$ Linearity and [\[thm:tpc21-masked-shared-character\]](TPC-021.md#ref-ceb040349e50c42e) give $$\boxed{  \widehat Z_q^{\,\mathfrak m,\times}(\chi)  =\overline{\chi(-h)}\,   \mathcal T_q^{\mathfrak m}(\chi).}  \label{eq:tpc21-aggregated-character-transform}$$ Since $(q,h)=1$, the phase on the right has modulus one.

###### ref-fa43e2cb68e5c1f1

###### ref-1a9a0dc037e5bbf4

###### ref-614ec42bca19a971

###### ref-681b046fff770cb8

###### ref-b8b024300ca8d435

> **Theorem: Exact masked connected character moment** \[thm:tpc21-exact-character-gate\] Define $$\mathfrak F_q^{\mathfrak m}  =\frac1{\varphi(q)}   \sum_{\substack{\chi\in\mathfrak X(q)\\\chi\ne\mathbf1_q}}   |\mathcal T_q^{\mathfrak m}(\chi)|^2.  \label{eq:tpc21-masked-fourth-moment}$$ Then $$\boxed{  \mathfrak F_q^{\mathfrak m}  =\|D_q^{\mathfrak m}\|_{2,\mathcal U_q}^2.}  \label{eq:tpc21-character-gate-identity}$$ Thus the inherited sufficient condition [\[eq:tpc21-inherited-gate\]](TPC-021.md#ref-75c50c89f498413c) is exactly a bound for $$\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q}  \bigl(\mathfrak F_q^{\mathfrak m}\bigr)^{1/2}.  \label{eq:tpc21-family-character-gate}$$ For example, with the epsilon bookkeeping of TPC-20, it suffices to prove $$\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q}  \bigl(\mathfrak F_q^{\mathfrak m}\bigr)^{1/2}  \ll X^{-\varepsilon}\frac{XQ}{\sqrt J}(\log X)^{-B}.  \label{eq:tpc21-sufficient-character-gate}$$ This is a sufficient input for the chosen short–short discrepancy packet, not a necessary condition for cancellation of the scalar correlation.

> **Proof** Apply [\[eq:tpc21-nonprincipal-parseval\]](TPC-021.md#ref-41e94f9f8cf84e4d) to $f=Z_q^{\mathfrak m}$, and then use [\[eq:tpc21-aggregated-character-transform\]](TPC-021.md#ref-842942c6d30369c5). The phase $\overline{\chi(-h)}$ disappears after taking absolute values.

In the unmasked case, substituting [\[eq:tpc21-unmasked-B-factorization\]](TPC-021.md#ref-889f59e14c2529cf) into [\[eq:tpc21-masked-fourth-moment\]](TPC-021.md#ref-1a9a0dc037e5bbf4) makes it literally a fourth moment of one-row Dirichlet polynomials, coupled by the shared $g$-character convolution and the outer lcm sum. In the generic case it is the square of a masked two-row character sum, hence still a four-row moment, but not a product of two independent second moments. The word “connected” removes only the principal fiber mode. The generic row mask must remain inside $\mathcal B_{\mathfrak m}^{u,v}$; why scalar degenerate removal cannot be performed after this nonlinear norm is explained in [7](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-021.md#src-5d71ccdd3635f521).

## The Gauss–conductor bridge back to additive frequency

###### ref-3ed088499582e190

###### ref-a28f2b5eea892b8e

Put $e_s(z)=\exp(2\pi iz/s)$, and for $\chi\in\mathfrak X(q)$ define the generalized Gauss sum $$G_q(\chi;t)  =\sum_{k\in G_q}\chi(k)e_q(tk).  \label{eq:tpc21-generalized-Gauss}$$ Multiplicative inversion gives the exact bridge $$\boxed{  S_q(r)  =\frac1{\varphi(q)}   \sum_{\chi\in\mathfrak X(q)}   \overline{\chi(-h)}   \mathcal T_q^{\mathfrak m}(\chi)   G_q(\chi;r\overline H).}  \label{eq:tpc21-additive-character-bridge}$$

For completeness, the Gauss factor in [\[eq:tpc21-additive-character-bridge\]](TPC-021.md#ref-a28f2b5eea892b8e) can be evaluated at every integer, not merely at primitive additive frequencies.

###### ref-812206bc6e832a14

###### ref-3244aebd794546d0

> **Proposition: Induced-character Gauss factor** \[prop:tpc21-induced-Gauss\] Suppose that $q$ is squarefree. Let $f\mid q$ be the conductor of $\chi$, let $\chi^*\pmod f$ be the inducing primitive character, and put $d=q/f$. Then, for every integer $t$, $$G_q(\chi;t)  =  \begin{cases}   \overline{\chi^*(t)}\chi^*(d)   \tau_f(\chi^*)c_d(t),&(t,f)=1,\\   0,&(t,f)>1,  \end{cases}  \label{eq:tpc21-induced-Gauss}$$ where $\tau_f(\chi^*)=\sum_{x\bmod f}\chi^*(x)e_f(x)$, and the conductor-one conventions are $\tau_1(\mathbf1)=1$ and $\chi^*(d)=1$.

> **Proof** Factor the sum by CRT modulo $f$ and $d$. The two factors are $$\sum_{x\in G_f}\chi^*(x)e_f(t\overline d\,x)  \quad\text{and}\quad  \sum_{y\in G_d}e_d(t\overline f\,y).$$ The first vanishes when $(t,f)>1$, and otherwise equals $\overline{\chi^*(t)}\chi^*(d)\tau_f(\chi^*)$; the second is $c_d(t)$.

###### ref-deced10a3555d62a

###### ref-3e64f0d54ad64f6a

As $(H,q)=1$, inserting [\[eq:tpc21-induced-Gauss\]](TPC-021.md#ref-3244aebd794546d0) in [\[eq:tpc21-additive-character-bridge\]](TPC-021.md#ref-a28f2b5eea892b8e) yields $$\begin{aligned}  S_q(r)  ={}&\frac1{\varphi(q)}  \sum_{\substack{f\mid q\\(f,r)=1}}c_{q/f}(r)  \sum_{\chi^*\ (\bmod f)}^{*}  \chi^*(q/f)\tau_f(\chi^*)  \overline{\chi^*(-hr\overline H)}  \mathcal T_q^{\mathfrak m}(\chi_q),  \label{eq:tpc21-conductor-expansion}\end{aligned}$$ where $\chi_q$ is the character modulo $q$ induced by $\chi^*$, and the star denotes primitive characters. The $f=1$ term is exactly $$\frac{\mathcal T_q^{\mathfrak m}(\mathbf1_q)}{\varphi(q)}c_q(r)  =\overline Z_q^{\mathfrak m}c_q(r),  \label{eq:tpc21-conductor-principal-term}$$ so the terms $f>1$ give the additive discrepancy.

###### ref-7d3f389951c59cb1

The conductor normalization is important. If $(r,f)=1$, then $$|G_q(\chi;r\overline H)|  =\sqrt f\,|c_{q/f}(r)|.  \label{eq:tpc21-Gauss-conductor-size}$$ In particular, for $(r,q)=1$ the magnitude is $\sqrt f$, not $\sqrt q$ unless $\chi$ is primitive modulo $q$. Applying the primitive Gauss magnitude $\sqrt q$ to every character modulo $q$ would therefore be incorrect.

###### ref-64c8922c755ecc05

There is also no automatic average gain hidden in the conductors. A local count at each prime $p\mid q$ gives one principal character of conductor factor $1$ and $p-2$ nonprincipal characters of conductor factor $p$. Hence $$\sum_{\chi\in\mathfrak X(q)}\operatorname{cond}(\chi)  =\prod_{p\mid q}\bigl(1+p(p-2)\bigr)  =\varphi(q)^2.  \label{eq:tpc21-conductor-mass}$$ For $(t,q)=1$, this is equivalently recovered by applying character Parseval to the unit-modulus function $k\mapsto e_q(tk)$, which gives $\sum_\chi|G_q(\chi;t)|^2=\varphi(q)^2$. Thus a generic Cauchy–Schwarz estimate in the character variable merely transfers the full fiber energy; it does not create cancellation.

## The naive large-sieve boundary

###### ref-95e3dbf5c062eee0

The standard multiplicative large sieve states, in one common normalization, that $$\sum_{s\leq S}\frac{s}{\varphi(s)}  \sum_{\chi\ (\bmod s)}^{*}  \left|\sum_{n\leq N}a_n\chi(n)\right|^2  \ll (S^2+N)\sum_{n\leq N}|a_n|^2  \label{eq:tpc21-standard-character-large-sieve}$$ [`\cite[Chapter~7]{IwaniecKowalski2004}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-021.md#cite-49dfea87eaada156). This applies directly to one of the polynomials in [\[eq:tpc21-row-Dirichlet-polynomials\]](TPC-021.md#ref-55590589e87a8341). It does not, without further work, estimate [\[eq:tpc21-masked-fourth-moment\]](TPC-021.md#ref-1a9a0dc037e5bbf4): that expression contains a shared-$g$ convolution, an lcm aggregation, the square of a bilinear row sum, and the genuinely two-row mask $\mathfrak m$. Dropping the mask to manufacture a product of polynomials changes the nonlinear quantity, as [7](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-021.md#src-5d71ccdd3635f521) makes explicit.

###### ref-eb7677f99f7cfd97

At a fixed modulus, complete character orthogonality already gives $$\frac1{\varphi(s)}  \sum_{\rho\in\mathfrak X(s)}  |\mathcal A_x^{(s)}(\rho)|^2  =\sum_{c\in G_s}   \left|\sum_{\substack{\omega\in\mathcal A\\m_\omega\equiv c\ (s)}}   x_\omega\right|^2.  \label{eq:tpc21-fixed-character-occupancy}$$ Thus the naive second-moment route is exactly a row-occupancy bound unless additional cancellation in the actual coefficients is proved. The unconditional occupancy estimate developed in [4](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-021.md#src-6c49cc6a34bcc34f), when inserted in the inherited restricted-frequency gate, has relative size $X^{o(1)}\sqrt{Y/J}$ on $q\asymp Y$. It closes only the short-joint-period side $Y<J$ with a fixed margin and supplies no fixed-power saving in a genuinely long block $Y\geq JX^\eta$.

Consequently the character change of basis does not by itself produce a new long-lcm wedge. Such a wedge would require a theorem for the actual masked moment [\[eq:tpc21-masked-fourth-moment\]](TPC-021.md#ref-1a9a0dc037e5bbf4), or another arithmetic estimate that retains its row-pair mask and beats the occupancy scale by a quantified margin. No such estimate is claimed here; the large-divisor legs excluded in [\[eq:tpc21-theta-choices\]](TPC-021.md#ref-6e75cf956fbaf4b3) also remain outside this reduction.

# Degenerate scalar savings do not commute with fiber energy

The scalar unpruning statement in [\[prop:tpc21-scalar-unpruning\]](TPC-021.md#ref-8a5d6488cbaaf861) is linear: after exact spectral reassembly, the excluded row-pair sums may be subtracted at fixed-power cost. The sufficient gate [\[eq:tpc21-inherited-gate\]](TPC-021.md#ref-75c50c89f498413c) is nonlinear because it takes a fiber $L^2$ norm first. We now show that moving the degenerate removal through this norm is not merely unproved; it is false as a formal implication.

> **Proposition: A row diagonal is an exact one-fiber spike** \[prop:tpc21-row-diagonal-spike\] The contribution of the single row pair $(m,m)$ to the unmasked aggregated $q$-fiber is $$\boxed{  Z_{q,m}^{\rm rowdiag}(\kappa)  =|x_m|^2\Gamma_\vartheta(q)   \mathbf 1_{\{\kappa=\kappa_q(m)\}}.}  \label{eq:tpc21-row-diagonal-spike}$$ If $A=|x_m|^2\Gamma_\vartheta(q)$, then its centered fiber satisfies $$\begin{aligned}  \lVert D_{q,m}^{\rm rowdiag}\rVert_{2,\mathcal U_q}^2  &=|A|^2\left(1-\frac1{\varphi(q)}\right),  \label{eq:tpc21-row-diagonal-L2}\\  \widehat D_{q,m}^{\rm rowdiag}(r)  &=A\left\{e_q(r\overline H\kappa_q(m))        -\frac{c_q(r)}{\varphi(q)}\right\}.  \label{eq:tpc21-row-diagonal-additive}\end{aligned}$$ For every nonprincipal multiplicative character $\chi$ of $\mathcal U_q$, $$\left|\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal U_q}  D_{q,m}^{\rm rowdiag}(\kappa)\overline{\chi(\kappa)}\right|  =|A|.  \label{eq:tpc21-row-diagonal-multiplicative}$$ Thus the spike occupies every nonprincipal multiplicative mode with the same magnitude and, when $\varphi(q)>1$, retains order-$A$ additive discrepancy at every frequency coprime to $q$.

> **Proof** For each $u\mid q$, the row $m$ belongs to exactly the class $\kappa_q(m)\bmod u$. Hence every pair $u,v$ with lcm $q$ contributes at the same joint class, proving [\[eq:tpc21-row-diagonal-spike\]](TPC-021.md#ref-dd304d10ce204569). Subtracting the unit-class mean $A/\varphi(q)$ and summing the two possible squared values gives [\[eq:tpc21-row-diagonal-L2\]](TPC-021.md#ref-dbab70a449418484). The additive transform is immediate. For a nonprincipal $\chi$, the constant mean has zero transform, so the transform is $A\overline{\chi(\kappa_q(m))}$, of modulus $|A|$.

> **Proposition: The scalar estimate supplies no fiberwise saving** \[prop:tpc21-nonlinear-noncommutation\] For every $q$ with $\Gamma_\vartheta(q)\ne0$ and $\varphi(q)>1$, the single-row diagonal satisfies the exact relative identity $$\frac{\lVert D_{q,m}^{\rm rowdiag}\rVert_2}  {|x_m|^2|\Gamma_\vartheta(q)|}  =\left(1-\frac1{\varphi(q)}\right)^{1/2}.  \label{eq:tpc21-no-fiberwise-saving}$$ Consequently [\[prop:tpc21-scalar-unpruning\]](TPC-021.md#ref-8a5d6488cbaaf861), by itself, supplies no coefficientwise small factor for the row-diagonal fiber array and no bound for its sum of fiber norms. In particular, that scalar estimate does not justify restoring the full Gram fiber inside a norm, applying the triangle inequality, and subtracting the degenerate scalar error afterwards. This statement does not assert that the actual summed row-diagonal fiber norm is large; additional cancellation in $\Gamma_\vartheta(q)$ or among rows is not ruled out.

> **Proof** Identity [\[eq:tpc21-no-fiberwise-saving\]](TPC-021.md#ref-4da0c3b0af3fc431) is [\[eq:tpc21-row-diagonal-L2\]](TPC-021.md#ref-dbab70a449418484) divided by the spike amplitude. Its right-hand side tends to $1$ as $\varphi(q)\to\infty$, so the fiber norm has no uniform relative saving at the coefficient level. By contrast, the scalar estimate sums the diagonal along the physical orbit and uses the row count $LDJ\asymp X$. It contains no estimate for the coefficient array obtained after discarding the Poisson weights and taking a fiber norm. Thus the displayed triangle-inequality inference does not follow from that scalar estimate.

The correctly pruned off-diagonal object removes the row diagonal before the norm. For one fixed $u,v$, put $$C_{u,v}^{\rm rowdiag}(\kappa)  =\sum_{\substack{\alpha\in\mathcal A\\  m_\alpha\equiv-h\overline\kappa\ (q)}}x_\alpha y_\alpha,  \qquad q=[u,v],  \label{eq:tpc21-connected-diagonal-subtraction}$$ with the summand restricted to rows admissible for both divisor legs. Then $Z_{u,v}^{\ne}=Z_{u,v}^{1}-C_{u,v}^{\rm rowdiag}$ contains only distinct rows. Its centered norm is nonnegative and has the multiplicative Parseval formula of [6](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-021.md#src-139a1a6727279e7c). Subtracting a scalar self-energy from an already squared raw norm would not give the same quantity and need not remain nonnegative.

> **Proposition: Distinctness alone does not force fiber spread** \[prop:tpc21-offdiagonal-spike\] Even after row-diagonal subtraction, rank-one provenance and off-diagonal support alone imply no uniform fiber saving. Fix one admissible divisor pair $u,v$, put $g=(u,v)$, $q=[u,v]$, and take one distinct compatible row pair $m\ne n$ with $g\mid m-n$. If $$A=\vartheta_R(u)\vartheta_R(v)x_my_n$$ is its isolated $(u,v;m,n)$ amplitude, then the contribution is $A$ at exactly one joint class and zero elsewhere. After centering, its fiber $L^2$ energy is exactly $$|A|^2\left(1-\frac1{\varphi(q)}\right).  \label{eq:tpc21-offdiagonal-spike-energy}$$ Consequently a saving must use the distribution and signs of the full arithmetic row family, not only the labels *Gram* or *off diagonal*.

> **Remark: The legitimate order** \[rem:tpc21-legitimate-order\] There are two legitimate routes. One may retain the generic Schur mask inside the exact character transform and estimate its connected moment. Alternatively, one may reassemble the complete scalar spectrum first, estimate its rank-one part without taking a lossy raw fiber norm, and then subtract the scalar degenerate contributions using [\[prop:tpc21-scalar-unpruning\]](TPC-021.md#ref-8a5d6488cbaaf861). What is forbidden is to replace the generic fiber by the unpruned fiber inside a nonlinear norm merely because the excluded physical correlation is small.

# The quantitative character-moment gate

The preceding sections remove the deterministic mean and identify two coefficient-independent bounds for the discrepancy. This section records the exact improvement still required in a long dyadic lcm block and compares it with standard character and Kloosterman technology.

## The gain ledger

Write $$q\asymp Y=X^{y+o(1)},\qquad  J=X^{j+o(1)},\qquad  Q=X^{\beta+o(1)},\qquad j=1-\beta.  \label{eq:tpc21-exponent-notation}$$ The occupancy estimate gives, up to logarithms, $$\sum_{q\asymp Y}  \lVert D_q^{\mathfrak m}\rVert_{2,\mathcal U_q}  \ll Q^2\sqrt Y\,X^\varepsilon.  \label{eq:tpc21-occupancy-block-norm}$$ Suppose a future arithmetic estimate improves this by a fixed power: $$\sum_{q\asymp Y}  \lVert D_q^{\mathfrak m}\rVert_{2,\mathcal U_q}  \ll Q^2\sqrt Y\,X^{-\omega+\varepsilon}.  \label{eq:tpc21-future-character-gain}$$

> **Proposition: Gain threshold for the sufficient norm route** \[prop:tpc21-required-gain\] The combination of [\[eq:tpc21-future-character-gain\]](TPC-021.md#ref-15e1a4888787f9bb) with the TPC-20 restricted-frequency inequality gives a fixed power saving relative to $XQ=Q^2J$ whenever $$\boxed{\qquad  \omega>\frac{y-j}{2}  \qquad}  \label{eq:tpc21-required-gain}$$ with a fixed margin. Neither displayed gain is proved in this paper.

## What the ordinary character large sieve returns

The classical multiplicative large sieve in primitive-character form is recorded in [\[eq:tpc21-standard-character-large-sieve\]](TPC-021.md#ref-95e3dbf5c062eee0); see also [`\citet[Chapter~7]{IwaniecKowalski2004}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-021.md#cite-49dfea87eaada156). Passing between all and primitive characters costs only the usual divisor bookkeeping in the present squarefree setting.

For a one-row TPC polynomial, the ambient length is $N\asymp Q$ and the coefficient energy is $O(QX^\varepsilon)$. When $U\le\sqrt Q$, [\[eq:tpc21-standard-character-large-sieve\]](TPC-021.md#ref-95e3dbf5c062eee0) therefore has size $Q^2X^\varepsilon$, the same normalization already supplied by deterministic occupancy. When $U>\sqrt Q$, its $U^2$ term is larger. Applying Cauchy–Schwarz to the shared-$g$ convolution in [\[thm:tpc21-masked-shared-character\]](TPC-021.md#ref-ceb040349e50c42e) introduces a fourth moment rather than removing this cost. Thus the ordinary large sieve, used without additional arithmetic structure, proves no $\omega>0$ in a block with $Y>J$.

This comparison does not say that character methods are irrelevant. It says that the required input must exploit the specific masked convolution over the common factor $g$, rather than apply the one-polynomial large sieve independently to every factor.

## Why one-fraction black boxes do not yet apply

The bilinear Kloosterman-fraction estimate of [`\citet{DukeFriedlanderIwaniec1997}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-021.md#cite-63bff44e59766643) and the trilinear estimate of [`\citet{BettinChandee2018}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-021.md#cite-20c5dcaa0c8d6228) take separated coefficient sequences. The partially fixed-modulus preprint of [`\citet{Wright2026}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-021.md#cite-f8666ba79358b969) retains an analogous separability requirement. The generic transform in [\[thm:tpc21-masked-shared-character\]](TPC-021.md#ref-ceb040349e50c42e) contains instead $$\mathcal B_{\mathfrak m}(\rho_1,\rho_2)  =\sum_{\alpha_1,\alpha_2}  x_{\alpha_1}y_{\alpha_2}  \mathfrak m(\alpha_1,\alpha_2)  \rho_1(m_1)\rho_2(m_2),$$ where the determinant and gcd restrictions remain inside the two-row coefficient. Replacing this by a product of one-row polynomials would delete the generic Schur mask before a nonlinear moment is taken, an operation not justified by the scalar estimate; see [\[prop:tpc21-nonlinear-noncommutation\]](TPC-021.md#ref-2a10be430ca95cbb).

Likewise, the exceptional-spectrum large sieve of [`\citet{Pascadi2026LargeSieve}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-021.md#cite-362d37d3b467d698) requires a representing measure satisfying its size and two-frequency concentration hypotheses. The exact character normal form derived here does not by itself verify those hypotheses. None of these cited results is used as a black-box estimate for [\[eq:tpc21-future-character-gain\]](TPC-021.md#ref-15e1a4888787f9bb).

## Three defensible next routes

The remaining short–short problem now has three explicit routes.

1.  Prove [\[eq:tpc21-future-character-gain\]](TPC-021.md#ref-15e1a4888787f9bb) directly for the masked shared-$g$ transform, with the gain ledger [\[eq:tpc21-required-gain\]](TPC-021.md#ref-99d55b83863e101d).

2.  Decompose the generic Schur mask into separated pieces with a fully controlled projective norm, then insert the exact parameters into one specified character or Kloosterman theorem. Merely counting pieces is insufficient; the complete norm cost must retain a fixed margin after [\[eq:tpc21-required-gain\]](TPC-021.md#ref-99d55b83863e101d).

3.  Avoid the sufficient fiber norm and estimate the complete scalar spectral sum while preserving cancellation between the connected piece and the explicitly removable degenerate pieces. This route must keep the scalar order of operations in [\[rem:tpc21-legitimate-order\]](TPC-021.md#ref-ecfa099ef2fe452d).

Even a successful short–short estimate leaves the residual legs with $u>R$ or $v>R$. Closing the full TPC-18 residual would additionally require uniform reassembly of the separated packets and all earlier endpoint estimates. Nothing in this ledger is a twin-prime lower bound, a Hardy–Littlewood asymptotic, or a breach of the sieve parity barrier.

# Finite spectral meaning, certificate, and conclusion

## Two finite dualities

The word *spectrum* has two finite meanings in this paper. After zero extension from $\mathcal U_q$ to $\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z$, the additive transform $$r\longmapsto  \sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal U_q}D_q(\kappa)e_q(r\overline H\kappa)$$ is the spectral coordinate of the observable $D_q$ for translation on the finite cycle $\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z$. The multiplicative transform $$\chi\longmapsto  \sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal U_q}D_q(\kappa)\overline{\chi(\kappa)}$$ is Fourier analysis on the finite abelian group $\mathcal U_q$. The former couples directly to Poisson summation; the latter exposes the row Dirichlet polynomials and the shared-$g$ convolution. The induced character Gauss formula in [\[prop:tpc21-induced-Gauss\]](TPC-021.md#ref-812206bc6e832a14) is the exact bridge between them.

These are literal spectra of finite group actions. They do not assert that zeros of the Riemann zeta function are eigenvalues of a dynamical system, and no such interpretation is used anywhere in the proofs.

## Conclusion

TPC-20 left a Ramanujan mean packet and a centered CRT-fiber discrepancy. The first is now closed. Reduced-class Poisson summation identifies it with an ordinary smooth coprime-lattice discrepancy of size $O_F(\tau(Hq))$, and the full lcm arithmetic cost is summable. The total mean is $O_\varepsilon(Q^2X^\varepsilon)$, one complete orbit-length factor below the natural scale.

For the discrepancy, deterministic row occupancy gives the genuine normalization $Q^2q^{-1/2}$, but its dyadic sum crosses the target exactly at $q\asymp J$. It therefore does not penetrate the long-lcm range. The new exact normal forms explain what extra information is needed. Unmasked rank-one packets are divisor-lattice Möbius differences of cumulative row-fiber products. Generic packets are shared-factor convolutions of masked two-row Dirichlet-character sums, and their nonprincipal multiplicative energy is the connected fourth moment that remains to be bounded.

The row-diagonal spike shows why the order matters: a contribution may be power-saving after the complete scalar orbit sum and still saturate the raw fiber norm relative to its coefficient amplitude. Thus generic pruning cannot be removed inside a nonlinear norm merely by citing its scalar negligibility. This rules out a tempting shortcut and leaves a quantitatively specified gain target, $\omega>(y-j)/2$, for the sufficient character-moment route.

Accordingly, this paper advances the short–short branch by one positive closure and one exact reduction. It does not estimate the long connected character moment, control the large-divisor legs, prove the full TPC-18 residual-dispersion statement, breach sieve parity, or establish a fixed-shift Hardy–Littlewood asymptotic or a twin-prime lower bound.
