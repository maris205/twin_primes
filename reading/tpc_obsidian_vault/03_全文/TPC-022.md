## Abstract

TPC-21 leaves the selected short–short part of a primitive Möbius residual correlation as a connected multiplicative-character moment on unit fibers modulo squarefree least common multiples. Its shared-factor formula appears to couple the two row variables through a character convolution. We prove that this convolution has an exact pairwise rectangularization. If $$g=(u,v),\qquad u=ga,\qquad v=gb,\qquad q=gab,$$ then, for the actual row-pair mask, character orthogonality converts the shared-$g$ average into the compatibility condition $m\equiv n\pmod g$, and the remaining character coordinates form the Cartesian product $\mathfrak X(u)\times\mathfrak X(b)$. The global conductor is the product of the two coordinate conductors. This identity is applied separately to every lcm divisor pair; no common rectangular coordinate across different pairs is asserted.

For coefficients on an interval of length $N$, we derive from the primitive character large sieve the weighted exact-conductor estimate $$\sum_{\substack{s\asymp S\\s\ {\rm squarefree}}}\frac1{\varphi(s)}  \sum_{\substack{\chi\ (\bmod s)\\\operatorname{cond}\chi\asymp F}}  \left|\sum_n a_n\chi(n)\right|^2  \ll_\varepsilon X^\varepsilon\left(F+\frac NF\right)\sum_n|a_n|^2.$$ It includes conductor one and hence retains induced principal modes. Tensorization estimates an arbitrary masked coefficient matrix; no rank-one factorization or deletion of the determinant–gcd pruning is used. A second estimate exploits the exact induced Gauss factor at all additive frequencies. The Ramanujan factor $c_{q/f}(r)$ is summed against the rapidly decaying Fourier weight without imposing $(r,q)=1$.

Writing $Q=X^{\beta+o(1)}$, $R=X^{1/2-\delta+o(1)}$, and splitting coordinate conductors at $Q^{1/2}$, the tensor-norm and direct-Gauss estimates are complementary. Every conductor block has a fixed-power saving whenever $$1-4\delta<\beta<\min\left\{\frac23,6\delta\right\}.$$ Subject to the inherited no-wrap and polynomial tail-mass hypotheses, either the row-occupancy range or the hybrid range above, together with the strict single-leg condition $\beta<1/2+\delta$, closes the connected discrepancy for the actual row-pair-only generic mask in the selected short–short packet of TPC-20–21, including the two inherited parameter profiles stated below. Combining with the Ramanujan-mean closure of TPC-21 closes that selected packet. We do not treat either large-divisor leg, reassemble the full residual-dispersion argument, prove a prime-pair asymptotic, or breach the sieve parity barrier.

# Introduction

After smooth separation, one-leg completion, and compression of the compatible inverse phases, TPC-20 represents a selected short–short piece of a primitive Möbius residual correlation by one finite Fourier transform on each unit fiber modulo a squarefree least common multiple [`\citep{WangTPC18,WangTPC20}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-022.md#cite-4abf939102bd1649); [\[WangTPC20\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-022.md#cite-7525f88be3fcfa5f). TPC-21 closes the constant, or Ramanujan-mean, mode and identifies the centered part exactly as a nonprincipal multiplicative-character energy [`\citep{WangTPC21}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-022.md#cite-8d248780f3293b33).

###### ref-320ac979eb5eea47

That energy retains the provenance of both rows, but its initial form appears coupled. If $$g=(u,v),\qquad u=ga,\qquad v=gb,\qquad q=[u,v]=gab,$$ then $g,a,b$ are pairwise coprime and TPC-21 obtains $$\widehat Z_{u,v}^{\,\mathfrak m,\times}(\chi)  =\frac{\overline{\chi(-h)}}{\varphi(g)}  \sum_{\psi\in\mathfrak X(g)}  \mathcal B_{\mathfrak m}^{u,v}  \bigl((\chi_g\psi)\boxtimes\chi_a,        \overline\psi\boxtimes\chi_b\bigr).  \label{eq:intro-shared-convolution}$$ The actual generic row-pair mask $\mathfrak m$ cannot be discarded merely to factor $\mathcal B_{\mathfrak m}$ into two Dirichlet polynomials.

###### ref-6957ae025ebb4aed

###### ref-1195fa465321c011

The first observation here is that the convolution is exactly rectangular. Character orthogonality gives $$\frac1{\varphi(g)}\sum_{\psi\bmod g}  \mathcal B_{\mathfrak m}^{u,v}  \bigl((\chi_g\psi)\boxtimes\chi_a,        \overline\psi\boxtimes\chi_b\bigr)  =\sum_{m,n}c_{m,n}\mathbf 1_{m\equiv n\ (g)}    (\chi_g\boxtimes\chi_a)(m)\chi_b(n),  \label{eq:intro-rectangularization}$$ where $c_{m,n}=x_my_n\mathfrak m(m,n)$. Since $q=ub$ and $(u,b)=1$, $$\mathfrak X(q)\simeq\mathfrak X(u)\times\mathfrak X(b),  \label{eq:intro-product-coordinates}$$ and the two exact conductors multiply to $\operatorname{cond}\chi$. The shared factor remains, correctly, as compatibility inside an arbitrary matrix.

The coordinates in [\[eq:intro-product-coordinates\]](TPC-022.md#ref-1195fa465321c011) depend on $(u,v)$. Different lcm decompositions of the same $q$ do not share one common rectangle. We therefore split by divisor pair first, use triangle or Cauchy over at most $3^{\omega(q)}$ pairs, and only then rectangularize. The cost is $X^{o(1)}$.

## Two complementary conductor estimates

###### ref-3f1178060c8ce37d

For coefficients on an interval of length $N$, the primitive large sieve [`\citep[Chapter~7]{IwaniecKowalski2004}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-022.md#cite-49dfea87eaada156) gives the weighted estimate $$\sum_{\substack{s\asymp S\\s\ {\rm squarefree}}}\frac1{\varphi(s)}  \sum_{\substack{\chi\ (\bmod s)\\\operatorname{cond}\chi\asymp F}}  \left|\sum_na_n\chi(n)\right|^2  \ll_\varepsilon X^\varepsilon  \left(F+\frac NF\right)\sum_n|a_n|^2.  \label{eq:intro-exact-conductor-LS}$$ Conductor one is included, so repeated induced principal characters are not silently removed. Applying this estimate in both coordinates of [\[eq:intro-rectangularization\]](TPC-022.md#ref-6957ae025ebb4aed) works for an arbitrary matrix and does not require a rank-one decomposition of the mask.

###### ref-e622d77c6db87db4

###### ref-6c39eac33e7d7f9d

The second route uses the exact induced Gauss sum. If $f=\operatorname{cond}\chi\mid q$, $d=q/f$, and $q$ is squarefree, then $$|G_q(\chi;r\overline H)|  =\begin{cases}    \sqrt f\,|c_d(r)|,&(r,f)=1,\\    0,&(r,f)>1.   \end{cases}  \label{eq:intro-induced-Gauss}$$ No condition $(r,q)=1$ is imposed. Rapid Fourier decay gives $$\sum_{r\ne0}|c_d(r)|  \left|\widehat F\left(\frac{rJ}{Hq}\right)\right|  \ll_{F,H,\varepsilon}X^\varepsilon\frac qJ\tau(d),  \label{eq:intro-Ramanujan-weight}$$ which cancels the Poisson normalization $J/q$. The direct-Gauss route is strongest at low coordinate conductor, while the inherited fiber-norm route is strongest at high conductor.

> **Theorem: Pairwise rectangularization** \[thm:pairwise-rectangularization\] Put $$c_{m,n}^{(g)}  :=x_my_n\mathfrak m(m,n)\mathbf 1_{m\equiv n\ (g)}.  \label{eq:cg-matrix}$$ Then $$\begin{aligned}  \widehat Z_{u,v}^{\,\times}(\chi)  &=\frac{\overline{\chi(-h)}}{\varphi(g)}  \sum_{\psi\in\mathfrak X(g)}  \mathcal B_{\mathfrak m}^{u,v}  \bigl((\chi_g\psi)\boxtimes\chi_a,        \overline\psi\boxtimes\chi_b\bigr)  \notag\\  &=\overline{\chi(-h)}  \sum_{m,n}c_{m,n}^{(g)}  (\chi_g\boxtimes\chi_a)(m)\chi_b(n).  \label{eq:exact-rectangularization}\end{aligned}$$ There is no conjugation of the $n$-character in the last line and no remaining factor $1/\varphi(g)$.

> **Proof** The first equality is the exact shared-factor transform of TPC-21. Expanding its right side gives $$\sum_{m,n}x_my_n\mathfrak m(m,n)  \chi_g(m)\chi_a(m)\chi_b(n)  \frac1{\varphi(g)}\sum_{\psi\in\mathfrak X(g)}  \psi(m)\overline{\psi(n)}.$$ For $m,n\in G_g$, the normalized inner sum is $\mathbf 1_{m\equiv n\ (g)}$. If $m\notin G_g$, then $\chi_g(m)=0$. If $m\in G_g$ and $m\equiv n\pmod g$, then $n\in G_g$ automatically. The zero-extended formula is therefore exactly [\[eq:exact-rectangularization\]](TPC-022.md#ref-45a3daab7cd59aba).

Put $$\alpha=\chi_g\boxtimes\chi_a\in\mathfrak X(u),  \qquad \beta=\chi_b\in\mathfrak X(b).$$

> **Corollary: Product coordinates and conductors** \[cor:product-conductors\] The map $\chi\mapsto(\alpha,\beta)$ is a bijection $$\mathfrak X(q)\simeq\mathfrak X(u)\times\mathfrak X(b).  \label{eq:character-product}$$ If $f_1=\operatorname{cond}\alpha$, $f_2=\operatorname{cond}\beta$, then $$\operatorname{cond}\chi=f_1f_2.  \label{eq:conductor-product}$$ The global principal character corresponds exactly to $(\mathbf1_u,\mathbf1_b)$.

> **Proof** Since $q=ub$ and $(u,b)=1$, CRT gives $G_q=G_u\times G_b$, proving the bijection. Exact conductors on coprime factors multiply. The principal assertion follows locally.

## Why pairwise must not be replaced by global

###### ref-3f7006ccc8b4a9be

For squarefree $q$, every prime divisor can occur in the first modulus only, the second only, or both. Thus $$\#\{(u,v):[u,v]=q\}=3^{\omega(q)}.  \label{eq:lcm-pair-count}$$ Restrictions $u,v\le R$ only reduce this count. By [\[eq:centered-pair-sum\]](TPC-022.md#ref-da2caa8ab84fb716), either Minkowski in the fiber norm or Cauchy in character energy costs only $X^{o(1)}$.

The coordinate $\mathfrak X(u)\times\mathfrak X(b)$ changes with the divisor pair. We first split the scalar or norm into pair contributions, then split exact conductors in that pair’s coordinates, and only afterward collect dyadic conductor sizes.

## The shared-factor coefficient energy

###### ref-d8f83196f9069244

###### ref-e7f8503f6f0ab68b

> **Proposition: Total compatibility-matrix energy** \[prop:compatibility-energy\] Under [\[eq:row-energy\]](TPC-022.md#ref-cde3a11cdd11fadb) and [\[eq:mask-properties\]](TPC-022.md#ref-f2cbc36f84a9b4b2), $$\boxed{\sum_{g\le R}\sum_{m,n}|c_{m,n}^{(g)}|^2  \ll_\varepsilon Q^2X^\varepsilon.}  \label{eq:compatibility-energy}$$ The estimate remains true after imposing squarefreeness, $(g,H)=1$, or any inherited divisor-pair restriction.

> **Proof** The mask forces $m\ne n$. Therefore $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_{g\le R}\sum_{m,n}|c_{m,n}^{(g)}|^2  &\le\sum_{m\ne n}|x_m|^2|y_n|^2\sum_{g\mid m-n}1\\  &\le \max_{0<|t|\ll Q}\tau(|t|)  \left(\sum_m|x_m|^2\right)  \left(\sum_n|y_n|^2\right).\end{aligned}$$ Use [\[eq:row-energy\]](TPC-022.md#ref-cde3a11cdd11fadb) and the divisor bound. Every omitted restriction removes nonnegative summands.

If the diagonal $m=n$ were restored, every $g\le R$ would divide the zero difference. The divisor-count proof above would then acquire an additional diagonal term that must be estimated separately. In the present bounded-weight interface that term is still acceptable, since $$R\sum_m|x_my_m|^2  \le R\|x\|_\infty^2\|y\|_2^2  \ll_\varepsilon RQX^\varepsilon  \ll_\varepsilon Q^2X^\varepsilon,$$ where $R<Q$ follows from [\[eq:row-scales\]](TPC-022.md#ref-24e5c8df6186bee4) and nonempty integer row support. Thus retaining the actual mask is essential for the exact packet identity, but no false claim that the diagonal alone breaks the displayed scale is needed.

# A weighted exact-conductor large sieve

###### ref-ef5781d017339f24

The primitive multiplicative large sieve states that, for coefficients on an interval of length $N$, $$\sum_{f\le T}\frac f{\varphi(f)}  \sum_{\chi^*\ (\bmod f)}^*  \left|\sum_na_n\chi^*(n)\right|^2  \ll (T^2+N)\sum_n|a_n|^2  \label{eq:primitive-large-sieve}$$ [`\citep[Chapter~7]{IwaniecKowalski2004}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-022.md#cite-49dfea87eaada156). We need a normalization that keeps the exact conductor while the ambient modulus varies.

###### ref-c1282deeeec0e3b4

###### ref-1beec00d76c17cbc

> **Theorem: Weighted exact-conductor large sieve** \[thm:exact-conductor-large-sieve\] Let $a_n$ be supported on an interval of length at most $N$. Let $s\asymp S$ run over squarefree moduli, and let $\operatorname{cond}\chi\asymp F$ denote a dyadic exact-conductor block, with the block $F=1$ containing only conductor one. If all parameters are at most $X^{O(1)}$, then $$\boxed{  \sum_{\substack{s\asymp S\\s\ {\rm squarefree}}}  \frac1{\varphi(s)}  \sum_{\substack{\chi\in\mathfrak X(s)\\\operatorname{cond}\chi\asymp F}}  \left|\sum_na_n\chi(n)\right|^2  \ll_\varepsilon X^\varepsilon  \left(F+\frac NF\right)\sum_n|a_n|^2.}  \label{eq:exact-conductor-large-sieve}$$ The implied constant is uniform in the location of the coefficient interval and in $S$.

###### ref-e8145944b95c1253

###### ref-4b22de109feeb5d3

> **Proof** First suppose $F>1$. If $\chi\bmod s$ has exact conductor $f$, then $s=fd$, $(f,d)=1$, and the squarefree hypothesis makes the factorization unique. For the inducing primitive character $\chi^*\bmod f$, $$\chi(n)=\chi^*(n)\mathbf 1_{(n,d)=1}.  \label{eq:induced-zero-extension}$$ For fixed $d$, set $a_n^{(d)}=a_n\mathbf 1_{(n,d)=1}$. The conditions $s\asymp S$, $f\asymp F$ confine $d$ to $d\asymp S/F$, up to a bounded dyadic refinement. Moreover $$\frac1{\varphi(fd)}  =\frac1{fd}\frac f{\varphi(f)}\frac d{\varphi(d)}  \ll_\varepsilon\frac{X^\varepsilon}{S}\frac f{\varphi(f)}.  \label{eq:exact-conductor-weight}$$ Dropping coprimality and range restrictions only enlarges the nonnegative sum. Applying [\[eq:primitive-large-sieve\]](TPC-022.md#ref-ef5781d017339f24) in $f$ for each $d$ gives $$\frac{X^\varepsilon}{S}(F^2+N)\sum_n|a_n^{(d)}|^2  \le \frac{X^\varepsilon}{S}(F^2+N)\sum_n|a_n|^2.$$ There are $O(S/F)$ possible $d$, so the total is $$\ll X^\varepsilon\frac{F^2+N}{F}\sum_n|a_n|^2,$$ which is [\[eq:exact-conductor-large-sieve\]](TPC-022.md#ref-1beec00d76c17cbc).
>
> For $F=1$, the only character modulo $s$ is the principal character induced from modulus one. By Cauchy–Schwarz, $$\left|\sum_{(n,s)=1}a_n\right|^2  \le N\sum_n|a_n|^2.$$ The standard dyadic estimate $\sum_{s\asymp S}1/\varphi(s)\ll_\varepsilon X^\varepsilon$ then gives $$\ll X^\varepsilon N\sum_n|a_n|^2,$$ which is bounded by the right side of [\[eq:exact-conductor-large-sieve\]](TPC-022.md#ref-1beec00d76c17cbc) with $F=1$.

###### ref-137dabc989a4b2d4

> **Remark: Why conductor one matters** \[rem:conductor-one\] A fixed primitive character is repeated on every ambient modulus to which it induces, with an additional coprimality cutoff as in [\[eq:induced-zero-extension\]](TPC-022.md#ref-e8145944b95c1253). Thus a principle treating all nonprincipal characters as primitive characters of size $S$ is false. The factor $F+N/F$ retains low-conductor repetition. In the two-dimensional application, blocks with $F_1=1<F_2$ or $F_2=1<F_1$ are genuine connected modes and must not be deleted.

###### ref-bfc7dcee68a4b184

The theorem also has a Hilbert-valued form. If $a_n$ takes values in a finite-dimensional Hilbert space $\mathcal H$, then $$\sum_{s\asymp S}\frac1{\varphi(s)}  \sum_{\operatorname{cond}\chi\asymp F}  \left\|\sum_na_n\chi(n)\right\|_{\mathcal H}^2  \ll_\varepsilon X^\varepsilon\left(F+\frac NF\right)  \sum_n\|a_n\|_{\mathcal H}^2.  \label{eq:Hilbert-exact-conductor}$$ Expand in an orthonormal basis, apply [\[thm:exact-conductor-large-sieve\]](TPC-022.md#ref-c1282deeeec0e3b4) coordinatewise, and sum.

# Tensor conductor energy with the mask retained

###### ref-c55a909e6f64baad

For $u=ga$, $v=gb$, set $$C_{g,a,b}(\alpha,\beta)  :=\sum_{m,n}c_{m,n}^{(g)}\alpha(m)\beta(n),  \qquad  \alpha\in\mathfrak X(u),\quad\beta\in\mathfrak X(b).  \label{eq:rectangular-C}$$ By [\[thm:pairwise-rectangularization\]](TPC-022.md#ref-0a8f41137f7b830a), this is the full masked pair transform after removal of the harmless phase $\overline{\chi(-h)}$.

###### ref-277f924648dd50a9

###### ref-08051971c01aee30

Put $$\mathcal K_Q(F):=F+\frac QF.  \label{eq:KQ}$$ For dyadic coordinate-conductor sizes $F_1,F_2$, define the pair-separated energy $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal E_Y(F_1,F_2)  :={}&  \sum_{\substack{g,a,b\ {\rm pairwise\ coprime}\\                   ga,gb\le R,\ gab\asymp Y\\                   gab\ {\rm squarefree},\ (gab,H)=1}}  \frac{|\vartheta_R(ga)\vartheta_R(gb)|^2}{\varphi(gab)}  \notag\\  &\times  \sum_{\substack{\alpha\in\mathfrak X(ga)\\\operatorname{cond}\alpha\asymp F_1}}  \sum_{\substack{\beta\in\mathfrak X(b)\\\operatorname{cond}\beta\asymp F_2}}  |C_{g,a,b}(\alpha,\beta)|^2.  \label{eq:pair-energy}\end{aligned}$$ This is intentionally a sum of pair energies, not the energy of a fictional global rectangular projection of $Z_q$.

###### ref-c884311a76c788ee

###### ref-ad275bfcadba6a93

> **Theorem: Arbitrary-matrix tensor conductor bound** \[thm:tensor-energy\] Uniformly for $Y\le R^2$ and $1\le F_i\le R$, $$\boxed{  \mathcal E_Y(F_1,F_2)  \ll_\varepsilon X^\varepsilon Q^2  \mathcal K_Q(F_1)\mathcal K_Q(F_2).}  \label{eq:tensor-energy-bound}$$ No separability assumption on $c_{m,n}^{(g)}$ is made.

###### ref-bbe8517cf187d727

###### ref-bd161079cf27c7d9

> **Proof** Use [\[eq:theta-bound\]](TPC-022.md#ref-f2ecf6b8d5494eac), split $u=ga$ and $b$ into dyadic ranges, and fix $g$. Since $q=gab=ub$ and $(u,b)=1$, $\varphi(q)=\varphi(u)\varphi(b)$. Retaining squarefreeness but removing the conditions $$g\mid u,\qquad (u,b)=1,\qquad ub\asymp Y,\qquad gb\le R$$ enlarges the nonnegative sum. It is therefore enough to bound $$\begin{aligned}  &\sum_{\substack{u\asymp U\\u\ {\rm squarefree}}}\frac1{\varphi(u)}  \sum_{\substack{\alpha\in\mathfrak X(u)\\\operatorname{cond}\alpha\asymp F_1}}  \sum_{\substack{b\asymp B\\b\ {\rm squarefree}}}\frac1{\varphi(b)}  \sum_{\substack{\beta\in\mathfrak X(b)\\\operatorname{cond}\beta\asymp F_2}}  \left|\sum_{m,n}c_{m,n}^{(g)}\alpha(m)\beta(n)\right|^2.  \label{eq:enlarged-tensor-sum}\end{aligned}$$
>
> Fix $b,\beta$ and apply [\[thm:exact-conductor-large-sieve\]](TPC-022.md#ref-c1282deeeec0e3b4) in $u,\alpha$ to $$a_m(\beta)=\sum_n c_{m,n}^{(g)}\beta(n).$$ This contributes $\mathcal K_Q(F_1)$ and leaves $$\mathcal K_Q(F_1)  \sum_{\substack{b\asymp B\\b\ {\rm squarefree}}}\frac1{\varphi(b)}  \sum_{\operatorname{cond}\beta\asymp F_2}  \sum_m\left|\sum_nc_{m,n}^{(g)}\beta(n)\right|^2.$$ Apply the same theorem in $b,\beta$, separately for each $m$. The result is $$\ll_\varepsilon X^\varepsilon\mathcal K_Q(F_1)\mathcal K_Q(F_2)  \sum_{m,n}|c_{m,n}^{(g)}|^2.  \label{eq:fixed-g-tensor-bound}$$ This is a composition of two Hilbert-space operator bounds, so it does not factor the coefficient matrix.
>
> Sum [\[eq:fixed-g-tensor-bound\]](TPC-022.md#ref-bd161079cf27c7d9) over $g$ and the logarithmically many dyadic $u,b$-ranges. Then [\[prop:compatibility-energy\]](TPC-022.md#ref-d8f83196f9069244) proves [\[eq:tensor-energy-bound\]](TPC-022.md#ref-ad275bfcadba6a93).

## The inherited norm route

###### ref-67d2db77bb8c7630

For a fixed pair, multiplicative Parseval and [\[cor:product-conductors\]](TPC-022.md#ref-da8ef8bb18ed1143) decompose its centered fiber into the blocks $(F_1,F_2)\ne(1,1)$. Let $D_{u,v}^{F_1,F_2}$ denote the inverse character transform of one such block, with the factor $\vartheta_R(u)\vartheta_R(v)$ included. Then $$\|D_{u,v}^{F_1,F_2}\|_{2,\mathcal U_q}^2  =\frac{|\vartheta_R(u)\vartheta_R(v)|^2}{\varphi(q)}  \sum_{\substack{\operatorname{cond}\alpha\asymp F_1\\                   \operatorname{cond}\beta\asymp F_2}}  |C_{g,a,b}(\alpha,\beta)|^2.  \label{eq:pair-block-parseval}$$ The phase $\overline{\chi(-h)}$ has modulus one.

###### ref-e306906bf1d34454

###### ref-2e3f0ba599709c6f

Let $\mathcal L_{u,v}^{F_1,F_2}$ be the scalar packet [\[eq:scalar-packet\]](TPC-022.md#ref-1031ce0aefaf5416) formed from this pair block, and define the pair-separated dyadic aggregate $$\mathfrak L_Y(F_1,F_2)  :=\sum_{\substack{g,a,b\ {\rm pairwise\ coprime}\\                    ga,gb\le R,\ gab\asymp Y\\                    gab\ {\rm squarefree},\ (gab,H)=1}}  |\mathcal L_{ga,gb}^{F_1,F_2}|.  \label{eq:pair-separated-scalar-block}$$ This is one object. The subscripts $A$ and $B$ used informally below name two estimates for it, not two different projections. By linearity, pairwise triangle inequality, and dyadic conductor decomposition, $$\sum_{q\asymp Y}|\mathcal L_q^{\rm disc}|  \le\sum_{\substack{F_1,F_2\\(F_1,F_2)\ne(1,1)}}  \mathfrak L_Y(F_1,F_2).  \label{eq:block-reassembly-inequality}$$

###### ref-5478e10498b35529

The number of triples $(g,a,b)$ with $gab\asymp Y$ is $\ll YX^\varepsilon$, by [\[eq:lcm-pair-count\]](TPC-022.md#ref-3f7006ccc8b4a9be). Cauchy–Schwarz and [\[thm:tensor-energy\]](TPC-022.md#ref-c884311a76c788ee) therefore give $$\sum_{\substack{q\asymp Y\\\operatorname{lcm}(u,v)=q}}  \|D_{u,v}^{F_1,F_2}\|_{2,\mathcal U_q}  \ll_\varepsilon  X^\varepsilon Q\sqrt{Y\mathcal K_Q(F_1)\mathcal K_Q(F_2)}.  \label{eq:block-fiber-norm}$$ The notation on the left means the sum over the pair contributions before their lcm reassembly.

###### ref-27bbdaf05f50395a

###### ref-4f6cfe1bcdfce67c

> **Corollary: Tensor-norm scalar bound** \[cor:tensor-norm-route\] Under the inherited hypotheses of [\[eq:inherited-norm-route\]](TPC-022.md#ref-a653f5610139512e), every nonprincipal coordinate-conductor block satisfies $$\boxed{  \mathfrak L_Y(F_1,F_2)  \ll_\varepsilon X^\varepsilon Q  \sqrt{JY\mathcal K_Q(F_1)\mathcal K_Q(F_2)}.}  \label{eq:tensor-norm-scalar}$$

> **Proof** Use [\[eq:centered-pair-sum\]](TPC-022.md#ref-da2caa8ab84fb716) and Minkowski before [\[eq:inherited-norm-route\]](TPC-022.md#ref-a653f5610139512e), then insert [\[eq:block-fiber-norm\]](TPC-022.md#ref-5478e10498b35529). The Fourier-tail error is already part of the inherited polynomial-mass hypothesis.

The pairwise triangle inequality may discard cancellation between lcm decompositions, but it costs no fixed power and is sufficient for the claimed closure. The next section proves a second estimate for this same aggregate.

# The direct Gauss–Ramanujan route

###### ref-6ac40db5f2f7d9e5

The norm route does not use the conductor sensitivity of the additive Gauss factor. We now exploit it before taking a fiber norm. For $\chi\in\mathfrak X(q)$, define $$G_q(\chi;t)=\sum_{\xi\in G_q}\chi(\xi)e_q(t\xi).  \label{eq:generalized-Gauss}$$

###### ref-5727600cc01c477e

###### ref-d581c8ee5f95c142

###### ref-06f84e54577f9179

> **Lemma: Induced Gauss factor at every frequency** \[lem:induced-Gauss\] Let $q$ be squarefree, let $f=\operatorname{cond}\chi\mid q$, let $\chi^*\bmod f$ induce $\chi$, and put $d=q/f$. Then $$G_q(\chi;t)=  \begin{cases}   \overline{\chi^*(t)}\chi^*(d)\tau_f(\chi^*)c_d(t),      &(t,f)=1,\\   0,&(t,f)>1.  \end{cases}  \label{eq:induced-Gauss}$$ Here $\tau_1(\mathbf1)=1$. Consequently, for $f>1$, $$|G_q(\chi;t)|  =\sqrt f\,|c_d(t)|\mathbf 1_{(t,f)=1}.  \label{eq:induced-Gauss-size}$$

> **Proof** CRT factors [\[eq:generalized-Gauss\]](TPC-022.md#ref-6ac40db5f2f7d9e5) into a primitive Gauss sum modulo $f$ and a Ramanujan sum modulo $d$. The first factor vanishes when $(t,f)>1$; otherwise the usual change of variables gives $\overline{\chi^*(t)}\chi^*(d)\tau_f(\chi^*)$. The second factor is $c_d(t)$. Primitive Gauss magnitude is $\sqrt f$.

Since $(H,q)=1$, replacing $t$ by $r\overline H$ does not change the condition $(r,f)=1$, and it changes only a unit-modulus character phase.

###### ref-ad8376fc03665102

###### ref-72439bbb1782e948

> **Lemma: Ramanujan-weighted nonzero frequencies** \[lem:Ramanujan-Fourier\] If $s$ is squarefree, $K>0$, and $F$ is fixed and smooth with rapidly decaying Fourier transform, then $$\boxed{  \sum_{r\ne0}|c_s(r)|\,|\widehat F(r/K)|  \ll_F K\tau(s).}  \label{eq:Ramanujan-Fourier}$$ The exclusion of $r=0$ is essential.

###### ref-c00641e7483b69e1

###### ref-64b8be99902376dc

> **Proof** For squarefree $s$, $$|c_s(r)|=\varphi((s,r))  \le(s,r)=\sum_{e\mid(s,r)}\varphi(e).  \label{eq:Ramanujan-gcd}$$ For every $e\ge1$, rapid decay gives uniformly in $K>0$ $$\sum_{\substack{r\ne0\\e\mid r}}|\widehat F(r/K)|  =\sum_{k\ne0}|\widehat F(ke/K)|  \ll_F \frac Ke.  \label{eq:Fourier-multiples}$$ When $e>K$, take two or more powers of decay; this is precisely where removing $r=0$ matters. Insert [\[eq:Ramanujan-gcd\]](TPC-022.md#ref-c00641e7483b69e1), interchange the divisor and frequency sums, and use $\sum_{e\mid s}\varphi(e)/e\le\tau(s)$.

## One exact pair–conductor block

###### ref-77f4b4c2a61f69d6

###### ref-b5287d4fc903c060

###### ref-a43ed9737198db06

###### ref-f647d1325c64998b

Fix $g,a,b$, let $u=ga$, $v=gb$, $q=gab$, and fix exact coordinate conductors $$f_1=\operatorname{cond}\alpha,\qquad f_2=\operatorname{cond}\beta,\qquad  f=f_1f_2,\qquad d=\frac qf.  \label{eq:exact-coordinate-conductors}$$ Define $$A_{g,a,b;f_1,f_2}^2  :=|\vartheta_R(u)\vartheta_R(v)|^2  \sum_{\substack{\alpha\in\mathfrak X(u),\ \operatorname{cond}\alpha=f_1\\                   \beta\in\mathfrak X(b),\ \operatorname{cond}\beta=f_2}}  |C_{g,a,b}(\alpha,\beta)|^2.  \label{eq:exact-block-amplitude}$$ By [\[eq:conductor-product\]](TPC-022.md#ref-07352fd3bbb6e412), all characters in this block have global conductor $f$. Let $D_{g,a,b;f_1,f_2}$ be its inverse multiplicative-character transform, including the factor $\vartheta_R(u)\vartheta_R(v)$ and the unit-modulus phase $\overline{\chi(-h)}$, and define $$S_{g,a,b;f_1,f_2}(r)  :=\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal U_q}D_{g,a,b;f_1,f_2}(\xi)  e_q(r\overline H\xi).  \label{eq:exact-block-additive-transform}$$ Multiplicative inversion and [\[lem:induced-Gauss\]](TPC-022.md#ref-5727600cc01c477e) show that $$|S_{g,a,b;f_1,f_2}(r)|  \le \frac f{\varphi(q)}|c_d(r)|  A_{g,a,b;f_1,f_2}.  \label{eq:exact-block-S}$$ Indeed, the number of primitive characters of conductor $f$ is at most $f$; Cauchy–Schwarz combines its square root with the Gauss magnitude $\sqrt f$ to give exactly $f$. Terms with $(r,f)>1$ vanish and may be retained harmlessly on the right.

###### ref-2759dbf990a75089

Insert [\[eq:exact-block-S\]](TPC-022.md#ref-f647d1325c64998b) into [\[eq:scalar-packet\]](TPC-022.md#ref-1031ce0aefaf5416). Since $(H,d)=1$, multiplicativity gives $|c_H(r)c_d(r)|=|c_{Hd}(r)|$. Apply [\[lem:Ramanujan-Fourier\]](TPC-022.md#ref-ad8376fc03665102) with $s=Hd$ and $K=Hq/J$. The factors $J/(Hq)$ and $Hq/J$ cancel, yielding $$\begin{aligned}  |\mathcal L_{g,a,b;f_1,f_2}|  &\ll_{F,H}\frac f{\varphi(q)}\tau(Hd)  A_{g,a,b;f_1,f_2}\notag\\  &\ll_{F,H,\varepsilon}X^\varepsilon\frac fq  A_{g,a,b;f_1,f_2}.  \label{eq:exact-direct-bound}\end{aligned}$$ The second line uses $q/\varphi(q)\ll_\varepsilon X^\varepsilon$. This estimate uses all nonzero frequencies, not merely those coprime to $q$.

## Dyadic conductor aggregation

Exact conductors must be split before dyadic aggregation: the Ramanujan factor $c_{q/(f_1f_2)}(r)$ changes with the exact pair $(f_1,f_2)$. The number of exact conductor pairs and lcm divisor pairs associated with a fixed squarefree $q$ is $\tau(q)^{O(1)}$, hence $X^{o(1)}$.

###### ref-da31f519f213026b

Sum [\[eq:exact-direct-bound\]](TPC-022.md#ref-2759dbf990a75089) over $f_i\asymp F_i$, over divisor pairs, and over $q\asymp Y$. Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz gives $$\begin{aligned}  \sum \frac{A_{g,a,b;f_1,f_2}}q  &\le  \left(\sum \frac{A_{g,a,b;f_1,f_2}^2}{\varphi(q)}\right)^{1/2}  \left(\sum \frac{\varphi(q)}{q^2}\right)^{1/2}\notag\\  &\ll_\varepsilon X^\varepsilon\mathcal E_Y(F_1,F_2)^{1/2}.  \label{eq:weighted-q-Cauchy}\end{aligned}$$ More explicitly, if $I=(g,a,b,f_1,f_2)$ is the full summation index, then $$\sum_I\frac{\varphi(q_I)}{q_I^2}  \ll X^\varepsilon\sum_{q\asymp Y}\frac{\varphi(q)}{q^2}  \ll X^\varepsilon.$$ There is no factor $\sqrt Y$.

###### ref-34721219ba66ab0f

###### ref-0ba2377fc16382b0

> **Theorem: Direct-Gauss conductor bound** \[thm:direct-Gauss-bound\] Every nonprincipal dyadic coordinate-conductor block satisfies $$\boxed{  \mathfrak L_Y(F_1,F_2)  \ll_\varepsilon X^\varepsilon QF_1F_2  \sqrt{\mathcal K_Q(F_1)\mathcal K_Q(F_2)}.}  \label{eq:direct-Gauss-bound}$$

> **Proof** Equations [\[eq:exact-direct-bound\]](TPC-022.md#ref-2759dbf990a75089) and [\[eq:weighted-q-Cauchy\]](TPC-022.md#ref-da31f519f213026b) give $$\mathfrak L_Y(F_1,F_2)  \ll_\varepsilon X^\varepsilon F_1F_2\mathcal E_Y(F_1,F_2)^{1/2}.$$ Insert [\[eq:tensor-energy-bound\]](TPC-022.md#ref-ad275bfcadba6a93).

###### ref-f08c4b85b1482cd8

Combining [\[eq:tensor-norm-scalar\]](TPC-022.md#ref-4f6cfe1bcdfce67c) and [\[eq:direct-Gauss-bound\]](TPC-022.md#ref-0ba2377fc16382b0) for the same object gives the compact cell estimate $$\boxed{  \mathfrak L_Y(F_1,F_2)  \ll_\varepsilon X^\varepsilon Q  \sqrt{\mathcal K_Q(F_1)\mathcal K_Q(F_2)}  \min\{\sqrt{JY},F_1F_2\}.}  \label{eq:combined-cell-bound}$$

The block $(F_1,F_2)=(1,1)$ is the global principal character and belongs to the mean already handled in [\[eq:imported-mean-bound\]](TPC-022.md#ref-81236ad5e05ef757). The one-sided blocks $(1,F_2)$ and $(F_1,1)$ with the other conductor nontrivial remain in [\[thm:direct-Gauss-bound\]](TPC-022.md#ref-34721219ba66ab0f).

# Conductor partition and exponent closure

###### ref-8e0718f363a702b0

###### ref-8b7b4765fdc9ed4d

###### ref-cb272c3c89c7c5f7

Write $$Q=X^{\beta+o(1)},\qquad J=X^{1-\beta+o(1)},\qquad  Y=X^{y+o(1)},\qquad F_i=X^{f_i+o(1)}.  \label{eq:exponent-notation}$$ Since $F_i\le R$, $$0\le f_i\le r_0:=\frac12-\delta,  \qquad y\le1-2\delta.  \label{eq:exponent-ranges}$$ Set $$k_i:=\max\{f_i,\beta-f_i\}.  \label{eq:k-def}$$ Then $\mathcal K_Q(F_i)=X^{k_i+o(1)}$.

###### ref-d730e42d655d3b34

The natural scale [\[eq:natural-scale\]](TPC-022.md#ref-ccf8fc51dce033d9) has exponent $1+\beta$. The tensor-norm bound [\[eq:tensor-norm-scalar\]](TPC-022.md#ref-4f6cfe1bcdfce67c) has exponent $$\beta+\frac{1-\beta+y+k_1+k_2}{2},$$ and the direct bound [\[eq:direct-Gauss-bound\]](TPC-022.md#ref-0ba2377fc16382b0) has exponent $$\beta+f_1+f_2+\frac{k_1+k_2}{2}.$$ Thus their saving exponents are $$\eta_A=\frac{\beta+1-y-k_1-k_2}{2},  \qquad  \eta_B=1-f_1-f_2-\frac{k_1+k_2}{2}.  \label{eq:eta-AB}$$ For each block we use the larger of these two savings.

###### ref-3227164b54275180

###### ref-27860e03ea65b260

###### ref-2b72ab3861f1b208

> **Proposition: Three-cell conductor ledger** \[prop:three-cell-ledger\] Under [\[eq:exponent-ranges\]](TPC-022.md#ref-8b7b4765fdc9ed4d), every conductor pair satisfies $$\max\{\eta_A,\eta_B\}\ge  \eta_*(\beta,\delta),  \label{eq:eta-star-bound}$$ where $$\boxed{  \eta_*(\beta,\delta)=  \min\left\{  1-\frac{3\beta}{2},\  \frac{\beta+4\delta-1}{2},\  \frac{6\delta-\beta}{4}  \right\}.}  \label{eq:eta-star}$$

###### ref-514c69beed4c2bd8

###### ref-214ef4bc572eda2c

> **Proof** It suffices to take the worst denominator exponent $y=1-2\delta$, since decreasing $y$ improves $\eta_A$.
>
> If $f_1,f_2\le\beta/2$, then $$k_1+k_2=2\beta-f_1-f_2.$$ Consequently $$\eta_B=1-\beta-\frac{f_1+f_2}{2}  \ge1-\frac{3\beta}{2}.$$
>
> If $f_1,f_2\ge\beta/2$, then $$k_1+k_2=f_1+f_2\le1-2\delta.$$ Hence $$\eta_A\ge\frac{\beta+4\delta-1}{2}.$$
>
> It remains to consider a mixed block. Write $$x\le\frac\beta2\le z,\qquad d=z-x\ge0.$$ Then $k_1+k_2=\beta+d$, after interchanging the coordinates if necessary, and $$\eta_A\ge\delta-\frac d2.  \label{eq:mixed-A}$$ Since $z\le1/2-\delta$, direct substitution in $\eta_B$ gives $$\eta_B  =1-\frac\beta2-\frac x2-\frac{3z}{2}  \ge2\delta+\frac d2-\frac\beta2.  \label{eq:mixed-B}$$ The maximum is at least the average of the right sides of [\[eq:mixed-A\]](TPC-022.md#ref-514c69beed4c2bd8) and [\[eq:mixed-B\]](TPC-022.md#ref-214ef4bc572eda2c), namely $(6\delta-\beta)/4$. Combining the three cases proves [\[eq:eta-star\]](TPC-022.md#ref-2b72ab3861f1b208).

###### ref-21fb96f3498c62a0

###### ref-97adb66e051d8868

There are $O((\log X)^2)$ coordinate-conductor blocks. Exact-conductor and divisor-pair refinements cost $X^{o(1)}$, so choose $\varepsilon$ smaller than the fixed saving margin. The principal block is the mean, not the discrepancy.

## The occupancy alternative and its gap

###### ref-73b8476d696eda07

TPC-21 occupancy gives on $q\asymp Y$ $$\sum_q\|D_q\|_2\ll_\varepsilon Q^2\sqrt Y\,X^\varepsilon.$$ After [\[eq:inherited-norm-route\]](TPC-022.md#ref-a653f5610139512e), its ratio to $Q^2J$ is $X^{(y-(1-\beta))/2+o(1)}$. At the largest $y=1-2\delta$, the saving is $$\eta_{\rm occ}=\frac{2\delta-\beta}{2}.  \label{eq:occupancy-saving}$$ Thus occupancy closes every $Y\le R^2$ only when $\beta<2\delta$ with a fixed margin. At equality the top block has zero margin. If $\delta<1/6$, the interval between $2\delta$ and $1-4\delta$ is a genuine gap between the two sufficient routes. The actual profiles below avoid it; no general claim that the gap is empty is made.

> **Proof: Proof of [\[thm:main-closure\]](TPC-022.md#ref-b732207817bee458)** In the occupancy alternative, [\[eq:occupancy-saving\]](TPC-022.md#ref-73b8476d696eda07) and the fixed margin in $\beta<2\delta$ give a uniform positive saving for every $Y\le R^2$. In the hybrid alternative, [\[cor:hybrid-closure-region\]](TPC-022.md#ref-21fb96f3498c62a0) gives the uniform positive saving $\eta_*$. In either case, choose $\eta$ below the relevant fixed margin and absorb the logarithmically many dyadic blocks into $X^\varepsilon$. The no-wrap hypothesis licenses [\[eq:inherited-norm-route\]](TPC-022.md#ref-a653f5610139512e); since $\mathcal Z_1=X^{O(1)}$, its tail is absorbed by taking $A$ sufficiently large. Finally the strict single-leg condition licenses the inherited separated-packet reassembly. This proves [\[eq:main-discrepancy-bound\]](TPC-022.md#ref-62baf7956ac78229); combining it with [\[eq:imported-mean-bound\]](TPC-022.md#ref-81236ad5e05ef757) closes exactly the selected packet claimed in the theorem.

# Barriers, order of operations, and strict scope

The positive theorem depends on several exact choices. This section separates them from tempting but invalid shortcuts.

## A low-conductor repetition no-go

###### ref-498d86ddf886b344

The exact-conductor decomposition is not optional. Set $S\asymp N^{1/2}$, fix a nonprincipal primitive character $\chi_0\bmod f_0$ of fixed squarefree conductor $f_0>1$, and induce it to squarefree moduli $s=f_0d\asymp S$. Take $$a_n=\overline{\chi_0(n)}\mathbf 1_{1\le n\le N}.$$ Restrict $d$ to primes in a fixed dyadic interval, excluding the finitely many divisors of $f_0$. The prime number theorem and direct inclusion–exclusion give $$\left|\sum_na_n\chi_s(n)\right|  =\sum_{\substack{n\le N\\(n,f_0d)=1}}1  =N\frac{\varphi(f_0d)}{f_0d}+O(\tau(f_0d))  \gg_{f_0}N.$$ Consequently the weighted sum over these $d\asymp S/f_0$ has size $$\gg \frac{N^2}{f_0\log S}.  \label{eq:induced-repetition-lower}$$ An estimate treating these characters as primitive at ambient scale would have only $N^{3/2+o(1)}$ on the right. This is an operator obstruction to the false large sieve, not a lower bound for the actual TPC scalar packet.

## Four orders that cannot be interchanged

1.  *Divisor pair before rectangular coordinates.* Different $(u,v)$ with the same lcm use different products $\mathfrak X(u)\times\mathfrak X(v/(u,v))$. A common global $(F_1,F_2)$-coordinate is not defined.

2.  *Exact conductor before the Ramanujan frequency sum.* Inside one dyadic block, $c_{q/(f_1f_2)}(r)$ still depends on the exact conductors. We first apply [\[eq:exact-direct-bound\]](TPC-022.md#ref-2759dbf990a75089) at exact $f_1,f_2$, then pay the divisor-soft dyadic reassembly.

3.  *Remove zero frequency before [\[lem:Ramanujan-Fourier\]](TPC-022.md#ref-ad8376fc03665102).* If $r=0$ were included, $c_s(0)=\varphi(s)$ would destroy [\[eq:Fourier-multiples\]](TPC-022.md#ref-64b8be99902376dc) when $e>K$. The selected Poisson packet already has $r\ne0$.

4.  *Keep the mask before nonlinear energy.* Estimate [\[eq:compatibility-energy\]](TPC-022.md#ref-e7f8503f6f0ab68b) uses the actual off-diagonal support. Deleting the mask before squaring would restore $m=n$, for which every shared factor divides the difference.

## Why this is not a projective-mask theorem

Some mask components admit inexpensive separated representations, but the divisor-gcd cutoff does not automatically have a sufficiently small naive projective norm. The tensor proof avoids the issue: $c_{m,n}^{(g)}$ is arbitrary. It does require the same matrix while the output moduli vary for fixed $g$. A mask depending on $u,v$ or $q$ is therefore not covered without a new argument.

The large shared-factor and moment-diagonal bounds obtainable directly from TPC-21 remain useful cross-checks, but they are not needed to manufacture the saving here. The power saving comes from product coordinates, the weighted conductor large sieve, and the complementary induced-Gauss estimate.

## Parity

The character tensor supplies cancellation in a previously unresolved analytic norm. It does not alter the sign-insensitivity of the underlying upper-bound sieve and does not produce a positive lower bound for prime pairs. Nothing here distinguishes an actual prime-pair main term from every parity-compatible surrogate at the final sieve stage. The theorem is a rigorous advance inside the TPC normal form, not a claim to have crossed the classical parity barrier.

# Exact finite certificate and conclusion

The proofs above are symbolic. A deterministic finite certificate is included to audit the identities most vulnerable to normalization or coordinate errors. It is not numerical evidence for an unproved asymptotic and is not used to replace any analytic estimate.

## Certificate design

The script $$\texttt{experiments/tpc22\_certificate.py}$$ uses only the Python standard library. Roots of unity are represented exactly in $\mathbf F_{61}$, whose multiplicative group contains the orders needed for the squarefree test moduli. Rational exponent calculations use `fractions.Fraction`.

The primary model is $$q=30,\qquad (u,v)=(10,15),\qquad (g,a,b)=(5,2,3),$$ together with the reversed divisor pair. The certificate checks:

1.  the shared-$g$ convolution and [\[eq:exact-rectangularization\]](TPC-022.md#ref-45a3daab7cd59aba) character by character;

2.  both product-coordinate choices and exact conductor multiplication;

3.  the identification of the global principal character with the joint principal coordinate, while retaining one-sided principal modes;

4.  the induced-character Gauss formula at every residue frequency, including frequencies not coprime to $q$;

5.  the exact rational low–low, high–high, and mixed ledger;

6.  the two actual-tail profile endpoint intervals, gains, and arithmetic endpoint-stability thresholds.

Hard-failure variants deliberately omit compatibility, replace $b=v/g$ by $v$, omit the shared $g$-character, retain an extra $1/\varphi(g)$, or delete a one-sided principal block. Each altered formula is required to disagree with the exact model. The ordinary and `python -O` runs must produce byte-identical JSON, so correctness does not depend on Python assertions. All flags for a twin-prime result, Hardy–Littlewood asymptotic, full residual closure, or parity breakthrough are set to false.

## Conclusion

The shared-factor character convolution left by TPC-21 is not an irreducible fourth-moment obstruction. Pairwise character orthogonality turns it into an arbitrary compatibility matrix tested by two independent character coordinates. Exact conductor resolution then exposes two complementary estimates: $$Q\sqrt{JY\mathcal K_Q(F_1)\mathcal K_Q(F_2)}$$ from the inherited norm route, and $$QF_1F_2\sqrt{\mathcal K_Q(F_1)\mathcal K_Q(F_2)}$$ from the direct induced-Gauss route. Neither dominates everywhere, but their conductor cells cover the complete selected short–short range in the stated profiles with a fixed power margin.

This closes one concrete analytic gate and removes the arbitrary-fiber obstruction for the actual generic mask in that packet. The remaining route to the full fixed-shift problem still includes the large-divisor legs and global residual reassembly, followed eventually by the classical parity obstruction. Those are separate problems; no claim about them is encoded in the present closure theorem.
