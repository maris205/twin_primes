## Abstract

The matched divisor expansion of a primitive fixed-shift Möbius residual has coefficient $b_R(u)=-\mu(u)\log u$ beyond its finite Ramanujan cutoff $R=X^{1/2-\delta+o(1)}$. Earlier work closes only the selected packet $u,v\le R$. We replace the static support by a dynamic cutoff $S=X^{s+o(1)}>R$, without changing the finite model, and prove that the conductor-rectangularized tensor and induced-Gauss estimates remain valid for $u,v\le S$. If $Q=X^{\beta+o(1)}$, $J=X^{1-\beta+o(1)}$, and the inherited no-wrap and polynomial Fourier-tail hypotheses hold, the enlarged packet has a fixed-power saving whenever $s<1-\beta$ and $$\eta_S=\min\left\{  1-\frac{3\beta}{2},  \frac{\beta+1-4s}{2},  \frac{3-\beta-6s}{4}  \right\}>0.$$ Consequently every strict interior point of the earlier hybrid closure region admits a genuine fixed-power strip $S=RX^\rho$ with $\rho>0$. This closes parts of both mixed support quadrants and also the square $(R,S]^2$; it is the first estimate in this chain for divisor coefficients strictly beyond the sieve cutoff. When $3/5<\beta<2/3$, the hybrid argument reaches every fixed-power cutoff $S<JX^{-\eta}$.

For the remaining terms we prove an exact complementary-divisor normal form. On a row $n=mj+h$, the ultra-long tail is $$\sum_{\substack{u\mid n\\u>S}}-\mu(u)\log u  =\sum_{\substack{r\mid n\\r<n/S}}    -\mu(n/r)\log(n/r).$$ For two rows, fixing complementary divisors $r,s$ parameterizes the orbit by one residue class and produces two affine forms whose determinant is exactly $h(m_1-m_2)/(r,s)\ne0$. The unresolved coefficient is therefore an affine two-point Möbius correlation, not an arbitrary large-modulus fiber. Tao’s proved logarithmically averaged two-point Elliott theorem cancels every fixed complement fiber, and hence every fixed finite complement truncation. We also prove that the absolute value of the unweighted $r=s=1$ fiber for each fixed row pair has mass $\gg J\log^2J$, so a coefficientwise triangle inequality on that fiber cannot close it. The missing input is quantitative uniformity for polynomially growing rows and complement fibers in a sharp dyadic orbit window. No full residual-dispersion theorem, Hardy–Littlewood asymptotic, twin-prime lower bound, or general breach of sieve parity is claimed.

# Introduction

###### ref-e34f6e000e8f40b4

The primitive residual isolated in TPC-18–20 has an exact centered divisor expansion on every finite target horizon. If $$a(u)=-\mu(u)\log u,$$ then its common four-channel coefficient is $$b_R(u)=a(u)-\lambda'_R(u),  \qquad \lambda'_R(u)=0\quad(u>R).  \label{eq:intro-bR}$$ Thus $b_R(u)=a(u)$ beyond the finite Ramanujan cutoff. TPC-21 removes the deterministic Ramanujan mean of the selected packet $u,v\le R$, and TPC-22 closes its centered discrepancy in explicit parameter regions by exact-conductor rectangularization [`\citep{WangTPC18,WangTPC19,WangTPC20,WangTPC21,WangTPC22}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-023.md#cite-4abf939102bd1649); [\[WangTPC19\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-023.md#cite-09db42d5184e63ba); [\[WangTPC20\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-023.md#cite-7525f88be3fcfa5f); [\[WangTPC21\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-023.md#cite-8d248780f3293b33); [\[WangTPC22\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-023.md#cite-6ea7e7d72ef27c8f).

###### ref-f5a15c44d463bc6e

The support remaining after that result has four quadrants: $$\begin{array}{c|cc}   &v\le R&v>R\\ \hline  u\le R&\mathrm{SS}&\mathrm{SL}\\  u>R&\mathrm{LS}&\mathrm{LL}.  \end{array}  \label{eq:intro-four-quadrants}$$ Only SS was previously closed. In particular, both-large terms exist: one may take $u=m_1j+h$ and $v=m_2j+h$. We therefore avoid the ambiguous phrase “one-sided large-divisor terms” for the whole complement. We also distinguish support legs from the *single-indicator Fourier channels* completed in TPC-20; the latter theorem did not estimate SL or LS in [\[eq:intro-four-quadrants\]](TPC-023.md#ref-f5a15c44d463bc6e).

## A dynamic cutoff

###### ref-5efcad13f44dd17d

Our first result moves the closed support itself. Choose $$R<S\le X^{1-o(1)},  \qquad \vartheta_{R,S}(u)=b_R(u)\mathbf 1_{u\le S}.  \label{eq:intro-dynamic-coefficient}$$ This does not replace the finite model cutoff $R$: it truncates the *residual* divisor coefficient at a later point. The enlarged packet $u,v\le S$ contains SS, both mixed collars, and the nonempty both-large square $(R,S]^2$.

Write $$Q=X^{\beta+o(1)},\qquad J=X^{1-\beta+o(1)},  \qquad S=X^{s+o(1)}.$$ The TPC-22 character coordinates, exact-conductor large sieve, and all-frequency induced-Gauss estimate remain valid with $S$ in place of $R$. The actual off-diagonal row-pair mask is essential: it gives $$\sum_{g\le S}\sum_{m,n}|c_{m,n}^{(g)}|^2  \ll Q^2X^\varepsilon$$ because $g\mid m-n$, not because $S$ is below the row length. No new hypothesis $L>2S$ is required for the hybrid argument. What changes is the denominator range $q\le S^2$, the conductor range $F_i\le S$, and the two-scale completion condition $HS\ll JX^{-\eta}$.

###### ref-6f5ac39ed38ba4d6

###### ref-68bb13ab2324b992

###### ref-f17628d3575b87ef

The resulting hybrid saving is $$\eta_S(\beta,s)=  \min\left\{  1-\frac{3\beta}{2},  \frac{\beta+1-4s}{2},  \frac{3-\beta-6s}{4}  \right\}.  \label{eq:intro-etaS}$$ There is also an occupancy route when $2s<1-\beta$, provided the stronger source margin $L>2S$ is imposed. Consequently, putting $$s=\frac12-\delta+\rho$$ gives a genuine strip beyond $R$. In the hybrid region it is enough that $$0<\rho<\min\left\{  \frac12+\delta-\beta,  \frac{\beta+4\delta-1}{4},  \frac{6\delta-\beta}{6}  \right\},  \label{eq:intro-rho-hybrid}$$ while the occupancy region permits $$0<\rho<\frac{2\delta-\beta}{2}.  \label{eq:intro-rho-occupancy}$$ Every bound is strict and carries a fixed margin. These formulas show that every strict interior point of the TPC-22 hybrid closure region has a positive collar. The occupancy branch has the same conclusion only when its additional source margin $L>2S$ is available. In either case, this is a rigorous estimate for genuinely new large-divisor coefficients under the inherited packet interfaces stated below.

## What the next support looks like

###### ref-4cc6adb4aa5e625b

###### ref-0d0e7ca16a235a8e

Let $n=mj+h$. The part beyond $S$, after a uniformly negligible scalar centering tail is removed, is $$\mathcal T_{m,S}(j)  =\sum_{\substack{u\mid n\\u>S}}a(u)  =\sum_{\substack{r\mid n\\r<n/S}}a(n/r).  \label{eq:intro-complement}$$ The second equality is exact and valid without assuming that $n$ is squarefree. Since $\mu(k)=\lambda(k)\mu^2(k)$, it also gives the Liouville-cocycle identity $$\mathcal T_{m,S}(j)  =-\lambda(n)  \sum_{\substack{r\mid n\\r<n/S}}  \lambda(r)\mu^2(n/r)\log(n/r).  \label{eq:intro-Liouville-cocycle}$$ Thus divisor reflection turns geometric size into a target-dependent parity phase.

###### ref-d0ffa0696602f26e

More usefully for two rows, fix complement divisors $r,s$. Their two divisibility conditions select either no orbit points or one class $j=j_0+[r,s]t$. The quotient divisors are affine forms $$U(t)=\frac{m_1(j_0+[r,s]t)+h}{r},\qquad  V(t)=\frac{m_2(j_0+[r,s]t)+h}{s},$$ and $$\det(U,V)=\frac{h(m_1-m_2)}{(r,s)}\ne0.  \label{eq:intro-affine-determinant}$$ The remaining LL coefficient is therefore $\mu(U(t))\mu(V(t))\log U(t)\log V(t)$. This exact normal form identifies the next analytic input: a quantitatively uniform two-point affine Möbius estimate for growing coefficients, summed over moving complement fibers.

For each fixed fiber, the logarithmically averaged estimate is already known: Tao’s two-point Elliott theorem applies because [\[eq:intro-affine-determinant\]](TPC-023.md#ref-d0ffa0696602f26e) is nonzero [`\citep{Tao2016}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-023.md#cite-0bccf4cf69786109). We record the corresponding log-weighted corollary and a finite-complement reassembly. We also prove that the absolute value of the $r=s=1$ fiber has positive squarefree density and mass of order $J\log^2J$. Hence a coefficientwise absolute-value estimate cannot close that component.

The organization is as follows. fixes the exact primitive packet. extends the Ramanujan-mean bound and removes the scalar ultra-long tail. prove the dynamic-cutoff closure and its exponent ledgers. derives the complementary affine forms. gives the fixed-fiber logarithmic theorem and the absolute-value no-go. states the remaining uniform gate and strict scope. The exact certificate and conclusion are in [9](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-023.md#src-c12ca1e925f811d0).

Fix $h\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}$, put $H=\operatorname{rad}|h|$, and choose $$R=\lfloor X^{1/2-\delta}\rfloor,  \qquad 0<\delta<\frac12.  \label{eq:R}$$ The finite Ramanujan model and residual are $$\Lambda_R(n)=\sum_{q\le R}\frac{\mu(q)}{\varphi(q)}c_q(n),  \qquad r_R(n)=\Lambda(n)-\Lambda_R(n).  \label{eq:finite-model}$$ In one separated primitive row packet, $$Q=LD,\qquad QJ\asymp X,\qquad  L>\max\{2R,2|h|\},  \label{eq:row-scales}$$ and the rows are $$m=\ell d\asymp Q,\qquad  \ell\in[L/2,2L]\text{ prime},\qquad  d\in[D,2D],\quad d\le R^{1/2}.  \label{eq:rows}$$ We impose $(m,H)=1$, $(j,H)=1$, and put $$n_m(j)=mj+h.  \label{eq:target}$$ Then $$(n_m(j),mH)=1.  \label{eq:target-coprime}$$ In particular, every divisor of a target is coprime to the appropriate row and to $H$; this exact fact replaces any unnecessary comparison between that divisor and the source prime $\ell$.

# Dynamic Ramanujan mean and scalar-tail removal

Two scalar issues must be settled before the conductor argument. The principal character of the enlarged packet has to remain harmless, and the centered coefficient beyond $S$ has to be replaced by its actual divisor sum without a formal deletion.

## The mean packet for $u,v\le S$

###### ref-70fb90eeca7ec8be

###### ref-2a6e9dee2e51e84b

The application has $$\mathcal Z_1\ll Q^2X^\varepsilon.  \label{eq:row-l1}$$ For each compatible pair $u,v\le S$, let $\mathfrak R_{u,v}$ denote its row-pair mass after the primitive and row-pair masks. If $q=[u,v]$, the unit-fiber mean is $$\overline Z_{q,S}  =\frac1{\varphi(q)}  \sum_{\substack{u,v\le S\\\operatorname{lcm}(u,v)=q}}  \vartheta_{R,S}(u)\vartheta_{R,S}(v)\mathfrak R_{u,v}.  \label{eq:dynamic-fiber-mean}$$ This is exact and uses no row equidistribution.

> **Lemma: Dynamic lcm cost** \[lem:dynamic-lcm-cost\] For $S\le X^{O(1)}$ and every $\varepsilon>0$, $$\boxed{  \sum_{u,v\le S}  \frac{|\vartheta_{R,S}(u)\vartheta_{R,S}(v)|        \tau(H[u,v])}{\varphi([u,v])}  \ll_{\varepsilon,H}X^\varepsilon.}  \label{eq:dynamic-lcm-cost}$$

###### ref-8d9de9c20e52235a

> **Proof** By [\[eq:b-soft\]](TPC-023.md#ref-1a2742a1fbbc48ac), each coefficient is $O(X^{\varepsilon/4})$ after shrinking the auxiliary epsilon. Also, uniformly for $q\le S^2\le X^{O(1)}$, $$\frac{\tau(Hq)}{\varphi(q)}\ll_{\varepsilon,H}\frac{X^{\varepsilon/4}}q.$$ Writing $u=ga,v=gb$, then dropping $(a,b)=1$, gives $$\sum_{u,v\le S}\frac1{[u,v]}  \le\sum_{g\le S}\frac1g  \left(\sum_{a\le S/g}\frac1a\right)^2  \ll\log^3(2S).  \label{eq:reciprocal-lcm}$$ Absorb the logarithms and coefficient factors into $X^\varepsilon$.

###### ref-2d9168a5312cecd0

> **Theorem: Dynamic mean closure** \[thm:dynamic-mean\] For any collection of denominators from the selected packet $u,v\le S$, $$\boxed{  \sum_q|\mathcal L_{q,S}^{\rm mean}|  \ll_{\varepsilon,F,H}\mathcal Z_1X^\varepsilon.}  \label{eq:dynamic-mean-bound}$$ Under [\[eq:row-l1\]](TPC-023.md#ref-70fb90eeca7ec8be), this is $O(Q^2X^\varepsilon)$, and therefore has a fixed-power saving relative to $XQ=Q^2J$ whenever $J$ has a fixed positive power of $X$.

## Uniform removal of the ultra-long scalar tail

###### ref-77c57de0ad0d4175

###### ref-7a9baf0b40df0ce7

Put $$A_T(m)=\sum_{\substack{u\le T\\(u,mH)=1}}\frac{a(u)}u,  \qquad \rho_{P,H}(m)=\frac{mH}{\varphi(mH)}.  \label{eq:AT}$$ The uniform Möbius calibration theorem of TPC-20, based only on the classical zero-free region for $\zeta(s)$, says that for every fixed $A,C>0$, uniformly for $mH\le T^C$, $$A_T(m)=\rho_{P,H}(m)  +O_{A,C}\left(\rho_{P,H}(m)(\log T)^{-A}\right).  \label{eq:uniform-calibration}$$ In the present fixed-power ranges, one fixed $C$ covers both $T=S$ and $T=U\asymp X$.

###### ref-5ed66d22dc763bd4

###### ref-1bbee8e98180b280

###### ref-2ac2091679a42cb3

###### ref-35f61d0d9bc1d163

> **Proposition: The centered ultra-long tail is its divisor tail** \[prop:scalar-tail\] Define $$\tau_{S,U}(m)=  \sum_{\substack{S<u\le U\\(u,mH)=1}}\frac{a(u)}u.  \label{eq:scalar-tail}$$ Then, for every $A>0$, $$\tau_{S,U}(m)  \ll_{A,h}\rho_{P,H}(m)(\log X)^{-A}  \ll_{A,h,\varepsilon}X^\varepsilon(\log X)^{-A}.  \label{eq:scalar-tail-small}$$ Consequently $$Z_m^{>S}(j)=\mathcal T_{m,S}(j)-\tau_{S,U}(m),  \qquad  \mathcal T_{m,S}(j)=\sum_{\substack{u\mid n_m(j)\\u>S}}a(u).  \label{eq:centered-to-divisor-tail}$$ Replacing every $Z_m^{>S}$ by $\mathcal T_{m,S}$ in a separated two-row packet changes it by $O_B(XQ(\log X)^{-B})$ for every prescribed $B>0$.

###### ref-a04263943edf8b51

> **Proof** Equation [\[eq:scalar-tail\]](TPC-023.md#ref-1bbee8e98180b280) is $A_U(m)-A_S(m)$, so [\[eq:scalar-tail-small\]](TPC-023.md#ref-2ac2091679a42cb3) follows by applying [\[eq:uniform-calibration\]](TPC-023.md#ref-7a9baf0b40df0ce7) at both endpoints. Divisors of $n_m(j)$ are automatically coprime to $mH$, which proves [\[eq:centered-to-divisor-tail\]](TPC-023.md#ref-35f61d0d9bc1d163).
>
> For the packet error, the elementary bounds $$|Z_m^{\le S}(j)|+|\mathcal T_{m,S}(j)|  \ll_\varepsilon X^\varepsilon  \label{eq:pointwise-signals}$$ follow from the divisor bound, [\[eq:b-soft\]](TPC-023.md#ref-1a2742a1fbbc48ac), and $\sum_{u\le U}|b_R(u)|/u\ll(\log X)^2$. Expand the product after inserting [\[eq:centered-to-divisor-tail\]](TPC-023.md#ref-35f61d0d9bc1d163), use $O(Q^2J)$ row–orbit tuples and $Q^2J\asymp XQ$, then choose $A$ in terms of $B$.

###### ref-758c195a5757ce7c

> **Remark: Calibration is not cancellation of the parity core** \[rem:calibration-not-parity\] The proposition removes only the deterministic densities $a(u)/u$. It does not estimate the signed divisor tail $\mathcal T_{m,S}$. In particular, its $u=n_m(j)$ term remains present whenever the target is squarefree.

# Conductor rectangularization at a dynamic cutoff

We now prove that the centered part of the selected packet extends from $u,v\le R$ to $u,v\le S$. Every step is performed before summing different lcm decompositions; there is no global character rectangle shared by all divisor pairs.

## Pairwise coordinates and compatibility energy

###### ref-0b039793bc899e96

###### ref-0b9e3568153a59fd

For one squarefree pair, use [\[eq:gab\]](TPC-023.md#ref-cc82c6baeff2b403). Since $g,a,b$ are pairwise coprime, $$\mathfrak X(q)\simeq\mathfrak X(u)\times\mathfrak X(b),  \qquad \chi\longmapsto(\alpha,\gamma),  \label{eq:product-characters}$$ and $$\operatorname{cond}\chi=(\operatorname{cond}\alpha)(\operatorname{cond}\gamma).  \label{eq:product-conductors}$$ The global principal character is exactly the pair of coordinate principals.

###### ref-cc190d322c8602de

###### ref-2565090b4153981f

Put $$c_{m,n}^{(g)}  =x_my_n\mathfrak m(m,n)\mathbf 1_{m\equiv n\pmod g}.  \label{eq:compatibility-matrix}$$ Character orthogonality on the shared factor gives the exact pairwise transform $$\widehat Z_{u,v}^{\,\times}(\chi)  =\overline{\chi(-h)}  \sum_{m,n}c_{m,n}^{(g)}\alpha(m)\gamma(n).  \label{eq:pair-rectangularization}$$ There is no residual $1/\varphi(g)$, and the character on the second row is not conjugated. This is the TPC-22 rectangularization, whose proof is purely finite-group algebra and is unchanged by $u,v>R$.

###### ref-d8f83196f9069244

###### ref-e7f8503f6f0ab68b

> **Proposition: Compatibility energy is cutoff-independent** \[prop:compatibility-energy\] Under [\[eq:row-energy\]](TPC-023.md#ref-cde3a11cdd11fadb)–[\[eq:mask\]](TPC-023.md#ref-c1c686fe419337bb), $$\boxed{  \sum_{g\le S}\sum_{m,n}|c_{m,n}^{(g)}|^2  \ll_\varepsilon Q^2X^\varepsilon.}  \label{eq:compatibility-energy}$$ No comparison $S<L/2$ is required.

> **Proof** The mask removes $m=n$. Hence $$\sum_{g\le S}\sum_{m,n}|c_{m,n}^{(g)}|^2  \le\sum_{m\ne n}|x_m|^2|y_n|^2        \sum_{g\mid m-n}1.$$ The last multiplicity is at most $\tau(|m-n|)\ll_\varepsilon X^\varepsilon$, independently of $S$. Apply [\[eq:row-energy\]](TPC-023.md#ref-cde3a11cdd11fadb).

The row-pair-only nature of the mask is used here and below. If the mask changed with $u,v,q$, the same matrix would no longer be tested over the ambient character moduli, and the tensor step would not follow.

## The two estimates for one conductor cell

###### ref-73e4752641863f34

We recall the weighted exact-conductor large sieve in the normalization needed here. If $w\asymp W$ runs over squarefree moduli, $a_n$ is supported on an interval of length $N$, and exact conductors lie in a dyadic block $f\asymp F$, then $$\sum_{w\asymp W}\frac1{\varphi(w)}  \sum_{\substack{\chi\in\mathfrak X(w)\\\operatorname{cond}\chi\asymp F}}  \left|\sum_na_n\chi(n)\right|^2  \ll_\varepsilon X^\varepsilon\left(F+\frac NF\right)  \sum_n|a_n|^2.  \label{eq:exact-conductor-LS}$$ The block $F=1$ contains conductor one. The proof writes $w=fd$, applies the primitive multiplicative large sieve at the true conductor $f$, and sums the inducing factors $d$; it is uniform in the ambient modulus $W$. This is Theorem 4.1 of TPC-22 [`\citep{WangTPC22}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-023.md#cite-6ea7e7d72ef27c8f), derived there from the primitive multiplicative large sieve [`\citep[Chapter~7]{IwaniecKowalski2004}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-023.md#cite-49dfea87eaada156).

###### ref-277f924648dd50a9

###### ref-f1a944f684addffe

Put $$\mathcal K_Q(F)=F+\frac QF.  \label{eq:KQ}$$ For $q=gab\asymp Y$, $ga,gb\le S$, let $\mathcal E_{Y,S}(F_1,F_2)$ be the sum of the squared transforms [\[eq:pair-rectangularization\]](TPC-023.md#ref-2565090b4153981f), weighted by $$\frac{|\vartheta_{R,S}(ga)\vartheta_{R,S}(gb)|^2}{\varphi(gab)},  \label{eq:cell-weight}$$ over all pairwise-coprime $g,a,b$, coordinate characters with $\operatorname{cond}\alpha\asymp F_1$, $\operatorname{cond}\gamma\asymp F_2$, and all inherited admissibility conditions. This is a sum of pair energies, not the norm of a fictitious common projection.

###### ref-fad93340e14e997e

###### ref-d6367fc25225962a

> **Theorem: Dynamic tensor conductor energy** \[thm:dynamic-tensor-energy\] Uniformly for $Y\le S^2$ and $1\le F_i\le S$, $$\boxed{  \mathcal E_{Y,S}(F_1,F_2)  \ll_\varepsilon Q^2X^\varepsilon  \mathcal K_Q(F_1)\mathcal K_Q(F_2).}  \label{eq:dynamic-tensor-energy}$$

> **Proof** Fix $g$, enlarge the nonnegative sum by dropping divisibility and coprimality restrictions among the ambient moduli, and apply [\[eq:exact-conductor-LS\]](TPC-023.md#ref-73e4752641863f34) in the $m$-coordinate. Its Hilbert-valued form permits the entire $n$-coordinate to remain as a vector. Apply the same theorem in the $n$-coordinate. This gives $$X^\varepsilon\mathcal K_Q(F_1)\mathcal K_Q(F_2)  \sum_{m,n}|c_{m,n}^{(g)}|^2.$$ Sum in $g$, use [\[prop:compatibility-energy\]](TPC-023.md#ref-d8f83196f9069244), and absorb the dyadic and divisor-pair multiplicities into $X^\varepsilon$. The soft coefficient bound [\[eq:b-soft\]](TPC-023.md#ref-1a2742a1fbbc48ac) is the only property of $\vartheta_{R,S}$ used.

###### ref-a6a6683de4ace7c4

###### ref-6b6ef327c2c10244

Let $\mathcal L_{u,v}^{F_1,F_2}$ denote the scalar functional [\[eq:dynamic-scalar-packet\]](TPC-023.md#ref-675353d2409589f9) formed from the $(F_1,F_2)$-coordinate-character projection of the unweighted centered pair $D_{u,v}$; the dynamic divisor coefficients are inserted externally below. Define the pair-separated cell sum $$\mathfrak L_{Y,S}(F_1,F_2)  =  \sum_{\substack{g,a,b\ {\rm pairwise\ coprime}\\                   ga,gb\le S,\ gab\asymp Y}}  |\vartheta_{R,S}(ga)\vartheta_{R,S}(gb)|  |\mathcal L_{ga,gb}^{F_1,F_2}|.  \label{eq:dynamic-cell-definition}$$ The inherited restricted-frequency norm route, licensed by [\[eq:no-wrap\]](TPC-023.md#ref-a910d27babd2d450) and the polynomial tail-mass assumption, gives $$\mathfrak L_{Y,S}(F_1,F_2)  \ll_\varepsilon QX^\varepsilon  \sqrt{JY\mathcal K_Q(F_1)\mathcal K_Q(F_2)}.  \label{eq:dynamic-norm-route}$$

###### ref-9cb7d9cf4f423a47

###### ref-7d9e3ffc9fad5259

There is a second estimate. If a character modulo the squarefree $q$ has exact conductor $f\mid q$, its additive transform at every nonzero frequency factors into a primitive Gauss sum of size $\sqrt f$ and a Ramanujan sum modulo $q/f$. Summing that Ramanujan factor against the smooth Fourier weight gives $$\sum_{r\ne0}|c_d(r)|  \left|\widehat F\left(\frac{rJ}{Hq}\right)\right|  \ll_{F,H} \frac{Hq}{J}\tau(d).  \label{eq:Ramanujan-frequency}$$ The zero frequency must remain excluded. Resolving exact conductors before this sum and applying [\[thm:dynamic-tensor-energy\]](TPC-023.md#ref-fad93340e14e997e) gives $$\mathfrak L_{Y,S}(F_1,F_2)  \ll_\varepsilon QF_1F_2X^\varepsilon  \sqrt{\mathcal K_Q(F_1)\mathcal K_Q(F_2)}.  \label{eq:dynamic-Gauss-route}$$ This is exactly the all-frequency argument of Lemmas 6.1–6.2 and Theorem 6.3 of TPC-22 [`\citep{WangTPC22}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-023.md#cite-6ea7e7d72ef27c8f): replacing $R$ by $S$ changes only the ambient ranges $q\le S^2$ and $F_i\le S$. The Ramanujan-frequency sum in [\[eq:Ramanujan-frequency\]](TPC-023.md#ref-9cb7d9cf4f423a47) cancels the Poisson normalization, while the two primitive Gauss factors and Cauchy–Schwarz contribute $F_1F_2$.

###### ref-7c6e03391a483b2e

###### ref-3d259bc133468b62

Combining the two estimates for the same scalar cell proves the dynamic cell bound $$\boxed{  \mathfrak L_{Y,S}(F_1,F_2)  \ll_\varepsilon QX^\varepsilon  \sqrt{\mathcal K_Q(F_1)\mathcal K_Q(F_2)}  \min\{\sqrt{JY},F_1F_2\}.}  \label{eq:dynamic-cell-bound}$$ The omitted block $(F_1,F_2)=(1,1)$ is the principal mean already treated in [\[thm:dynamic-mean\]](TPC-023.md#ref-1516eeed79d16006); one-sided principal coordinate cells remain in [\[eq:dynamic-cell-bound\]](TPC-023.md#ref-7c6e03391a483b2e). Linearity and the pairwise triangle inequality give the formal reassembly $$\sum_{q\asymp Y}|\mathcal L_{q,S}^{\rm disc}|  \le  \sum_{\substack{F_1,F_2\\(F_1,F_2)\ne(1,1)}}  \mathfrak L_{Y,S}(F_1,F_2)  +O_A(X^{-A}\mathcal Z_1),  \label{eq:dynamic-cell-reassembly}$$ where the final term is the inherited polynomial Fourier-tail error.

###### ref-8df2c0b7ad888279

> **Remark: What was needed to cross $R$** \[rem:no-L2S\] Neither the character algebra nor the energy estimates require $L>2S$. The inherited condition $L>2R$ belongs to the row/source construction. For a target divisor $u>R$, the necessary primitivity is supplied by [\[eq:target-coprime\]](TPC-023.md#ref-2d2870669332e0d9). The genuinely new analytic restriction is the Poisson-short condition [\[eq:S-single-leg\]](TPC-023.md#ref-ed67a9f22afda229).

# Exponent ledger and a closed strip beyond $R$

###### ref-2ea112f220fd16e8

###### ref-2216487c0f2c2a20

###### ref-5d9133fcff984bc7

###### ref-2054a2e46a6589d8

Write $$Q=X^{\beta+o(1)},\qquad J=X^{1-\beta+o(1)},  \qquad S=X^{s+o(1)},  \label{eq:exponent-main}$$ and, on a denominator and conductor cell, $$Y=X^{y+o(1)},\qquad F_i=X^{f_i+o(1)}.  \label{eq:exponent-cells}$$ The dynamic support gives $$0\le y\le2s,\qquad 0\le f_i\le s.  \label{eq:dynamic-ranges}$$ Put $$k_i=\max\{f_i,\beta-f_i\};  \label{eq:ki}$$ then $\mathcal K_Q(F_i)=X^{k_i+o(1)}$.

###### ref-8c953979a2449eb9

The natural scale has exponent $1+\beta$. The norm and direct-Gauss bounds in [\[eq:dynamic-cell-bound\]](TPC-023.md#ref-7c6e03391a483b2e) have respective exponents $$\beta+\frac{1-\beta+y+k_1+k_2}{2},  \qquad  \beta+f_1+f_2+\frac{k_1+k_2}{2}.$$ Their saving exponents are therefore $$\eta_A=\frac{\beta+1-y-k_1-k_2}{2},  \qquad  \eta_B=1-f_1-f_2-\frac{k_1+k_2}{2}.  \label{eq:etaAB}$$

###### ref-a5dac0bf2b942fcb

###### ref-46f1a7dae8185f30

###### ref-fff0228b5a6f0a54

> **Proposition: Dynamic three-cell ledger** \[prop:dynamic-ledger\] Every cell in [\[eq:dynamic-ranges\]](TPC-023.md#ref-5d9133fcff984bc7) satisfies $$\max\{\eta_A,\eta_B\}\ge \eta_S(\beta,s),  \label{eq:etaS-bound}$$ where $$\boxed{  \eta_S(\beta,s)=  \min\left\{  1-\frac{3\beta}{2},  \frac{\beta+1-4s}{2},  \frac{3-\beta-6s}{4}  \right\}.}  \label{eq:etaS}$$

###### ref-514c69beed4c2bd8

###### ref-214ef4bc572eda2c

> **Proof** Decreasing $y$ improves $\eta_A$, so take $y=2s$.
>
> If $f_1,f_2\le\beta/2$, then $k_1+k_2=2\beta-f_1-f_2$, and $$\eta_B=1-\beta-\frac{f_1+f_2}{2}  \ge1-\frac{3\beta}{2}.$$ If $f_1,f_2\ge\beta/2$, then $k_1+k_2=f_1+f_2\le2s$, so $$\eta_A\ge\frac{\beta+1-4s}{2}.$$
>
> In a mixed cell, write $x\le\beta/2\le z$ and $d=z-x\ge0$. Then $k_1+k_2=\beta+d$, whence $$\eta_A\ge\frac12-s-\frac d2.  \label{eq:mixed-A}$$ Since $z\le s$, direct substitution gives $$\eta_B=1-2z-\frac\beta2+\frac d2  \ge1-2s-\frac\beta2+\frac d2.  \label{eq:mixed-B}$$ The maximum is at least the average of the two displayed lower bounds, namely $(3-\beta-6s)/4$.

###### ref-02e7cb2268f47b28

###### ref-f3f329e029dca1a0

###### ref-3e5d97d4d529deb5

> **Theorem: Dynamic-cutoff closure** \[thm:dynamic-closure\] Assume the primitive interface of [2](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-023.md#src-e0f5dbff7527ee1b), including the row-pair-only off-diagonal mask, no-wrap condition, polynomial Fourier-tail mass, and the strict Poisson-short condition $$s<1-\beta  \label{eq:poisson-exponent}$$ with a fixed margin. If $$\eta_S(\beta,s)>0  \label{eq:hybrid-positive}$$ with a fixed margin, then some $\eta>0$ satisfies $$\boxed{  \sum_{q\le S^2}  \left|\mathcal L_{q,S}^{\rm disc}\right|  \ll_{\varepsilon,F,H}XQX^{-\eta+\varepsilon}.}  \label{eq:dynamic-discrepancy-closure}$$ Together with [\[thm:dynamic-mean\]](TPC-023.md#ref-1516eeed79d16006), this closes the selected packet $u,v\le S$, after the inherited dyadic and separated-packet reassembly.

> **Proof** Apply [\[prop:dynamic-ledger\]](TPC-023.md#ref-a5dac0bf2b942fcb) to every dyadic nonprincipal coordinate-conductor cell in [\[eq:dynamic-cell-reassembly\]](TPC-023.md#ref-3d259bc133468b62), using [\[eq:dynamic-cell-bound\]](TPC-023.md#ref-7c6e03391a483b2e). The fixed margin absorbs the $X^\varepsilon$, divisor-pair, and logarithmic conductor multiplicities. Condition [\[eq:poisson-exponent\]](TPC-023.md#ref-02e7cb2268f47b28) is precisely [\[eq:S-single-leg\]](TPC-023.md#ref-ed67a9f22afda229) at exponent level. The inherited no-wrap and tail-mass hypotheses license the norm route and discard the smooth Fourier tail. The principal cell is bounded by [\[thm:dynamic-mean\]](TPC-023.md#ref-1516eeed79d16006) at $Q^2X^\varepsilon=(XQ/J)X^\varepsilon$, which is also power-saving because $J=X^{1-\beta+o(1)}$.

There is a useful alternative on the short-period side. If the row source has the stronger margin $L>2S$, the TPC-21 deterministic occupancy proof extends with $S$ in place of $R$ and closes the enlarged packet under $$2s<1-\beta.  \label{eq:dynamic-occupancy}$$ The extra condition $L>2S$ is needed for that particular proof to replace $O(L/u+1)$ by $O(L/u)$; it is not needed in [\[thm:dynamic-closure\]](TPC-023.md#ref-894a72733fcf5ad3). We keep the hybrid theorem as the main cutoff-independent statement.

# Complementary divisors and affine parity fibers

The dynamic theorem stops before the Poisson-short or conductor margin is exhausted. We now give an exact normal form for what remains. No estimate in this section assumes that a target is squarefree.

## Hyperbola reflection

###### ref-ae27086cceb1e588

For $n\ge1$ and $Y\ge1$, put $$\mathcal T_Y(n)=\sum_{\substack{u\mid n\\u>Y}}a(u),  \qquad a(u)=-\mu(u)\log u.  \label{eq:TY}$$

###### ref-1dd1767929e0a4cd

###### ref-60b621732223ec66

###### ref-8877a5d063966b2e

> **Proposition: Exact complement and Liouville cocycle** \[prop:complement-cocycle\] For every $n,Y\ge1$, $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal T_Y(n)  &=\sum_{\substack{r\mid n\\r<n/Y}}a(n/r)  \label{eq:exact-complement}\\  &=-\lambda(n)  \sum_{\substack{r\mid n\\r<n/Y}}  \lambda(r)\mu^2(n/r)\log(n/r).  \label{eq:exact-Liouville-cocycle}\end{aligned}$$ Here $\lambda$ is the Liouville function. In particular, the sign on the reflected quotient is $\mu(n/r)$, not $\mu(r)$.

> **Proof** The involution $u\mapsto r=n/u$ bijects divisors satisfying $u>Y$ with those satisfying $r<n/Y$, proving [\[eq:exact-complement\]](TPC-023.md#ref-60b621732223ec66). Since $\mu(k)=\lambda(k)\mu^2(k)$ and $\lambda$ is completely multiplicative, $$\mu(n/r)=\lambda(n/r)\mu^2(n/r)  =\lambda(n)\lambda(r)\mu^2(n/r).$$ Insert this identity into $a(n/r)$.

###### ref-f4478bfb8fbe1c78

###### ref-d61e806c2c2903f6

###### ref-3bacb50dc869afb6

If $n>Y^2$, the tail has the exact hyperbola split $$\mathcal T_Y(n)  =\underbrace{\sum_{\substack{r\mid n\\r\le Y}}a(n/r)}_{\mathcal C_Y(n)}  +\underbrace{\sum_{\substack{r\mid n\\Y<r<n/Y}}a(n/r)}_{\mathcal B_Y(n)},  \label{eq:balanced-split}$$ with the evident adjustment when a boundary divisor occurs. The first piece has a short complementary divisor. In the second, both factors of $n=r(n/r)$ exceed $Y$; it is a genuine Type–II hyperbola band. Pairing divisors about $\sqrt n$ gives $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal B_Y(n)  ={}&\sum_{\substack{d\mid n\\Y<d<\sqrt n}}  \{a(d)+a(n/d)\}  +\mathbf 1_{n=z^2,\ z>Y}a(z).  \label{eq:folded-balanced-band}\end{aligned}$$ On a squarefree target, the paired coefficient is $$a(d)+a(n/d)  =-\lambda(d)\{\log d+\lambda(n)\log(n/d)\}.  \label{eq:squarefree-parity-pair}$$ Thus even and odd factor parity are different eigensectors of divisor reflection. This is an identity, not a bound for either sector.

For the TPC target $n\asymp X$ and $Y=R=X^{1/2-\delta}$, the balanced factor exponents lie between $1/2-\delta$ and $1/2+\delta$. The dynamic theorem removes a strict lower part of this geometry before the quotient-Möbius normal form is needed.

## Two complement fibers

###### ref-caf0c5097d285ef4

###### ref-e356d6e5eb6a8145

###### ref-b7c36e71d5b1ed73

Fix distinct positive primitive rows $m_1,m_2$, meaning $(m_1m_2,h)=1$, and write $$n_i(j)=m_i j+h\qquad(i=1,2).  \label{eq:two-targets}$$ Let $r,s\ge1$. Since $(m_1m_2,h)=1$, any solution of $r\mid n_1(j)$, $s\mid n_2(j)$ necessarily satisfies $$(r,m_1)=(s,m_2)=1.  \label{eq:complement-primitivity}$$ We restrict to such pairs and put $$d=(r,s),\qquad q=[r,s]=\frac{rs}{d}.  \label{eq:rsdq}$$

###### ref-98b0ec397cc3bcbc

###### ref-3aba8a2d9d5e68ab

###### ref-fc290f322aa3fb22

###### ref-bed3d8f1278b95af

###### ref-df33a7a17db1d6d5

###### ref-3a03b9a760f1ef72

###### ref-447d9af5762cfd84

> **Theorem: Exact affine complement fiber** \[thm:affine-complement-fiber\] The two congruences $$r\mid m_1j+h,  \qquad s\mid m_2j+h  \label{eq:two-complement-congruences}$$ are jointly soluble if and only if $$d\mid h(m_1-m_2).  \label{eq:complement-compatibility}$$ When they are soluble, they select a unique class $j_0\pmod q$. Writing $j=j_0+qt$, define $$\begin{aligned}  U_{r,s}(t)  &=\frac{m_1(j_0+qt)+h}{r}=A_1t+B_1,  \label{eq:U-form}\\  V_{r,s}(t)  &=\frac{m_2(j_0+qt)+h}{s}=A_2t+B_2.  \label{eq:V-form}\end{aligned}$$ These are integer affine forms with $$\boxed{  A_1B_2-A_2B_1  =\frac{h(m_1-m_2)}d\ne0.}  \label{eq:affine-determinant}$$ Consequently, for any finitely supported orbit weight $W$, $$\begin{aligned}  &\sum_jW(j)\mathcal T_S(n_1(j))\mathcal T_S(n_2(j))  \notag\\  &\quad=  \sum_{\substack{r,s\ge1\\(r,m_1)=(s,m_2)=1\\                   (r,s)\mid h(m_1-m_2)}}  \ \sum_{t\in\mathbb Z}  W(j_0+qt)  a(U_{r,s}(t))a(V_{r,s}(t))  \mathbf 1_{U_{r,s}(t)>S}  \mathbf 1_{V_{r,s}(t)>S}.  \label{eq:exact-two-fiber-reassembly}\end{aligned}$$ All terms with nonpositive targets or outside the finite horizon are understood to be absent.

> **Proof** Each congruence in [\[eq:two-complement-congruences\]](TPC-023.md#ref-3aba8a2d9d5e68ab) selects one class modulo its divisor. Their reductions modulo $d$ agree exactly when $-h\overline{m_1}\equiv-h\overline{m_2}\pmod d$. Since $(m_1m_2,d)=1$, multiplication by $m_1m_2$ shows that this is equivalent to [\[eq:complement-compatibility\]](TPC-023.md#ref-fc290f322aa3fb22). The Chinese remainder theorem then gives one class modulo $q$.
>
> The quantities in [\[eq:U-form\]](TPC-023.md#ref-bed3d8f1278b95af)–[\[eq:V-form\]](TPC-023.md#ref-df33a7a17db1d6d5) are integers, with $$A_1=\frac{m_1q}{r},\quad  B_1=\frac{m_1j_0+h}{r},\quad  A_2=\frac{m_2q}{s},\quad  B_2=\frac{m_2j_0+h}{s}.$$ Direct calculation gives $$A_1B_2-A_2B_1  =\frac{qh(m_1-m_2)}{rs}  =\frac{h(m_1-m_2)}d.$$ It is nonzero because the generic mask has $m_1\ne m_2$. Finally, expand both tails using [\[eq:exact-complement\]](TPC-023.md#ref-60b621732223ec66), group by $(r,s)$, and parameterize the common progression. The inequalities on $U,V$ are exactly the original requirements that the quotient divisors exceed $S$.

Because $a(U)a(V)=\mu(U)\mu(V)\log U\log V$, [\[eq:exact-two-fiber-reassembly\]](TPC-023.md#ref-447d9af5762cfd84) is an exact family of two-point affine Möbius correlations. The determinant content $d=(r,s)$ divides $h(m_1-m_2)$. On the primitive orbit $(j,H)=1$, it is coprime to $H$ and therefore divides the row difference. In a mixed large–short leg there is also the original divisor gcd $(U,v)\mid m_1-m_2$; the two contents can overlap when the target has square factors. They must not be declared coprime.

###### ref-6be6bedd43425193

###### ref-90114ada12755d9c

###### ref-0f091c3d98c85ff4

> **Remark: The $r=s=1$ parity core** \[rem:unit-complement\] The first complement fiber is always compatible and has $$U_{1,1}(j)=m_1j+h,\qquad  V_{1,1}(j)=m_2j+h.  \label{eq:unit-complement-forms}$$ Its coefficient is $$\mu(m_1j+h)\mu(m_2j+h)  \log(m_1j+h)\log(m_2j+h).  \label{eq:unit-parity-coefficient}$$ On prime targets, each individual factor $-\mu(m_i j+h)\log(m_i j+h)$ is the positive prime weight. Thus a generic assertion that this fiber is negligible by “Möbius randomness” would be a parity-level overclaim; the precise averaging and uniformity are indispensable.

# A logarithmic bridge and an absolute-value no-go

The affine normal form permits one rigorous step through the parity gate, but only in a weaker averaging regime and without the uniformity required by the TPC packet.

## Every fixed fiber cancels logarithmically

###### ref-da482338def5eab8

###### ref-8359b217f3946149

Tao proved the logarithmically averaged two-point Elliott conjecture [`\citep{Tao2016}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-023.md#cite-0bccf4cf69786109). In the special case needed here, if $$L_1(t)=A_1t+B_1,\qquad L_2(t)=A_2t+B_2,  \qquad A_1B_2-A_2B_1\ne0,  \label{eq:fixed-affine-forms}$$ are fixed and positive for large $t$, then $$\sum_{t\le T}\frac{\mu(L_1(t))\mu(L_2(t))}{t}  =o(\log T).  \label{eq:Tao-mu-correlation}$$ This is Corollary 5 of [`\citet{Tao2016}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-023.md#cite-0bccf4cf69786109), applied with $g_1=g_2=\mu$ and $\omega(T)=T$. The Möbius function satisfies the corrected strong nonpretentiousness condition used there; Tao deduces this from the classical zero-free region. A fixed initial shift may be made first if one of the constants $B_i$ is negative, so both forms lie in the positive integers throughout the sum.

###### ref-c911f7db5784047c

###### ref-92c2037caa961e3d

> **Proposition: Log-weighted cancellation on a fixed fiber** \[prop:fixed-fiber-log\] Under [\[eq:fixed-affine-forms\]](TPC-023.md#ref-da482338def5eab8), $$\boxed{  \sum_{t\le T}\frac{a(L_1(t))a(L_2(t))}{t}  =o((\log T)^3).}  \label{eq:fixed-fiber-log}$$ In particular, every fixed fiber in [\[thm:affine-complement-fiber\]](TPC-023.md#ref-98b0ec397cc3bcbc) has logarithmically averaged cancellation.

> **Proof** Set $$A(x)=\sum_{t\le x}\frac{\mu(L_1(t))\mu(L_2(t))}{t}  =o(\log x)$$ by [\[eq:Tao-mu-correlation\]](TPC-023.md#ref-8359b217f3946149). The weight $w(t)=\log L_1(t)\log L_2(t)$ satisfies $$w(t)\ll(\log(2t))^2,  \qquad w'(t)\ll\frac{\log(2t)}t$$ away from a fixed initial interval. Partial summation gives $$A(T)w(T)-\int_2^T A(t)w'(t)\,dt=o((\log T)^3).$$ The two minus signs in $a(L_1)a(L_2)$ cancel.

###### ref-298dbd214cf8df65

For a fixed complement cutoff $Z$, define $$\mathcal T_{m}^{[Z]}(j)  =\sum_{\substack{r\mid mj+h\\r\le Z}}a((mj+h)/r).  \label{eq:fixed-complement-truncation}$$

###### ref-cb6fa277be15debf

###### ref-4a6ac8ba1ab07e2b

> **Corollary: Finite complement reassembly** \[cor:finite-complement-log\] Fix $h\ne0$, distinct positive rows $m_1,m_2$ coprime to $h$, and $Z\ge1$. Then $$\boxed{  \sum_{j\le J}\frac{   \mathcal T_{m_1}^{[Z]}(j)\mathcal T_{m_2}^{[Z]}(j)}j  =o_{m_1,m_2,h,Z}((\log J)^3).}  \label{eq:finite-complement-log}$$

> **Proof** Expand the two finite divisor sums. For each fixed $r,s\le Z$, the two divisibility conditions are either incompatible or select one class $j=j_0+[r,s]t$. In the latter case, [\[thm:affine-complement-fiber\]](TPC-023.md#ref-98b0ec397cc3bcbc) gives two affine quotient forms with nonzero determinant. Also $$\frac1{j_0+[r,s]t}  =\frac1{[r,s]t}+O_{r,s}(t^{-2}).$$ Apply [\[prop:fixed-fiber-log\]](TPC-023.md#ref-c911f7db5784047c); the $t^{-2}$ error converges after the logarithmic weights. There are only finitely many pairs.

The fixedness can be relaxed only ineffectively by a diagonal argument.

###### ref-fdbc60372264a07a

###### ref-bb50cfacb24d0d0e

> **Corollary: An arbitrarily slow moving window** \[cor:slow-window\] For fixed $h\ne0$, there exists a nondecreasing function $K(J)\to\infty$ such that $$\max_{\substack{1\le m_1,m_2,Z\le K(J)\\m_1\ne m_2\\                   (m_1m_2,h)=1}}  \frac1{(\log J)^3}  \left|\sum_{j\le J}\frac{  \mathcal T_{m_1}^{[Z]}(j)\mathcal T_{m_2}^{[Z]}(j)}j\right|  \longrightarrow0.  \label{eq:slow-window}$$

> **Proof** For each integer $K$, [\[cor:finite-complement-log\]](TPC-023.md#ref-cb6fa277be15debf) holds uniformly over the finite set of triples $m_1,m_2,Z\le K$, with the maximum over an empty set interpreted as zero. Choose strictly increasing thresholds $J_K\ge K$ beyond which the normalized maximum is at most $1/K$, and set $K(J)=\max\{K:J_K\le J\}$, with $K(J)=1$ below $J_1$.

This is a genuine moving-parameter consequence, but it supplies no effective rate at all. It is many orders of magnitude weaker than the polynomial scales $m_i\asymp Q=X^\beta$ and $r,s\ll X/S$ in [\[eq:exact-two-fiber-reassembly\]](TPC-023.md#ref-447d9af5762cfd84).

## Why absolute values cannot replace the correlation

We next show that the unit-complement fiber is large after signs are removed. This is a componentwise obstruction, not a lower bound for the complete signed residual packet.

###### ref-5566d15f97b54269

###### ref-0bbef30f4261d0c1

For fixed primitive rows, let $\nu_p$ be the number of solutions $j\pmod{p^2}$ of $$(m_1j+h)(m_2j+h)\equiv0\pmod{p^2}.  \label{eq:nu-p}$$ Each form has at most one root modulo $p^2$, and it has none if $p\mid m_i$, because then $p\nmid h$. Hence $0\le\nu_p\le2$, and $$\mathfrak S_{\rm sf}(m_1,m_2;h)  =\prod_p\left(1-\frac{\nu_p}{p^2}\right)>0.  \label{eq:squarefree-singular-series}$$

###### ref-50bfe72990ea0aaf

###### ref-43971ab1fe82a676

> **Lemma: Simultaneous squarefree density** \[lem:simultaneous-squarefree\] For fixed $m_1\ne m_2$ with $(m_1m_2,h)=1$, $$\sum_{j\le J}  \mu^2(m_1j+h)\mu^2(m_2j+h)  =\mathfrak S_{\rm sf}(m_1,m_2;h)J+o(J).  \label{eq:simultaneous-squarefree}$$

> **Proof** For a fixed prime bound $P$, the Chinese remainder theorem gives the exact density $\prod_{p\le P}(1-\nu_p/p^2)$ of classes avoiding all square divisors $p^2$ with $p\le P$, up to $O_P(1/J)$. The number of $j\le J$ for which one of the two forms is divisible by $p^2$ for some $p>P$ is $$\ll\sum_{P<p\ll\sqrt J}\left(\frac J{p^2}+1\right)  =O(J/P)+O(\sqrt J/\log J),$$ where the implied constants may depend on the fixed rows and $h$. Let $J\to\infty$ and then $P\to\infty$. Absolute convergence and positivity of [\[eq:squarefree-singular-series\]](TPC-023.md#ref-0bbef30f4261d0c1) follow from $\nu_p\le2$.

###### ref-e3af90f367ea7469

###### ref-144c04dab3d14803

> **Proposition: Absolute-mass no-go** \[prop:absolute-nogo\] For the unit-complement coefficient in [\[eq:unit-parity-coefficient\]](TPC-023.md#ref-0f091c3d98c85ff4), $$\boxed{  \sum_{J/2<j\le J}  \left|\mu(m_1j+h)\mu(m_2j+h)\right|  \log(m_1j+h)\log(m_2j+h)  \gg_{m_1,m_2,h}J(\log J)^2.}  \label{eq:absolute-nogo}$$ Therefore a fiberwise triangle inequality or sign-blind estimate cannot make this fixed, unweighted component power-saving.

> **Proof** By [\[lem:simultaneous-squarefree\]](TPC-023.md#ref-50bfe72990ea0aaf), a positive proportion of the integers in $(J/2,J]$ make both forms squarefree. On that interval both logarithms are $\asymp\log J$, and $|\mu(n)|=\mu^2(n)$.

There is no contradiction between \[[prop:fixed-fiber-log](TPC-023.md#ref-c911f7db5784047c),[prop:absolute-nogo](TPC-023.md#ref-e3af90f367ea7469)\]: the first retains the product of the two Möbius signs and uses logarithmic averaging; the second deliberately removes exactly that cancellation.

# The remaining uniform gate and a stepwise roadmap

The dynamic theorem and complement theorem meet at a precise boundary. Inside $u,v\le S$, exact-conductor averaging supplies a fixed-power saving. Outside, divisor reflection exposes affine Möbius correlations. This section records the order of operations needed to continue and the conclusions that are not available.

## A quantitative form of the next gate

###### ref-cea77f5ebf6e0d45

###### ref-2067ee1a63b9ce31

For a compatible complement pair, define the sharp fiber sum $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal M_{m_1,m_2;r,s}(W;S)  :={}&\sum_t W\left(\frac{j_0+[r,s]t}{J}\right)  \mu(U_{r,s}(t))\mu(V_{r,s}(t))  \notag\\  &\times\log U_{r,s}(t)\log V_{r,s}(t)  \mathbf 1_{U_{r,s}(t)>S}\mathbf 1_{V_{r,s}(t)>S}.  \label{eq:sharp-affine-fiber}\end{aligned}$$ The smooth weight $W$ ranges over a fixed family with uniformly bounded support and derivative seminorms. The forms and their nonzero determinant are given by [\[thm:affine-complement-fiber\]](TPC-023.md#ref-98b0ec397cc3bcbc). The fiber has length $$\asymp 1+\frac{J}{[r,s]}.  \label{eq:fiber-length}$$

Tao’s theorem controls [\[eq:sharp-affine-fiber\]](TPC-023.md#ref-cea77f5ebf6e0d45) when every coefficient is fixed, the average is cumulative logarithmic, and the limit is taken before the coefficients grow. The TPC packet instead requires all of the following simultaneously:

1.  $m_i\asymp Q=X^{\beta+o(1)}$ and complement divisors as large as $X/S$;

2.  a prescribed smooth dyadic window of length $J$, not merely a cumulative $dt/t$ average;

3.  a rate strong enough to sum the row pairs, complement pairs, determinant-content refinements, and separated smooth packets;

4.  a separate treatment of the sparse range $[r,s]\gtrsim J$, where a fiber has at most $O(1)$ orbit points and no one-fiber asymptotic can create cancellation.

The next useful theorem would therefore be a *uniform affine Möbius dispersion estimate*, not a pointwise appeal to randomness. One possible sufficient interface is the following.

###### ref-c62e12e0171ebb8e

###### ref-891ccaf3eb33c403

> **Assumption: Dyadic affine-fiber dispersion** \[ass:uniform-affine-dispersion\] After inserting the actual row weights and generic row-pair mask, every dyadic block of compatible complement moduli with $[r,s]\le JX^{-\kappa}$ has total contribution from [\[eq:sharp-affine-fiber\]](TPC-023.md#ref-cea77f5ebf6e0d45) at most $$XQ(\log X)^{-B}  \label{eq:uniform-affine-gate}$$ for arbitrarily prescribed $B>0$, uniformly over the separated weights. The complementary sparse blocks admit the same total bound after using their exact determinant and incidence geometry rather than a one-fiber asymptotic.

This is deliberately an input, not a theorem of the present paper. It keeps the two Möbius signs together and specifies the growing parameters, sharp averaging, coefficient summation, and sparse regime that a usable result must cover.

## Why several shortcuts fail

1.  *Taking absolute values fiber by fiber.* gives natural-scale mass already on $r=s=1$. The two Möbius signs are the mechanism, not a nuisance that can be discarded.

2.  *Replacing the reflected sign by $\mu(r)$.* The exact coefficient is $\mu(n/r)$; see [\[eq:exact-complement\]](TPC-023.md#ref-60b621732223ec66). The Liouville rewriting introduces the global target phase $\lambda(n)$ and a squarefree quotient cutoff. It does not turn the coefficient into an ordinary short-divisor Möbius sum.

3.  *Assuming the two determinant contents are coprime.* In a mixed large–short term, both the complement gcd and the quotient–short-divisor gcd divide $m_1-m_2$, but they may overlap when the target has a square factor. Declaring them coprime silently deletes valid nonsquarefree targets.

4.  *Applying TPC-22 with an arbitrarily large cutoff.* The dynamic conductor ledger loses its margin at the explicit faces in [\[eq:etaS\]](TPC-023.md#ref-fff0228b5a6f0a54), and TPC-20’s Fourier-channel reduction loses the strict Poisson-short condition at $S\asymp J$. Beyond those faces, additional cancellation between joint and single spectra or a new quotient-variable argument is required.

5.  *Using logarithmic Elliott as a black box for the full packet.* is intentionally ineffective. It supplies no bound uniform over polynomially many forms, and logarithmic averaging cannot simply be removed by partial summation.

## A staged continuation

The exact reductions suggest the following sequence of independent tasks.

###### ref-b50ca9e033c00546

1.  Push the dynamic cutoff to the largest certified $S$ on each row slice, using [\[thm:dynamic-closure\]](TPC-023.md#ref-894a72733fcf5ad3). This has been completed here for the full square $u,v\le S$.

2.  Split the remaining both-ultra terms by the complement modulus $q=[r,s]$, and the mixed terms by their complementary–short modulus. Preserve the row mask, the exact affine determinant $h(m_1-m_2)/(r,s)$, and the corresponding mixed gcd content.

3.  In $q\ll J$, seek a quantitative version of two-point Möbius cancellation that is uniform in the polynomial coefficients generated by the complement fibers. A logarithmically averaged or averaged-row theorem would already be publishable if its precise range exceeds the current slow diagonal window.

4.  In $q\gtrsim J$, use sparse incidence geometry, the equation $$m_2U r-m_1V s=h(m_2-m_1),  \label{eq:sparse-incidence-equation}$$ and the generic determinant/gcd pruning. A theorem here need not solve an individual two-point Chowla problem because each fiber is short; it must instead save after summing the family.

5.  Recombine the long and sparse complement blocks with the mixed $Z^{\le S}Z^{>S}$ terms. Only after this scalar recombination may one restore all separated packets and the earlier TPC-18 endpoint pieces.

6.  A full residual-dispersion estimate would still be an analytic upper/cancellation theorem. Converting it into a positive Hardy–Littlewood main term or an infinite twin-prime conclusion is a separate parity-sensitive step.

Each stage has a useful fallback. Failure of a uniform estimate can be recorded as a sharp exponent boundary or a componentwise no-go; success in a logarithmic, averaged-row, or shorter-parameter regime is still a rigorous intermediate theorem.

## Conclusion

The large-divisor support left after TPC-22 is not an indivisible black box. The exact-conductor method is stable under a dynamic residual cutoff, and its new exponent ledger proves that every strict interior point of the old hybrid closure region contains a nonzero collar beyond $R$. The enlarged square $u,v\le S$ includes two mixed pieces and a both-large piece. This is a genuine fixed-power analytic advance, not a numerical observation or a renamed short–short theorem.

At the next boundary, divisor reflection changes the problem. It shortens the complementary modulus but transfers the coefficient to the quotient $\mu((mj+h)/r)$. On two rows, the quotient variables are nonproportional affine forms with exact determinant $h(m_1-m_2)/(r,s)$. This identifies a real two-point parity cocycle. Known logarithmic Elliott theory cancels each fixed fiber and every fixed finite complement family; a diagonal argument even permits an ineffective slowly growing family. The absolute-mass theorem shows why the signs cannot be removed.

What remains is now narrower and more explicit: obtain a rate uniform over polynomially growing affine coefficients and complement fibers in the long range, and a summed incidence estimate in the sparse range. Either advance would move the full residual program forward. Neither is supplied here, and neither may be replaced by an assertion of random Möbius signs. Full residual reassembly, a positive prime-pair main term, and the twin-prime conjecture remain separate open stages.
