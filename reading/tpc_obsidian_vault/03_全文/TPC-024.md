## Abstract

Fix $h\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}$, put $H=\operatorname{rad}|h|$, and let $R=X^{1/2-\delta+o(1)}$ be the finite Ramanujan cutoff in the matched divisor expansion of a primitive fixed-shift Möbius residual. The earlier centered-block argument closes a square $u,v\le S$ under the condition that the common short prefix satisfies $HS<JX^{-\eta_0}$. We keep that prefix on one row but attach on the other row a new shell $S<v\le T$, where $T$ may exceed the Poisson length $J=X^{1-\beta+o(1)}$. We write $Q=X^{\beta+o(1)}$, so that $QJ\asymp X$.

The correct object is the exact calibrated-prefix increment. If $P_{m,S}$ is the calibrated residual prefix and $L_{n;S,T}=P_{n,T}-P_{n,S}$, then pointwise $$P_{m,S}L_{n;S,T}  =\sum_{u\le S}\sum_{S<v\le T}  b_R(u)a(v)\mathbf 1_{u\mid mj+h}\mathbf 1_{v\mid nj+h}  -\delta_H(m)L_{n;S,T},$$ where $a(w)=-\mu(w)\log w$ and $b_R(w)=a(w)-\lambda'_R(w)$, with the inherited coprimality conditions understood. Thus the apparently surviving long single channel cancels algebraically; the remaining scalar drift satisfies $\delta_H(\ell d_0)\ll X^{o(1)}/L$. The joint nonzero channel admits an asymmetric exact-conductor rectangle. If $S=X^{s+o(1)}$, $T=X^{t+o(1)}$, and $s\le t$, its certified saving is $$\eta_{\rm asym}(\beta;s,t)=  \min\left\{  1-\frac{3\beta}{2},  \frac{\beta+1-2s-2t}{2},  \frac{3-\beta-s-5t}{4}  \right\}.$$

The additive zero frequency is not covered by the nonzero Ramanujan–Gauss estimate. We prove a separate shell theorem. For each off-diagonal row pair, split the shared factor $g=(u,v)\mid m-n$ at $SX^{-\kappa}$, where $0<\kappa<s$. For small $g$, uniform Möbius calibration at the two endpoints $S/g$ and $T/g$ cancels the common main constant. For large $g$, divisibility of the nonzero row determinant gives a factor $X^\kappa/S$. If $\mathcal Z_1$ is the absolute row-pair mass, then for every fixed $A>0$ the resulting zero-mode bound is $$\ll J\mathcal Z_1  \left\{(\log X)^{-A}+X^\kappa S^{-1}X^{o(1)}\right\}.$$ This mass factor is not hidden: the usual soft estimate $\mathcal Z_1\ll Q^2X^\varepsilon$ does not turn the first term into logarithmic saving. When $\eta_{\rm asym}>0$ with fixed margins and the stated interfaces hold, the explicit fixed-projective-mass hypothesis $\mathcal Z_1\ll Q^2(\log X)^K$ makes the entire calibrated mixed increment $O_B(XQ(\log X)^{-B})$ for every fixed $B>0$. With only the soft mass hypothesis, test functions with $\widehat F(0)=0$ give fixed-power closure; the displayed zero mode remains the sole mass-sensitive term in general.

For $S=R$ and $T=RX^\xi$, the joint saving becomes $$\min\left\{  1-\frac{3\beta}{2},  \frac{\beta+4\delta-1}{2}-\xi,  \frac{6\delta-\beta-5\xi}{4}  \right\}.$$ Consequently there is a strict one-sided passage beyond $T=J$ when $$\frac12+\delta-\beta<\xi<  \min\left\{  \frac{\beta+4\delta-1}{2},  \frac{6\delta-\beta}{5}  \right\},$$ provided $\beta<2/3$, $\beta<1/2+\delta$, and all inequalities have fixed margins. This is an incremental mixed-shell estimate, not a closure of the full residual, a Hardy–Littlewood asymptotic, a twin-prime lower bound, or a general breach of sieve parity.

# Introduction

The primitive residual program has reached two different analytic boundaries. The first is a conductor boundary: after exact shared-factor rectangularization, two complementary estimates for the same conductor cell can produce a fixed power saving. The second is a Poisson boundary: the four centered divisibility channels have periods $[u,v]$, $u$, $v$, and $1$, so a single channel is completed by smoothness only when its period is shorter than the orbit length.

###### ref-7cc60c821c122565

TPC-20 derived the exact four-channel formula and completed both single channels under $$H\max(u,v)\le JX^{-\eta_0}.  \label{eq:intro-both-short}$$ TPC-22 closed the resulting joint short–short packet by exact conductors, and TPC-23 moved the common cutoff from $R=X^{1/2-\delta+o(1)}$ to $S>R$, still subject to [\[eq:intro-both-short\]](TPC-024.md#ref-7cc60c821c122565) [`\citep{WangTPC20,WangTPC22,WangTPC23}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-024.md#cite-7525f88be3fcfa5f); [\[WangTPC22\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-024.md#cite-6ea7e7d72ef27c8f); [\[WangTPC23\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-024.md#cite-f24192cbd2a455cd). The longer single channel, not the conductor tensor, stops that centered-block argument at $S<J$.

###### ref-ed0f3dd3a392f6c2

###### ref-b2a5d12058132d09

This paper moves only one divisor leg. Let $$R\le S<T\le U,\qquad  S=X^{s+o(1)},\quad T=X^{t+o(1)},\quad 0<s\le t,  \label{eq:intro-ST}$$ where $U=X^{O(1)}$ is the finite target horizon. Put $$a(v)=-\mu(v)\log v,  \qquad  b_R(u)=a(u)-\lambda'_R(u),$$ with $\lambda'_R(u)=0$ for $u>R$. The new divisor region is the incremental rectangle $$u\le S,\qquad S<v\le T.  \label{eq:intro-mixed-shell}$$ It is essential that the second leg is a complete Möbius shell, not an isolated long modulus or an arbitrary dyadic subset.

## The calibrated increment is the stable object

###### ref-0d531b183983ab49

###### ref-aea52e7ccdd01965

###### ref-c66119eab00c219a

Let $P_{m,W}(j)$ be the locally calibrated residual prefix defined in [\[eq:calibrated-prefix\]](TPC-024.md#ref-a8dcbaf019b8251c). Its exact finite identity is $$P_{m,W}(j)  =\sum_{\substack{w\le W\\w\mid mj+h}}b_R(w)-\delta_H(m),  \qquad  \delta_H(\ell d_0)\ll_{\epsilon,h}\frac{X^\epsilon}{L}.  \label{eq:intro-calibrated-prefix}$$ Because the finite model ends at $R$, the increment is $$L_{m;S,T}(j):=P_{m,T}(j)-P_{m,S}(j)  =\sum_{\substack{S<w\le T\\w\mid mj+h}}a(w).  \label{eq:intro-exact-increment}$$ We study the separated packet $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal I_{S,T}:={}&  \sum_{m,n}x_my_n\mathfrak m(m,n)  \sum_{\substack{j\in\mathbb Z\\(j,H)=1}}F(j/J)  P_{m,S}(j)L_{n;S,T}(j).  \label{eq:intro-calibrated-packet}\end{aligned}$$ Its transpose is identical after exchanging the row labels.

###### ref-6de5437742f7b676

The pointwise product in [\[eq:intro-calibrated-packet\]](TPC-024.md#ref-c66119eab00c219a) has the exact expansion $$\begin{aligned}  P_{m,S}(j)L_{n;S,T}(j)  ={}&  \sum_{\substack{u\le S\\u\mid mj+h}}b_R(u)  \sum_{\substack{S<v\le T\\v\mid nj+h}}a(v)  -\delta_H(m)L_{n;S,T}(j).  \label{eq:intro-joint-minus-drift}\end{aligned}$$ This identity is the first structural result. If one starts instead from two bare centered divisor sums, a long $v$-single Poisson channel survives and is multiplied by a merely logarithmically calibrated short density. In the genuine prefix increment, the scalar calibration terms restore the omitted channels and cancel that long single exactly. What remains is one joint congruence lattice and the already power-saving row drift in [\[eq:intro-calibrated-prefix\]](TPC-024.md#ref-0d531b183983ab49).

## The asymmetric conductor rectangle

###### ref-b30f369094dd0623

For one compatible pair, write $$g=(u,v),\qquad u=gc,\qquad v=gd,\qquad q=[u,v]=g\,c\,d.$$ The TPC-22 character coordinates and weighted exact-conductor large sieve extend to the rectangle $u\le S$, $v\le T$. If $Q=X^{\beta+o(1)}$, $J=X^{1-\beta+o(1)}$, then every joint nonzero conductor cell has one of two complementary bounds. Their three-cell ledger gives $$\boxed{  \eta_{\rm asym}(\beta;s,t)=  \min\left\{  1-\frac{3\beta}{2},  \frac{\beta+1-2s-2t}{2},  \frac{3-\beta-s-5t}{4}  \right\}.}  \label{eq:intro-asym-saving}$$ Thus the joint nonzero discrepancy has a fixed power saving whenever $\eta_{\rm asym}>0$ with a fixed margin. No hypothesis $T<J$ or $L>2T$ occurs in this conductor estimate.

## The physical zero mode and the mass ledger

###### ref-628e425faff13e36

###### ref-3ee604c107f1e46f

###### ref-ed3a59ef8687c3f0

The induced-Gauss and Ramanujan-frequency estimates explicitly exclude additive frequency zero. For an off-diagonal row pair put $\Delta=m-n$. The zero-frequency kernel of the joint term in [\[eq:intro-joint-minus-drift\]](TPC-024.md#ref-6de5437742f7b676) is $$K^0_{S,T}(m,n)=  \sum_{\substack{u\le S,\ S<v\le T\\                   (u,mH)=(v,nH)=1}}  \frac{b_R(u)a(v)}{uv}  (u,v)\mathbf 1_{(u,v)\mid\Delta}.  \label{eq:intro-zero-kernel}$$ Choose $0<\kappa<s$. Write $u=gc$, $v=gd$, with $g\mid\Delta$. If $g\le SX^{-\kappa}$, then $S/g\ge X^\kappa$. For fixed $g,c$, the inner $d$-sum is a difference of uniformly calibrated Möbius sums at $T/g$ and $S/g$; their common main constant cancels. If $g>SX^{-\kappa}$, then the off-diagonal mask gives $$\sum_{\substack{g\mid\Delta\\g>SX^{-\kappa}}}\frac1g  \le \frac{X^\kappa}{S}\tau(|\Delta|).  \label{eq:intro-large-g}$$ Consequently, for every fixed $A>0$, $$\boxed{  K^0_{S,T}(m,n)  \ll_{A,\kappa}  (\log X)^{-A}+X^{-s+\kappa+o(1)}.}  \label{eq:intro-zero-bound}$$

###### ref-789134ebc70421d5

###### ref-9b8ff145c91e8f15

###### ref-ab8dc69703561be2

Let $$\mathcal Z_1=\sum_{m,n}|x_my_n\mathfrak m(m,n)|.  \label{eq:intro-Z1}$$ The full physical zero mode is therefore bounded by $$J\mathcal Z_1\left\{(\log X)^{-A}               +X^{-s+\kappa+o(1)}\right\}.  \label{eq:intro-zero-mass-bound}$$ The factor $\mathcal Z_1$ is deliberate. From the soft estimate $\mathcal Z_1\ll Q^2X^\epsilon$, one may close the large-$g$ term by choosing $\epsilon<s-\kappa$, but one may not convert $X^\epsilon(\log X)^{-A}$ into logarithmic saving. Under the stronger and independently checkable projective hypothesis $$\mathcal Z_1\ll Q^2(\log X)^K  \label{eq:intro-polylog-mass}$$ for a fixed $K$, the exponent $A$ can be chosen after $B,K$, and [\[eq:intro-zero-mass-bound\]](TPC-024.md#ref-9b8ff145c91e8f15) is $O_B(XQ(\log X)^{-B})$. This distinction is part of the theorem, not an $X^\epsilon$-notation convention. If $\widehat F(0)=0$, the entire mass-sensitive term vanishes and the soft row estimates suffice.

## A genuine passage beyond $J$

###### ref-f16ec8838797acb2

Specialize to $$S=R=X^{1/2-\delta+o(1)},\qquad T=RX^\xi.  \label{eq:intro-Rxi}$$ Then [\[eq:intro-asym-saving\]](TPC-024.md#ref-b30f369094dd0623) becomes $$\eta_{\rm asym}(\beta,\delta,\xi)=  \min\left\{  1-\frac{3\beta}{2},  \frac{\beta+4\delta-1}{2}-\xi,  \frac{6\delta-\beta-5\xi}{4}  \right\}.  \label{eq:intro-R-saving}$$ The old Poisson face is $$\xi_{\rm P}=\frac12+\delta-\beta,  \qquad T=J\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \xi=\xi_{\rm P}.$$ Suppose $$\beta<\frac23,\qquad \beta<\frac12+\delta,  \label{eq:intro-beta-conditions}$$ with fixed margins. Then the strict interval $$\boxed{  \frac12+\delta-\beta<\xi<  \min\left\{  \frac{\beta+4\delta-1}{2},  \frac{6\delta-\beta}{5}  \right\}}  \label{eq:intro-passage-window}$$ is a certified one-sided passage beyond $J$. A convenient nonempty region is $$\delta>\frac1{10},\qquad  \frac58-\frac\delta4<\beta<  \min\left\{\frac23,\frac12+\delta\right\}.  \label{eq:intro-nonempty-region}$$ For example, $(\delta,\beta,\xi)=(0.15,0.62,0.04)$ gives $$R=X^{0.35},\qquad J=X^{0.38},\qquad T=X^{0.39},  \qquad \eta_{\rm asym}=0.02.$$

## Scope and organization

The theorem treats the incremental mixed shell $[1,S]\times(S,T]$, and by transposition its mirror. Under [\[eq:intro-polylog-mass\]](TPC-024.md#ref-ab8dc69703561be2) it closes that calibrated increment, including physical frequency zero. Under only the generic soft mass it proves the fixed-power nonzero theorem and the exact mass-sensitive zero estimate [\[eq:intro-zero-mass-bound\]](TPC-024.md#ref-9b8ff145c91e8f15); this is not silently promoted to full closure. The result does not re-prove the old base square, control the both-ultra region, restore every endpoint of the TPC-18 residual [`\citep{WangTPC18}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-024.md#cite-4abf939102bd1649), or produce a positive prime-pair main term.

Fix $h\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}$, put $H=\operatorname{rad}|h|$, and choose $$R=\lfloor X^{1/2-\delta}\rfloor,\qquad  0<\delta<\frac12,\qquad R\ge H^2.  \label{eq:R-def}$$ In one separated primitive row packet, $$Q=LD,\qquad QJ\asymp X,\qquad  L>\max\{2R,2|h|\},  \label{eq:row-scales}$$ and $$m=\ell d_0\asymp Q,\qquad  \ell\in[L/2,2L]\text{ prime},\qquad  d_0\in[D,2D],\qquad d_0\le R^{1/2}.  \label{eq:row-support}$$ The inherited primitive conditions include $(d_0,H)=1$, $(j,H)=1$, and $d_0H\le R$. Hence $$(w,mH)=1  \qquad\text{for every }w\mid mj+h.  \label{eq:target-primitivity}$$

###### ref-cde3a11cdd11fadb

###### ref-bba276fd36f22f46

###### ref-c1c686fe419337bb

The separated row weights satisfy $$\|x\|_\infty+\|y\|_\infty\ll_\varepsilon X^\varepsilon,\qquad  \sum_m|x_m|^2+\sum_n|y_n|^2\ll QX^\varepsilon,  \label{eq:row-energy}$$ and their absolute pair mass is denoted by $$\mathcal Z_1:=\sum_{m,n}|x_my_n\mathfrak m(m,n)|  \ll_\varepsilon Q^2X^\varepsilon.  \label{eq:row-pair-mass}$$ Here $\mathfrak m$ is the actual bounded generic row-pair mask. We use only $$|\mathfrak m(m,n)|\le1,\qquad  \mathfrak m(m,m)=0,\qquad  \mathfrak m\text{ is independent of all divisor variables}.  \label{eq:mask}$$ The last two properties are essential in different places: the conductor tensor uses divisor independence, while the large-$g$ zero-mode argument uses $m-n\ne0$.

###### ref-b6bd4c253a6cc718

For the strongest logarithmic conclusion we state separately the fixed-projective-mass hypothesis $$\mathcal Z_1\ll Q^2(\log X)^K  \label{eq:polylog-projective-mass}$$ for some fixed $K\ge0$. If a scalar packet is reassembled from a projective sum $\sum_\nu x^{(\nu)}\otimes y^{(\nu)}$, the left side of [\[eq:polylog-projective-mass\]](TPC-024.md#ref-b6bd4c253a6cc718) is replaced by $\sum_\nu\|x^{(\nu)}\|_1\|y^{(\nu)}\|_1$. The soft estimate [\[eq:row-pair-mass\]](TPC-024.md#ref-bba276fd36f22f46) does not imply [\[eq:polylog-projective-mass\]](TPC-024.md#ref-b6bd4c253a6cc718); the two hypotheses are kept distinct throughout.

## Uniform endpoint calibration

###### ref-e221a27f0d5c8ff5

For $M\ge1$ and $Y\ge1$, set $$A_M(Y)=  \sum_{\substack{w\le Y\\(w,M)=1}}  \frac{-\mu(w)\log w}{w},  \qquad  B_M(Y)=  \sum_{\substack{w\le Y\\(w,M)=1}}\frac{\mu(w)}w.  \label{eq:ABM}$$

> **Lemma: Uniform calibration and its companion** \[lem:uniform-companion\] For every fixed $A,C>0$, uniformly for $Y\ge3$ and $M\le Y^C$, $$\begin{aligned}  A_M(Y)  &=  \frac M{\varphi(M)}  +O_{A,C}\left(  \frac M{\varphi(M)}(\log Y)^{-A}\right),  \label{eq:A-calibration}\\  B_M(Y)  &\ll_{A,C}  \frac M{\varphi(M)}(\log Y)^{-A}.  \label{eq:B-calibration}\end{aligned}$$ Consequently, if $Y_2\ge Y_1\ge X^\kappa$ and $M\le X^{C_0}$, where $C_0,\kappa>0$ are fixed, then for every $A>0$, $$\begin{aligned}  A_M(Y_2)-A_M(Y_1)  &\ll_{A,C_0,\kappa}(\log X)^{-A+1},  \label{eq:A-interval}\\  B_M(Y_2)-B_M(Y_1)  &\ll_{A,C_0,\kappa}(\log X)^{-A+1},  \label{eq:B-interval}\end{aligned}$$ uniformly with constants depending on $A,C_0,\kappa$.

###### ref-cab59c5224688b4d

> **Proof** Equation [\[eq:A-calibration\]](TPC-024.md#ref-662a58179fe52c94) is the uniform Möbius calibration theorem of TPC-20 [`\citep{WangTPC20}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-024.md#cite-7525f88be3fcfa5f). We recall why the same proof gives [\[eq:B-calibration\]](TPC-024.md#ref-5732403d24170adf). Let $$\mathscr S(M)=\{k\ge1:p\mid k\Longrightarrow p\mid M\},  \qquad  G_M(s)=\prod_{p\mid M}(1-p^{-s})^{-1}.$$ The exact convolution $$\mu(n)\mathbf 1_{(n,M)=1}=(\mu*\mathbf 1_{\mathscr S(M)})(n)$$ gives $$B_M(Y)=  \sum_{\substack{k\le Y\\k\in\mathscr S(M)}}  \frac1k  \sum_{r\le Y/k}\frac{\mu(r)}r.  \label{eq:B-convolution}$$ The classical zero-free region for $\zeta(s)$ implies $$\sum_{r\le Z}\frac{\mu(r)}r\ll_A(\log Z)^{-A}  \qquad(Z\ge3).$$ Split [\[eq:B-convolution\]](TPC-024.md#ref-cab59c5224688b4d) at $k=Y/\exp(\sqrt{\log Y})$. The central range is bounded with the displayed zero-free-region estimate. The remaining range is handled by the same Rankin bound for $G_M(1-\eta)$ used in the proof of [\[eq:A-calibration\]](TPC-024.md#ref-662a58179fe52c94); it is uniform for $M\le Y^C$. This proves [\[eq:B-calibration\]](TPC-024.md#ref-5732403d24170adf).
>
> Apply the first part with $C=C_0/\kappa$, and subtract [\[eq:A-calibration\]](TPC-024.md#ref-662a58179fe52c94) at $Y_2$ and $Y_1$: the common main term $M/\varphi(M)$ cancels. Do the same with [\[eq:B-calibration\]](TPC-024.md#ref-5732403d24170adf), use $M/\varphi(M)\ll\log\log(3M)$, and increase $A$ to absorb this factor. Since $\log Y_1\ge\kappa\log X$, the interval estimates follow. The only external analytic input is the classical zero-free region; see [`\citet[Chapter~5]{IwaniecKowalski2004}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-024.md#cite-49dfea87eaada156).

## The two surviving channels of a bare centered pair

###### ref-f1edeab341ccd6fb

###### ref-b70718ee8a514f8b

> **Theorem: Two surviving channels** \[thm:two-surviving-channels\] Assume [\[eq:short-side\]](TPC-024.md#ref-6549a01ba70e7238). Uniformly for the pairs above and every prescribed $A>0$, $$\begin{aligned}  &\sum_{\substack{j\in\mathbb Z\\(j,H)=1}}  F(j/J)C_{m,u}(j)C_{n,v}(j)  \notag\\  &\quad=  \frac{J}{Huv}\Bigg[  g\iota_{m,n}(g)  \sum_{r\in\mathbb Z}  c_H(r)e_q(r\overline H\rho)  \widehat F\left(\frac{rJ}{Hq}\right)  \notag\\  &\hspace{7.2em}  -\sum_{r\in\mathbb Z}  c_H(r)e_v(r\overline H\rho_2)  \widehat F\left(\frac{rJ}{Hv}\right)  \Bigg]  +O_{A,F,H,\eta_0}(X^{-A}).  \label{eq:two-surviving-formula}\end{aligned}$$ The joint sum is omitted when $\iota_{m,n}(g)=0$. The error remains arbitrarily power-saving after all polynomially many row and divisor sums in the selected packet. The additive zero frequency in [\[eq:two-surviving-formula\]](TPC-024.md#ref-b70718ee8a514f8b) is $$J\widehat F(0)\frac{\varphi(H)}H  \frac{g\iota_{m,n}(g)-1}{uv}.  \label{eq:pair-zero-mode}$$

> **Proof** The four exact natural Poisson lattices are the joint, $u$-single, $v$-single, and constant lattices of TPC-20 [`\citep{WangTPC20}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-024.md#cite-7525f88be3fcfa5f). In the normalization above their bracket is $$\begin{aligned}  &g\iota_{m,n}(g)\sum_r  c_H(r)e_q(r\overline H\rho)  \widehat F\left(\frac{rJ}{Hq}\right)\\  &\quad-\sum_r c_H(r)e_u(r\overline H\rho_1)  \widehat F\left(\frac{rJ}{Hu}\right)  -\sum_r c_H(r)e_v(r\overline H\rho_2)  \widehat F\left(\frac{rJ}{Hv}\right)  +\sum_r c_H(r)\widehat F\left(\frac{rJ}{H}\right).\end{aligned}$$ By [\[eq:short-side\]](TPC-024.md#ref-6549a01ba70e7238), every nonzero frequency in the $u$-single channel has Fourier argument at least $X^{\eta_0}$. The constant channel is shorter still. Repeated integration by parts removes their nonzero frequencies, including the fixed polynomial multiplicity. Their zero frequencies have coefficients $-1$ and $+1$, so they cancel. This proves [\[eq:two-surviving-formula\]](TPC-024.md#ref-b70718ee8a514f8b). Since $c_H(0)=\varphi(H)$, the two remaining zero modes give [\[eq:pair-zero-mode\]](TPC-024.md#ref-d086c65aea5f1841).

###### ref-44795f8277d738d2

> **Remark: Why the long channel cannot be deleted** \[rem:long-channel-retained\] When $v$ is comparable with or larger than $J$, the first nonzero Fourier argument $J/(Hv)$ need not grow. Smoothness alone therefore does not remove the second sum in [\[eq:two-surviving-formula\]](TPC-024.md#ref-b70718ee8a514f8b). Bounding a complete long Möbius divisor shell pointwise gives only $X^{o(1)}$, not a fixed power of $\log X$; this distinction cannot be repaired by changing the letter $\epsilon$.

> **Proof** Put $g=(u,v)$, $u=gc$, and $v=gd$. Exactness of the gcd and squarefree support make $g,c,d$ pairwise coprime. The coefficient identity $$a(gd)=\mu(g)a(d)-\mu(g)\log g\,\mu(d)  \label{eq:a-gd-split}$$ therefore turns the inner sum into $$\begin{aligned}  \mu(g)\{A_M(T/g)-A_M(S/g)\}  -\mu(g)\log g\{B_M(T/g)-B_M(S/g)\}.  \label{eq:inner-endpoint-difference}\end{aligned}$$ By [\[lem:uniform-companion\]](TPC-024.md#ref-92ca117479696b54), the main constants $M/\varphi(M)$ in the first difference cancel exactly; both differences have arbitrary logarithmic decay. Moreover, $$\frac M{\varphi(M)}\ll\log\log(3X),\qquad  \sum_{g\mid\Delta}\frac1g\ll\log\log(3X),$$ and [\[lem:prefix-mass\]](TPC-024.md#ref-a7537fef63c221fa) bounds the $c$-sum by a fixed power of $\log X$. Increasing the calibration exponent in terms of the desired $A$ proves $$K^{0,\,g\le G}_{S,T}(m,n)\ll_A(\log X)^{-A}.  \label{eq:small-g-zero}$$
>
> For $g>G$, use absolute values. The cofactor estimates $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_{c\le S/g}\frac{|b_R(gc)|}{c}  &\ll(\log(2X))^4,  \label{eq:large-g-c-mass}\\  \sum_{d\le T/g}\frac{|a(gd)|}{d}  &\ll(\log(2X))^2  \label{eq:large-g-d-mass}\end{aligned}$$ follow from [\[lem:prefix-mass\]](TPC-024.md#ref-a7537fef63c221fa) and a harmonic logarithmic sum. Since $\Delta\ne0$, $$\sum_{\substack{g\mid\Delta\\g>G}}\frac1g  \le\frac{\tau(|\Delta|)}G  =X^{-s+\kappa+o(1)}.  \label{eq:large-g-divisor-saving}$$ Combining [\[eq:small-g-zero\]](TPC-024.md#ref-43f55d28c509d1aa)– [\[eq:large-g-divisor-saving\]](TPC-024.md#ref-f84738c0a05c4154) proves the theorem.

# The asymmetric exact-conductor rectangle

> **Theorem: Asymmetric tensor conductor energy** \[thm:asymmetric-tensor-energy\] Uniformly for $Y\le ST$, $F_1\le S$, and $F_2\le T$, $$\boxed{  \mathcal E_{Y;S,T}(F_1,F_2)  \ll_\epsilon Q^2X^\epsilon  \mathcal K_Q(F_1)\mathcal K_Q(F_2).}  \label{eq:asymmetric-tensor-energy}$$

## Two estimates for the same nonzero cell

###### ref-bd65440c13e5c3c6

###### ref-f2a3ed751a34ef34

For a function $D$ on $\mathcal U_q$, use the multiplicative Fourier normalization $$\widehat D^{\,\times}(\chi)  =\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal U_q}D(\xi)\overline{\chi(\xi)},  \qquad  D(\xi)=\frac1{\varphi(q)}  \sum_{\chi\in\mathfrak X(q)}  \widehat D^{\,\times}(\chi)\chi(\xi).$$ For the fixed pair $u=gc,v=gd$, let $D_{u,v}^{F_1,F_2}$ be the inverse multiplicative transform of $\widehat D_{u,v}^{\,\times}$, restricted under [\[eq:asym-product-characters\]](TPC-024.md#ref-33cfe7bb85b1d007), to $$\operatorname{cond}\alpha\asymp F_1,\qquad  \operatorname{cond}\gamma\asymp F_2.$$ Define the corresponding retained-frequency scalar functional $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal L_{u,v}^{F_1,F_2,\le\omega}  :={}&  \frac{J}{Hq}\sum_{0<|r|\le X^\omega Hq/J}c_H(r)  \widehat F\left(\frac{rJ}{Hq}\right)  \sum_{\xi\in\mathcal U_q}D_{u,v}^{F_1,F_2}(\xi)  e_q(r\overline H\xi),  \label{eq:pair-cell-functional}\end{aligned}$$ and the pair-separated cell aggregate $$\begin{aligned}  \mathfrak L_{Y;S,T}^{\le\omega}(F_1,F_2)  :={}&  \sum_{\substack{g,c,d\ \mathrm{pairwise\ coprime}\\                   gc\le S,\ S<gd\le T,\ g\,c\,d\asymp Y}}  |b_R(gc)a(gd)|  |\mathcal L_{gc,gd}^{F_1,F_2,\le\omega}|.  \label{eq:asymmetric-cell-definition}\end{aligned}$$ This is a sum of pairwise scalar functionals, not the norm of a fictitious global rectangular projection.

###### ref-59fa0b1364d29e42

The restricted-frequency norm route, licensed by [\[eq:no-wrap\]](TPC-024.md#ref-a910d27babd2d450), gives $$\mathfrak L_{Y;S,T}^{\le\omega}(F_1,F_2)  \ll_\epsilon QX^\epsilon  \sqrt{JY\mathcal K_Q(F_1)\mathcal K_Q(F_2)}.  \label{eq:asymmetric-norm-route}$$

###### ref-68cce68c2b001d35

There is a second estimate. Resolve both exact conductors before summing additive frequencies. If $f_1=\operatorname{cond}\alpha$, $f_2=\operatorname{cond}\gamma$, and $e=q/(f_1f_2)$ is the inducing quotient, then at $r\ne0$ the induced Gauss sum is a primitive Gauss factor times the Ramanujan sum $c_e(r)$. The smooth nonzero-frequency estimate $$\sum_{r\ne0}|c_e(r)|  \left|\widehat F\left(\frac{rJ}{Hq}\right)\right|  \ll_{F,H}\frac{Hq}{J}\tau(e)  \label{eq:asym-Ramanujan-frequency}$$ then gives $$\mathfrak L_{Y;S,T}^{\le\omega}(F_1,F_2)  \ll_\epsilon QF_1F_2X^\epsilon  \sqrt{\mathcal K_Q(F_1)\mathcal K_Q(F_2)}.  \label{eq:asymmetric-Gauss-route}$$ This is the TPC-22 induced-Gauss argument with rectangular ambient ranges; restricting its left side to the retained frequencies only makes the absolute majorant smaller. Formula [\[eq:asym-Ramanujan-frequency\]](TPC-024.md#ref-2544c754af594d8e) must not include $r=0$, because $c_e(0)=\varphi(e)$; that frequency was treated in [4](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-024.md#src-e88460336e343304).

Combining the two estimates for the same scalar cell gives $$\boxed{  \mathfrak L_{Y;S,T}^{\le\omega}(F_1,F_2)  \ll_\epsilon QX^\epsilon  \sqrt{\mathcal K_Q(F_1)\mathcal K_Q(F_2)}  \min\{\sqrt{JY},F_1F_2\}.}  \label{eq:asymmetric-cell-bound}$$ One-sided principal coordinate cells $(1,F_2)$ and $(F_1,1)$ remain genuine discrepancy cells.

Let $\mathscr J_{q;S,T}^{\mathrm{disc},\ne0}$ denote the nonprincipal part of the joint $r\ne0$ packet at denominator $q$. Linearity, exact conductor decomposition, and pairwise triangle inequality give the formal reassembly $$\sum_{q\asymp Y}  |\mathscr J_{q;S,T}^{\mathrm{disc},\ne0}|  \le  \sum_{\substack{F_1,F_2\\(F_1,F_2)\ne(1,1)}}  \mathfrak L_{Y;S,T}^{\le\omega}(F_1,F_2)  +O_A(X^{-A}\mathcal Z_1).  \label{eq:asymmetric-cell-reassembly}$$ The last term is the single undecomposed smooth Fourier-tail error from [\[eq:Fourier-tail-interface\]](TPC-024.md#ref-be0a4ec638e9b333). It is inserted before the exact-conductor projection; the retained cells above therefore never require a pair-separated tail estimate.

The global principal cell $(1,1)$ is the Ramanujan mean. The rectangular form of the TPC-23 mean argument [`\citep{WangTPC23}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-024.md#cite-f24192cbd2a455cd) gives $$\sum_q|\mathscr J_{q;S,T}^{\mathrm{mean},\ne0}|  \ll_{\epsilon,F,H}\mathcal Z_1X^\epsilon,  \label{eq:asymmetric-principal-mean}$$ because $$\sum_{u\le S,\,v\le T}\frac1{[u,v]}  \ll\log^3(2ST).$$ Under [\[eq:row-pair-mass\]](TPC-024.md#ref-bba276fd36f22f46), this is $Q^2X^\epsilon$, a fixed power below $XQ=Q^2J$ whenever $J$ has fixed positive power.

# Exponent ledger and passage beyond the Poisson face

###### ref-65be07645ecd2171

###### ref-5c903ca8bc864b35

###### ref-c9490631a32d14c7

Write $$Q=X^{\beta+o(1)},\qquad J=X^{1-\beta+o(1)},  \qquad S=X^{s+o(1)},\qquad T=X^{t+o(1)},  \label{eq:asym-exponent-main}$$ with $0<s\le t$. On a denominator and conductor cell put $$Y=X^{y+o(1)},\qquad F_i=X^{f_i+o(1)},  \qquad k_i=\max\{f_i,\beta-f_i\}.  \label{eq:asym-cell-exponents}$$ The rectangular support gives $$0\le y\le s+t,\qquad 0\le f_1\le s,\qquad 0\le f_2\le t,  \label{eq:asym-cell-ranges}$$ and $\mathcal K_Q(F_i)=X^{k_i+o(1)}$.

###### ref-d93e6d7b0de3eabc

The natural scale $XQ=Q^2J$ has exponent $1+\beta$. The norm and direct-Gauss routes in [\[eq:asymmetric-cell-bound\]](TPC-024.md#ref-6a2153d564462aa9) have exponents $$\beta+\frac{1-\beta+y+k_1+k_2}{2},  \qquad  \beta+f_1+f_2+\frac{k_1+k_2}{2},$$ respectively. Their saving exponents are therefore $$\eta_A=\frac{\beta+1-y-k_1-k_2}{2},  \qquad  \eta_B=1-f_1-f_2-\frac{k_1+k_2}{2}.  \label{eq:asym-etaAB}$$

###### ref-68494c275fdc3034

###### ref-edb3bf26ab4d2f3c

> **Proposition: Asymmetric three-cell conductor ledger** \[prop:asymmetric-ledger\] Every cell in [\[eq:asym-cell-ranges\]](TPC-024.md#ref-c9490631a32d14c7) satisfies $$\max\{\eta_A,\eta_B\}\ge  \eta_{\rm asym}(\beta;s,t),  \label{eq:asym-ledger-bound}$$ where $$\boxed{  \eta_{\rm asym}(\beta;s,t)  =\min\left\{  1-\frac{3\beta}{2},  \frac{\beta+1-2s-2t}{2},  \frac{3-\beta-s-5t}{4}  \right\}.}  \label{eq:eta-asymmetric}$$

###### ref-0dcbde36e1a1ed29

###### ref-efbdb143790e0bb5

## The mass-sensitive mixed-increment theorem

###### ref-2b258de3b922e361

###### ref-983d7ad5981a107f

> **Theorem: One-sided calibrated-increment estimate** \[thm:calibrated-increment-estimate\] Assume the row and target-primitivity interfaces of [2](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-024.md#src-5b421430e6174d2c), together with the divisor-independent off-diagonal mask, [\[eq:no-wrap\]](TPC-024.md#ref-a910d27babd2d450), and [\[eq:Fourier-tail-interface\]](TPC-024.md#ref-be0a4ec638e9b333). The short-side condition [\[eq:short-side\]](TPC-024.md#ref-6549a01ba70e7238) is not required for this calibrated formulation. Suppose $\eta_{\rm asym}(\beta;s,t)>0$ with a fixed margin and $0<\kappa<s$. Then for every fixed $A>0$ and every sufficiently small fixed $\epsilon>0$, there is a fixed $\eta>0$ such that $$\begin{aligned}  |\mathcal I_{S,T}|  \ll{}&XQX^{-\eta}  \notag\\  &+J\mathcal Z_1\left\{  (\log X)^{-A}+X^{-s+\kappa+o(1)}+L^{-1}X^\epsilon  \right\}.  \label{eq:mass-sensitive-main-theorem}\end{aligned}$$ Here $\eta$ may be any sufficiently small fixed number below the conductor margin and the principal-mean margin $1-\beta$, after the auxiliary $\epsilon$’s are chosen. The transpose packet obeys the same estimate.

> **Proof** Use [\[prop:joint-minus-drift\]](TPC-024.md#ref-011f4d1c8dcfc2aa). Reassemble the joint $r\ne0$ discrepancy through [\[eq:asymmetric-cell-reassembly\]](TPC-024.md#ref-895d04318ccf6102). Apply [\[prop:asymmetric-ledger\]](TPC-024.md#ref-483c0baae7418132) and [\[eq:asymmetric-cell-bound\]](TPC-024.md#ref-6a2153d564462aa9); the fixed margin absorbs all dyadic and divisor multiplicities. The global principal cell is bounded by [\[eq:asymmetric-principal-mean\]](TPC-024.md#ref-f2cc6e5e202ff176); under [\[eq:row-pair-mass\]](TPC-024.md#ref-bba276fd36f22f46) it is $Q^2X^\epsilon=XQX^{-(1-\beta)+\epsilon}$. The physical zero mode is [\[eq:mass-sensitive-zero\]](TPC-024.md#ref-18078f3efa9ecde1), and the drift is [\[eq:drift-packet-bound\]](TPC-024.md#ref-bf786b10750502da). The inherited Fourier tail is made smaller than the displayed fixed-power term by choosing the smooth truncation order after its polynomial mass. This proves [\[eq:mass-sensitive-main-theorem\]](TPC-024.md#ref-983d7ad5981a107f).

> 2.  Under only the soft mass hypothesis [\[eq:row-pair-mass\]](TPC-024.md#ref-bba276fd36f22f46), if $\widehat F(0)=0$, then some fixed $\eta'>0$ satisfies $$\boxed{\mathcal I_{S,T}\ll XQX^{-\eta'}.}   \label{eq:mean-zero-full-closure}$$ after the conventional auxiliary $X^\epsilon$ losses are chosen smaller than the fixed margins.

> **Proof** In (i), choose the calibration exponent $A$ after $B,K$. The large-$g$, drift, conductor, and principal-mean terms have fixed power savings. In (ii), the physical zero mode is identically zero by [\[cor:mass-sensitive-zero\]](TPC-024.md#ref-9989ee8d4905fab8); every remaining term has a fixed-power margin.

For a genuine crossing of the old Poisson face, require $$s<1-\beta<t.  \label{eq:general-poisson-crossing}$$ Positivity of the three entries in [\[eq:eta-asymmetric\]](TPC-024.md#ref-edb3bf26ab4d2f3c) gives the explicit window $$\boxed{  1-\beta<t<  \min\left\{  \frac{\beta+1}{2}-s,  \frac{3-\beta-s}{5}  \right\},}  \label{eq:general-passage-window}$$ together with $\beta<2/3$ and $s<1-\beta$, all with fixed margins. # Exact splicing, a failed shortcut, and scope

## Where the mixed increment sits in the residual

###### ref-dcc78cf27c7c9ae3

###### ref-ac0cf142e455ded5

###### ref-3058ad49affc0849

Let $U$ contain every positive target in the selected orbit packet. By [\[eq:full-residual-prefix\]](TPC-024.md#ref-c8aebb103a913cc3), $$P_{m,U}(j)=  \Lambda(mj+h)-\Lambda_R(mj+h)-\delta_H(m).  \label{eq:full-calibrated-residual}$$ For $R\le S<T\le U$, the prefix has the exact decomposition $$P_{m,U}=P_{m,S}+L_{m;S,T}+L_{m;T,U}.  \label{eq:three-piece-prefix}$$ Therefore a two-row residual product expands into nine explicitly labelled pieces. The present theorem treats $$P_{m,S}L_{n;S,T}  \qquad\text{and, by transposition,}\qquad  L_{m;S,T}P_{n,S}.  \label{eq:treated-two-pieces}$$ It does not estimate $L_{m;S,T}L_{n;S,T}$, either component involving $L_{\cdot;T,U}$, or the already studied base product $P_{m,S}P_{n,S}$. Consequently [\[eq:polylog-full-closure\]](TPC-024.md#ref-27c3fcc32ec75f5e) is a closure of a genuine residual increment, but not a closure of the complete residual correlation from TPC-18 [`\citep{WangTPC18}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-024.md#cite-4abf939102bd1649).

The shell lower endpoint is also structural. In [\[eq:inner-endpoint-difference\]](TPC-024.md#ref-6d54639a11685d33), the main constant in $A_M(Y)$ disappears only because the long divisor variable is summed over the difference $(S/g,T/g]$. Replacing that shell by a single modulus destroys the mechanism.

## Pointwise calibration at a fixed modulus is false

###### ref-b68d999385b7dea1

###### ref-fc95cad77a1265f8

###### ref-7417455359269097

The last warning has a finite counterexample. For a cutoff $R$, define the centered response of the complete short prefix to one modulus $v$ by $$\Gamma_R(v;\Delta)=  \sum_{u\le R}\frac{b_R(u)}u  \left((u,v)\mathbf 1_{(u,v)\mid\Delta}-1\right),  \label{eq:fixed-modulus-response}$$ temporarily suppressing row coprimality. Choose a prime $R/2<p\le R$, put $v=p$, and take $p\mid\Delta$. For every $u\le R$ other than $u=p$, either $(u,p)=1$, in which case the bracket vanishes, or $b_R(u)=0$ for a nonsquarefree multiple of $p$. Since only $b=1$ occurs in [\[eq:lambda-def\]](TPC-024.md#ref-6916d81f2ce0f086), $$\lambda'_R(p)=-\frac p{p-1},  \qquad  b_R(p)=\log p+\frac p{p-1}.  \label{eq:prime-edge-coefficient}$$ Thus $$\Gamma_R(p;\Delta)  =\frac{p-1}{p}b_R(p)  =\left(1-\frac1p\right)\log p+1  \asymp\log R.  \label{eq:fixed-modulus-failure}$$ Bertrand’s postulate supplies such a prime for every sufficiently large $R$. Hence a uniform assertion $\Gamma_R(v;\Delta)=o(1)$ is false. This does not contradict [\[thm:pointwise-zero-shell\]](TPC-024.md#ref-7b00fda63b56f25f): that theorem retains the full outer Möbius shell and uses cancellation between its two endpoints.

## What is unconditional, conditional, and still open

The result has three logically different levels.

1.  The prefix, four-channel, recombination, and gcd identities are finite and exact. So are the fixed-modulus counterexample and the exponent ledger.

2.  The nonzero conductor theorem and pointwise zero-shell theorem are proved without conjectural number-theoretic input for packets satisfying the displayed primitive, smoothness, no-wrap, and fixed-power-range interfaces. They use the primitive multiplicative large sieve and the classical zero-free region for $\zeta(s)$, not a Chowla or Elliott conjecture.

3.  Full logarithmic closure for general $\widehat F(0)\ne0$ additionally uses the explicit fixed-projective-mass hypothesis [\[eq:polylog-projective-mass\]](TPC-024.md#ref-b6bd4c253a6cc718). Under only the generic soft mass, the honest conclusion is [\[eq:mass-sensitive-main-theorem\]](TPC-024.md#ref-983d7ad5981a107f); the mean-zero test-function corollary [\[eq:mean-zero-full-closure\]](TPC-024.md#ref-88bd5354e56f14ee) remains fully closed.

In particular, nothing here proves a Hardy–Littlewood asymptotic, a positive lower bound for prime pairs, the twin-prime conjecture, or a general violation of the sieve parity barrier. The phrase “passage beyond the Poisson boundary” refers only to the support coordinate $T>J$ in the calibrated mixed increment.

## The next precise gates

The theorem leaves several separable research problems.

1.  Replace absolute use of $\mathcal Z_1$ in [\[eq:mass-sensitive-zero\]](TPC-024.md#ref-18078f3efa9ecde1) by a row-averaged operator or Hilbert–Schmidt estimate for the small-$g$ kernel. Such an estimate would remove the fixed-projective-mass hypothesis without requiring pointwise fixed-modulus calibration, which [\[eq:fixed-modulus-failure\]](TPC-024.md#ref-7417455359269097) rules out.

2.  Treat the both-new square $L_{m;S,T}L_{n;S,T}$. It has two long legs and no calibrated short prefix, so the present one-sided algebra does not apply.

3.  Reassemble the remaining $L_{\cdot;T,U}$ complement fibers. TPC-23 identifies their affine Möbius parity cocycle; polynomially uniform cancellation over those growing fibers remains open [`\citep{WangTPC23}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-024.md#cite-f24192cbd2a455cd).

4.  Only after these analytic components have compatible bounds may one return to the global endpoint and positivity ledgers. A prime-pair main term is a separate stage, not a consequence of mixed shell cancellation alone.

Each gate has a valid negative fallback: failure of a proposed uniform estimate can be recorded by an exact obstruction such as [\[eq:fixed-modulus-failure\]](TPC-024.md#ref-7417455359269097), without being presented as evidence for or against the twin-prime conjecture.

# Exact certificate and conclusion

The analytic arguments above are symbolic. We include a deterministic finite certificate to audit the identities and rational exponent ledger most vulnerable to sign, gcd, normalization, or boundary errors. It does not test the zero-free region, estimate a Möbius shell asymptotically, or sample primes for evidential purposes.

## Conclusion

The old Poisson-short boundary is not a wall for every residual component. Once the object is organized as an exact calibrated-prefix increment, the misleading long single channel cancels algebraically. The remaining nonzero joint spectrum has a rectangular true-conductor decomposition, whose certified saving is [\[eq:eta-asymmetric\]](TPC-024.md#ref-edb3bf26ab4d2f3c). Its physical zero mode has a different mechanism: the complete long Möbius shell cancels the common endpoint constant for small shared factor, while the off-diagonal row determinant controls large shared factor.

This proves a genuine support advance. The explicit region [\[eq:special-passage-window\]](TPC-024.md#ref-851c7a5fc31ef4d2) contains parameters with $S=R<J<T$, for example [\[eq:rational-passage-sample\]](TPC-024.md#ref-dcb218283dc0ff45). The advance is stronger than a numerical observation: the nonzero spectrum has fixed-power saving, and the zero kernel has the pointwise theorem [\[eq:pointwise-zero-shell\]](TPC-024.md#ref-300f4974b1d91549).

The paper also preserves the exact remaining boundary. Pointwise shell cancellation becomes a packet estimate with the actual absolute row mass $\mathcal Z_1$. Fixed-polylogarithmic projective mass yields the full logarithmic closure [\[eq:polylog-full-closure\]](TPC-024.md#ref-27c3fcc32ec75f5e); a mean-zero test function yields the soft-mass closure [\[eq:mean-zero-full-closure\]](TPC-024.md#ref-88bd5354e56f14ee). Generic soft mass and nonzero physical mean leave the explicit term in [\[eq:mass-sensitive-main-theorem\]](TPC-024.md#ref-983d7ad5981a107f). Calling that term negligible would require a new row-averaged zero-mode estimate, not a change of notation.

The next useful target is therefore precise: prove cancellation of the small-$g$ zero kernel against the actual separated row family, or derive a fixed-polylog projective decomposition for the relevant packet, and then attack the both-new square. Neither step is supplied here. In particular, this one-sided passage is not a twin-prime theorem and is not described as a general breach of parity.
