## Abstract

Let $P_{m,S}$ be the calibrated residual prefix in a primitive opened row $m=\ell d$, where $\ell\asymp L$ is prime, $d$ is squarefree, and $R\le S$. Earlier exact-conductor estimates close the retained nonzero additive spectrum of the centered selected square when $S$ is strictly below the Poisson length. They do not, by their displayed formulas, estimate the connected additive zero mode. This paper closes that missing base mode for the actual opened-row provenance and then restores the calibrated physical square.

On the primitive orbit $(j,H)=1$, write $$Z_{m,S}(j)=\sum_{\substack{u\le S\\(u,mH)=1}}  b_R(u)\left(\mathbf 1_{u\mid mj+h}-\frac1u\right),  \qquad P_{m,S}=Z_{m,S}+E_S(m).$$ For distinct rows $m,n$, the complete-period zero kernel of $Z_{m,S}Z_{n,S}$ is $$K_S^0(m,n)=  \sum_{u,v\le S}\frac{b_R(u)b_R(v)}{uv}  \left((u,v)\mathbf 1_{(u,v)\mid m-n}-1\right),$$ with the primitive row-coprimality restrictions understood. The identity $$g\mathbf 1_{g\mid\Delta}-1  =\sum_{\substack{k\mid g\\k>1}}c_k(\Delta)$$ factorizes it exactly as $$K_S^0(m,n)=\sum_{k>1}c_k(m-n)B_{k,S}(m)B_{k,S}(n),  \qquad  B_{k,S}(m)=  \sum_{\substack{u\le S\\k\mid u\\(u,mH)=1}}  \frac{b_R(u)}u.$$ For $k\le X^\kappa$, an exact multiple-density identity for the finite Ramanujan model and the classical uniform Möbius calibration give $$B_{k,S}(m)=\frac{\mu(k)}{\varphi(k)}\delta_H(m)  +O_A\!\left(\frac1{\varphi(k)(\log X)^A}\right),  \qquad  \delta_H(m)\ll \frac{\log\log(3X)}L.$$ For $k>X^\kappa$, the cofactor mass bound and $m-n\ne0$ yield a Ramanujan tail of size $X^{-\kappa+o(1)}$. Consequently, for every fixed $A>0$ and every fixed $0<\kappa<\min\{s,\tfrac12(\tfrac12-\delta)\}$, $$K_S^0(m,n)  \ll_A (\log X)^{-A}  +L^{-2}(\log\log(3X))^3+X^{-\kappa+o(1)}.$$

The actual physical row coefficients have $O(Q)$ one-row absolute mass. Extracting frequency zero before Mellin separation therefore closes the physical base zero mode. Uniform endpoint calibration also closes the three scalar channels $EZ,ZE,EE$. Combining these facts with the inherited static or dynamic exact-conductor theorem proves $$\mathcal Q_S^{\rm phys}  \ll_B XQ(\log X)^{-B}$$ throughout its strict Poisson-short base region. Splicing this base estimate with the annular cutoff-stability theorem closes the selected calibrated square at certain $T>J$. In particular, the rational sample $$\delta=\frac3{20},\quad \beta=\frac{31}{50},\quad  s=\frac7{20},\quad t=\frac{39}{100}$$ is upgraded from conditional cutoff stability to a closed selected truncated square. The higher-$\beta$ sample whose base already lies beyond the Poisson length is not covered. No ultra-long complement, positivity statement, Hardy–Littlewood asymptotic, twin-prime theorem, or general parity breakthrough is claimed.

# Introduction

###### ref-8b80ac200f42d39c

The primitive Möbius-tail program of TPC-20–26 rewrites a selected fixed-shift prime-pair residual as a bilinear correlation of opened rows $$m=\ell d,  \qquad \ell\asymp L\text{ prime},  \qquad d\asymp D,  \qquad Q=LD,  \qquad QJ\asymp X.$$ The common residual divisor coefficient is $$a(u)=-\mu(u)\log u,  \qquad  b_R(u)=a(u)-\lambda'_R(u),  \qquad  \lambda'_R(u)=0\quad(u>R).  \label{eq:tpc27-intro-coefficients}$$ TPC-20 derives the exact four-channel centered kernel, TPC-21 closes its multiplicative-character mean at nonzero additive frequency, and TPC-22–23 close the corresponding connected fiber discrepancy in explicit short-support regions [`\citep{WangTPC20,WangTPC21,WangTPC22,WangTPC23}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-027.md#cite-7525f88be3fcfa5f); [\[WangTPC21\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-027.md#cite-8d248780f3293b33); [\[WangTPC22\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-027.md#cite-6ea7e7d72ef27c8f); [\[WangTPC23\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-027.md#cite-f24192cbd2a455cd). TPC-24–26 then use endpoint calibration and exact-conductor estimates to pass selected cutoffs across the old Poisson face [`\citep{WangTPC24,WangTPC25,WangTPC26}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-027.md#cite-eb33ec2ce36e9859); [\[WangTPC25\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-027.md#cite-39e8dc14c55a2411); [\[WangTPC26\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-027.md#cite-ad014f62a6ac3092).

There is a precise logical gap between those statements. The scalar packets displayed and estimated in TPC-22–23 are sums over $r\ne0$. Their formulas do not estimate the additive zero term retained by the TPC-20 two-scale completion. Moreover, the physical prefix used in TPC-26 is calibrated rather than merely centered. Thus one cannot infer a physical base estimate by replacing the words “centered selected square” with “calibrated square.” Two separate operations are required:

1.  close the connected additive zero mode of the centered square;

2.  restore the scalar calibration channels without losing the natural scale.

The present paper performs both operations for the actual physical opened-row provenance.

For one row put $$Z_{m,S}(j)=  \sum_{\substack{u\le S\\(u,mH)=1}}  b_R(u)C_{m,u}(j),  \qquad  C_{m,u}(j)=\mathbf 1_{u\mid mj+h}-\frac1u.  \label{eq:tpc27-intro-centered-signal}$$ If $m\ne n$, the primitive complete-period average of $C_{m,u}C_{n,v}$ is $$\frac1{uv}  \left(g\mathbf 1_{g\mid m-n}-1\right),  \qquad g=(u,v).  \label{eq:tpc27-intro-connected-zero}$$ The elementary Ramanujan identity $$g\mathbf 1_{g\mid\Delta}-1  =\sum_{\substack{k\mid g\\k>1}}c_k(\Delta)  \label{eq:tpc27-intro-Ramanujan-identity}$$ turns the two-divisor zero kernel into a sum of products of one-row multiple densities: $$\boxed{  K_S^0(m,n)=  \sum_{k>1}c_k(m-n)B_{k,S}(m)B_{k,S}(n).}  \label{eq:tpc27-intro-factorization}$$ Here $$B_{k,S}(m)=  \sum_{\substack{u\le S\\k\mid u\\(u,mH)=1}}  \frac{b_R(u)}u.  \label{eq:tpc27-intro-Bk}$$ The row coprimality restrictions separate exactly; there is no approximation in [\[eq:tpc27-intro-factorization\]](TPC-027.md#ref-bf4e6f0ffdfd08d1).

###### ref-3617fe258ae1ece3

###### ref-841cc88c8ffeafac

This factorization changes the nature of the problem. It is not necessary to estimate the centered two-divisor kernel by an operator norm. Instead, one calibrates each short one-row multiple density. For squarefree $k$ coprime to the row, the finite model satisfies an exact identity $$\sum_{\substack{u\le R\\k\mid u\\(u,mH)=1}}  \frac{\lambda'_R(u)}u  =\frac{\mu(k)}{\varphi(k)}\rho_{M,H,R}(m)  \label{eq:tpc27-intro-model-multiple}$$ whenever $kdH\le R$. The corresponding prime coefficient has main term $$\sum_{\substack{u\le S\\k\mid u\\(u,mH)=1}}  \frac{a(u)}u  =\frac{\mu(k)}{\varphi(k)}\rho_{P,H}(m)  +O_A\!\left(  \frac{\rho_{P,H}(m)(1+\log k)}  {\varphi(k)(\log X)^A}\right).  \label{eq:tpc27-intro-prime-multiple}$$ Their difference is the already power-saving row drift $$\delta_H(m)=\rho_{P,H}(m)-\rho_{M,H,R}(m)  \ll \frac{\log\log(3X)}L.$$

###### ref-f603153276e5742e

###### ref-3374376cc3ddcd68

###### ref-87267d21f578bc3c

Choose $K_0=X^\kappa$ below $R^{1/2}$. The identities above control $k\le K_0$. For $k>K_0$, the cofactor estimate $$|B_{k,S}(m)|\ll \frac{(\log X)^4}{k}  \label{eq:tpc27-intro-large-B}$$ and the off-diagonal relation $m-n\ne0$ give $$\sum_{k>K_0}\frac{|c_k(m-n)|}{k^2}  \ll X^{o(1)}K_0^{-1}.  \label{eq:tpc27-intro-Ramanujan-tail}$$ This proves the pointwise zero theorem $$K_S^0(m,n)  \ll_A(\log X)^{-A}  +L^{-2}(\log\log(3X))^3+X^{-\kappa+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc27-intro-zero-theorem}$$ The condition $m\ne n$ is structural. When $m=n$, one has $c_k(0)=\varphi(k)$, and the large-$k$ argument does not give [\[eq:tpc27-intro-Ramanujan-tail\]](TPC-027.md#ref-3374376cc3ddcd68).

## From the centered base to the physical prefix

###### ref-17e6977d44c92126

###### ref-39209a9d0f59f2d0

TPC-24’s exact calibration identity on the primitive orbit $(j,H)=1$ is $$P_{m,S}(j)=Z_{m,S}(j)+E_S(m),  \qquad  E_S(m)=A_{mH}(S)-\rho_{P,H}(m).  \label{eq:tpc27-intro-PZE}$$ The classical zero-free region gives $E_S(m)\ll_A(\log X)^{-A}$, uniformly in the polynomial row ranges used here. Therefore $$P_{m,S}P_{n,S}  =ZZ+EZ+ZE+EE.  \label{eq:tpc27-intro-four-scalar-channels}$$ The last three terms are bounded before spectral separation. The actual one-row absolute masses are $O(Q)$, while $$\sum_j|Z_{m,S}(j)|\,|F(j/J)|  \ll (J+S)(\log(2X))^4.$$ Thus the scalar channels have arbitrary logarithmic saving whenever $S\le JX^{-\eta_0}$. This is precisely the strict Poisson-short condition already required by the dynamic nonzero theorem.

###### ref-2821274b2f3cd2bf

For the $ZZ$ term, physical frequency zero is extracted before Mellin separation. The new pointwise bound [\[eq:tpc27-intro-zero-theorem\]](TPC-027.md#ref-87267d21f578bc3c) combines with the actual $O(Q^2)$ row-pair absolute mass. Only the nonzero spectrum is then sent through the applicable TPC-22 static or TPC-23 dynamic conductor theorem. The correct order is $$\boxed{  \begin{gathered}  \text{extract and factor the centered physical zero mode}\\  \longrightarrow\ \text{calibrate its one-row multiples}\\  \longrightarrow\ \text{separate the nonzero spectrum}\\  \longrightarrow\ \text{restore }EZ,ZE,EE.  \end{gathered}}  \label{eq:tpc27-intro-order}$$

## Consequences and boundary

###### ref-60f03330a3239af9

###### ref-8bccb82f7bb5e5ab

###### ref-4eade14a224d40cf

Write $$Q=X^{\beta+o(1)},  \qquad J=X^{1-\beta+o(1)},  \qquad S=X^{s+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc27-intro-exponents}$$ Under the inherited no-wrap, physical provenance, off-diagonal mask, and fixed-margin hypotheses, the base closes when $$s<1-\beta,  \qquad  \eta_S(\beta,s)=  \min\left\{  1-\frac{3\beta}{2},  \frac{\beta+1-4s}{2},  \frac{3-\beta-6s}{4}  \right\}>0.  \label{eq:tpc27-intro-base-region}$$ Combining this with the TPC-26 annular theorem closes a selected calibrated square at an upper cutoff $T=X^{t+o(1)}>J$ whenever its annular minimax $M_\beta(t)$ is positive. The sample $$(\delta,\beta,s,t)=  \left(\frac3{20},\frac{31}{50},\frac7{20},\frac{39}{100}\right)  \label{eq:tpc27-intro-strict-sample}$$ has centered nonzero conductor ledger margin $7/100$ and annular minimax margin $1/100$; the complete calibrated conclusion has arbitrary logarithmic saving.

This does not make the higher-$\beta$ cutoff-stability sample of TPC-26 into a closed square. In that sample $S>J$, so the inherited base nonzero theorem is unavailable. Nor does the argument estimate the ultra-long complement after the upper cutoff. It proves one selected physical upper-bound/cancellation statement; it does not produce positivity, a Hardy–Littlewood main term, twin primes, or a general breach of sieve parity.

The paper is organized as follows. fixes the physical packet and the exact centered-to-calibrated bridge. proves the zero-mode factorization. proves the exact model-multiple identity and its calibrated prime companion. obtains the pointwise and physical zero theorems. transfers the inherited nonzero theorem and closes the scalar channels. gives the base and upper-cutoff closure, and [8](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-027.md#src-42c36b960b617428) records the exact certificate and strict scope.

# Physical interface and the exact calibration bridge

We retain the actual opened-row provenance before any Mellin or Fourier separation. This is essential only at physical additive frequency zero; the nonzero spectrum will be separated later.

Fix $h\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}$, and put $$H=\operatorname{rad}|h|,  \qquad H_0=|h|.  \label{eq:tpc27-H-H0}$$ Let $$R=X^{r_0+o(1)},  \qquad r_0=\frac12-\delta>0,  \qquad R\le S\le X^{C_S},  \label{eq:tpc27-RS}$$ where all exponent inequalities below have fixed positive margins. In one opened slice, rows are indexed by $$\alpha=(\ell,d_\alpha),  \qquad m_\alpha=\ell d_\alpha\asymp Q=LD,  \label{eq:tpc27-row-index}$$ with $$\ell\in[L/2,2L]\text{ prime},  \quad d_\alpha\in[D,2D]\cap[1,R^{1/2}],  \quad d_\alpha\text{ squarefree},  \quad(d_\alpha,H)=1.  \label{eq:tpc27-row-support}$$ We assume $$QJ\asymp X,  \qquad L>\max\{2R,2H_0\},  \qquad L\ge X^{\lambda_0}  \label{eq:tpc27-row-scales}$$ for a fixed $\lambda_0>0$. In particular, $\ell>R$. On the primitive orbit $(j,H)=1$, target primitivity gives $$(u,m_\alpha H)=1  \qquad\text{whenever }u\mid m_\alpha j+h  \text{ and }(j,H)=1.  \label{eq:tpc27-target-primitivity}$$ Since $H$ is fixed and $d_\alpha\le R^{1/2}$, we also have $d_\alpha H\le R$ for all sufficiently large $X$. This is the finite-model completeness condition used below.

The second condition will be used in the large-Ramanujan-modulus tail.

The row–orbit weight $\mathcal W_{\alpha,\gamma}(j/J)$ belongs to the factorable physical family of TPC-25. > **Proof** The finite model has the exact density identity $$\sum_{\substack{u\le R\\(u,mH)=1}}  \frac{\lambda'_R(u)}u=\rho_{M,H,R}(m).  \label{eq:tpc27-model-density-identity}$$ Since $\lambda'_R$ is supported on $u\le R$, subtraction from $A_{mH}(S)$ gives $$\sum_{\substack{u\le S\\(u,mH)=1}}  \frac{b_R(u)}u  =\delta_H(m)+E_S(m).  \label{eq:tpc27-prefix-density}$$ Expand the centered indicator in [\[eq:tpc27-Zms\]](TPC-027.md#ref-1ef9e364308a4a2a), insert [\[eq:tpc27-prefix-density\]](TPC-027.md#ref-456ec19c90387bee), and use target primitivity [\[eq:tpc27-target-primitivity\]](TPC-027.md#ref-b0735aad96c0a7c0). This proves [\[eq:tpc27-PZE\]](TPC-027.md#ref-91f8f34a080fafae). Estimate [\[eq:tpc27-E-bound\]](TPC-027.md#ref-46f2ab9a4d99e061) is [\[eq:tpc27-uniform-A\]](TPC-027.md#ref-1b3216d9bd8c23bd) with $M=mH$, since all relevant quantities are fixed powers of $X$. The factor $mH/\varphi(mH)\ll_h\log\log(3X)$ is absorbed by first invoking [\[eq:tpc27-uniform-A\]](TPC-027.md#ref-1b3216d9bd8c23bd) with one additional logarithmic power.

###### ref-55fd7139aa90bef5

###### ref-5262916e8792f168

###### ref-6c70ea51a8eea62b

The physical base packet is $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal Q_S^{\rm phys}  :={}&\sum_{\alpha,\gamma}  \gamma_\alpha^{(1)}\gamma_\gamma^{(2)}  \mathfrak m(\alpha,\gamma)  \notag\\  &\quad\times  \sum_{\substack{j\in\mathbb Z\\(j,H)=1}}  \Xi_{\alpha,\gamma}(j)  \mathcal W_{\alpha,\gamma}(j/J)  P_{m_\alpha,S}(j)P_{m_\gamma,S}(j).  \label{eq:tpc27-physical-base}\end{aligned}$$ The natural scale is $$JQ^2\asymp XQ.  \label{eq:tpc27-natural-scale}$$ By [\[prop:tpc27-PZE\]](TPC-027.md#ref-122af68f797a7fe4), the integrand in [\[eq:tpc27-physical-base\]](TPC-027.md#ref-55fd7139aa90bef5) has the exact four-term decomposition $$P_{m,S}P_{n,S}  =Z_{m,S}Z_{n,S}  +E_S(m)Z_{n,S}  +Z_{m,S}E_S(n)  +E_S(m)E_S(n).  \label{eq:tpc27-ZZ-EZ-ZE-EE}$$ The first term contains the new additive-zero problem. The last three will be closed directly in [6](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-027.md#src-6edc319184498d0d).

# Ramanujan-square factorization of the connected zero mode

The base zero mode is finite and admits an exact one-row factorization. This section contains no asymptotic estimate.

## Primitive complete-period covariance

###### ref-ff1ae3ee9b16624e

For admissible rows and divisors, recall $$C_{m,u}(j)=\mathbf 1_{u\mid mj+h}-\frac1u.$$ Let $\mathbb E_H$ denote the complete-period average conditional on $(j,H)=1$. The primitive CRT calculation of TPC-20 gives $$\mathbb E_H\!\left[C_{m,u}C_{n,v}\right]  =\frac1{uv}  \left(g\mathbf 1_{g\mid m-n}-1\right),  \qquad g=(u,v),  \label{eq:tpc27-connected-covariance}$$ provided $(u,mH)=(v,nH)=1$ [`\citep{WangTPC20}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-027.md#cite-7525f88be3fcfa5f). The normalization in [\[eq:tpc27-connected-covariance\]](TPC-027.md#ref-ff1ae3ee9b16624e) omits the fixed outer density $\varphi(H)/H$; that density is restored in the physical Poisson zero coefficient and is harmless.

###### ref-a6d0ad686fc12d5a

Define the centered base zero kernel $$\begin{aligned}  K_S^0(m,n)  :={}&  \sum_{\substack{u,v\le S\\                    (u,mH)=1\\(v,nH)=1}}  \frac{b_R(u)b_R(v)}{uv}  \left((u,v)\mathbf 1_{(u,v)\mid m-n}-1\right).  \label{eq:tpc27-base-zero-kernel}\end{aligned}$$ It is exactly the arithmetic factor multiplying the physical additive zero functional for $Z_{m,S}Z_{n,S}$.

###### ref-85957888db52638f

> **Remark: Fixed residue cells** \[rem:tpc27-fixed-residue-zero\] The physical factor $\Xi_{\alpha,\gamma}$ may first be split into its finitely many classes $j\equiv\varrho\pmod{H_0}$. Every effective divisor is coprime to $H_0$, so the joint and one-leg densities inside one such class are respectively $1/(H_0[u,v])$, $1/(H_0u)$, and $1/(H_0v)$. Thus [\[eq:tpc27-base-zero-kernel\]](TPC-027.md#ref-a6d0ad686fc12d5a) is unchanged up to a bounded fixed local factor. No growing residue complexity is hidden here.

## The divisor identity

###### ref-2b7ac0e05f0da39f

We use the Ramanujan sum $$c_k(a)=\sum_{\substack{x\bmod k\\(x,k)=1}}e_k(ax).  \label{eq:tpc27-Ramanujan-sum}$$

###### ref-766aa098f9eee079

###### ref-93bcf281624d3cf2

> **Lemma: Ramanujan resolution of compatibility** \[lem:tpc27-Ramanujan-resolution\] For all positive integers $g$ and all $\Delta\in\mathbb Z$, $$\boxed{  g\mathbf 1_{g\mid\Delta}-1  =\sum_{\substack{k\mid g\\k>1}}c_k(\Delta).}  \label{eq:tpc27-Ramanujan-resolution}$$

###### ref-bba399791650741d

> **Proof** The standard divisor formula $$c_k(\Delta)=  \sum_{e\mid(k,\Delta)}e\,\mu(k/e)  \label{eq:tpc27-c-divisor-formula}$$ gives $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_{k\mid g}c_k(\Delta)  &=\sum_{e\mid(g,\Delta)}e    \sum_{r\mid g/e}\mu(r).\end{aligned}$$ The inner sum is nonzero only when $e=g$, in which case it equals one. Hence the full sum is $g$ if $g\mid\Delta$ and zero otherwise. Subtract the $k=1$ term $c_1(\Delta)=1$.

###### ref-ae388ed9685d9aeb

For $k\ge1$, define the one-row multiple density $$B_{k,S}(m)  :=\sum_{\substack{u\le S\\k\mid u\\(u,mH)=1}}  \frac{b_R(u)}u.  \label{eq:tpc27-Bks}$$ Because $b_R$ is squarefree supported, $B_{k,S}(m)=0$ unless $k$ is squarefree and $(k,mH)=1$.

###### ref-d467f9f412e6cdbb

###### ref-db839e43b5fae669

> **Theorem: Exact Ramanujan-square factorization** \[thm:tpc27-Ramanujan-square\] For every pair of rows $m,n$, $$\boxed{  K_S^0(m,n)=  \sum_{k>1}c_k(m-n)B_{k,S}(m)B_{k,S}(n).}  \label{eq:tpc27-Ramanujan-square}$$ The sum is finite: only squarefree $k\le S$ coprime to $mnH$ can contribute.

> **Proof** Insert [\[lem:tpc27-Ramanujan-resolution\]](TPC-027.md#ref-766aa098f9eee079) with $g=(u,v)$ and $\Delta=m-n$ into [\[eq:tpc27-base-zero-kernel\]](TPC-027.md#ref-a6d0ad686fc12d5a). All sums are finite, so their order may be interchanged. The condition $k\mid(u,v)$ is the product of the two independent conditions $k\mid u$ and $k\mid v$. The row coprimality restrictions also separate, giving $$\begin{aligned}  K_S^0(m,n)  &=\sum_{k>1}c_k(m-n)  \left(   \sum_{\substack{u\le S\\k\mid u\\(u,mH)=1}}   \frac{b_R(u)}u  \right)  \left(   \sum_{\substack{v\le S\\k\mid v\\(v,nH)=1}}   \frac{b_R(v)}v  \right).\end{aligned}$$ This is [\[eq:tpc27-Ramanujan-square\]](TPC-027.md#ref-db839e43b5fae669).

###### ref-3c006c9571221134

> **Remark: What the factorization removes** \[rem:tpc27-g1-removal\] The coherent $g=1$ zero block vanishes identically in the centered base: its multiplier is $1-1=0$. This is different from an uncentered one-sided shell kernel, where a nonzero $g=1$ principal row direction may remain. The sum in [\[eq:tpc27-Ramanujan-square\]](TPC-027.md#ref-db839e43b5fae669) therefore starts at $k>1$ for an exact algebraic reason, not because a principal mode has been deleted.

## Two Ramanujan ledgers

The following elementary bounds will be used after calibration.

###### ref-ed58ff2bc2e4dcec

###### ref-a701b4be9676b002

###### ref-76182b2e1e46356d

> **Lemma: Ramanujan weight and tail** \[lem:tpc27-Ramanujan-ledgers\] Let $0<|\Delta|\le X^C$, and let $2\le K\le X^C$. Then $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_{\substack{k\ge1\\k\ \mathrm{squarefree}}}  \frac{|c_k(\Delta)|}{\varphi(k)^2}  &\ll  \frac{\operatorname{rad}|\Delta|}{\varphi(\operatorname{rad}|\Delta|)}  \ll_C\log\log(3|\Delta|),  \label{eq:tpc27-small-Ramanujan-ledger}\\  \sum_{k>K}\frac{|c_k(\Delta)|}{k^2}  &\ll \frac{\tau(|\Delta|)}K.  \label{eq:tpc27-large-Ramanujan-ledger}\end{aligned}$$ The second estimate remains true after restricting to squarefree $k$.

###### ref-23a06053ae142dae

> **Proof** For squarefree $k$, multiplicativity and the prime identity $$|c_p(\Delta)|=  \begin{cases}  p-1,&p\mid\Delta,\\  1,&p\nmid\Delta  \end{cases}$$ give the absolutely convergent Euler product $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_{\substack{k\ge1\\k\ \mathrm{squarefree}}}  \frac{|c_k(\Delta)|}{\varphi(k)^2}  &=  \prod_{p\mid\Delta}\left(1+\frac1{p-1}\right)  \prod_{p\nmid\Delta}\left(1+\frac1{(p-1)^2}\right)\\  &\ll \prod_{p\mid\Delta}\frac p{p-1}  =\frac{\operatorname{rad}|\Delta|}{\varphi(\operatorname{rad}|\Delta|)}.\end{aligned}$$ The standard elementary maximal-order bound for $n/\varphi(n)$ proves the last estimate in [\[eq:tpc27-small-Ramanujan-ledger\]](TPC-027.md#ref-a701b4be9676b002).
>
> For the tail, formula [\[eq:tpc27-c-divisor-formula\]](TPC-027.md#ref-bba399791650741d) implies $$|c_k(\Delta)|\le(k,\Delta)  \le\sum_{e\mid(k,\Delta)}\varphi(e).  \label{eq:tpc27-c-gcd-bound}$$ Apply this bound and write $k=er$: $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_{k>K}\frac{|c_k(\Delta)|}{k^2}  &\le  \sum_{e\mid\Delta}\frac{\varphi(e)}{e^2}  \sum_{r>K/e}\frac1{r^2}.\end{aligned}$$ If $e\le K$, the inner sum is $O(e/K)$; if $e>K$, it is $O(1)$. Both ranges contribute $O(\tau(|\Delta|)/K)$, proving [\[eq:tpc27-large-Ramanujan-ledger\]](TPC-027.md#ref-76182b2e1e46356d).

The second estimate fails in the form required here when $\Delta=0$, because $c_k(0)=\varphi(k)$. This is precisely why the physical off-diagonal mask in [\[eq:tpc27-mask\]](TPC-027.md#ref-22132737679c2307) cannot be removed before the zero-mode estimate.

# Exact multiple densities and uniform cofactor calibration

We now estimate the factors $B_{k,S}(m)$ in [\[eq:tpc27-Ramanujan-square\]](TPC-027.md#ref-db839e43b5fae669). The main cancellation is between an exact finite-model identity and the main term of a proved uniform Möbius calibration theorem.

## An exact finite-model multiple identity

###### ref-1a60a5b66e12c3a4

Let $m=\ell d_m$ be one retained row and put $$M_m=d_mH.  \label{eq:tpc27-Mm}$$ The integer $M_m$ is squarefree, while $\ell>R$.

###### ref-c40b337c600011fa

###### ref-15e7d403a6049e58

###### ref-4bcc4fd13e0c0f34

> **Proposition: Exact model density on multiples** \[prop:tpc27-model-multiple\] Let $k$ be squarefree and $(k,mH)=1$. Then $$\boxed{  \sum_{\substack{u\le R\\k\mid u\\(u,mH)=1}}  \frac{\lambda'_R(u)}u  =\frac{\mu(k)}{\varphi(k)}  \sum_{\substack{a\mid M_m\\ak\le R}}  \frac1{\varphi(a)}.}  \label{eq:tpc27-truncated-model-multiple}$$ In particular, if $kM_m\le R$, then $$\boxed{  \sum_{\substack{u\le R\\k\mid u\\(u,mH)=1}}  \frac{\lambda'_R(u)}u  =\frac{\mu(k)}{\varphi(k)}  \rho_{M,H,R}(m).}  \label{eq:tpc27-model-multiple}$$

###### ref-7b52658ee99fa08a

###### ref-6d42427e52c7b5d9

###### ref-86e2b8de6afa6939

> **Proof** From [\[eq:tpc27-lambda-prime\]](TPC-027.md#ref-e3c8a24c856cd734), the left side of [\[eq:tpc27-truncated-model-multiple\]](TPC-027.md#ref-15e7d403a6049e58) is $$\sum_{\substack{ub\le R\\k\mid u\\(u,mH)=1}}  \frac{\mu(ub)\mu(b)}{\varphi(ub)}.  \label{eq:tpc27-model-double-sum}$$ Only squarefree $q=ub$ contributes. Grouping by $q$ gives $$\sum_{q\le R}\frac{\mu(q)}{\varphi(q)}  \sum_{\substack{b\mid q\\k\mid q/b\\(q/b,mH)=1}}  \mu(b).  \label{eq:tpc27-model-group-q}$$ Because $\ell>R$, no $q\le R$ contains the large row prime. Put $a=(q,M_m)$. The condition $(q/b,M_m)=1$ forces $a\mid b$. Since $(k,M_m)=1$, write $q=a q_0$ and $b=a b_0$. The inner sum becomes $$\mu(a)\sum_{b_0\mid q_0/k}\mu(b_0),  \label{eq:tpc27-inner-Mobius-collapse}$$ and is zero unless $q_0=k$, in which case it equals $\mu(a)$. Thus $q=ak$ with $a\mid M_m$, and the surviving summand is $$\frac{\mu(ak)\mu(a)}{\varphi(ak)}  =\frac{\mu(k)}{\varphi(k)\varphi(a)}.$$ This proves [\[eq:tpc27-truncated-model-multiple\]](TPC-027.md#ref-15e7d403a6049e58). If $kM_m\le R$, every divisor $a\mid M_m$ occurs, and $$\sum_{a\mid M_m}\frac1{\varphi(a)}  =\prod_{p\mid M_m}\left(1+\frac1{p-1}\right)  =\frac{M_m}{\varphi(M_m)}  =\rho_{M,H,R}(m).$$ This proves [\[eq:tpc27-model-multiple\]](TPC-027.md#ref-4bcc4fd13e0c0f34).

The cutoff condition in this proposition is why the opened-row bound $d_m\le R^{1/2}$ matters. It leaves a fixed-power range of multipliers $k$ on which the model identity is complete.

## The matching prime multiple

###### ref-60146bf43c244bb3

Define $$A_{k,S}^{\rm mult}(m)  :=\sum_{\substack{u\le S\\k\mid u\\(u,mH)=1}}  \frac{a(u)}u.  \label{eq:tpc27-prime-multiple-def}$$

###### ref-770329cd788f4142

###### ref-e85602d37d617cea

###### ref-0dae8ff0831ab113

> **Lemma: Prime multiple calibration** \[lem:tpc27-prime-multiple\] Let $k$ be squarefree and $(k,mH)=1$. Then the exact identity $$A_{k,S}^{\rm mult}(m)  =\frac{\mu(k)}k  \left\{  A_{kmH}(S/k)-(\log k)C_{kmH}(S/k)  \right\}  \label{eq:tpc27-prime-multiple-exact}$$ holds. Fix $0<\kappa<s$, where $S=X^{s+o(1)}$. Uniformly for $k\le X^\kappa$ and every fixed $A>0$, $$\boxed{  A_{k,S}^{\rm mult}(m)  =\frac{\mu(k)}{\varphi(k)}\rho_{P,H}(m)  +O_{A,\mathscr D}\!\left(  \frac{\rho_{P,H}(m)(1+\log k)}  {\varphi(k)(\log X)^A}  \right).}  \label{eq:tpc27-prime-multiple}$$

###### ref-73f19dfbd6e31215

> **Proof** On the squarefree support, write $u=kw$. Then $(w,kmH)=1$ and $$a(kw)=\mu(k)a(w)-\mu(k)\mu(w)\log k.  \label{eq:tpc27-a-kw}$$ Division by $kw$ and summation prove [\[eq:tpc27-prime-multiple-exact\]](TPC-027.md#ref-e85602d37d617cea).
>
> For $k\le X^\kappa$, the calibration length $Y=S/k$ is at least a fixed positive power of $X$. Also $kmH\le X^{O(1)}$, so one fixed constant $C$, depending only on the exponent data, gives $kmH\le Y^C$. Apply \[[eq:tpc27-uniform-A](TPC-027.md#ref-1b3216d9bd8c23bd),[eq:tpc27-uniform-C](TPC-027.md#ref-19b07e95aaf85d03)\] with a calibration exponent larger than the requested $A$. Since $(k,mH)=1$, the main term is $$\frac{\mu(k)}k\frac{kmH}{\varphi(kmH)}  =\frac{\mu(k)}{\varphi(k)}\rho_{P,H}(m).$$ The two error terms are bounded by the displayed remainder because $\log(S/k)\asymp\log X$ uniformly in this fixed-power range. This proves [\[eq:tpc27-prime-multiple\]](TPC-027.md#ref-0dae8ff0831ab113).

## Small and large multiple densities

###### ref-f1c10623de864ca1

###### ref-3799345cdcea832d

Set $$K_0=X^{\kappa+o(1)},  \qquad  0<\kappa<\min\left\{s,\frac{r_0}{2}\right\}.  \label{eq:tpc27-K0}$$ Because $d_m\le R^{1/2}$ and $H$ is fixed, all sufficiently large $X$ satisfy $$kM_m\le R  \qquad(k\le K_0).  \label{eq:tpc27-small-k-model-range}$$

###### ref-06a4df1c225db93a

###### ref-e50ed3b747a75558

> **Corollary: Calibrated small multiples** \[cor:tpc27-small-Bk\] For every retained row, every effective squarefree $k\le K_0$, and every fixed $A>0$, $$\boxed{  B_{k,S}(m)  =\frac{\mu(k)}{\varphi(k)}\delta_H(m)  +O_{A,\mathscr D}\!\left(  \frac1{\varphi(k)(\log X)^A}  \right).}  \label{eq:tpc27-small-Bk}$$

> **Proof** By [\[eq:tpc27-bR\]](TPC-027.md#ref-9e14d354088f1824), $B_{k,S}(m)$ is the prime multiple [\[eq:tpc27-prime-multiple-def\]](TPC-027.md#ref-60146bf43c244bb3) minus the model multiple. Apply \[[prop:tpc27-model-multiple](TPC-027.md#ref-c40b337c600011fa),[lem:tpc27-prime-multiple](TPC-027.md#ref-770329cd788f4142)\] and use [\[eq:tpc27-row-drift\]](TPC-027.md#ref-15dc21688ea0dc77). Since $\rho_{P,H}(m)\ll_h\log\log(3X)$ and $1+\log k\ll\log X$, invoke [\[lem:tpc27-prime-multiple\]](TPC-027.md#ref-770329cd788f4142) with three additional logarithmic powers and then rename the exponent. This absorbs the explicit polylogarithmic factors without introducing an $X^\varepsilon$ loss.

###### ref-ec7fd75d87284af6

For large $k$, cancellation is unnecessary. We import the elementary cofactor mass lemma of TPC-24: uniformly for squarefree $k\le S$, $$\sum_{\substack{w\le S/k\\(w,k)=1}}  \frac{|b_R(kw)|}{w}  \ll_{C_S}(\log(2X))^4.  \label{eq:tpc27-cofactor-L1}$$ It follows from the positive divisor formula for $\lambda'_R$ and harmonic summation, and uses no cancellation [`\citep{WangTPC24}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-027.md#cite-eb33ec2ce36e9859).

###### ref-d604a3b79d92fe99

###### ref-131da44de515674e

> **Lemma: Absolute large-multiple bound** \[lem:tpc27-large-Bk\] Uniformly for every row and every $k\ge1$, $$\boxed{  |B_{k,S}(m)|\ll_{\mathscr D}  \frac{(\log(2X))^4}{k}.}  \label{eq:tpc27-large-Bk}$$

> **Proof** There is nothing to prove unless $k$ is squarefree and $(k,mH)=1$. Write $u=kw$ in [\[eq:tpc27-Bks\]](TPC-027.md#ref-ae388ed9685d9aeb). Squarefree support forces $(w,k)=1$, while the remaining row-coprimality condition only decreases the absolute sum. Thus $$|B_{k,S}(m)|  \le\frac1k  \sum_{\substack{w\le S/k\\(w,k)=1}}  \frac{|b_R(kw)|}{w},$$ and [\[eq:tpc27-cofactor-L1\]](TPC-027.md#ref-ec7fd75d87284af6) proves the result.

The two bounds [\[eq:tpc27-small-Bk\]](TPC-027.md#ref-e50ed3b747a75558) and [\[eq:tpc27-large-Bk\]](TPC-027.md#ref-131da44de515674e) concern the same exact one-row factor. The split at $K_0$ therefore loses no intermediate term.

# Pointwise and physical closure of the base zero mode

We combine the exact factorization with the two multiple-density estimates. The only use of the row off-diagonal condition is in the large-$k$ Ramanujan tail.

## The pointwise theorem

###### ref-fdb05d3add85234f

###### ref-1fc093f4330d0fbc

> **Theorem: Pointwise centered base-zero theorem** \[thm:tpc27-pointwise-base-zero\] Retain the row and cutoff hypotheses of [2](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-027.md#src-ddfce758055b5bcc), and let $K_0=X^{\kappa+o(1)}$ satisfy [\[eq:tpc27-K0\]](TPC-027.md#ref-f1c10623de864ca1). Uniformly for distinct retained rows $m\ne n$ and every fixed $A>0$, $$\boxed{  K_S^0(m,n)  \ll_{A,\mathscr D}  (\log X)^{-A}  +L^{-2}(\log\log(3X))^3  +X^{-\kappa+o(1)}.}  \label{eq:tpc27-pointwise-base-zero}$$ No mean-zero condition on the row weights or the physical orbit test is assumed.

###### ref-337378f113ed110d

###### ref-f74713b960615f43

###### ref-8b0da17277dbf017

###### ref-53f3759ab471af2c

###### ref-f3ce3c7719780a37

> **Proof** Use the exact factorization [\[eq:tpc27-Ramanujan-square\]](TPC-027.md#ref-db839e43b5fae669) and split its sum at $K_0$. For $k\le K_0$, write $$B_{k,S}(m)=  \frac{\mu(k)}{\varphi(k)}\delta_H(m)+e_k(m),  \qquad  |e_k(m)|\ll_A  \frac1{\varphi(k)(\log X)^{A+10}}.  \label{eq:tpc27-small-B-error}$$ The harmless extra ten logarithms anticipate the fixed reassembly losses. By [\[eq:tpc27-row-drift\]](TPC-027.md#ref-15dc21688ea0dc77), $$|B_{k,S}(m)B_{k,S}(n)|  \ll_A\frac1{\varphi(k)^2}  \left\{  \frac{(\log\log(3X))^2}{L^2}  +(\log X)^{-A-5}  \right\}.  \label{eq:tpc27-small-product}$$ Apply [\[eq:tpc27-small-Ramanujan-ledger\]](TPC-027.md#ref-a701b4be9676b002) and increase the calibration exponent once more. The small-$k$ contribution is $$\ll_A  L^{-2}(\log\log(3X))^3+(\log X)^{-A}.  \label{eq:tpc27-small-k-zero}$$
>
> For $k>K_0$, [\[lem:tpc27-large-Bk\]](TPC-027.md#ref-d604a3b79d92fe99) gives $$|B_{k,S}(m)B_{k,S}(n)|  \ll\frac{(\log(2X))^8}{k^2}.  \label{eq:tpc27-large-product}$$ Since $m\ne n$ and $|m-n|\le X^{O(1)}$, apply [\[eq:tpc27-large-Ramanujan-ledger\]](TPC-027.md#ref-76182b2e1e46356d) with $\Delta=m-n$. The large-$k$ contribution is $$\ll X^{-\kappa+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc27-large-k-zero}$$ Combining [\[eq:tpc27-small-k-zero\]](TPC-027.md#ref-8b0da17277dbf017) and [\[eq:tpc27-large-k-zero\]](TPC-027.md#ref-f3ce3c7719780a37) proves the theorem.

###### ref-cecfe396ea031b58

> **Remark: Why no operator hypothesis remains** \[rem:tpc27-no-zero-operator\] Entrywise decay alone does not close a dense bilinear row matrix under only soft $\ell^2$ energy; TPC-25 gives an exact interface counterexample. The present physical packet has more information: its one-row $\ell^1$-mass is $O(Q)$. We use that actual provenance before separation. Thus [\[thm:tpc27-pointwise-base-zero\]](TPC-027.md#ref-fdb05d3add85234f) does not contradict the earlier operator obstruction and does not assert a black-box theorem for arbitrary soft-energy row families.

## Physical frequency zero before separation

###### ref-cfffada9e5c7f6df

###### ref-44a4ea2666198237

Let $\mathcal Z_S^{ZZ,0}$ denote the physical additive-zero contribution of the $Z_{m_\alpha,S}Z_{m_\gamma,S}$ term in [\[eq:tpc27-ZZ-EZ-ZE-EE\]](TPC-027.md#ref-6c70ea51a8eea62b). It is taken before continuous Mellin separation. The bound in [\[eq:tpc27-bounded-zero-factor\]](TPC-027.md#ref-44a4ea2666198237) follows from [\[eq:tpc27-zero-functional\]](TPC-027.md#ref-27bce492f7e23625).

> **Theorem: Physical centered base-zero closure** \[thm:tpc27-physical-base-zero\] Under the hypotheses of [\[thm:tpc27-pointwise-base-zero\]](TPC-027.md#ref-fdb05d3add85234f), for every fixed $B>0$, $$\boxed{  \mathcal Z_S^{ZZ,0}  \ll_{B,\mathscr D}XQ(\log X)^{-B}.}  \label{eq:tpc27-physical-base-zero}$$

###### ref-886caa76eac65b43

> **Proof** The mask in [\[eq:tpc27-mask\]](TPC-027.md#ref-22132737679c2307) restricts [\[eq:tpc27-physical-ZZ-zero\]](TPC-027.md#ref-cfffada9e5c7f6df) to $m_\alpha\ne m_\gamma$, so [\[thm:tpc27-pointwise-base-zero\]](TPC-027.md#ref-fdb05d3add85234f) applies. This gives $$\begin{aligned}  |\mathcal Z_S^{ZZ,0}|  \ll_{A,\mathscr D}  JQ^2\left\{  (\log X)^{-A}  +L^{-2}(\log\log(3X))^3  +X^{-\kappa+o(1)}  \right\}.  \label{eq:tpc27-physical-zero-prebound}\end{aligned}$$ By [\[eq:tpc27-natural-scale\]](TPC-027.md#ref-5262916e8792f168), $JQ^2\asymp XQ$. This proves [\[eq:tpc27-physical-base-zero\]](TPC-027.md#ref-354ccf608025bc74).

Write $$Q=X^{\beta+o(1)},  \qquad J=X^{1-\beta+o(1)},  \qquad S=X^{s+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc27-base-exponents}$$ For the selected square $u,v\le S$, the common exact-conductor ledger of TPC-22 at $S=R$ and TPC-23 at $S>R$ is $$\eta_S(\beta,s)  =\min\left\{  1-\frac{3\beta}{2},  \frac{\beta+1-4s}{2},  \frac{3-\beta-6s}{4}  \right\}.  \label{eq:tpc27-etaS}$$ The corresponding nonzero theorem assumes $$s<1-\beta,  \qquad \eta_S(\beta,s)>0  \label{eq:tpc27-dynamic-region}$$ with fixed margins, together with the primitive row interface, divisor-independent off-diagonal mask, no-wrap condition, and the stated smooth Fourier-tail control [`\citep{WangTPC23}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-027.md#cite-f24192cbd2a455cd). > **Lemma: Physical transfer to the dynamic interface** \[lem:tpc27-physical-nonzero-transfer\] After splitting the $O_h(1)$ residue cells, the physical nonzero packet has a continuous representation $$\gamma_\alpha^{(1)}\gamma_\gamma^{(2)}  \mathcal W_{\alpha,\gamma}(j/J)  =\int  x_{\boldsymbol\tau}(\alpha)  y_{\boldsymbol\tau}(\gamma)  \Phi_{\boldsymbol\tau}(j/J)  \,d\nu(\boldsymbol\tau),  \label{eq:tpc27-continuous-transfer}$$ such that, for every fixed integer $M\ge0$, $$\begin{aligned}  \int(1+|\boldsymbol\tau|)^M  \|x_{\boldsymbol\tau}\|_1  \|y_{\boldsymbol\tau}\|_1  \,d|\nu|(\boldsymbol\tau)  &\ll_{M,\mathscr D}Q^2,  \label{eq:tpc27-transfer-l1}\\  \int(1+|\boldsymbol\tau|)^M  \|x_{\boldsymbol\tau}\|_2  \|y_{\boldsymbol\tau}\|_2  \,d|\nu|(\boldsymbol\tau)  &\ll_{M,\mathscr D}Q\log(2L).  \label{eq:tpc27-transfer-l2}\end{aligned}$$ Every component has the same divisor-independent mask, row scales, and no-wrap margins. The applicable TPC-22 static or TPC-23 dynamic mean, discrepancy, and Fourier-tail bounds may be integrated componentwise. For a fixed cell $j\equiv\varrho\pmod{H_0}$, the bounded factor $\Xi_{\alpha,\gamma}(\varrho)$ is absorbed into the row-pair mask. It remains independent of every divisor, additive frequency, and conductor variable.

> **Proof** This is the continuous log-Mellin separation proved for the same physical provenance in TPC-25. Each fixed smooth row–orbit factor has a Schwartz Mellin transform. Its row phase and orbit phase have modulus one, so the row norms are exactly \[[eq:tpc27-row-l1](TPC-027.md#ref-00cf3494cd12aa31),[eq:tpc27-row-l2](TPC-027.md#ref-15ff8548e798d36d)\] up to the Schwartz density. Schwartz moments prove \[[eq:tpc27-transfer-l1](TPC-027.md#ref-d3636abb477bb2c0),[eq:tpc27-transfer-l2](TPC-027.md#ref-c1a6ff33bcb6f917)\]. Smooth separation does not touch the generic mask.
>
> The constants in the applicable TPC-22 or TPC-23 component estimates grow at most polynomially in finitely many seminorms of $\Phi_{\boldsymbol\tau}$, while the weighted moments above are finite. Apply the triangle inequality and Tonelli’s theorem to finite Riemann sums, then use dominated convergence. The physical Fourier tail is automatic for this provenance by the rapid-decay argument of TPC-26; the base support $u,v\le S$ is a subfamily of the polynomial divisor box treated there. This proves the transfer.

The relevant Ramanujan-mean theorem bounds the global principal character cell by $Q^2X^\varepsilon$, a fixed-power saving from $Q^2J$ because $J$ is a fixed positive power. ## The three calibration channels

The fact that $E_S(m)$ depends on the row prevents it from being removed as one global scalar. Uniform pointwise calibration and the actual physical $\ell^1$-mass nevertheless suffice.

###### ref-c7e7cae84ef0c229

###### ref-287cace09f090437

> **Lemma: Orbit mass of one centered prefix** \[lem:tpc27-centered-orbit-mass\] For every retained row and every uniformly bounded physical orbit test supported on an interval of length $O(J)$, $$\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}  \left|F(j/J)Z_{m,S}(j)\right|  \ll_{\mathscr D}(J+S)(\log(2X))^4.  \label{eq:tpc27-centered-orbit-mass}$$ The same bound holds after imposing $(j,H)=1$ and a fixed residue cell modulo $H_0$.

> **Proof** For $(u,mH)=1$, the congruence $u\mid mj+h$ selects one class modulo $u$. Hence its intersection with an interval of length $O(J)$ has $O(J/u+1)$ points. From [\[eq:tpc27-Zms\]](TPC-027.md#ref-1ef9e364308a4a2a), $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_j|F(j/J)Z_{m,S}(j)|  &\ll  \sum_{u\le S}|b_R(u)|\left(\frac Ju+1\right).\end{aligned}$$ TPC-24’s prefix mass bound gives $\sum_{u\le S}|b_R(u)|/u\ll(\log(2X))^3$. Pointwise, the defining formula [\[eq:tpc27-lambda-prime\]](TPC-027.md#ref-e3c8a24c856cd734) gives $$|\lambda'_R(u)|  \le \frac{u}{\varphi(u)}  \sum_{b\le R/u}\frac{\mu^2(b)}{\varphi(b)}  \ll (\log(2X))^2$$ on its squarefree support; also $|a(u)|\le\log(2X)$. Hence $\sum_{u\le S}|b_R(u)|\ll S(\log(2X))^2$. Combining these estimates proves [\[eq:tpc27-centered-orbit-mass\]](TPC-027.md#ref-287cace09f090437). Additional fixed congruence restrictions only reduce the number of orbit points.

Let $\mathcal E_S^{EZ}$, $\mathcal E_S^{ZE}$, and $\mathcal E_S^{EE}$ denote the three physical terms obtained from the last three summands of [\[eq:tpc27-ZZ-EZ-ZE-EE\]](TPC-027.md#ref-6c70ea51a8eea62b).

Then, for every fixed $B>0$, $$\boxed{  |\mathcal E_S^{EZ}|+|\mathcal E_S^{ZE}|+|\mathcal E_S^{EE}|  \ll_{B,\mathscr D}XQ(\log X)^{-B}.}  \label{eq:tpc27-scalar-closure}$$

> **Theorem: Physical calibrated base closure** \[thm:tpc27-calibrated-base\] Retain all hypotheses and definitions of \[[sec:tpc27-interface-bridge](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-027.md#src-ddfce758055b5bcc),[sec:tpc27-multiple-calibration](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-027.md#src-de3186487262e58b)\], in particular $R\le S$, the actual opened-row coefficients, and the divisor-independent off-diagonal mask. For $ZZ$, first extract physical additive frequency zero before smooth separation and apply [\[thm:tpc27-physical-base-zero\]](TPC-027.md#ref-5e6d20d6a5f44569). Then separate only the nonzero spectrum and apply [\[thm:tpc27-physical-nonzero\]](TPC-027.md#ref-5409dfe2bb8586d7). The single and constant nonzero channels removed by the TPC-20 two-scale completion, the dynamic Ramanujan mean, and the physical Fourier tail are already included in that theorem’s reassembly. All fixed dyadic, sign, content, residue, and smooth decompositions cost at most a fixed power of $\log X$, which is absorbed by choosing the calibration and tail orders after $B$.

## What remains

After [\[cor:tpc27-selected-passage\]](TPC-027.md#ref-f1cc4ad71c93b210), three independent walls remain:

1.  a base nonzero theorem when the lower cutoff itself is beyond the Poisson length, needed for samples such as [\[eq:tpc27-high-sample\]](TPC-027.md#ref-8b750ff884211fa2);

2.  the ultra-long complement beyond the selected upper cutoff, whose divisor reflection produces polynomial families of two-point affine Möbius correlations;

3.  the separate positivity and main-term problem required to pass from an analytic upper/cancellation estimate to a prime-pair asymptotic or lower bound.

The first two are analytic cancellation gates. The third remains parity-sensitive. None is resolved by the calibrated base theorem.

## Why the interfaces cannot be silently dropped

Three finite examples isolate the logical boundaries. First, if a bilinear form is $\mathfrak B(x,y)=xy$, taking $Z_1=Z_2=0$ and $E_1=E_2=1$ makes the centered square vanish but the physical square equal one. Thus a centered estimate does not restore the scalar channels. Second, on an even orbit the two legs $C(j)=(-1)^j/2$ each have mean zero, while the additive zero mode of $C(j)^2$ is $1/4$. Separate centering does not remove the joint zero frequency. Third, on the row diagonal, $c_k(0)=\varphi(k)$, so the large-modulus $K_0^{-1}$ tail used in [\[thm:tpc27-pointwise-base-zero\]](TPC-027.md#ref-fdb05d3add85234f) disappears. These examples explain, respectively, the explicit $EZ,ZE,EE$ restoration, zero extraction before separation, and the off-diagonal mask.

## Next analytic gate

The next step has two branches: prove a centered nonzero base theorem for $S>J$, or isolate a verifiable affine two-point Möbius subfamily in the reflected complement. Failure can yield a precise barrier theorem; success would still require a separate positivity and main-term mechanism before twin primes.
