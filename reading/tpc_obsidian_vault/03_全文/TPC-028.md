## Abstract

Let $P_{m,S}$ be the calibrated divisor prefix in a primitive opened row $m=\ell d$, and suppose that the base cutoff $S$ may already exceed the orbit length $J\asymp X/Q$. The centered nonzero-spectrum theorem used previously requires $S<J$. Treating its scalar correction terms separately also incurs the polynomial loss $1+S/J$. We remove both appearances of that restriction by changing the order of the decomposition.

Write $$\mathsf A_{m,S}(j)=  \sum_{\substack{u\le S\\u\mid mj+h}}b_R(u),  \qquad P_{m,S}=\mathsf A_{m,S}-\delta_H(m).$$ At additive frequency zero, the raw joint kernel, both drift legs, and the constant term recombine exactly as $$\mathcal R_S^0(m,n)-\delta_H(m)D_{n,S}  -\delta_H(n)D_{m,S}+\delta_H(m)\delta_H(n)  =K_S^0(m,n)+E_S(m)E_S(n).$$ Here $D_{m,S}=\delta_H(m)+E_S(m)$, while $K_S^0$ is the Ramanujan-square connected kernel already known to have arbitrary logarithmic physical saving. Thus no Poisson-short hypothesis is needed at zero frequency.

Away from zero, we prove a full-rectangle version of the symmetric exact-conductor argument. For $u,v\le S$, denominator $[u,v]\asymp Y$, and coordinate conductors $F_1,F_2$, the raw joint cell satisfies $$\mathcal L^{\rm base}_{Y;S}(F_1,F_2)  \ll QX^\varepsilon  \sqrt{\mathcal K_Q(F_1)\mathcal K_Q(F_2)}  \min\{\sqrt{JY},F_1F_2\},  \qquad \mathcal K_Q(F)=F+Q/F.$$ The corresponding relaxed-box saving is the same piecewise function $M_\beta(s)$ that occurs in the annular joint problem. A pointwise target-divisor bound gives $\mathsf A_{m,S}(j)\ll_\varepsilon X^\varepsilon$, so the two remaining drift legs are power-small from the existing row-prime condition $L\ge X^{\lambda_0}$; no factor $1+S/J$ remains. Consequently $M_\beta(s)>0$, together with the physical row, no-wrap, Fourier-tail, and fixed-margin interfaces, closes the selected calibrated base packet even when $S>J$.

For the exact source-compatible sample $$\beta=\frac{267}{400},\qquad  s=\frac{23}{60},\qquad  t=\frac{193}{500},$$ the old centered ledger has the negative face $-1/800$, whereas the full-rectangle base margin is $M_\beta(s)=13/1600$. The row-prime exponent is $99979/210000$, and the inherited annular margin to $T=X^t$ is $33/8000$. Hence the selected calibrated truncated square closes at this particular $\beta>2/3$ sample. The ultra-long complement, positivity, a Hardy–Littlewood asymptotic, twin primes, and any general breach of the parity barrier remain outside the result.

# Introduction

This paper addresses one precise initial-value gap in the primitive Möbius-tail program of TPC-18–27. The target is not a prime-pair asymptotic. It is a cancellation estimate for one selected calibrated quadratic packet that occurs before the ultra-long complement and before any positivity argument.

###### ref-933d59ee1ef2e464

###### ref-eb3e9a01a701d6a0

The previous chain separates into two facts. First, TPC-26 proves cutoff stability $$\mathcal Q_T^{\rm phys}-\mathcal Q_S^{\rm phys}  \ll_B XQ(\log X)^{-B}  \label{eq:tpc28-intro-annular}$$ in a sharp joint-conductor region that can contain $S>J$ and $T>J$ [`\citep{WangTPC26}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-028.md#cite-ad014f62a6ac3092). Second, TPC-27 proves the matching initial value $$\mathcal Q_S^{\rm phys}  \ll_B XQ(\log X)^{-B}  \label{eq:tpc28-intro-old-base}$$ only when $S<J$ with a fixed power margin [`\citep{WangTPC27}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-028.md#cite-897cae50dd571a84). The logical difference matters: [\[eq:tpc28-intro-annular\]](TPC-028.md#ref-933d59ee1ef2e464) alone does not estimate either endpoint.

###### ref-985aa82d8f5f3953

The high-$\beta$ sample recorded in TPC-26 has $$\beta=\frac{267}{400},\qquad  J=X^{133/400+o(1)},\qquad  S=X^{23/60+o(1)},\qquad  T=X^{193/500+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc28-intro-high-sample}$$ Both cutoffs exceed $J$. Its annular margin is positive, but its initial value was therefore left open.

## Why the earlier base proof stops

###### ref-864d48fd909750ef

For a row $m=\ell d$, the calibrated prefix admits two exact representations: $$P_{m,S}=Z_{m,S}+E_S(m)  =\mathsf A_{m,S}-\delta_H(m).  \label{eq:tpc28-two-representations}$$ The first uses a centered divisor process and a calibration error $E_S(m)\ll_A(\log X)^{-A}$. The second uses the raw divisor prefix $\mathsf A_{m,S}$ and the row drift $\delta_H(m)\ll X^\varepsilon/L$.

###### ref-f66943ba118e2787

TPC-27 uses the centered representation at every frequency. Its nonzero exact-conductor ledger is $$\eta_S(\beta,s)=  \min\left\{  1-\frac{3\beta}{2},  \frac{\beta+1-4s}{2},  \frac{3-\beta-6s}{4}  \right\}.  \label{eq:tpc28-old-centered-ledger}$$ At [\[eq:tpc28-intro-high-sample\]](TPC-028.md#ref-985aa82d8f5f3953), its first entry is $-1/800$. Moreover, estimating $E_SZ_S$ by an orbitwise $\ell^1$ bound introduces $J+S$. An error that is smaller than every logarithmic power cannot absorb the fixed power $S/J$.

Neither obstruction occurs if the two representations in [\[eq:tpc28-two-representations\]](TPC-028.md#ref-864d48fd909750ef) are used at different additive frequencies. This is the central observation of the paper.

## The frequency-dependent recombination

###### ref-669d15013a75ccd1

At additive frequency zero we keep the centered representation. The complete-period mean of $Z_{m,S}$ vanishes, so the zero kernel of $P_{m,S}P_{n,S}$ is exactly $$K_S^0(m,n)+E_S(m)E_S(n).  \label{eq:tpc28-intro-zero-result}$$ The potentially dangerous $EZ$ and $ZE$ terms are absent by exact algebra, not by an estimate. TPC-27 already closes $K_S^0$ for every polynomial $S\ge R$; its zero theorem never required $S<J$.

###### ref-d188b4eea68690b3

###### ref-31c91f25e8892671

At nonzero frequency we instead expand $$\begin{aligned}  P_{m,S}P_{n,S}  ={}&\mathsf A_{m,S}\mathsf A_{n,S}  -\delta_H(m)\mathsf A_{n,S}  -\delta_H(n)\mathsf A_{m,S}  +\delta_H(m)\delta_H(n).  \end{aligned}  \label{eq:tpc28-intro-raw-expansion}$$ The coefficient-one term is one raw joint rectangle $u,v\le S$. The other three terms contain the fixed-power row drift. On the physical target support, a divisor bound gives $$\mathsf A_{m,S}(j)\ll_\varepsilon X^\varepsilon,  \label{eq:tpc28-intro-divisor-bound}$$ uniformly in $S$. Hence the full drift contribution is already power-small from $L\ge X^{\lambda_0}$; no $J+S$ loss is needed.

###### ref-a2c32842b38418b1

For the raw joint rectangle we prove the full-base analogue of the TPC-26 annular cell theorem. The annular lower restriction was deleted when enlarging the nonnegative tensor energy, but the full-base result is not obtained merely by setting an endpoint to zero. We define the full fibers, their exact-conductor projections, principal mean, and Fourier reassembly anew. The resulting cell estimate is $$\mathcal L^{\rm base}_{Y;S}(F_1,F_2)  \ll_\varepsilon QX^\varepsilon  \sqrt{\mathcal K_Q(F_1)\mathcal K_Q(F_2)}  \min\{\sqrt{JY},F_1F_2\}.  \label{eq:tpc28-intro-cell-bound}$$ It gives the sharp relaxed-box margin $M_\beta(s)$, without the old face $1-3\beta/2$.

## Main consequence

###### ref-db1d68a326d34305

###### ref-4d2667e5b0d05504

###### ref-aafbcdf5c2d76126

Under the actual opened-row provenance, the divisor-independent off-diagonal mask, the common physical weight, no-wrap, and fixed Fourier-tail interfaces, we prove $$\boxed{  M_\beta(s)>0  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad  \mathcal Q_S^{\rm phys}  \ll_B XQ(\log X)^{-B}}  \label{eq:tpc28-intro-main}$$ for every fixed $B>0$. There is no condition $S<J$. At the high sample, $$M_{267/400}(23/60)=\frac{13}{1600}>0.  \label{eq:tpc28-intro-base-margin}$$ Combining [\[eq:tpc28-intro-main\]](TPC-028.md#ref-db1d68a326d34305) with the TPC-26 annular margin $$M_{267/400}(193/500)=\frac{33}{8000}>0  \label{eq:tpc28-intro-upper-margin}$$ closes the selected calibrated truncated square through $T$.

The improvement is a recombination theorem inside a specific analytic packet. It does not estimate the complement beyond $T$, whose divisor reflection exposes polynomial families of affine two-point Möbius correlations [`\citep{WangTPC23,WangTPC26}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-028.md#cite-f24192cbd2a455cd); [\[WangTPC26\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-028.md#cite-ad014f62a6ac3092). It also does not produce a positive main term. In particular, the result neither implies twin primes nor claims a general breach of the sieve parity barrier.

## Rows, scales, and coefficients

###### ref-56683c5fa097da51

Fix $h\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}$, and put $$H=\operatorname{rad}|h|,\qquad H_0=|h|.  \label{eq:tpc28-H-H0}$$ Let $$R=X^{r_0+o(1)},\qquad r_0=\frac12-\delta>0,  \qquad R\le S\le X^{C_S}.  \label{eq:tpc28-RS}$$ Rows in one opened slice are $$\alpha=(\ell,d_\alpha),\qquad  m_\alpha=\ell d_\alpha\asymp Q=LD,  \label{eq:tpc28-row-index}$$ where $\ell\asymp L$ is prime, $d_\alpha\asymp D$ is squarefree, $(d_\alpha,H)=1$, and $$d_\alpha\le R^{1/2},\qquad  QJ\asymp X,\qquad  L>\max\{2R,2H_0\},\qquad  L\ge X^{\lambda_0}  \label{eq:tpc28-row-scales}$$ for a fixed $\lambda_0>0$. All exponent inequalities have fixed positive margins. The physical orbit support is chosen so that $$|m_\alpha j+h|\asymp X  \label{eq:tpc28-target-support}$$ whenever its smooth weight is nonzero. In particular the target is never zero.

###### ref-22f763cf4a5ff2a5

###### ref-ce90de696cae15ef

On the primitive orbit $(j,H)=1$, divisibility gives $$(u,m_\alpha H)=1  \quad\text{whenever}\quad u\mid m_\alpha j+h.  \label{eq:tpc28-target-primitivity}$$ The two physical row coefficients have the opened-$d$ provenance $$\gamma_\alpha^{(i)}  =\mu(d_\alpha)(\log\ell)\omega_D^{(i)}(d_\alpha)  \psi_L^{(i)}(\ell/L)\zeta_\alpha^{(i)},  \qquad |\zeta_\alpha^{(i)}|\ll1,  \label{eq:tpc28-row-provenance}$$ and satisfy $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_\alpha|\gamma_\alpha^{(i)}|&\ll Q,  \label{eq:tpc28-row-l1}\\  \|\gamma^{(i)}\|_2^2&\ll Q\log(2L).  \label{eq:tpc28-row-l2}\end{aligned}$$ These are the physical provenance bounds isolated in TPC-25 [`\citep{WangTPC25}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-028.md#cite-39e8dc14c55a2411).

The mask $\mathfrak m(\alpha,\gamma)$ is bounded by one, independent of every divisor and conductor variable, and vanishes when $m_\alpha=m_\gamma$. The periodic factor $\Xi_{\alpha,\gamma}(j)$ has fixed period $H_0$. The smooth row–orbit weight $\mathcal W_{\alpha,\gamma}(j/J)$ belongs to the same factorable bounded $C_c^\infty$ family as in TPC-25–27. Its support has $O(J)$ integer points, its zero Fourier functional is uniformly bounded, and its Mellin representation has finite weighted variation of every fixed order. All fixed source, residue, smoothness, and margin data are denoted by $\mathscr D$.

Put $$a(u)=-\mu(u)\log u,  \qquad  \lambda'_R(u)=  \begin{cases}  u\displaystyle\sum_{b\le R/u}  \dfrac{\mu(ub)\mu(b)}{\varphi(ub)},&u\le R,\\[7pt]  0,&u>R,  \end{cases}  \qquad b_R(u)=a(u)-\lambda'_R(u).  \label{eq:tpc28-coefficients}$$ All three sequences are supported on squarefree integers. The elementary positive majorant used in TPC-24 and TPC-27 gives $$|b_R(u)|\ll_{\mathscr D}(\log(2X))^3  \qquad (u\le X^{C_S}).  \label{eq:tpc28-pointwise-b}$$ Indeed, $u/\varphi(u)\ll\log\log(3X)$ and $\sum_{b\le R/u}\mu^2(b)/\varphi(b)\ll\log(2X)$; the displayed third power is a convenient uniform envelope.

Consequently $$\boxed{P_{m,S}=\mathsf A_{m,S}-\delta_H(m)  =Z_{m,S}+E_S(m).}  \label{eq:tpc28-two-prefix-bridges}$$

## The exact zero-frequency identity

###### ref-66f92ef48434396f

###### ref-1030443b0c725c8e

For distinct rows $m,n$, put $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal R_S^0(m,n)  :={}&  \sum_{\substack{u,v\le S\\(u,mH)=1\\(v,nH)=1}}  \frac{b_R(u)b_R(v)}{uv}  (u,v)\mathbf 1_{(u,v)\mid m-n},  \label{eq:tpc28-raw-zero-kernel}\\  K_S^0(m,n)  :={}&  \sum_{\substack{u,v\le S\\(u,mH)=1\\(v,nH)=1}}  \frac{b_R(u)b_R(v)}{uv}  \left((u,v)\mathbf 1_{(u,v)\mid m-n}-1\right).  \label{eq:tpc28-connected-zero-kernel}\end{aligned}$$ The fixed outer primitive density is suppressed in both definitions; it is restored once in the physical Poisson coefficient. The same formulas hold inside a fixed residue class modulo $H_0$, up to one bounded local factor.

###### ref-108f552bcbc2c6d1

###### ref-d915418480850c3b

###### ref-0d17996121863fea

> **Proposition: Raw-to-centered zero recombination** \[prop:tpc28-zero-recombination\] For every retained pair of rows, $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal R_S^0(m,n)&=K_S^0(m,n)+D_{m,S}D_{n,S},  \label{eq:tpc28-raw-connected-zero}\\  \mathcal R_S^0(m,n)  -\delta_H(m)D_{n,S}  -\delta_H(n)D_{m,S}  +\delta_H(m)\delta_H(n)  &=K_S^0(m,n)+E_S(m)E_S(n).  \label{eq:tpc28-calibrated-zero-identity}\end{aligned}$$ After factoring out the fixed outer primitive density, the left side of [\[eq:tpc28-calibrated-zero-identity\]](TPC-028.md#ref-0d17996121863fea) is exactly the complete-period arithmetic zero kernel of $P_{m,S}P_{n,S}$.

###### ref-1bb352291e7e2425

> **Proof** The primitive CRT density of the two target congruences is $$\frac{(u,v)}{uv}\mathbf 1_{(u,v)\mid m-n},  \label{eq:tpc28-joint-density}$$ after suppressing the fixed outer density. This proves that [\[eq:tpc28-raw-zero-kernel\]](TPC-028.md#ref-66f92ef48434396f) is the raw zero kernel. Subtracting [\[eq:tpc28-connected-zero-kernel\]](TPC-028.md#ref-1030443b0c725c8e) leaves $$\sum_u\frac{b_R(u)}u  \sum_v\frac{b_R(v)}v  =D_{m,S}D_{n,S},$$ with the row-coprimality restrictions in the corresponding factors. This proves [\[eq:tpc28-raw-connected-zero\]](TPC-028.md#ref-d915418480850c3b). Insert $D_{m,S}=\delta_H(m)+E_S(m)$ and expand. The two mixed terms and the $\delta_H(m)\delta_H(n)$ term cancel exactly, giving [\[eq:tpc28-calibrated-zero-identity\]](TPC-028.md#ref-0d17996121863fea). The last assertion follows by taking the complete-period mean in $P_{m,S}=\mathsf A_{m,S}-\delta_H(m)$.

###### ref-c2719c7ef3aaf594

> **Remark: Why the order matters** \[rem:tpc28-zero-order\] If one first takes absolute values of the three scalar channels in $(Z+E)(Z+E)$, the exact cancellation in [\[eq:tpc28-calibrated-zero-identity\]](TPC-028.md#ref-0d17996121863fea) is lost. The identity is a frequency-zero statement for the complete calibrated product, not an estimate for its four expanded pieces.

## Physical zero closure

###### ref-bfc49c3ac7a5ef78

###### ref-391e3f06daa8f593

TPC-27 proves that for every fixed $A>0$, every fixed $$0<\kappa<\min\{s,r_0/2\},  \qquad S=X^{s+o(1)},  \label{eq:tpc28-kappa-range}$$ and distinct retained rows, $$K_S^0(m,n)  \ll_{A,\mathscr D}  (\log X)^{-A}  +L^{-2}(\log\log(3X))^3  +X^{-\kappa+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc28-imported-zero-bound}$$ This is the Ramanujan-square theorem; it has no hypothesis $S<J$ [`\citep{WangTPC27}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-028.md#cite-897cae50dd571a84).

###### ref-0cd3238a6bc66082

###### ref-c9974107c7532c72

> **Theorem: Physical calibrated zero closure** \[thm:tpc28-physical-zero\] Retain the physical interface above, the parameter range [\[eq:tpc28-kappa-range\]](TPC-028.md#ref-bfc49c3ac7a5ef78), and the imported bound [\[eq:tpc28-imported-zero-bound\]](TPC-028.md#ref-391e3f06daa8f593). Then the additive zero contribution of the complete product in [\[eq:tpc28-physical-base\]](TPC-028.md#ref-f15c0df1e7c7c882) satisfies, for every fixed $B>0$, $$\boxed{  (\mathcal Q_S^{\rm phys})^0  \ll_{B,\mathscr D}XQ(\log X)^{-B}.}  \label{eq:tpc28-physical-zero-closure}$$ No Poisson-short condition is assumed.

> **Proof** Split the fixed $H_0$-periodic factor into its finitely many residue classes and take physical frequency zero before Mellin separation. The zero functional of the smooth weight and the fixed local density are uniformly bounded. By [\[prop:tpc28-zero-recombination\]](TPC-028.md#ref-108f552bcbc2c6d1), the arithmetic kernel is $K_S^0(m,n)+E_S(m)E_S(n)$. The mask deletes $m=n$, so [\[eq:tpc28-imported-zero-bound\]](TPC-028.md#ref-391e3f06daa8f593) applies. Delete the bounded mask and zero functional, then use [\[eq:tpc28-row-l1\]](TPC-028.md#ref-ce90de696cae15ef) twice. The result is $$\begin{aligned}  |(\mathcal Q_S^{\rm phys})^0|  \ll JQ^2\{&(\log X)^{-A}  +L^{-2}(\log\log(3X))^3  +X^{-\kappa+o(1)}  +(\log X)^{-2A}\}.\end{aligned}$$ Now use [\[eq:tpc28-natural-scale\]](TPC-028.md#ref-7e44bf9c477f0290), choose $A$ after $B$, and use $L\ge X^{\lambda_0}$ and $\kappa>0$. This proves the theorem.

# The full-base exact-conductor rectangle

We now treat the nonzero additive spectrum of the coefficient-one term $\mathsf A_{m,S}\mathsf A_{n,S}$ in [\[eq:tpc28-intro-raw-expansion\]](TPC-028.md#ref-d188b4eea68690b3). All divisor pairs $u,v\le S$ are retained. There is no annular lower cutoff.

## Physical separation after zero extraction

###### ref-097ed25002f0125c

###### ref-f7e19c42a9d75a13

After the zero term of [\[thm:tpc28-physical-zero\]](TPC-028.md#ref-0cd3238a6bc66082) has been removed, split the fixed $H_0$-periodic multiplier into its finitely many residue cells. On one cell the physical coefficient–weight product has the continuous representation $$\gamma_\alpha^{(1)}\gamma_\gamma^{(2)}  \mathcal W_{\alpha,\gamma}(j/J)  =\int  x_{\boldsymbol\tau}(\alpha)  y_{\boldsymbol\tau}(\gamma)  \Phi_{\boldsymbol\tau}(j/J)  \,d\nu(\boldsymbol\tau),  \label{eq:tpc28-continuous-transfer}$$ where every fixed weighted moment satisfies $$\begin{aligned}  \int(1+|\boldsymbol\tau|)^M  \|x_{\boldsymbol\tau}\|_2  \|y_{\boldsymbol\tau}\|_2\,d|\nu|  &\ll_{M,\mathscr D}Q\log(2L),  \label{eq:tpc28-transfer-l2}\\  \int(1+|\boldsymbol\tau|)^M  \|x_{\boldsymbol\tau}\|_1  \|y_{\boldsymbol\tau}\|_1\,d|\nu|  &\ll_{M,\mathscr D}Q^2.  \label{eq:tpc28-transfer-l1}\end{aligned}$$ The row scales, target primitivity, and divisor-independent mask are unchanged. This is the physical transfer lemma of TPC-25–27 applied to the same provenance. We prove the conductor estimates for one component and integrate only after reassembly.

Fix one parameter $\boldsymbol\tau$ and abbreviate $$x=x_{\boldsymbol\tau},\qquad  y=y_{\boldsymbol\tau},\qquad  G=\Phi_{\boldsymbol\tau}.  \label{eq:tpc28-component-abbreviation}$$ The Schwartz seminorms of $G$ grow at most polynomially in $1+|\boldsymbol\tau|$, and those powers are absorbed by the weighted moments in \[[eq:tpc28-transfer-l2](TPC-028.md#ref-f7e19c42a9d75a13),[eq:tpc28-transfer-l1](TPC-028.md#ref-257071e75e69f2c6)\].

Write $$e(t)=e^{2\pi it},\qquad e_q(t)=e(t/q),\qquad  \widehat G(y)=\int_\mathbb RG(x)e(-xy)\,dx.  \label{eq:tpc28-Fourier-conventions}$$ For an effective squarefree pair, put $$g=(u,v),\qquad u=gc,\qquad v=gd,\qquad  q=[u,v]=g\,c\,d.  \label{eq:tpc28-gcd-coordinates}$$ Then $g,c,d$ are pairwise coprime. The target congruences are compatible exactly when $$g\mid m-n.  \label{eq:tpc28-compatibility}$$ When compatible they determine one unit class $\rho_{m,n}^{u,v}\pmod q$. Suppressing the harmless fixed residue cell, Poisson summation gives $$\begin{aligned}  &\sum_{\substack{j\in\mathbb Z\\(j,H)=1}}  G(j/J)\mathbf 1_{u\mid mj+h}\mathbf 1_{v\mid nj+h}  \notag\\  &\qquad=  \frac{J}{Hq}\sum_{r\in\mathbb Z}c_H(r)  e_q(r\overline H\rho_{m,n}^{u,v})  \widehat G\left(\frac{rJ}{Hq}\right),  \label{eq:tpc28-joint-Poisson}\end{aligned}$$ where $\overline H$ is the inverse of $H\pmod q$. The term $r=0$ has already been treated.

## Pair fibers and exact coordinate conductors

###### ref-b64a09ec067bfaee

###### ref-fa95beefe40ff65e

###### ref-7fdf27e5ea7f0d61

###### ref-666e1284556d0cd1

###### ref-44398fc598df6d94

From this point onward, row labels are written as $m,n$; the symbols $\alpha,\gamma$ denote multiplicative characters. Let $\mathfrak X(w)$ denote the character group of $(\mathbb Z/w\mathbb Z)^\times$. Since $q=ud$ and $(u,d)=1$, $$\mathfrak X(q)\simeq\mathfrak X(u)\times\mathfrak X(d),  \qquad \chi=\alpha\otimes\gamma,  \qquad \operatorname{cond}\chi=(\operatorname{cond}\alpha)(\operatorname{cond}\gamma).  \label{eq:tpc28-product-characters}$$ For separated row weights $x_m,y_n$, define $$c_{m,n}^{(g)}  =x_my_n\mathfrak m(m,n)\mathbf 1_{g\mid m-n}.  \label{eq:tpc28-compatibility-matrix}$$ The unit fiber and its discrepancy are $$\begin{aligned}  Z_{u,v}(\xi)  &:=\sum_{\substack{m,n\\g\mid m-n\\  \rho_{m,n}^{u,v}\equiv\xi\bmod q}}  x_my_n\mathfrak m(m,n),  \label{eq:tpc28-unit-fiber}\\  \overline Z_{u,v}  &:=\frac1{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\xi\in(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^\times}Z_{u,v}(\xi),  \qquad D_{u,v}=Z_{u,v}-\overline Z_{u,v}.  \label{eq:tpc28-unit-discrepancy}\end{aligned}$$ Finite CRT algebra gives the exact multiplicative transform $$\widehat Z_{u,v}^{\,\times}(\alpha\otimes\gamma)  =\overline{(\alpha\otimes\gamma)(-h)}  \sum_{m,n}c_{m,n}^{(g)}\alpha(m)\gamma(n).  \label{eq:tpc28-exact-transform}$$ For every nonprincipal pair, centering changes no transform.

###### ref-47154afdd99b780f

Let $\mathfrak X_F(w)$ be the characters modulo $w$ whose exact conductor lies in a dyadic interval $\asymp F$, with $F=1$ denoting the principal character. The pairwise conductor projection is $$\begin{aligned}  D_{u,v}^{F_1,F_2}(\xi)  :={}&\frac1{\varphi(q)}  \sum_{\alpha\in\mathfrak X_{F_1}(u)}  \sum_{\gamma\in\mathfrak X_{F_2}(d)}  \widehat D_{u,v}^{\,\times}(\alpha\otimes\gamma)  (\alpha\otimes\gamma)(\xi).  \label{eq:tpc28-pair-projection}\end{aligned}$$

###### ref-2dd3eca02342b8fe

The off-diagonal mask gives the compatibility-energy bound $$\sum_{g\le S}\sum_{m,n}|c_{m,n}^{(g)}|^2  \ll_\varepsilon X^\varepsilon\|x\|_2^2\|y\|_2^2.  \label{eq:tpc28-compatibility-energy}$$ Indeed, after reversing the order, the multiplicity is bounded by $\tau(|m-n|)\ll_\varepsilon X^\varepsilon$. This is the only use of $m\ne n$ in the nonzero energy.

###### ref-72aeaa4afb02b7d2

###### ref-8a7f6ca7c1624d63

Put $$\mathcal K_Q(F)=F+\frac QF.  \label{eq:tpc28-KQ}$$ For dyadic $Y,F_1,F_2$, define the full-base tensor energy $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal E^{\rm base}_{Y;S}(F_1,F_2)  :={}&\sum_{\substack{g,c,d\ \mathrm{pairwise\ coprime}\\  gc,gd\le S\\g\,c\,d\asymp Y}}  \frac{|b_R(gc)b_R(gd)|^2}{\varphi(g\,c\,d)}  \notag\\  &\quad\times  \sum_{\alpha\in\mathfrak X_{F_1}(gc)}  \sum_{\gamma\in\mathfrak X_{F_2}(d)}  \left|\sum_{m,n}c_{m,n}^{(g)}  \alpha(m)\gamma(n)\right|^2.  \label{eq:tpc28-base-energy}\end{aligned}$$

###### ref-27910419276d8421

###### ref-ea042e7e2cfccf1f

> **Theorem: Full-base tensor conductor energy** \[thm:tpc28-base-energy\] Uniformly for $Y\le S^2$ and $F_1,F_2\le S$, $$\boxed{  \mathcal E^{\rm base}_{Y;S}(F_1,F_2)  \ll_\varepsilon X^\varepsilon\|x\|_2^2\|y\|_2^2  \mathcal K_Q(F_1)\mathcal K_Q(F_2).}  \label{eq:tpc28-base-energy-bound}$$

###### ref-d9fc83190ae93726

> **Proof** Fix $g$ and split both ambient moduli dyadically. Enlarge the nonnegative sum by deleting the divisibility and coprimality restrictions after the dyadic ranges have been retained. Apply the exact-conductor multiplicative large sieve in the following normalization, where $(a_r)$ is supported in an interval of length $N$: $$\sum_{\substack{w\asymp W\\w\ {\rm squarefree}}}  \frac1{\varphi(w)}  \sum_{\substack{\chi\bmod w\\\operatorname{cond}\chi\asymp F}}  \left|\sum_ra_r\chi(r)\right|^2  \ll_\varepsilon X^\varepsilon\left(F+\frac NF\right)  \sum_r|a_r|^2,  \label{eq:tpc28-exact-conductor-LS}$$ Here $F=1$ denotes the principal character induced from conductor one; the case $w=1$ is included and follows directly from Cauchy. Apply this inequality first in the $m$-coordinate in Hilbert-valued form and then in the $n$-coordinate [`\citep{IwaniecKowalski2004,WangTPC22,WangTPC26}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-028.md#cite-49dfea87eaada156); [\[WangTPC22\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-028.md#cite-6ea7e7d72ef27c8f); [\[WangTPC26\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-028.md#cite-ad014f62a6ac3092). Both row intervals have length $O(Q)$, so the contribution for fixed $g$ is $$\ll_\varepsilon X^\varepsilon\mathcal K_Q(F_1)\mathcal K_Q(F_2)  \sum_{m,n}|c_{m,n}^{(g)}|^2.$$ The pointwise bound [\[eq:tpc28-pointwise-b\]](TPC-028.md#ref-dacb9b916f48bcc2), the logarithmically many dyadic subdivisions, and squarefree multiplicities are absorbed by $X^\varepsilon$. Sum over $g$ and apply [\[eq:tpc28-compatibility-energy\]](TPC-028.md#ref-2dd3eca02342b8fe).

###### ref-9ac5d8d79bd7d3cd

> **Remark: Why this is a full-base theorem** \[rem:tpc28-not-endpoint-substitution\] The energy proof resembles the annular proof because that proof also deleted its lower support restriction when enlarging a nonnegative sum. Nevertheless, [\[eq:tpc28-base-energy\]](TPC-028.md#ref-8a7f6ca7c1624d63) is a newly defined full rectangle. No illegal substitution of a cutoff below $R$ is made in an annular prefix identity.

## Two estimates for one nonzero cell

###### ref-3801b55c83e5be86

###### ref-fe12f9c5224436d9

###### ref-d571715f198ba181

Assume the no-wrap margin $$J\ge4HX^{\varepsilon_0}  \label{eq:tpc28-no-wrap}$$ for a fixed $\varepsilon_0>0$, and choose $0<\omega<\varepsilon_0$. For one divisor pair define the retained scalar $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal L_{u,v}^{F_1,F_2,\le\omega}  :={}&\frac{J}{Hq}  \sum_{0<|r|\le X^\omega Hq/J}  c_H(r)\widehat G\left(\frac{rJ}{Hq}\right)  \notag\\  &\quad\times  \sum_{\xi\in(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^\times}  D_{u,v}^{F_1,F_2}(\xi)e_q(r\overline H\xi).  \label{eq:tpc28-pair-cell-functional}\end{aligned}$$ Aggregate it over the full rectangle by $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal L^{\rm base}_{Y;S,\boldsymbol\tau}(F_1,F_2)  :={}&\sum_{\substack{g,c,d\ \mathrm{pairwise\ coprime}\\  gc,gd\le S\\g\,c\,d\asymp Y}}  |b_R(gc)b_R(gd)|  |\mathcal L_{gc,gd}^{F_1,F_2,\le\omega}|.  \label{eq:tpc28-base-cell}\end{aligned}$$

For some fixed $C_{\mathscr D}>0$, the polynomial seminorm growth described after [\[eq:tpc28-component-abbreviation\]](TPC-028.md#ref-83295bb130fc7bd7) gives the following two homogeneous bounds.

###### ref-a9064976f651e6ef

###### ref-d569e3b73a51072e

> **Lemma: Restricted-frequency norm route** \[lem:tpc28-norm-route\] For every exact-conductor cell, $$\mathcal L^{\rm base}_{Y;S,\boldsymbol\tau}(F_1,F_2)  \ll_{\varepsilon,\mathscr D}X^\varepsilon  (1+|\boldsymbol\tau|)^{C_{\mathscr D}}  \|x\|_2\|y\|_2  \sqrt{JY\mathcal K_Q(F_1)\mathcal K_Q(F_2)}.  \label{eq:tpc28-norm-route}$$

###### ref-87623a63ecb681e0

###### ref-346788326ebc5ed9

> **Proof** For one $q$, let $$\mathcal P_q(\xi)=\frac{J}{Hq}  \sum_{0<|r|\le X^\omega Hq/J}  c_H(r)\widehat G\left(\frac{rJ}{Hq}\right)  e_q(r\overline H\xi).$$ The no-wrap condition makes the retained frequencies distinct modulo $q$. Additive orthogonality on all residues and Schwartz decay give $$\sum_{\xi\in(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^\times}|\mathcal P_q(\xi)|^2  \ll_{\mathscr D}(1+|\boldsymbol\tau|)^{C_{\mathscr D}}J.  \label{eq:tpc28-Pq-norm}$$ Multiplicative Parseval then bounds one pair by $$|\mathcal L_{u,v}^{F_1,F_2,\le\omega}|  \ll_{\mathscr D}(1+|\boldsymbol\tau|)^{C_{\mathscr D}}  \left\{\frac J{\varphi(q)}  \sum_{\alpha,\gamma}  |\widehat D_{u,v}^{\,\times}  (\alpha\otimes\gamma)|^2\right\}^{1/2},  \label{eq:tpc28-pair-norm-bound}$$ where the characters lie in the displayed conductor cells. There are $O_\varepsilon(YX^\varepsilon)$ pairwise-coprime triples with $g\,c\,d\asymp Y$. Apply Cauchy–Schwarz over the triples and then [\[thm:tpc28-base-energy\]](TPC-028.md#ref-27910419276d8421).

###### ref-680efe088e62538b

###### ref-c643bb699d46b64f

For the second route, let the exact conductors be $f_1,f_2$, put $f=f_1f_2$, and write $q=ef$. Squarefreeness gives $(e,f)=1$. The induced Gauss sum satisfies $$\left|\sum_{\xi\in(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^\times}  (\alpha\otimes\gamma)(\xi)e_q(r\overline H\xi)\right|  \le(f_1f_2)^{1/2}|c_e(r)|.  \label{eq:tpc28-induced-Gauss}$$ At $r\ne0$, $$\sum_{r\ne0}|c_H(r)c_e(r)|  \left|\widehat G\left(\frac{rJ}{Hq}\right)\right|  \ll_{\mathscr D}(1+|\boldsymbol\tau|)^{C_{\mathscr D}}  \frac{Hq}{J}\tau(e).  \label{eq:tpc28-Ramanujan-frequency}$$

###### ref-630ecde10c27dc79

###### ref-551f84dfdf9377f5

> **Lemma: Induced-Gauss route** \[lem:tpc28-Gauss-route\] For every nonprincipal exact-conductor cell, $$\mathcal L^{\rm base}_{Y;S,\boldsymbol\tau}(F_1,F_2)  \ll_{\varepsilon,\mathscr D}X^\varepsilon  (1+|\boldsymbol\tau|)^{C_{\mathscr D}}  \|x\|_2\|y\|_2F_1F_2  \sqrt{\mathcal K_Q(F_1)\mathcal K_Q(F_2)}.  \label{eq:tpc28-Gauss-route}$$

> **Proof** Insert [\[eq:tpc28-pair-projection\]](TPC-028.md#ref-47154afdd99b780f) into [\[eq:tpc28-pair-cell-functional\]](TPC-028.md#ref-fe12f9c5224436d9). The elementary exact-conductor count is $\#\mathfrak X_{F_i}(w)\ll_\varepsilon F_iX^\varepsilon$ for squarefree $w$. Use [\[eq:tpc28-induced-Gauss\]](TPC-028.md#ref-680efe088e62538b) and [\[eq:tpc28-Ramanujan-frequency\]](TPC-028.md#ref-c643bb699d46b64f), followed by Cauchy–Schwarz in the characters. Sum over divisor triples using Cauchy–Schwarz with weights $|b_R(u)b_R(v)|^2/\varphi(q)$ and $1/\varphi(q)$. The second weight has total $O_\varepsilon(X^\varepsilon)$ on $q\asymp Y$, since the number of pairwise-coprime factorizations is at most $3^{\omega(q)}$. The first is bounded by [\[thm:tpc28-base-energy\]](TPC-028.md#ref-27910419276d8421).

###### ref-63aea6465a955498

###### ref-68790af350ee40f0

###### ref-1dbbb39abfd5362f

> **Theorem: Full-rectangle cell bound** \[thm:tpc28-cell-bound\] For every nonprincipal conductor cell, $$\mathcal L^{\rm base}_{Y;S,\boldsymbol\tau}(F_1,F_2)  \ll_{\varepsilon,\mathscr D}X^\varepsilon  (1+|\boldsymbol\tau|)^{C_{\mathscr D}}  \|x_{\boldsymbol\tau}\|_2\|y_{\boldsymbol\tau}\|_2  \sqrt{\mathcal K_Q(F_1)\mathcal K_Q(F_2)}  \min\{\sqrt{JY},F_1F_2\}.  \label{eq:tpc28-cell-bound-homogeneous}$$ Consequently, $$\boxed{  \int\mathcal L^{\rm base}_{Y;S,\boldsymbol\tau}(F_1,F_2)  \,d|\nu|(\boldsymbol\tau)  \ll_{\varepsilon,\mathscr D}QX^\varepsilon  \sqrt{\mathcal K_Q(F_1)\mathcal K_Q(F_2)}  \min\{\sqrt{JY},F_1F_2\}.}  \label{eq:tpc28-cell-bound}$$ The global principal pair $(F_1,F_2)=(1,1)$ is excluded; cells with exactly one principal coordinate are included.

> **Proof** Take the minimum of \[[lem:tpc28-norm-route](TPC-028.md#ref-a9064976f651e6ef),[lem:tpc28-Gauss-route](TPC-028.md#ref-630ecde10c27dc79)\] to obtain [\[eq:tpc28-cell-bound-homogeneous\]](TPC-028.md#ref-68790af350ee40f0). Integrate once and use [\[eq:tpc28-transfer-l2\]](TPC-028.md#ref-f7e19c42a9d75a13); the factor $\log(2L)$ is absorbed by $X^\varepsilon$. This proves [\[eq:tpc28-cell-bound\]](TPC-028.md#ref-1dbbb39abfd5362f).

# Sharp base minimax and nonzero reassembly

We optimize [\[thm:tpc28-cell-bound\]](TPC-028.md#ref-63aea6465a955498) over the complete base box and then restore the principal mean, Fourier tail, fixed residue cells, and physical Mellin components.

## The exponent ledger

###### ref-c886a77806fd9aaf

###### ref-380f33411425ad75

Write $$Q=X^{\beta+o(1)},\qquad  J=X^{1-\beta+o(1)},\qquad  S=X^{s+o(1)},  \qquad \frac12\le\beta<1.  \label{eq:tpc28-main-exponents}$$ On one denominator and exact-conductor cell let $$Y=X^{y+o(1)},\qquad  F_i=X^{f_i+o(1)},\qquad  k_i=\max\{f_i,\beta-f_i\}.  \label{eq:tpc28-cell-exponents}$$ The full rectangle gives $$0\le y\le2s,\qquad 0\le f_1,f_2\le s.  \label{eq:tpc28-base-box}$$ Relative to the natural exponent $1+\beta$, the norm and Gauss routes in [\[eq:tpc28-cell-bound\]](TPC-028.md#ref-1dbbb39abfd5362f) have savings $$\begin{aligned}  \eta_A(y,f_1,f_2)  &=\frac{\beta+1-y-k_1-k_2}{2},  \label{eq:tpc28-etaA}\\  \eta_B(f_1,f_2)  &=1-f_1-f_2-\frac{k_1+k_2}{2}.  \label{eq:tpc28-etaB}\end{aligned}$$ Since decreasing $y$ improves $\eta_A$, define $$M_\beta(s)  :=\inf_{0\le f_1,f_2\le s}  \max\{\eta_A(2s,f_1,f_2),\eta_B(f_1,f_2)\}.  \label{eq:tpc28-Mbeta-definition}$$

###### ref-898f3f8b1b0e2135

###### ref-6e95c01c4a6c2164

> **Proposition: Exact relaxed-box base minimax** \[prop:tpc28-sharp-minimax\] For $1/2\le\beta<1$, $$\boxed{  M_\beta(s)=  \begin{cases}  1-\beta-s,  &0\le s\le(1-\beta)/2,\\[3pt]  \dfrac{3-3\beta-2s}{4},  &(1-\beta)/2\le s\le\beta/2,\\[7pt]  \dfrac{3-\beta-6s}{4},  &\beta/2\le s\le(3\beta-1)/2,\\[7pt]  \dfrac{\beta+1-4s}{2},  &s\ge(3\beta-1)/2.  \end{cases}}  \label{eq:tpc28-sharp-minimax}$$ The formula is exact for the relaxed box [\[eq:tpc28-base-box\]](TPC-028.md#ref-25d97ba4f2fe682f); occupied arithmetic cells form a subset.

###### ref-5d6c385c1aa88915

###### ref-5532e5574c797451

###### ref-b0f9816ef53e201f

###### ref-26807b8c28937149

> **Proof** This is the same two-variable convex minimax that appears in the annular joint ledger, with the upper endpoint now equal to the base exponent $s$ [`\citep{WangTPC26}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-028.md#cite-ad014f62a6ac3092). We include the optimization to make the initial-value theorem self-contained.
>
> If $f_i\le\beta/2$, put $\sigma=f_1+f_2$. Then $$\eta_A=\frac{1-\beta-2s+\sigma}{2},  \qquad  \eta_B=1-\beta-\frac\sigma2.  \label{eq:tpc28-low-low}$$ They meet at $$\sigma_*=s+\frac{1-\beta}{2},  \qquad  \eta_A=\eta_B=\frac{3-3\beta-2s}{4}.  \label{eq:tpc28-low-witness}$$ For $s\le(1-\beta)/2$, this point lies beyond $0\le\sigma\le2s$, and the endpoint gives $1-\beta-s$. It is available for $(1-\beta)/2\le s\le\beta/2$.
>
> Now suppose $s\ge\beta/2$. On the high–high corner $f_1=f_2=s$, the two routes give $$\eta_A=\frac{\beta+1-4s}{2},  \qquad \eta_B=1-3s.  \label{eq:tpc28-high-high}$$ Here $$\eta_A-\eta_B=\frac{\beta-1+2s}{2}\ge0,$$ so the relevant upper envelope is the first expression. In a mixed cell write $x=f_{\rm low}\le\beta/2$ and $z=f_{\rm high}\ge\beta/2$. Both savings decrease with $z$, so $z=s$. The two remaining affine functions meet at $$x_*=\frac{1-\beta}{2},  \qquad  \eta_A=\eta_B=\frac{3-\beta-6s}{4}.  \label{eq:tpc28-mixed-witness}$$ While the low–low intersection remains available, its value exceeds the mixed value by $$\frac{3-3\beta-2s}{4}  -\frac{3-\beta-6s}{4}  =s-\frac\beta2\ge0.$$ The high–high and mixed faces cross at $s=(3\beta-1)/2$. Beyond that point the low–low endpoint is $1-3\beta/2$, which is also no smaller than the high–high face. This proves the last two branches. Continuity at every boundary is immediate.

> **Remark: Interpretation of the minimax** \[rem:tpc28-minimax-scope\] A nonpositive value means that the two displayed cell estimates do not give a uniform power saving over the relaxed box. It is not an arithmetic impossibility theorem. Conversely, a positive value is a valid lower bound for every occupied cell, whether or not all equality witnesses occur arithmetically.

## Principal mean and Fourier tail

###### ref-cd7d347be151ecac

###### ref-43c77e45d67b4511

For fixed $q$, assemble the raw full-base unit fiber before taking any conductor projection: $$Z_{q;S}^{\rm base}(\xi)  :=\sum_{\substack{u,v\le S\\\operatorname{lcm}(u,v)=q}}  b_R(u)b_R(v)Z_{u,v}(\xi).  \label{eq:tpc28-assembled-fiber}$$ For one Mellin parameter, write $G=\Phi_{\boldsymbol\tau}$ and define the nonzero principal-mean contribution at denominator $q$ by $$\begin{aligned}  \mathscr J_{q;S,\boldsymbol\tau}^{\rm mean,\ne0}  :={}&\frac{J}{Hq}\sum_{r\ne0}c_H(r)c_q(r)  \widehat G\left(\frac{rJ}{Hq}\right)  \notag\\  &\quad\times  \sum_{\substack{u,v\le S\\\operatorname{lcm}(u,v)=q}}  b_R(u)b_R(v)\overline Z_{u,v}.  \label{eq:tpc28-principal-mean-definition}\end{aligned}$$

###### ref-8476b1be308a5ab6

###### ref-d6a6a40cc012f3d5

###### ref-c47da83005a789c6

> **Lemma: Global principal mean** \[lem:tpc28-principal-mean\] For some fixed $C_{\mathscr D}>0$, $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_q|\mathscr J_{q;S,\boldsymbol\tau}^{\rm mean,\ne0}|  &\ll_{\varepsilon,\mathscr D}X^\varepsilon  (1+|\boldsymbol\tau|)^{C_{\mathscr D}}  \|x_{\boldsymbol\tau}\|_1\|y_{\boldsymbol\tau}\|_1,  \label{eq:tpc28-principal-mean-homogeneous}\\  \int\sum_q|\mathscr J_{q;S,\boldsymbol\tau}^{\rm mean,\ne0}|  \,d|\nu|(\boldsymbol\tau)  &\ll_{\varepsilon,\mathscr D}Q^2X^\varepsilon.  \label{eq:tpc28-principal-mean}\end{aligned}$$ This includes $u=v=1$; cells with exactly one principal conductor coordinate remain in [\[thm:tpc28-cell-bound\]](TPC-028.md#ref-63aea6465a955498).

###### ref-1e414e503eb631b4

> **Proof** The unit-fiber mean satisfies $$|\overline Z_{u,v}|  \le\frac{\|x_{\boldsymbol\tau}\|_1  \|y_{\boldsymbol\tau}\|_1}{\varphi(q)},  \qquad q=[u,v].$$ Put $a_q=J/(Hq)$. The inequality $|c_q(r)|\le\sum_{d\mid(q,r)}d$, Schwartz decay, and the divisor bound give $$a_q\sum_{r\ne0}|c_H(r)c_q(r)|  |\widehat G(a_qr)|  \ll_{\varepsilon,\mathscr D}  (1+|\boldsymbol\tau|)^{C_{\mathscr D}}X^\varepsilon.$$ Finally, $$\sum_{u,v\le S}\frac1{[u,v]}\ll(\log(2S))^3,  \label{eq:tpc28-lcm-cost}$$ while $1/\varphi(q)\ll_\varepsilon X^\varepsilon/q$ and [\[eq:tpc28-pointwise-b\]](TPC-028.md#ref-dacb9b916f48bcc2) absorbs the divisor coefficients. This proves [\[eq:tpc28-principal-mean-homogeneous\]](TPC-028.md#ref-d6a6a40cc012f3d5). Integrate with [\[eq:tpc28-transfer-l1\]](TPC-028.md#ref-257071e75e69f2c6) to obtain [\[eq:tpc28-principal-mean\]](TPC-028.md#ref-c47da83005a789c6). When $q=1$, the nonzero frequencies are even rapidly decreasing because $a_1\asymp J$.

The discrepancy is projected inside each genuine unit fiber and then summed over the $O(\log^2(2S))$ conductor cells. The retained part is bounded by [\[thm:tpc28-cell-bound\]](TPC-028.md#ref-63aea6465a955498). The unprojected high-frequency tail is removed before this pairwise triangle inequality.

###### ref-089e3dd7ba4f413c

> **Lemma: Automatic physical Fourier tail** \[lem:tpc28-Fourier-tail\] For every prescribed $A>0$, the complete raw-base frequency range $$|r|>X^\omega Hq/J  \label{eq:tpc28-tail-range}$$ contributes $O_{A,\mathscr D}(X^{-A}Q^2)$ after fixed-residue and physical Mellin reassembly.

###### ref-69543c7185148ef4

> **Proof** For one component and divisor pair, delete the row mask in absolute value and use the $\ell^1$ fiber mass $\sum_\xi|Z_{u,v}(\xi)|\le\|x\|_1\|y\|_1$. Repeated integration by parts gives, for every $N$, $$|\widehat\Phi_{\boldsymbol\tau}(y)|  \ll_N(1+|\boldsymbol\tau|)^N(1+|y|)^{-N}.$$ With $a_q=J/(Hq)$, an integral comparison yields uniformly in $q$ $$a_q\sum_{|r|>X^\omega/a_q}(1+a_q|r|)^{-N}  \ll_NX^{-\omega(N-1)}.  \label{eq:tpc28-tail-lattice}$$ The prefactor therefore cancels the frequency-lattice spacing. By [\[eq:tpc28-pointwise-b\]](TPC-028.md#ref-dacb9b916f48bcc2), the total divisor coefficient mass on $u,v\le S$ is at most a fixed power of $X$. Choose $N$ after $A$, integrate with [\[eq:tpc28-transfer-l1\]](TPC-028.md#ref-257071e75e69f2c6), and sum the fixed residue cells.

> **Theorem: Physical raw-joint nonzero closure** \[thm:tpc28-raw-nonzero\] Assume the physical interface of [2](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-028.md#src-629a4b755d31a097), the continuous transfer [\[eq:tpc28-continuous-transfer\]](TPC-028.md#ref-097ed25002f0125c)–[\[eq:tpc28-transfer-l1\]](TPC-028.md#ref-257071e75e69f2c6), the no-wrap condition [\[eq:tpc28-no-wrap\]](TPC-028.md#ref-3801b55c83e5be86), the fixed-power cutoff $S\le X^{C_S}$ from [\[eq:tpc28-RS\]](TPC-028.md#ref-56683c5fa097da51), and $$M_\beta(s)>0  \label{eq:tpc28-positive-base-ledger}$$ with a fixed margin. # Drift restoration and the Poisson-long base theorem

The raw joint nonzero theorem estimates only the coefficient-one term in [\[eq:tpc28-intro-raw-expansion\]](TPC-028.md#ref-d188b4eea68690b3). We now restore the two drift legs and the constant term. The key point is that $\delta_H(m)$ has a fixed-power saving, so a soft target-divisor bound is sufficient.

## A uniform target-divisor bound

###### ref-b546d615721ee0d1

###### ref-db6de7e550cac159

> **Lemma: Pointwise raw-prefix bound** \[lem:tpc28-raw-prefix-bound\] For every fixed $\varepsilon>0$, uniformly on the physical orbit support, $$\boxed{  |\mathsf A_{m,S}(j)|\ll_{\varepsilon,\mathscr D}X^\varepsilon.}  \label{eq:tpc28-raw-prefix-bound}$$ The estimate is independent of the comparison between $S$ and $J$.

###### ref-7f77d84bac235a67

> **Proof** Put $N=mj+h$. By [\[eq:tpc28-target-support\]](TPC-028.md#ref-481aca8e973da873), $1\le|N|\ll X^{C_0}$ for a fixed $C_0$. Every effective $u$ in [\[eq:tpc28-raw-prefix\]](TPC-028.md#ref-a3e543ad5b4aa8ce) divides $N$. Hence $$|\mathsf A_{m,S}(j)|  \le\tau(|N|)  \max_{\substack{u\mid N\\u\le S}}|b_R(u)|.  \label{eq:tpc28-divisor-majorant}$$ Use [\[eq:tpc28-pointwise-b\]](TPC-028.md#ref-dacb9b916f48bcc2) and the classical divisor bound $\tau(|N|)\ll_{\varepsilon,C_0}X^\varepsilon$, after reducing the auxiliary exponent. The cutoff $u\le S$ only deletes divisors.

###### ref-5490417e9df6c762

> **Remark: Why the target cannot be zero** \[rem:tpc28-nonzero-target\] If $N=0$, every positive integer would divide $N$, and [\[lem:tpc28-raw-prefix-bound\]](TPC-028.md#ref-b546d615721ee0d1) would be false. The physical target support [\[eq:tpc28-target-support\]](TPC-028.md#ref-481aca8e973da873) explicitly excludes this case. Equivalently, in the underlying positive dyadic packet one has $mj+h\asymp X$.

###### ref-00632b00fe088723

Let $\mathcal D_S^{\rm phys}$ denote the physical contribution of the last three terms in $$P_{m,S}P_{n,S}  =\mathsf A_{m,S}\mathsf A_{n,S}  -\delta_H(m)\mathsf A_{n,S}  -\delta_H(n)\mathsf A_{m,S}  +\delta_H(m)\delta_H(n).  \label{eq:tpc28-raw-drift-expansion}$$ It is the complete sampled orbit contribution, before any additive frequency projection.

###### ref-21327e68f2451373

###### ref-b80b9373e8a035a5

> **Proposition: Full and zero-frequency physical drift bounds** \[prop:tpc28-drift-bound\] For every fixed $\varepsilon>0$, $$\boxed{  |\mathcal D_S^{\rm phys}|+|(\mathcal D_S^{\rm phys})^0|  \ll_{\varepsilon,\mathscr D}  JQ^2\left(L^{-1}X^\varepsilon+L^{-2}X^\varepsilon\right).}  \label{eq:tpc28-drift-bound}$$ Consequently, under $L\ge X^{\lambda_0}$, the drift has a fixed-power saving relative to $XQ\asymp JQ^2$.

> **Proof** There are $O_{\mathscr D}(J)$ orbit points. Delete the bounded mask, periodic factor, and smooth weight. Apply [\[lem:tpc28-raw-prefix-bound\]](TPC-028.md#ref-b546d615721ee0d1), the drift estimate [\[eq:tpc28-row-drift\]](TPC-028.md#ref-835616380c1cf57b), and the two physical row $\ell^1$-bounds [\[eq:tpc28-row-l1\]](TPC-028.md#ref-ce90de696cae15ef). Each mixed drift leg is at most $$J\cdot Q\cdot Q\cdot L^{-1}X^\varepsilon.$$ The constant term is at most $JQ^2L^{-2}X^\varepsilon$.
>
> For the additive zero term, the complete-period mean of $\mathsf A_{m,S}$ is $D_{m,S}$. Directly from [\[eq:tpc28-prefix-density\]](TPC-028.md#ref-add6b32bbd5b60d8), [\[eq:tpc28-pointwise-b\]](TPC-028.md#ref-dacb9b916f48bcc2), and $S\le X^{C_S}$, $$|D_{m,S}|\ll_{\mathscr D}(\log(2X))^{O(1)}\ll_\varepsilon X^\varepsilon.$$ The bounded zero Fourier functional, the same row $\ell^1$ estimates, and [\[eq:tpc28-row-drift\]](TPC-028.md#ref-835616380c1cf57b) therefore give the identical bound for $|(\mathcal D_S^{\rm phys})^0|$. Renaming the auxiliary exponent proves [\[eq:tpc28-drift-bound\]](TPC-028.md#ref-b80b9373e8a035a5).

###### ref-5a59a54b9cf5a77d

> **Remark: Comparison with the centered scalar estimate** \[rem:tpc28-drift-vs-E\] The soft factor $X^\varepsilon$ in [\[eq:tpc28-raw-prefix-bound\]](TPC-028.md#ref-db6de7e550cac159) cannot be multiplied by $E_S(m)\ll_A(\log X)^{-A}$ and then absorbed by choosing $A$. This is why the proof must use $P=Z+E$ only at frequency zero and $P=\mathsf A-\delta_H$ at nonzero frequency. The fixed-power factor $L^{-1}$ in $\delta_H$ does absorb the soft divisor loss.

## Frequency-dependent assembly

###### ref-070392ba2eedec9a

###### ref-bf5dd81c34c504fc

For each finite divisor pair, fixed residue cell, and smooth Mellin component, Poisson summation decomposes the sampled orbit into its additive zero term and nonzero terms. By linearity, $$\mathcal Q_S^{\rm phys}  =(\mathcal Q_S^{\rm phys})^0  +(\mathcal Q_S^{\mathsf A\mathsf A})^{\ne0}  +(\mathcal D_S^{\rm phys})^{\ne0}.  \label{eq:tpc28-frequency-dependent-assembly}$$ The first term is the zero frequency of the complete calibrated product and is evaluated with $P=Z+E$. The last two terms use $P=\mathsf A-\delta_H$. In particular, $$|(\mathcal D_S^{\rm phys})^{\ne0}|  \le|\mathcal D_S^{\rm phys}|+|(\mathcal D_S^{\rm phys})^0|,  \label{eq:tpc28-drift-nonzero-majorant}$$ where the right side is bounded in [\[prop:tpc28-drift-bound\]](TPC-028.md#ref-21327e68f2451373). This formula does not double-count the zero frequency: the first term already contains the exact recombination [\[eq:tpc28-calibrated-zero-identity\]](TPC-028.md#ref-0d17996121863fea).

> **Theorem: Calibrated Poisson-long base closure** \[thm:tpc28-base-closure\] Retain the physical opened-row provenance, row norms, common smooth weight, divisor-independent off-diagonal mask, and nonzero target support of [2](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-028.md#src-629a4b755d31a097). > **Proof** Use the exact assembly [\[eq:tpc28-frequency-dependent-assembly\]](TPC-028.md#ref-070392ba2eedec9a). The complete calibrated zero term has arbitrary logarithmic saving by [\[thm:tpc28-physical-zero\]](TPC-028.md#ref-0cd3238a6bc66082). Each power-saving contribution is $O_B\!\left(XQ(\log X)^{-B}\right)$ for every fixed $B$ and sufficiently large $X$. The fixed dyadic, sign, content, and residue reassembly costs are absorbed by choosing the zero calibration order after $B$.

The full-base conductor proof uses the classical exact-conductor large sieve, not an unproved distribution conjecture. The zero theorem uses the proved uniform Möbius calibration and the exact finite Ramanujan-square factorization. Thus \[[thm:tpc28-base-closure](TPC-028.md#ref-482a160d2641f8ee),[thm:tpc28-high-upper](TPC-028.md#ref-b06fd7517e97e1fd)\] require no unproved conjecture beyond their explicitly stated physical packet hypotheses.

## Three invalid shortcuts

The proof also identifies three tempting but invalid replacements:

1.  One cannot estimate nonzero $EZ$ by multiplying $E_S\ll_A(\log X)^{-A}$ with the soft bound $Z\ll X^\varepsilon$. A fixed $X^\varepsilon$ is not absorbed by choosing $A$. The nonzero decomposition must use the power-small drift $\delta_H$.

2.  One cannot obtain the full-base theorem by formally setting the lower endpoint of an annular prefix theorem below $R$. The full unit fibers, energy, principal mean, and reassembly must be defined directly, as in \[[sec:tpc28-full-base](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-028.md#src-f01d7295810c0cc9),[sec:tpc28-minimax](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-028.md#src-aa8e9d913a8bbf45)\].

3.  One cannot conductor-project the coherent additive zero terms before their raw/centered recombination. In particular $b_R(1)$ is generally nonzero. The zero identity [\[eq:tpc28-calibrated-zero-identity\]](TPC-028.md#ref-0d17996121863fea) must be applied first.

## What is new and what remains open

The new conclusions are:

-   a full-rectangle nonzero conductor theorem governed by $M_\beta(s)$;

-   calibrated base closure without $S<J$;

-   closure of the particular source-compatible $\beta=267/400>2/3$ initial value;

-   after importing TPC-26 cutoff stability, closure of the corresponding selected square through $T=X^{193/500+o(1)}$.

The following are not conclusions of this paper:

-   the ultra-long complement beyond $T$;

-   the full TPC-18 residual or a complete original prime-pair correlation;

-   a polynomially uniform affine two-point Möbius theorem on the reflected surface [\[eq:tpc28-reflected-surface\]](TPC-028.md#ref-ec981c6c88b0adb8);

-   positivity, a Hardy–Littlewood asymptotic, or a twin-prime lower bound;

-   any general breach of the sieve parity barrier.

The next analytic gate is therefore cleaner than before: the selected high-$\beta$ square no longer lacks an initial value. What remains on this branch is the reflected ultra-long complement, where the exact surface [\[eq:tpc28-reflected-surface\]](TPC-028.md#ref-ec981c6c88b0adb8) must be combined with orbit congruences, sparse incidence geometry, and genuine cancellation in growing affine Möbius coefficients. A positive theorem, a precise conditional reduction, or a sharp barrier theorem at that gate would all be logically downstream of the present result.
