## Abstract

Let $P_{m,T}$ be the calibrated divisor prefix at the upper cutoff of a selected primitive opened-row packet, and let $U_0\asymp X$ contain all positive targets on its physical support. The preceding conductor argument closes the truncated quadratic packet at $T$, but does not estimate the ultra-long complement. We begin from the exact calibrated identity $$P_{m,U_0}-P_{m,T}=C_{m;T,U_0},\qquad  C_{m;T,U_0}(j)=  \sum_{\substack{T<w\le U_0\\w\mid mj+h}}-\mu(w)\log w.$$ It avoids a scalar-tail replacement whose available pointwise bounds do not by themselves imply arbitrary logarithmic saving. Expanding the joint cutoff difference leaves three raw ultra channels and two row-drift legs; the latter are power-small from the existing $L^{-1}$ row factor.

Reflect a long divisor $w$ to $r=(mj+h)/w$. For distinct primitive rows $m,n$, dyadic complement variables $$r\asymp A,\qquad s\asymp B,\qquad (r,s)\asymp G$$ select, when compatible, one orbit class modulo $[r,s]$. We prove the fixed-row incidence estimate $$\mathcal N_{m,n}(A,B,G)  \ll_{\varepsilon,h}X^\varepsilon  \min\left\{J,\frac{AB}{G^2}+\frac JG\right\}.$$ The first term is the pointwise divisor bound. For the second, each exact common content divides $h(m-n)$; after writing $r=dr_1$, $s=ds_1$, the number of coprime primitive pairs is $O(AB/d^2)$ and their orbit occupancy is $O(1+Jd/(AB))$. This argument neither removes the Möbius signs nor assumes that complement and quotient contents are coprime.

The actual opened-row coefficients have one-row $\ell^1$ mass $O(Q)$. Consequently every physical mixed or both-ultra dyadic block satisfies $$|\mathcal S(A,B,G)|  \ll_\varepsilon Q^2X^\varepsilon  \min\left\{J,\frac{AB}{G^2}+\frac JG\right\}.$$ Writing $q=[r,s]$ and $g=(r,s)$, this gives the unconditional content-rich sparse wedge $$q\ge J,\qquad \frac qg\le JX^{-\eta}  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad  \mathcal S_{\rm rich}\ll_\varepsilon XQX^{-\eta+\varepsilon}.$$ Here $q/g=(r/g)(s/g)$ is the primitive lcm after the shared content is removed. On the complementary long-fiber side $q\le Y\le J$, row residue energy also gives $$\mathcal S_{q\le Y,\,(r,s)>D}  \ll_\varepsilon X^\varepsilon\left\{  \frac{XQ}{D}\log^2(2Y)+X\log^3(2Y)  \right\}.$$ Thus every polynomially large shared-content sector is power-small on prescribed long-fiber scales as well.

At the high-$\beta$ sample $\beta=267/400$, $T=X^{193/500+o(1)}$, the block $A=B=X^{1/4}$, $G=X^{1/10}$ is genuinely sparse and has the exact incidence margin $13/400$.

This closes a nonempty exponent region of the ultra-long geometry without a Chowla or Elliott input. It does not close the primitive sparse core, the dense long-fiber region, the complete residual, positivity, a Hardy–Littlewood asymptotic, twin primes, or the sieve parity problem.

# Introduction and claim ledger

The primitive residual program separates a fixed-shift prime-pair correlation into opened row packets before confronting the remaining Möbius signs. TPC-23 identified the exact affine fibers produced by reflecting divisors beyond a dynamic cutoff [`\citep{WangTPC23}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-029.md#cite-f24192cbd2a455cd). TPC-26 then treated a finite annular difference jointly, and TPC-28 closed a source-compatible selected square through an upper cutoff $T$, even though its base already exceeds the Poisson length [`\citep{WangTPC26,WangTPC28}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-029.md#cite-ad014f62a6ac3092); [\[WangTPC28\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-029.md#cite-1f714b6620a45b76). The resulting statement is a rigorous truncated-packet estimate. The terms with a divisor larger than $T$ remain.

The present paper removes a new, explicitly defined part of that remainder. It does not ask for cancellation on any individual Möbius fiber. Instead it exploits the common content of the two reflected divisors. If $$r\mid mj+h,\qquad s\mid nj+h,$$ then $(r,s)$ divides $h(m-n)$. In the primitive physical packet it is coprime to $h$, hence divides the nonzero row difference. A large shared content therefore has two simultaneous effects: it restricts the row pair and reduces the number of primitive modulus pairs. These effects are strong enough to close two nonempty scale regions without using the signs of $\mu$.

The proof has four layers.

1.  We retain the calibrated prefix and take its exact cutoff difference. If $U_0$ contains all physical targets, then $$P_{m,U_0}-P_{m,T}=C_{m;T,U_0}   \label{eq:tpc29-intro-cutoff-difference}$$ with no scalar centering error. The joint difference has three raw ultra channels and two power-small row-drift legs.

2.  For a fixed off-diagonal row pair and dyadic divisor scales $A,B,G$, we prove $$\mathcal N_{m,n}(A,B,G)   \ll_{\varepsilon,h}X^\varepsilon   \min\left\{J,\frac{AB}{G^2}+\frac JG\right\}.   \label{eq:tpc29-intro-incidence}$$ The estimate is a count with multiplicity. It therefore applies after inserting arbitrary signs and all physical support selectors.

3.  The opened-row $\ell^1$ provenance converts [\[eq:tpc29-intro-incidence\]](TPC-029.md#ref-37a1297695de22e6) into a physical block bound. In the sparse range $q=[r,s]\ge J$, every fixed $\eta>0$ gives $$\frac{q}{(r,s)}\le JX^{-\eta}   \quad\Longrightarrow\quad   \mathcal S_{\rm rich}\ll_\varepsilon XQX^{-\eta+\varepsilon}.   \label{eq:tpc29-intro-sparse-wedge}$$ Thus a short fiber is useful only after the number of primitive modulus pairs is also controlled.

4.  In the long-fiber region $q\le Y\le J$, an independent row-residue argument removes every block with common content $d=(r,s)>D$: $$\mathcal S_{q\le Y,\,d>D}   \ll_\varepsilon X^\varepsilon   \left\{    \frac{XQ}{D}\log^2(2Y)+X\log^3(2Y)   \right\}.   \label{eq:tpc29-intro-long-pruning}$$ For $D\ge X^\eta$ this has a fixed-power saving relative to $XQ$.

These two closure regions share the same mechanism but cover different fiber lengths. The long theorem uses the $J/q$ occupancy and row residue energy. The sparse theorem keeps the unavoidable $+1$ in the occupancy and compensates for it by requiring the primitive lcm $q/(r,s)$ to be short.

At the TPC-28 high-$\beta$ sample, $$\beta=\frac{267}{400},\qquad  J=X^{133/400+o(1)},\qquad  T=X^{193/500+o(1)},$$ the dyadic choice $$A=B=X^{1/4},\qquad G=X^{1/10}$$ has lcm exponent $2/5$ and primitive-lcm exponent $3/10$. It is sparse by $27/400$ and the new incidence saving is exactly $13/400$. This $13/400$ is unrelated to the smaller $13/1600$ conductor margin that closed the TPC-28 base.

## What remains

The theorem does not estimate every short fiber. When $(r,s)=1$ and $[r,s]\ge J$, the exponent delivered by [\[eq:tpc29-intro-incidence\]](TPC-029.md#ref-37a1297695de22e6) is exactly the natural exponent. A new signed estimate after summing the growing family is still needed. Nor does [\[eq:tpc29-intro-long-pruning\]](TPC-029.md#ref-2ae046243197bb92) remove the long fibers with small content. Their affine slopes grow polynomially with the row scale, whereas the fixed-form logarithmic Elliott theorem has no uniform rate that can be summed over the physical family [`\citep{Tao2016}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-029.md#cite-0bccf4cf69786109).

No conclusion below concerns positivity, a prime-pair main term, or infinitely many twin primes. Even a future closure of every ultra channel would still have to be reassembled with the full TPC-18 endpoint and block ledger before the residual-dispersion target was reached [`\citep{WangTPC18}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-029.md#cite-4abf939102bd1649).

> **Proposition: Exact calibrated cutoff identity** \[prop:tpc29-exact-cutoff\] For every physical row and orbit point, $$\boxed{P_{m,U_0}(j)-P_{m,T}(j)=C_{m;T,U_0}(j).}  \label{eq:tpc29-exact-cutoff}$$ Consequently, for two rows $m,n$, $$\begin{aligned}  &P_{m,U_0}P_{n,U_0}-P_{m,T}P_{n,T}  \notag\\  &\quad=  \mathsf A_{m,T}C_n+C_m\mathsf A_{n,T}+C_mC_n  -\delta_H(m)C_n-\delta_H(n)C_m.  \label{eq:tpc29-five-term-identity}\end{aligned}$$ All identities are pointwise in $j$.

> **Proof** The drift $\delta_H(m)$ is independent of the cutoff and cancels in the difference. If $w>T\ge R$, then $\lambda'_R(w)=0$ and hence $b_R(w)=a(w)$. Subtracting the two finite divisor prefixes proves [\[eq:tpc29-exact-cutoff\]](TPC-029.md#ref-8dfe13d5d0146099). Now write $P_{m,T}=\mathsf A_{m,T}-\delta_H(m)$ and expand $$(P_{m,T}+C_m)(P_{n,T}+C_n)-P_{m,T}P_{n,T}.$$ This gives [\[eq:tpc29-five-term-identity\]](TPC-029.md#ref-30b79d71c9ec5c54).

The first three terms on the right of [\[eq:tpc29-five-term-identity\]](TPC-029.md#ref-30b79d71c9ec5c54) will be called the two mixed raw channels and the both-ultra raw channel. They are not asserted to be small separately on their full supports. Absolute estimates will be used only after restricting them to the content-rich regions defined later.

# Reflected fibers and a sparse incidence theorem

## Hyperbola reflection

###### ref-cd9ba742ffc42c37

For a positive target $N_m(j)=mj+h$, divisor reflection in [\[eq:tpc29-ultra-increment\]](TPC-029.md#ref-8e1088c709d4fba8) gives the exact identity $$C_m(j)=  \sum_{\substack{r\mid N_m(j)\\r<N_m(j)/T}}  a\!\left(\frac{N_m(j)}r\right).  \label{eq:tpc29-reflected-ultra}$$ The reflected variable $r$ is not required to be squarefree. Only the quotient coefficient $a(N_m/r)$ vanishes off squarefree quotients.

###### ref-bf10da1bb23ea15d

The both-ultra channel contains two variables of the form [\[eq:tpc29-reflected-ultra\]](TPC-029.md#ref-cd9ba742ffc42c37). In a mixed channel, one variable is an original prefix divisor $u\le T$ and the other is a reflected complement variable. Both situations reduce to the same pair of divisibility conditions $$r\mid mj+h,\qquad s\mid nj+h.  \label{eq:tpc29-two-divisibilities}$$ Support restrictions and coefficient types will be restored only after the common incidence count is proved.

> **Lemma: Compatibility and the reflected determinant** \[lem:tpc29-reflected-crt\] Let $m\ne n$ be primitive physical rows, and let $r,s$ satisfy [\[eq:tpc29-two-divisibilities\]](TPC-029.md#ref-bf10da1bb23ea15d) for some primitive orbit point. Put $$d=(r,s),\qquad q=[r,s]=\frac{rs}{d}.  \label{eq:tpc29-dq}$$ Then the two congruences in [\[eq:tpc29-two-divisibilities\]](TPC-029.md#ref-bf10da1bb23ea15d) are compatible if and only if $$d\mid h(m-n).  \label{eq:tpc29-compatibility}$$ When compatible, they select a unique class $j_0\pmod q$. Writing $j=j_0+qk$, the two quotient forms $$\begin{aligned}  U(k)&=\frac{m(j_0+qk)+h}{r}=A_1k+B_1,  \label{eq:tpc29-U-form}\\  V(k)&=\frac{n(j_0+qk)+h}{s}=A_2k+B_2  \label{eq:tpc29-V-form}\end{aligned}$$ have integer coefficients and satisfy $$\boxed{A_1B_2-A_2B_1=\frac{h(m-n)}d\ne0.}  \label{eq:tpc29-affine-determinant}$$ Moreover $(d,h)=1$, so compatibility implies $d\mid m-n$.

> **Proof** Target primitivity gives $(r,m)=(s,n)=1$. Hence the two congruences select $$j\equiv-h\overline m\pmod r,  \qquad  j\equiv-h\overline n\pmod s.$$ Their reductions modulo $d$ agree exactly when $d\mid h(m-n)$. The generalized Chinese remainder theorem then gives one class modulo $q$.
>
> The affine coefficients are $$A_1=\frac{mq}{r},\quad B_1=\frac{mj_0+h}{r},  \qquad  A_2=\frac{nq}{s},\quad B_2=\frac{nj_0+h}{s}.$$ Their determinant equals $$\frac{qh(m-n)}{rs}=\frac{h(m-n)}d.$$ It is nonzero by the off-diagonal mask. Finally, $d$ divides both positive targets. Each target is coprime to $H=\operatorname{rad}|h|$, so $(d,h)=1$; cancel $h$ in [\[eq:tpc29-compatibility\]](TPC-029.md#ref-48a489ed47945adc).

###### ref-4e494270baddc84e

###### ref-349873ee0345ceed

Let $\mathcal I\subset\mathbb Z$ be any set contained in an interval of at most $C_{\mathscr D}J$ consecutive integers, and suppose throughout this section that $$1\le mj+h,\ nj+h\le X^{C_{\mathscr D}}  \qquad(j\in\mathcal I).  \label{eq:tpc29-polynomial-target-window}$$ The physical target window [\[eq:tpc29-target-support\]](TPC-029.md#ref-3de18677a2543d92) is the case used later. For $A,B,G\ge1$, define $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal N_{m,n}(A,B,G;\mathcal I)  :={}&\#\bigl\{(j,r,s):j\in\mathcal I,\ A<r\le2A,\ B<s\le2B,  \notag\\  &\hspace{7em}G<(r,s)\le2G,  \ r\mid mj+h,\ s\mid nj+h\bigr\}.  \label{eq:tpc29-incidence-count}\end{aligned}$$ This is a triple count, not merely the number of occupied orbit points. At the left endpoint of a dyadic decomposition, the $G=1$ cell is understood as $1\le(r,s)\le2$; subsequent cells use the displayed half-open convention.

###### ref-73e637925228fc2e

###### ref-fb0ea188350bb1c3

> **Theorem: Fixed-row sparse incidence** \[thm:tpc29-incidence\] Uniformly for distinct physical rows $m,n$ and all $A,B,G\ge1$, $$\boxed{  \mathcal N_{m,n}(A,B,G;\mathcal I)  \ll_{\varepsilon,h,\mathscr D}X^\varepsilon  \min\left\{J,\frac{AB}{G^2}+\frac JG\right\}.}  \label{eq:tpc29-incidence-bound}$$ Empty dyadic cells are understood to contribute zero.

###### ref-47fd1eca111074bf

###### ref-466bd235ef06e67c

###### ref-3282beb54353e627

###### ref-4ecfef17ce36e637

###### ref-c78edaf09ab7e592

###### ref-6249880e40413de8

> **Proof** First fix an exact common content $d=(r,s)$. By [\[lem:tpc29-reflected-crt\]](TPC-029.md#ref-87c41c5126750d00), it must divide the nonzero integer $h(m-n)$. The number of possible exact $d$ in the dyadic interval is therefore at most $$\tau(|h(m-n)|)\ll_{\varepsilon,h,\mathscr D}X^\varepsilon.  \label{eq:tpc29-content-count}$$ Write $$r=dr_1,\qquad s=ds_1,\qquad(r_1,s_1)=1.  \label{eq:tpc29-primitive-parts}$$ For a nonempty cell, $d\le2A$ and $d\le2B$. Hence the number of candidate pairs at this fixed $d$ is $$\ll\left(\frac Ad+1\right)        \left(\frac Bd+1\right)  \ll\frac{AB}{d^2}.  \label{eq:tpc29-pair-count-fixed-d}$$ Deleting the coprimality condition only enlarges the count.
>
> Each compatible pair selects one residue class modulo $q=rs/d$. Since $r>A$ and $s>B$, its occupancy in $\mathcal I$ is $$\ll_{\mathscr D}1+\frac Jq  \ll_{\mathscr D}1+\frac{Jd}{AB}.  \label{eq:tpc29-fiber-occupancy}$$ Multiplying [\[eq:tpc29-pair-count-fixed-d\]](TPC-029.md#ref-3282beb54353e627) and [\[eq:tpc29-fiber-occupancy\]](TPC-029.md#ref-4ecfef17ce36e637), using $d\asymp G$ (also in the closed $G=1$ base cell), and then applying [\[eq:tpc29-content-count\]](TPC-029.md#ref-47fd1eca111074bf) gives $$\mathcal N_{m,n}(A,B,G;\mathcal I)  \ll X^\varepsilon\left(\frac{AB}{G^2}+\frac JG\right).  \label{eq:tpc29-incidence-crt-side}$$
>
> There is a second bound. For each fixed $j$, the numbers of possible $r$ and $s$ are at most $\tau(N_m(j))$ and $\tau(N_n(j))$. The pointwise divisor bound and $\#\mathcal I\ll J$ give $$\mathcal N_{m,n}(A,B,G;\mathcal I)\ll_{\varepsilon,\mathscr D}JX^\varepsilon.  \label{eq:tpc29-incidence-divisor-side}$$ Taking the smaller of [\[eq:tpc29-incidence-crt-side\]](TPC-029.md#ref-c78edaf09ab7e592) and [\[eq:tpc29-incidence-divisor-side\]](TPC-029.md#ref-6249880e40413de8) proves the theorem.

###### ref-943b6009fd3b3e1d

> **Remark: The indispensable $+1$** \[rem:tpc29-plus-one\] When $q\ge J$, [\[eq:tpc29-fiber-occupancy\]](TPC-029.md#ref-4ecfef17ce36e637) is $O(1)$, not $O(J/q)$. Dropping this $+1$ would incorrectly turn every sparse family into a saving. The term $AB/G^2$ in [\[eq:tpc29-incidence-bound\]](TPC-029.md#ref-fb0ea188350bb1c3) is precisely the total cost of those one-point occupancies.

# Physical weighting and the content-rich sparse wedge

###### ref-29805b97b11ae258

###### ref-006f4502fd32ba33

We now restore the physical coefficients. For one of the three raw channels in [\[eq:tpc29-five-term-identity\]](TPC-029.md#ref-30b79d71c9ec5c54), let $\kappa_{\alpha,\gamma}(j,r,s)$ include its two divisor coefficients, its prefix or reflected support restrictions, and any fixed residue selectors. Thus it is one of the types $$b_R(r)a(N_\gamma(j)/s),\qquad  a(N_\alpha(j)/r)b_R(s),\qquad  a(N_\alpha(j)/r)a(N_\gamma(j)/s),  \label{eq:tpc29-three-coefficient-types}$$ on the appropriate divisor support, and is zero otherwise. By [\[eq:tpc29-coefficient-envelope\]](TPC-029.md#ref-75207e85c4a95c63), $$|\kappa_{\alpha,\gamma}(j,r,s)|  \ll_{\mathscr D}(\log(2X))^6.  \label{eq:tpc29-kappa-envelope}$$

###### ref-1842f413706098a5

Define the corresponding dyadic physical block $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal S(A,B,G)  :={}&\sum_{\alpha,\gamma}  \gamma_\alpha^{(1)}\gamma_\gamma^{(2)}  \mathfrak m(\alpha,\gamma)  \sum_j\Xi_{\alpha,\gamma}(j)  \mathcal W_{\alpha,\gamma}(j/J)  \notag\\  &\times  \sum_{\substack{A<r\le2A,\ B<s\le2B\\                    G<(r,s)\le2G\\                    r\mid N_\alpha(j),\ s\mid N_\gamma(j)}}  \kappa_{\alpha,\gamma}(j,r,s).  \label{eq:tpc29-physical-block}\end{aligned}$$

###### ref-84c5b7d29e5a97bb

###### ref-7bf2f6eb31594d0d

> **Theorem: Weighted block estimate** \[thm:tpc29-weighted-block\] Every mixed or both-ultra block of the form [\[eq:tpc29-physical-block\]](TPC-029.md#ref-1842f413706098a5) satisfies $$\boxed{  |\mathcal S(A,B,G)|  \ll_{\varepsilon,h,\mathscr D}Q^2X^\varepsilon  \min\left\{J,\frac{AB}{G^2}+\frac JG\right\}.}  \label{eq:tpc29-weighted-block-bound}$$ No Poisson, Fourier, conductor, or Möbius-correlation theorem is used.

> **Proof** Take absolute values in [\[eq:tpc29-physical-block\]](TPC-029.md#ref-1842f413706098a5). The mask, periodic factor, and smooth weight are bounded. Absorb [\[eq:tpc29-kappa-envelope\]](TPC-029.md#ref-006f4502fd32ba33) into $X^\varepsilon$, and apply [\[thm:tpc29-incidence\]](TPC-029.md#ref-73e637925228fc2e) to each off-diagonal row pair. Finally use $$\sum_{\alpha,\gamma}  |\gamma_\alpha^{(1)}\gamma_\gamma^{(2)}|  \le  \|\gamma^{(1)}\|_1\|\gamma^{(2)}\|_1  \ll_{\mathscr D}Q^2$$ from [\[eq:tpc29-row-l1\]](TPC-029.md#ref-2f10c1084e5866d9).

## Primitive lcm and the sparse theorem

###### ref-183d14fda73ad04c

For a divisor pair, put $$q=[r,s],\qquad g=(r,s),\qquad  q_{\rm prim}:=\frac qg  =\frac r{(r,s)}\frac s{(r,s)}.  \label{eq:tpc29-primitive-lcm}$$ Thus $q_{\rm prim}$ is the product of the two coprime parts after the shared content is removed.

###### ref-634d57de8695cfcc

###### ref-a3f09d0eb1208691

> **Definition: Content-rich sparse region** \[def:tpc29-rich-sparse\] For fixed $\eta>0$, the content-rich sparse region consists of the divisor pairs satisfying $$q\ge J,  \qquad  q_{\rm prim}\le JX^{-\eta}.  \label{eq:tpc29-rich-sparse-region}$$ Dyadic endpoint constants may be changed by fixed factors; all inequalities retain their fixed exponent margin.

###### ref-2effbf37eb379090

###### ref-0d0841fd353a2a23

> **Theorem: Unconditional content-rich sparse closure** \[thm:tpc29-rich-sparse\] Let $\mathcal S_{\rm rich}$ be the sum, over the three raw channels in [\[eq:tpc29-five-term-identity\]](TPC-029.md#ref-30b79d71c9ec5c54), of all dyadic blocks restricted to [\[eq:tpc29-rich-sparse-region\]](TPC-029.md#ref-a3f09d0eb1208691). Then $$\boxed{  |\mathcal S_{\rm rich}|  \ll_{\varepsilon,\eta,h,\mathscr D}  XQX^{-\eta+\varepsilon}.}  \label{eq:tpc29-rich-sparse-closure}$$ In particular it is fixed-power small for $0<\varepsilon<\eta$.

###### ref-3ce2c487353327b0

###### ref-dfe272c3cbfcab33

> **Proof** On a dyadic block, $q\asymp AB/G$ and $q_{\rm prim}\asymp AB/G^2$. The second condition in [\[eq:tpc29-rich-sparse-region\]](TPC-029.md#ref-a3f09d0eb1208691) gives $$\frac{AB}{G^2}\ll JX^{-\eta}.  \label{eq:tpc29-first-rich-term}$$ Dividing the sparse lower bound for $q$ by the upper bound for $q_{\rm prim}$ gives $$G\gg X^\eta,  \qquad  \frac JG\ll JX^{-\eta}.  \label{eq:tpc29-second-rich-term}$$ Insert [\[eq:tpc29-first-rich-term\]](TPC-029.md#ref-3ce2c487353327b0) and [\[eq:tpc29-second-rich-term\]](TPC-029.md#ref-dfe272c3cbfcab33) into [\[eq:tpc29-weighted-block-bound\]](TPC-029.md#ref-7bf2f6eb31594d0d). Each block is $O(Q^2JX^{-\eta+\varepsilon})$. Since $Q^2J\asymp XQ$, summing the $O((\log X)^3)$ scale triples and the three channel types proves [\[eq:tpc29-rich-sparse-closure\]](TPC-029.md#ref-0d0841fd353a2a23) after reducing the auxiliary exponent.

## The exponent polytope

###### ref-6cb7bf3e27ea1bb3

###### ref-8aa18cdc050ab67c

###### ref-ea954fae053a72ba

###### ref-243bf82bb499b445

Write $$A=X^{a+o(1)},\qquad  B=X^{b+o(1)},\qquad  G=X^{c+o(1)},\qquad  \jmath=1-\beta.  \label{eq:tpc29-block-exponents}$$ The sparse condition is $$a+b-c\ge\jmath.  \label{eq:tpc29-sparse-exponent}$$ The weighted theorem gives the exact saving exponent available from this incidence method: $$\boxed{  \eta_{\rm sp}(a,b,c)  =\bigl(\jmath-a-b+2c\bigr)_+.}  \label{eq:tpc29-sparse-saving}$$ Indeed, the exponent of the second entry in the minimum in [\[eq:tpc29-weighted-block-bound\]](TPC-029.md#ref-7bf2f6eb31594d0d) is $\max\{a+b-2c,\jmath-c\}$. Under [\[eq:tpc29-sparse-exponent\]](TPC-029.md#ref-8aa18cdc050ab67c), one has $a+b-2c\ge\jmath-c$, so the first term is the larger one and [\[eq:tpc29-sparse-saving\]](TPC-029.md#ref-ea954fae053a72ba) follows. Therefore a fixed saving $\eta_0$ is equivalent to the two strict faces $$a+b-c\ge\jmath,  \qquad  a+b-2c\le\jmath-\eta_0.  \label{eq:tpc29-rich-polytope}$$

# Large-content pruning on long fibers

The $+1$ in [\[eq:tpc29-fiber-occupancy\]](TPC-029.md#ref-4ecfef17ce36e637) is harmless when $q=[r,s]\le J$. In that range we can sum the $J/q$ occupancy and use row residue energy to remove every polynomially large common content.

###### ref-43275fd25a46fbf6

For $d\ge1$, define the absolute row congruence mass $$\mathcal R_d  :=\sum_{\alpha,\gamma}  |\gamma_\alpha^{(1)}\gamma_\gamma^{(2)}|  \mathbf 1_{d\mid m_\alpha-m_\gamma}.  \label{eq:tpc29-row-congruence-mass}$$

###### ref-c073276c025ddee9

###### ref-53ac5fd7f9ca5611

> **Lemma: Row residue energy** \[lem:tpc29-row-residue-energy\] Uniformly for $d\ge1$, $$\boxed{  \mathcal R_d\ll_{\mathscr D}  \left(\frac{Q^2}{d}+Q\right)\log(2L).}  \label{eq:tpc29-row-residue-bound}$$

> **Proof** For $i=1,2$ and $a\pmod d$, put $$X_i(a)=\sum_{m_\alpha\equiv a\, (d)}|\gamma_\alpha^{(i)}|,  \qquad  E_i(a)=\sum_{m_\alpha\equiv a\, (d)}|\gamma_\alpha^{(i)}|^2.$$ The distinct row integers lie in an interval of length $O_{\mathscr D}(Q)$, so each residue class contains $O_{\mathscr D}(Q/d+1)$ rows. Cauchy–Schwarz gives $$X_i(a)^2\ll_{\mathscr D}\left(\frac Qd+1\right)E_i(a).$$ Hence $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal R_d  &=\sum_{a\, (d)}X_1(a)X_2(a)\\  &\ll_{\mathscr D}\left(\frac Qd+1\right)  \left(\sum_aE_1(a)\right)^{1/2}  \left(\sum_aE_2(a)\right)^{1/2}.\end{aligned}$$ Apply [\[eq:tpc29-row-l2\]](TPC-029.md#ref-34eb5c439663e144).

###### ref-cdc3f7efdeb92b67

Let $\mathcal S_{\le Y,>D}$ denote any one of the three raw ultra channels, restricted to $$\le Y\le J,  \qquad  (r,s)>D.  \label{eq:tpc29-long-content-region}$$ All original prefix and reflected-quotient support conditions remain in force.

###### ref-310efce0ec7c5758

###### ref-84fcfbd5dfd4c492

###### ref-d7431c392332601e

> **Theorem: Long-fiber large-content pruning** \[thm:tpc29-long-content\] Uniformly for $1\le D<Y\le J$, $$\boxed{  |\mathcal S_{\le Y,>D}|  \ll_{\varepsilon,h,\mathscr D}X^\varepsilon  \left\{   \frac{XQ}{D}\log^2(2Y)+X\log^3(2Y)  \right\}.}  \label{eq:tpc29-long-content-bound}$$ The same estimate holds for the sum of the three raw channels. If $D\ge X^\eta$ for fixed $\eta>0$, then $$|\mathcal S_{\le Y,>D}|  \ll_{\varepsilon,\eta,h,\mathscr D}  XQX^{-\min\{\eta,\beta\}+\varepsilon}.  \label{eq:tpc29-long-content-power}$$

###### ref-24258c6ca76b8a20

###### ref-3fa13ab177e65da6

###### ref-7c2b23e1a45d04c5

> **Proof** Put $d=(r,s)$ and write $$r=da,\qquad s=db,\qquad(a,b)=1.$$ By target primitivity, $(d,h)=1$; by [\[lem:tpc29-reflected-crt\]](TPC-029.md#ref-87c41c5126750d00), compatibility therefore gives $d\mid m_\alpha-m_\gamma$. Also $$[r,s]=dab.$$ For $dab\le Y\le J$, a compatible fiber has $$O_{\mathscr D}\left(\frac{J}{dab}\right)  \label{eq:tpc29-long-occupancy}$$ orbit points, with the constant absorbing the endpoint $+1$.
>
> Take absolute values and absorb the divisor coefficients and fixed weights into $X^\varepsilon$. We retain the necessary condition $d\mid m_\alpha-m_\gamma$ and the common interval support used in [\[eq:tpc29-long-occupancy\]](TPC-029.md#ref-24258c6ca76b8a20), but delete the channel-specific prefix, reflection, and squarefreeness selectors. For each exact $d$, the coprime parts contribute $$\sum_{\substack{a,b\ge1\\ab\le Y/d}}  \frac1{ab}  \ll\log^2(2Y).  \label{eq:tpc29-harmonic-pairs}$$ Thus [\[lem:tpc29-row-residue-energy\]](TPC-029.md#ref-c073276c025ddee9) gives $$\begin{aligned}  |\mathcal S_{\le Y,>D}|  &\ll X^\varepsilon J\log^2(2Y)  \sum_{D<d\le Y}\frac1d  \left(\frac{Q^2}{d}+Q\right)  \notag\\  &\ll X^\varepsilon\left\{   \frac{JQ^2}{D}\log^2(2Y)   +JQ\log^3(2Y)  \right\}.  \label{eq:tpc29-long-content-intermediate}\end{aligned}$$ Use $JQ\asymp X$ to obtain [\[eq:tpc29-long-content-bound\]](TPC-029.md#ref-84fcfbd5dfd4c492). If $D\ge X^\eta$ and $Q=X^{\beta+o(1)}$, the two terms save respectively $X^{-\eta}$ and $X^{-\beta}$ relative to $XQ$; logarithms and the $o(1)$ factors are absorbed into $X^\varepsilon$.

###### ref-78ae6a86f8d8b691

> **Remark: Two different content conditions** \[rem:tpc29-two-content-conditions\] For $q\le J$, the factor $J/q$ makes the simple condition $(r,s)>X^\eta$ sufficient. For $q\ge J$, the occupancy is only $O(1)$ and a large gcd alone does not control the number of primitive pairs. The sparse theorem therefore requires the sharper condition $q/(r,s)\le JX^{-\eta}$.

# The primitive-core boundary and the next gate

The two theorems remove large shared content on both sides of the fiber-length transition. They do not remove the whole complement. This section records the exact remainder and prevents a sign-blind interpretation of the result.

## The sparse method boundary

###### ref-ddd24defa2d7bdf1

###### ref-8a02939301a2c472

In exponent notation, a primitive complement pair has $c=0$. The sparse condition [\[eq:tpc29-sparse-exponent\]](TPC-029.md#ref-8aa18cdc050ab67c) becomes $$a+b\ge\jmath,  \label{eq:tpc29-primitive-sparse-condition}$$ whereas the incidence saving is $$\eta_{\rm sp}(a,b,0)  =(\jmath-a-b)_+=0.  \label{eq:tpc29-primitive-zero-saving}$$ Thus [\[thm:tpc29-weighted-block\]](TPC-029.md#ref-84c5b7d29e5a97bb) reaches only the natural scale on the coprime sparse core. This is a sharp boundary of the present absolute-incidence argument, not a lower bound for the signed arithmetic sum.

###### ref-ade5f445d32d12ce

For a fixed $\eta>0$, after removing [\[thm:tpc29-rich-sparse\]](TPC-029.md#ref-2effbf37eb379090), the remaining sparse region may be written as $$\ge J,  \qquad  \frac{[r,s]}{(r,s)}>JX^{-\eta}.  \label{eq:tpc29-remaining-sparse}$$ It contains every coprime pair with $rs\ge J$. Although each such fiber has $O(1)$ orbit points, the family has enough modulus pairs to restore the natural scale. A short fiber by itself is therefore not a cancellation theorem.

## The remaining long fibers

###### ref-d1d130e63b22ef3f

###### ref-9180a95351528074

For $[r,s]\le J$, [\[thm:tpc29-long-content\]](TPC-029.md#ref-310efce0ec7c5758) removes $(r,s)>X^\eta$ for any fixed $\eta>0$. What remains has $$\le J,\qquad (r,s)\le X^\eta.  \label{eq:tpc29-remaining-long}$$ On any further subpacket carrying the additional generic row separation $|m-n|\ge QX^{-\eta}$, the affine determinant in [\[eq:tpc29-affine-determinant\]](TPC-029.md#ref-4cccaf93ed4f7cc1) satisfies $$\left|\frac{h(m-n)}{(r,s)}\right|  \gg_{h}QX^{-2\eta}.  \label{eq:tpc29-large-remaining-determinant}$$ Thus the separated subpacket is not a small-determinant degeneracy. This conditional observation is not used in either closure theorem and does not remove any unseparated row pairs. The remaining long problem consists of small-content, polynomial-slope one- and two-point reflected Möbius families: one quotient Möbius factor moves in a mixed channel, and two move in the both-ultra channel.

For a fixed compatible quadruple $(m,n,r,s)$ in the both-ultra channel, the logarithmically averaged two-point Elliott theorem is a fixed-form cancellation benchmark for the two affine quotient forms in [\[eq:tpc29-U-form\]](TPC-029.md#ref-6d6e091890c50693)–[\[eq:tpc29-V-form\]](TPC-029.md#ref-c12923842fb2a367) [`\citep{Tao2016}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-029.md#cite-0bccf4cf69786109). A mixed channel has only a one-point moving Möbius factor. Neither fixed-form benchmark supplies an effective rate uniform over the polynomially growing slopes, the divisor family, and the physical row weights. It therefore cannot simply be summed over [\[eq:tpc29-remaining-long\]](TPC-029.md#ref-d1d130e63b22ef3f).

## A sufficient next interface

The next useful statement is a family estimate, not a pointwise asymptotic for an $O(1)$-point fiber. One possible interface is the following.

###### ref-ede7db74b87972c4

###### ref-db957e51e0507a9b

> **Definition: Primitive reflected-family dispersion** \[def:tpc29-next-interface\] For a fixed content threshold $X^\eta$, let $\mathcal P_\eta$ be the union of the remaining regions [\[eq:tpc29-remaining-sparse\]](TPC-029.md#ref-ade5f445d32d12ce) and [\[eq:tpc29-remaining-long\]](TPC-029.md#ref-d1d130e63b22ef3f), with the actual mixed and both-ultra coefficient types retained. A primitive reflected-family dispersion estimate is a bound $$\sum_{\textup{three raw channels}}  \mathcal S(\mathcal P_\eta)  \ll_{B,\eta,h,\mathscr D}XQ(\log X)^{-B}  \label{eq:tpc29-next-dispersion}$$ for every fixed $B>0$, uniformly over the physical opened-row weights and row-only mask.

Equation [\[eq:tpc29-next-dispersion\]](TPC-029.md#ref-db957e51e0507a9b) is not assumed or proved in this paper. Its formulation preserves the joint cutoff difference: one must not first assert that each complete mixed channel or the complete both-ultra channel is negligible. The content-rich pieces may be removed separately only because the present theorems bound them absolutely.

## Claims deliberately excluded

The following statements are not consequences of this paper:

-   closure of every sparse fiber or every long fiber;

-   closure of the complete ultra-long cutoff difference;

-   closure of the full TPC-18 residual or every endpoint packet;

-   a positive lower bound, a Hardy–Littlewood asymptotic, or infinitely many twin primes;

-   a general violation of the sieve parity barrier;

-   polynomially uniform two-point Chowla or Elliott cancellation.

The positive conclusion is narrower and exact: large shared content closes a complete long-fiber sector and a nonempty sparse incidence wedge in exponent space. The remaining core is explicitly identified rather than treated as random.
