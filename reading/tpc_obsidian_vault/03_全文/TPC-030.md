## Abstract

Let $$N_m(j)=mj+h,\qquad N_n(j)=nj+h$$ be two positive affine targets in the primitive opened-row packet of the preceding Möbius-tail reduction. The orbit length is $J=X^{1-\beta+o(1)}$, the rows have size $Q=X^{\beta+o(1)}$, and $QJ\asymp X$; $\mathscr D$ below denotes the fixed packet data. Previous reflected-incidence bounds prune sectors with large common content in the selected divisor variables. They do not use the full target content $$\mathcal G_{m,n}(j):=(N_m(j),N_n(j)).$$

On the primitive orbit we exploit the exact identity $$\mathcal G_{m,n}(j)=(N_m(j),m-n),$$ so the full target gcd divides the row difference. For a fixed row pair, its exact values therefore range over only the divisors of $|m-n|$. Combining this divisor sparsity with the opened-row $\ell^1$ mass gives, for each of the two mixed raw channels and the both-ultra raw channel, the unconditional bound $$\mathcal S_{\mathcal G>Z}  \ll_{\varepsilon,h,\mathscr D}X^\varepsilon  \left(Q^2+\frac{JQ^2}{Z}\right)  \ll_{\varepsilon,h,\mathscr D}X^\varepsilon  \left(Q^2+\frac{XQ}{Z}\right).$$ Thus $Z\ge X^\eta$ produces a fixed-power saving from the natural scale $XQ$, with exponent $\min\{\eta,1-\beta\}$ up to endpoint losses.

There is a second interpretation in the both-ultra channel. If $$N_m=rU,\qquad N_n=sV,$$ put $g=(r,s)$, $e=(U,V)$ and write $r=gr_0$, $s=gs_0$, $U=eU_0$, $V=eV_0$. Then, without any coprimality assumption between $g$ and $e$, $$\mathcal G_{m,n}(j)  =ge(r_0,V_0)(s_0,U_0)\mid m-n.$$ Consequently every sector with polynomially large cross-content on the left is power-small. This includes sectors with $(r,s)=1$, which are outside the large reflected-content theorem.

At the high-$\beta$ sample $\beta=267/400$, take $r,s=X^{1/4+o(1)}$, $(r,s)=X^{o(1)}$, and $(U,V)=X^{2/5+o(1)}$. The reflected lcm is $X^{1/2+o(1)}>J$, while the preceding absolute-incidence method has zero saving on this negligible-reflected-content cell. The new target-content estimate has relaxed margin $133/400$: every fixed exponent below this value is available.

The argument is absolute and uses neither Möbius-sign cancellation nor a Chowla–Elliott input. It does not close the small-target-content core or the complete ultra-long residual, and it yields no prime-pair asymptotic. It isolates the next gate as signed dispersion on the small-target-content residual, with the fully coprime target sector as its sharp model.

# Introduction

The ultra-long complement of a truncated divisor model is not made small merely by reflecting every long divisor across its target hyperbola. Reflection replaces a long divisor by a shorter complement and makes the orbit fibers visible, but the resulting family still contains enough short fibers to recover the natural scale. The preceding paper therefore used two kinds of shared content in the reflected variables: a large gcd on long fibers and a small primitive lcm on sparse fibers [`\citep{WangTPC29}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-030.md#cite-df4241c33f4a0394). Those arguments are absolute and leave the small-reflected-content core.

###### ref-f4d4259df092b6d6

###### ref-d49cfff055439168

This paper observes that the chosen divisor variables do not contain all of the available common-divisor information. For two physical rows $m\ne n$, put $$N_m(j)=mj+h,  \qquad  N_n(j)=nj+h,  \qquad  \mathcal G_{m,n}(j)=(N_m(j),N_n(j)).  \label{eq:tpc30-intro-target-gcd}$$ The full target gcd is independent of how either target is factored. On the primitive orbit it satisfies $$\boxed{  \mathcal G_{m,n}(j)=(N_m(j),m-n),  \qquad  \mathcal G_{m,n}(j)\mid m-n.}  \label{eq:tpc30-intro-gcd-identity}$$ The divisibility statement appeared in the earlier target-gcd geometry for prime source rows [`\citep{WangTPC18}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-030.md#cite-4abf939102bd1649). The new point here is a weighted large-content pruning theorem for the actual opened rows $m=\ell d$, the orbit variable, and all three raw ultra channels.

###### ref-75ce3bd8b1cd8813

###### ref-7e96fb39552f77fa

Let $Q$ be the row scale and $J$ the orbit length, with $$QJ\asymp X.  \label{eq:tpc30-intro-QJ}$$ Let $\mathcal I$ be the physical orbit interval, of length $O_{\mathscr D}(J)$. For a fixed off-diagonal row pair, the exact values of $\mathcal G_{m,n}(j)$ belong to the divisor set of $|m-n|$. If $\mathcal G_{m,n}(j)=c$, the orbit variable lies in one residue class modulo $c$. It follows that $$\#\{j\in\mathcal I:(j,h)=1,\ \mathcal G_{m,n}(j)>Z\}  \ll_{\varepsilon,\mathscr D}X^\varepsilon  \left(1+\frac JZ\right).  \label{eq:tpc30-intro-fixed-row-occupancy}$$ The physical row coefficients have one-row $\ell^1$ mass $O(Q)$. Summing [\[eq:tpc30-intro-fixed-row-occupancy\]](TPC-030.md#ref-7e96fb39552f77fa) over the row pairs gives the main estimate.

> **Theorem: Common-target-content pruning, informal form** \[thm:tpc30-intro-main\] For each of the two mixed raw channels and the both-ultra raw channel, the part on which $\mathcal G_{m,n}(j)>Z$ satisfies $$\boxed{  |\mathcal S_{\mathcal G>Z}|  \ll_{\varepsilon,h,\mathscr D}X^\varepsilon  \left(  Q^2+\frac{JQ^2}{Z}  \right).}  \label{eq:tpc30-intro-main-bound}$$ The same estimate holds for their sum.

###### ref-d64a9b95450fc536

Since $JQ^2\asymp XQ$ is the natural absolute scale, the two terms on the right of [\[eq:tpc30-intro-main-bound\]](TPC-030.md#ref-a2c25f0f658b1156) save respectively $J^{-1}$ and $Z^{-1}$. Thus if $$Q=X^{\beta+o(1)},\qquad  J=X^{1-\beta+o(1)},\qquad  Z\ge X^\eta,  \label{eq:tpc30-intro-exponents}$$ then every fixed $$\eta_0<\min\{\eta,1-\beta\}  \label{eq:tpc30-intro-saving}$$ is an available power saving. No cancellation between Möbius signs or between distinct rows is used.

The factorization consequence is especially useful. In the both-ultra channel write $$N_m=rU,  \qquad  N_n=sV.  \label{eq:tpc30-intro-factorizations}$$ Put $$g=(r,s),\quad e=(U,V),\quad  r=gr_0,\quad s=gs_0,\quad U=eU_0,\quad V=eV_0.  \label{eq:tpc30-intro-primitive-factors}$$ Then $(r_0,s_0)=(U_0,V_0)=1$ and the stronger exact identity is $$\boxed{  \mathcal G_{m,n}(j)  =ge(r_0,V_0)(s_0,U_0).}  \label{eq:tpc30-intro-cross-content}$$ No assumption such as $(g,e)=1$ is needed. The two final factors are the cross-contents left after the within-side gcds are removed. Therefore [\[eq:tpc30-intro-main-bound\]](TPC-030.md#ref-a2c25f0f658b1156) gives a power-saving upper bound for every retained sector on which the product in [\[eq:tpc30-intro-cross-content\]](TPC-030.md#ref-3fd69ab66d3559d0) exceeds $Z$. The factor $g=(r,s)$ is the reflected content used in TPC-29; $e=(U,V)$ and the two cross-contents are new to the present reduction’s bookkeeping. In particular, the present estimate remains effective when $(r,s)=1$.

At the exact high-$\beta$ sample inherited from TPC-28, $$\beta=\frac{267}{400},  \qquad  1-\beta=\frac{133}{400},  \label{eq:tpc30-intro-high-beta}$$ consider the exponent cell $$r,s=X^{1/4+o(1)},\qquad  (r,s)=X^{o(1)},\qquad  U,V=X^{3/4+o(1)},\qquad  (U,V)=X^{2/5+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc30-intro-high-cell}$$ Its reflected lcm has exponent $1/2$, strictly above the orbit exponent $133/400$, and its primitive reflected lcm has the same exponent. ###### ref-4dcd533d218cb979

The result does not solve the residual problem. Once all polynomially large target content is removed, the remaining core has $$\mathcal G_{m,n}(j)\le X^\eta  \label{eq:tpc30-intro-small-core}$$ for every fixed threshold used in the pruning, with the fully coprime case $\mathcal G_{m,n}(j)=1$ as its sharp model. An absolute gcd argument has no further power saving there. The next plausible interface is a signed dispersion theorem across normalized determinant windows or determinant residue classes. Fixed-form logarithmically averaged two-point results such as [`\citep{Tao2016}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-030.md#cite-0bccf4cf69786109) do not by themselves give a uniform estimate for the polynomially growing family retained here.

# The physical packet and its raw channels

###### ref-944b9738b8000e72

Fix $h\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}$ and put $H=\operatorname{rad}|h|$. We retain the primitive opened-row packet used in TPC-25–TPC-29 [`\citep{WangTPC25,WangTPC28,WangTPC29}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-030.md#cite-39e8dc14c55a2411); [\[WangTPC28\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-030.md#cite-1f714b6620a45b76); [\[WangTPC29\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-030.md#cite-df4241c33f4a0394). Its scales satisfy $$QJ\asymp_{\mathscr D}X,  \qquad  Q=X^{\beta+o(1)},  \qquad  J=X^{1-\beta+o(1)},  \qquad 0<\beta<1.  \label{eq:tpc30-row-scales}$$ Here $\mathscr D$ denotes the fixed smooth, residue, source, and support data.

The row labels are $\alpha=(\ell_\alpha,d_\alpha)$, where $\ell_\alpha\asymp L$ is prime, $d_\alpha$ is squarefree, and $$m_\alpha=\ell_\alpha d_\alpha\asymp_{\mathscr D}Q.  \label{eq:tpc30-opened-rows}$$ The size separation in the opened packet makes $m_\alpha$ determine $\alpha$ uniquely. In particular, the distinct row integers lie in an interval of length $O_{\mathscr D}(Q)$. The two row coefficients satisfy $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_\alpha|\gamma_\alpha^{(i)}|  &\ll_{\mathscr D}Q,  \label{eq:tpc30-row-l1}\\  \sum_\alpha|\gamma_\alpha^{(i)}|^2  &\ll_{\mathscr D}Q\log(2L),  \qquad i=1,2.  \label{eq:tpc30-row-l2}\end{aligned}$$

###### ref-7bcd89d6e35b4ac3

Every contributing row and orbit point obeys $$(m_\alpha,H)=1,  \qquad  (j,H)=1.  \label{eq:tpc30-primitive-orbit}$$ For $$N_\alpha(j)=m_\alpha j+h,  \label{eq:tpc30-target}$$ the joint support is contained in $O_{\mathscr D}(J)$ consecutive integers and $$c_{\mathscr D}X\le N_\alpha(j)  \le C_{\mathscr D}X.  \label{eq:tpc30-target-window}$$ Thus every target used below is positive. Moreover $$(m_\alpha,h)=1,  \qquad  (j,h)=1,  \label{eq:tpc30-full-primitivity}$$ because $H$ contains every prime divisor of $h$.

The row-pair mask is independent of all orbit and divisor variables and satisfies $$|\mathfrak m(\alpha,\gamma)|\le1,  \qquad  \mathfrak m(\alpha,\gamma)=0  \quad\text{if }m_\alpha=m_\gamma.  \label{eq:tpc30-row-mask}$$ The fixed-period factor $\Xi_{\alpha,\gamma}(j)$ and smooth weight $\mathcal W_{\alpha,\gamma}(j/J)$ are uniformly bounded. Their support may depend on the row pair, but it lies in an interval of $O_{\mathscr D}(J)$ consecutive integers.

# Full target content and orbit classes

The following identity is the arithmetic reason that target content can be summed through the row family. Its first divisibility consequence is the opened-row version of the target-gcd geometry in TPC-18 [`\citep{WangTPC18}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-030.md#cite-4abf939102bd1649); we include the short proof because the exact equality and the orbit class are used below.

###### ref-074a79c994cbd73c

###### ref-176e1b4fced33e5f

###### ref-d2468276858d9f72

###### ref-3e934fb867b9174f

> **Lemma: Primitive target-gcd identity** \[lem:tpc30-target-gcd\] Let $m\ne n$ obey $(mn,h)=1$, and let $(j,h)=1$. Put $$N_m=mj+h,  \qquad  N_n=nj+h,  \qquad  \mathcal G_{m,n}(j)=(N_m,N_n).  \label{eq:tpc30-target-gcd-definition}$$ Then $$\boxed{  \mathcal G_{m,n}(j)  =(N_m,m-n)\mid |m-n|.}  \label{eq:tpc30-target-gcd-identity}$$ In addition, $$(\mathcal G_{m,n}(j),mnh)=1.  \label{eq:tpc30-target-gcd-primitive}$$

> **Proof** Subtracting the two targets gives $$(N_m,N_n)=(N_m,(n-m)j).$$ But $$(N_m,j)=(mj+h,j)=(h,j)=1.$$ The factor $j$ may therefore be cancelled from the second argument, which proves [\[eq:tpc30-target-gcd-identity\]](TPC-030.md#ref-d2468276858d9f72). If a prime divides $N_m$ and $m$, it divides $h$, contrary to $(m,h)=1$; the same holds with $n$. Finally every prime dividing both $N_m$ and $h$ also divides $mj$ and hence $m$ or $j$ after using the relevant primitivity. More directly, $(N_m,h)=(mj,h)=1$ because $(m,h)=(j,h)=1$. This proves [\[eq:tpc30-target-gcd-primitive\]](TPC-030.md#ref-3e934fb867b9174f).

###### ref-ba5aacb79b9ebcac

> **Remark: Why orbit primitivity is essential** \[rem:tpc30-nonprimitive-gcd\] Without $(j,h)=1$, the equality in [\[eq:tpc30-target-gcd-identity\]](TPC-030.md#ref-d2468276858d9f72) need not hold: a factor shared by $j$ and $h$ can remain in both targets without dividing $m-n$. The nonprimitive content was treated separately by endpoint collapse in TPC-18. No such sector is silently included in the theorem below.

The exact target gcd selects a row congruence and a single orbit class.

###### ref-9be30ead6447a1c5

###### ref-66c64a6b80b2217c

###### ref-f46a7ef29e0bf884

###### ref-dc4e90677f034f80

> **Lemma: Exact-content orbit occupancy** \[lem:tpc30-content-occupancy\] Under the hypotheses of [\[lem:tpc30-target-gcd\]](TPC-030.md#ref-074a79c994cbd73c), suppose $$\mathcal G_{m,n}(j)=c.  \label{eq:tpc30-exact-content-c}$$ Then $$m\equiv n\pmod c,  \qquad  j\equiv-h\overline m\pmod c,  \label{eq:tpc30-content-congruences}$$ where $\overline m$ is the inverse of $m$ modulo $c$. Consequently, in any interval $\mathcal I$ of at most $C_{\mathscr D}J$ consecutive integers, $$\#\{j\in\mathcal I:(j,h)=1,\ \mathcal G_{m,n}(j)=c\}  \ll_{\mathscr D}1+\frac Jc.  \label{eq:tpc30-exact-content-occupancy}$$

> **Proof** The first congruence follows from $c\mid m-n$ in [\[eq:tpc30-target-gcd-identity\]](TPC-030.md#ref-d2468276858d9f72). Since $c\mid N_m$ and $(c,m)=1$ by [\[eq:tpc30-target-gcd-primitive\]](TPC-030.md#ref-3e934fb867b9174f), the divisibility $c\mid mj+h$ selects the second congruence. One residue class modulo $c$ occupies an interval of the stated length at most $O_{\mathscr D}(1+J/c)$ times. Requiring the gcd to equal $c$, rather than merely to be divisible by $c$, can only reduce this number.

###### ref-0f5da233e32a3b82

> **Remark: Exact layers prevent multiplicity loss** \[rem:tpc30-exact-layers\] The proof of the main theorem groups each orbit point by the single integer $c=\mathcal G_{m,n}(j)$. It does not sum the condition $c\mid\mathcal G_{m,n}(j)$ over every divisor $c$, so no additional divisor multiplicity is hidden in the content decomposition.

# Row–orbit energy and common-target pruning

###### ref-7c57c38615066550

For $c\ge1$, define the absolute row congruence mass $$\mathcal R_c  :=\sum_{\alpha,\gamma}  |\gamma_\alpha^{(1)}\gamma_\gamma^{(2)}|  \mathbf 1_{c\mid m_\alpha-m_\gamma}.  \label{eq:tpc30-row-congruence-mass}$$ The following estimate was used for reflected content in TPC-29. We record it in the present notation because it is now paired with the full target gcd rather than a selected divisor gcd.

###### ref-6a53f50708feae9d

###### ref-b817cb706f3e8d84

> **Lemma: Opened-row residue energy** \[lem:tpc30-row-residue-energy\] Uniformly for $c\ge1$, $$\boxed{  \mathcal R_c  \ll_{\mathscr D}  \left(\frac{Q^2}{c}+Q\right)\log(2L).}  \label{eq:tpc30-row-residue-bound}$$

> **Proof** For $i=1,2$ and $a\pmod c$, put $$X_i(a)=\sum_{m_\alpha\equiv a\, (c)}  |\gamma_\alpha^{(i)}|,  \qquad  E_i(a)=\sum_{m_\alpha\equiv a\, (c)}  |\gamma_\alpha^{(i)}|^2.$$ The row integers are distinct and lie in an interval of length $O_{\mathscr D}(Q)$. Each residue class contains $O_{\mathscr D}(Q/c+1)$ rows, so Cauchy–Schwarz gives $$X_i(a)^2  \ll_{\mathscr D}\left(\frac Qc+1\right)E_i(a).$$ It follows that $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal R_c  &=\sum_{a\, (c)}X_1(a)X_2(a)\\  &\ll_{\mathscr D}  \left(\frac Qc+1\right)  \left(\sum_aE_1(a)\right)^{1/2}  \left(\sum_aE_2(a)\right)^{1/2}.\end{aligned}$$ Apply the two estimates in [\[eq:tpc30-row-l2\]](TPC-030.md#ref-15dbd90c9bc2958b).

###### ref-63a4b916a04687c2

For a row pair and orbit point, abbreviate $$\mathcal G_{\alpha,\gamma}(j)  :=(N_\alpha(j),N_\gamma(j)).  \label{eq:tpc30-physical-target-gcd}$$ Taking absolute values in any raw channel gives $$|\mathcal K^{(\nu)}_{\alpha,\gamma}(j)|  \ll_{\varepsilon,\mathscr D}X^\varepsilon,  \qquad 1\le\nu\le3,  \label{eq:tpc30-kernel-envelope}$$ by [\[eq:tpc30-pointwise-envelope\]](TPC-030.md#ref-a7d7cbfdd4f7e8e4).

###### ref-19d1620d84f9eec8

> **Proposition: A uniform exact-content layer** \[prop:tpc30-exact-content-layer\] For every integer $c\ge1$ and $1\le\nu\le3$, $$\boxed{  |\mathcal S_\nu(\mathcal G_{\alpha,\gamma}(j)=c)|  \ll_{\varepsilon,h,\mathscr D}X^\varepsilon  \left(1+\frac Jc\right)  \left(\frac{Q^2}{c}+Q\right).}  \label{eq:tpc30-exact-content-layer}$$

> **Proof** The mask in [\[eq:tpc30-row-mask\]](TPC-030.md#ref-773fc75b5c4eaf4f) first deletes the diagonal rows. By [\[lem:tpc30-target-gcd\]](TPC-030.md#ref-074a79c994cbd73c), the exact layer is empty unless $c\mid m_\alpha-m_\gamma$. For each surviving row pair, [\[lem:tpc30-content-occupancy\]](TPC-030.md#ref-9be30ead6447a1c5) gives $O_{\mathscr D}(1+J/c)$ orbit points. Apply [\[eq:tpc30-kernel-envelope\]](TPC-030.md#ref-63a4b916a04687c2), sum the row weights through $\mathcal R_c$, and use [\[eq:tpc30-row-residue-bound\]](TPC-030.md#ref-b817cb706f3e8d84).

Summing [\[eq:tpc30-exact-content-layer\]](TPC-030.md#ref-19d1620d84f9eec8) over every integer $c>Z$ would introduce an avoidable endpoint term. The stronger tail estimate comes from first fixing the row pair.

> **Lemma: Fixed-row large-content occupancy** \[lem:tpc30-fixed-row-large-content\] Let $m\ne n$ be physical rows, let $\mathcal I$ be an interval of $O_{\mathscr D}(J)$ consecutive integers, and let $Z\ge1$. Then $$\boxed{  \#\{j\in\mathcal I:(j,h)=1,\ \mathcal G_{m,n}(j)>Z\}  \ll_{\varepsilon,h,\mathscr D}  X^\varepsilon\left(1+\frac JZ\right).}  \label{eq:tpc30-fixed-row-large-content}$$

###### ref-82cf48b5f8765672

> **Proof** Put $\Delta=|m-n|\ne0$. Every exact value $c=\mathcal G_{m,n}(j)$ divides $\Delta$ by [\[lem:tpc30-target-gcd\]](TPC-030.md#ref-074a79c994cbd73c). Grouping by the exact value and applying [\[lem:tpc30-content-occupancy\]](TPC-030.md#ref-9be30ead6447a1c5) gives $$\begin{aligned}  \#\{j\in\mathcal I:(j,h)=1,\ \mathcal G_{m,n}(j)>Z\}  &\le  \sum_{\substack{c\mid\Delta\\c>Z}}  O_{\mathscr D}\left(1+\frac Jc\right)  \notag\\  &\ll_{\mathscr D}  \tau(\Delta)\left(1+\frac JZ\right).  \label{eq:tpc30-fixed-row-divisor-sum}\end{aligned}$$ Since $\Delta\ll_{\mathscr D}Q\ll X^{O_{\mathscr D}(1)}$, the standard divisor bound absorbs $\tau(\Delta)$ into $X^\varepsilon$.

> **Theorem: Common-target-content pruning** \[thm:tpc30-common-target-pruning\] Let $Z\ge1$. For each $1\le\nu\le3$, the physical raw channel in [\[eq:tpc30-restricted-raw-channel\]](TPC-030.md#ref-9330825b5486b910), restricted by $$\mathcal E_Z(\alpha,\gamma,j)  :=\{\mathcal G_{\alpha,\gamma}(j)>Z\},  \label{eq:tpc30-large-target-content-event}$$ satisfies $$\boxed{  |\mathcal S_\nu(\mathcal E_Z)|  \ll_{\varepsilon,h,\mathscr D}X^\varepsilon Q^2  \left(1+\frac JZ\right)  \ll_{\varepsilon,h,\mathscr D}X^\varepsilon  \left(Q^2+\frac{XQ}{Z}\right).}  \label{eq:tpc30-main-content-bound}$$ The same bound holds for the sum of the three raw channels.

###### ref-132de6c163d3541e

> **Proof** The off-diagonal mask gives $m_\alpha\ne m_\gamma$, so [\[lem:tpc30-fixed-row-large-content\]](TPC-030.md#ref-29279f79884dc25c) applies to every row pair. Use [\[eq:tpc30-kernel-envelope\]](TPC-030.md#ref-63a4b916a04687c2) and then the row $\ell^1$ bounds: $$\begin{aligned}  |\mathcal S_\nu(\mathcal E_Z)|  &\ll X^\varepsilon\left(1+\frac JZ\right)  \sum_{\alpha,\gamma}  |\gamma_\alpha^{(1)}\gamma_\gamma^{(2)}|\\  &\ll X^\varepsilon Q^2\left(1+\frac JZ\right).\end{aligned}$$ Finally use $JQ\asymp X$. If $Z\gg_{\mathscr D}Q$, the event is empty because $$\mathcal G_{\alpha,\gamma}(j)  \mid m_\alpha-m_\gamma  \ll_{\mathscr D}Q.  \label{eq:tpc30-content-upper-range}$$

###### ref-d2228a3048c33c4a

###### ref-16eef84947c59f06

> **Corollary: Power-saving form** \[cor:tpc30-content-power-saving\] Suppose [\[eq:tpc30-row-scales\]](TPC-030.md#ref-944b9738b8000e72) holds and $Z\ge X^\eta$ for a fixed $\eta>0$. Then, for every fixed $$\eta_0<\min\{\eta,1-\beta\},  \label{eq:tpc30-saving-exponent}$$ one has $$\boxed{  \sum_{\nu=1}^3|\mathcal S_\nu(\mathcal E_Z)|  \ll_{\eta,\eta_0,h,\mathscr D}XQX^{-\eta_0}.}  \label{eq:tpc30-content-power-saving}$$

> **Proof** Relative to $XQ\asymp JQ^2$, the two terms in [\[eq:tpc30-main-content-bound\]](TPC-030.md#ref-ea2555ce6bbf9c25) have respective ratios $J^{-1}$ and $Z^{-1}$. Use [\[eq:tpc30-row-scales\]](TPC-030.md#ref-944b9738b8000e72); the strict gap in [\[eq:tpc30-saving-exponent\]](TPC-030.md#ref-d2228a3048c33c4a) absorbs logarithms and the $o(1)$ scale factors.

# Four-factor content and cross-factor pruning

The full target gcd can be read from any pair of target factorizations, but it is not usually the product of the two within-side gcds alone.

> **Proposition: Exact four-factor content decomposition** \[prop:tpc30-four-factor-content\] Let $$N_m=rU,\qquad N_n=sV  \label{eq:tpc30-two-factorizations}$$ with positive integers $r,s,U,V$. Put $$g=(r,s),\qquad e=(U,V),  \label{eq:tpc30-within-contents}$$ and write $$r=gr_0,\quad s=gs_0,\qquad  U=eU_0,\quad V=eV_0.  \label{eq:tpc30-primitive-factorizations}$$ Thus $(r_0,s_0)=(U_0,V_0)=1$. Define the two normalized cross-contents $$x=(r_0,V_0),\qquad y=(s_0,U_0).  \label{eq:tpc30-cross-contents}$$ Then $$\boxed{  (N_m,N_n)=gexy.}  \label{eq:tpc30-four-factor-identity}$$ In particular, on the primitive physical orbit, $$\boxed{  gexy=\mathcal G_{m,n}(j)\mid m-n.}  \label{eq:tpc30-four-factor-determinant}$$ No coprimality between $g$ and $e$ is asserted or required.

###### ref-493b265c52ea3796

###### ref-77614898de54a1a5

###### ref-24df8899f5174f71

> **Proof** Factoring the two common within-side contents gives $$(N_m,N_n)  =ge\,(r_0U_0,s_0V_0).  \label{eq:tpc30-remove-ge}$$ We prove $$(r_0U_0,s_0V_0)  =(r_0,V_0)(s_0,U_0)  \label{eq:tpc30-cross-gcd-identity}$$ prime by prime. Fix a prime $p$ and write $$a=v_p(r_0),\quad b=v_p(s_0),\quad  u=v_p(U_0),\quad v=v_p(V_0).$$ The two primitive conditions give $$\min\{a,b\}=0,\qquad \min\{u,v\}=0.$$ If $a>0$, then $b=0$. When $u>0$, one has $v=0$ and both sides of $$\min\{a+u,b+v\}  =\min\{a,v\}+\min\{b,u\}  \label{eq:tpc30-valuation-identity}$$ are zero; when $v>0$, one has $u=0$ and both sides are $\min\{a,v\}$. If $u=v=0$, both sides are again zero. The case $b>0$ is symmetric, and the case $a=b=0$ is immediate. This proves [\[eq:tpc30-cross-gcd-identity\]](TPC-030.md#ref-77614898de54a1a5). Equations [\[eq:tpc30-remove-ge\]](TPC-030.md#ref-493b265c52ea3796) and [\[eq:tpc30-target-gcd-identity\]](TPC-030.md#ref-d2468276858d9f72) now give the result.

###### ref-85ab599187472b42

> **Remark: Why the cross terms cannot be dropped** \[rem:tpc30-cross-terms-needed\] Take $r=2$, $U=3$, $s=3$, and $V=4$. Then $g=e=1$, but $$(rU,sV)=(6,12)=6.$$ The missing factors are exactly $x=(2,4)=2$ and $y=(3,3)=3$. Thus the generally valid statement is not $(N_m,N_n)=ge$, but the four-factor identity [\[eq:tpc30-four-factor-identity\]](TPC-030.md#ref-fa57b2d8315eeaff).

## Expanded divisor channels

###### ref-a2efeb6a3f9a7cfa

###### ref-767fa37e362d2483

###### ref-2664786a4a685b32

###### ref-def99a79994b7c51

Each kernel in [\[eq:tpc30-three-raw-kernels\]](TPC-030.md#ref-6087d79dff280d46) is a finite sum over one divisor of each target. In a mixed channel, one selected divisor comes from the prefix and the other from the ultra complement; in the both-ultra channel both are reflected complement variables. For any chosen pair $r\mid N_m$, $s\mid N_n$, put $$U=N_m/r,\qquad V=N_n/s.  \label{eq:tpc30-co-divisors}$$ Indeed, for a prefix leg and a reflected ultra leg, respectively, $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_{r\mid N_m}|b_R(r)|  &\ll \tau(N_m)(\log(2X))^3,  \label{eq:tpc30-prefix-absolute-mass}\\  \sum_{r\mid N_m}|a(N_m/r)|  &\ll \tau(N_m)\log(2X).  \label{eq:tpc30-ultra-absolute-mass}\end{aligned}$$ The same estimates hold for $N_n$. Since both targets are $O_{\mathscr D}(X)$, the divisor bound shows that the absolute product mass of any mixed or both-ultra subfamily is bounded by the full pointwise divisor envelope. Here $\kappa^{(\nu)}(j,r,s)$ denotes the product of the two divisor coefficients in the expanded $\nu$-th kernel, so $$\sum_{\substack{r\mid N_m,\ s\mid N_n\\                   \textup{retained channel supports}}}  |\kappa^{(\nu)}(j,r,s)|  \ll_{\varepsilon,\mathscr D}X^\varepsilon.  \label{eq:tpc30-expanded-envelope}$$ Consequently the proof of [\[thm:tpc30-common-target-pruning\]](TPC-030.md#ref-39672c3119a6ac34) applies unchanged after restricting the expanded divisor sum to any condition implying $\mathcal G_{m,n}(j)>Z$.

###### ref-9fdeada8b5839b1c

###### ref-03dbe60855db1212

> **Corollary: Cross-factor pruning** \[cor:tpc30-cross-factor-pruning\] For each mixed or both-ultra expanded channel, restrict its individual divisor terms by any one of $$ge>Z,\qquad e>Z,\qquad x>Z,\qquad y>Z,  \qquad\text{or more generally}\qquad gexy>Z.  \label{eq:tpc30-large-cross-events}$$ The resulting physical contribution satisfies $$\boxed{  |\mathcal S_{\rm cross>Z}|  \ll_{\varepsilon,h,\mathscr D}X^\varepsilon  \left(Q^2+\frac{XQ}{Z}\right).}  \label{eq:tpc30-cross-factor-bound}$$ If $Z\ge X^\eta$, every fixed $\eta_0<\min\{\eta,1-\beta\}$ is a power-saving exponent.

> **Proof** By [\[prop:tpc30-four-factor-content\]](TPC-030.md#ref-3a4e2a19f34961aa), each of the first four conditions in [\[eq:tpc30-large-cross-events\]](TPC-030.md#ref-9fdeada8b5839b1c) implies $\mathcal G_{m,n}(j)>Z$, while the last is equivalent to that condition. Use [\[eq:tpc30-expanded-envelope\]](TPC-030.md#ref-def99a79994b7c51) in place of [\[eq:tpc30-kernel-envelope\]](TPC-030.md#ref-63a4b916a04687c2) in the proof of [\[thm:tpc30-common-target-pruning\]](TPC-030.md#ref-39672c3119a6ac34).

## What is new relative to reflected content

TPC-29 tracks $$g=(r,s),\qquad q=[r,s],$$ and proves a power-saving bound for a long-fiber sector with large $g$ and a sparse sector in which the primitive reflected lcm $q/g$ is small. The factors $$e=(U,V),\qquad  x=(r/g,V/e),\qquad  y=(s/g,U/e)$$ are invisible if the two selected divisor variables are considered without their co-divisors. Thus [\[cor:tpc30-cross-factor-pruning\]](TPC-030.md#ref-bf5ebaeb06b29f38) can be effective with $g=1$ and $q/g$ large, exactly where the preceding absolute reflected-incidence argument reaches only the natural scale.

No squarefreeness of $r$ or $s$ is used. In an ultra term the co-divisor coefficient $a(U)$ or $a(V)$ vanishes unless the relevant co-divisor is squarefree, but the four-factor identity itself is an integer identity and requires no coefficient support.

# Comparison, the coprime core, and the next gate

## The inherited identity and the new estimate

TPC-18 already observed that, on a primitive common orbit, the gcd of two affine targets divides the source-row difference [`\citep{WangTPC18}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-030.md#cite-4abf939102bd1649). The Euclidean step behind [\[eq:tpc30-target-gcd-identity\]](TPC-030.md#ref-d2468276858d9f72) is therefore inherited. The present additions are:

1.  the exact identity for the actual composite opened rows $m=\ell d$ and their physical primitive support;

2.  the fixed-row large-content occupancy [\[eq:tpc30-fixed-row-large-content\]](TPC-030.md#ref-1ef0de9270c633ab);

3.  the weighted family estimate [\[eq:tpc30-main-content-bound\]](TPC-030.md#ref-ea2555ce6bbf9c25) for all three raw channels;

4.  the exact four-factor identity [\[eq:tpc30-four-factor-identity\]](TPC-030.md#ref-fa57b2d8315eeaff) and its application to quotient-rich and cross-rich divisor sectors.

Thus there is no claim that the Euclidean gcd observation was unknown. The contribution within the present reduction is the physical family-level pruning bound obtained by placing that observation before divisor-variable separation.

## Strict separation from the TPC-29 regions

###### ref-700efeae985cd32a

###### ref-0518d6712f231cf5

Write $$q=[r,s],\qquad g=(r,s).$$ TPC-29 proves power saving in the two regions $$\begin{aligned}  q\le J,\qquad &g>X^\eta,  \label{eq:tpc30-old-long-region}\\  q\ge J,\qquad &q/g\le JX^{-\eta}.  \label{eq:tpc30-old-sparse-region}\end{aligned}$$ The new theorem depends instead on the full target content $$\mathcal G=gexy.$$ It is independent of whether $q$ is below or above $J$. In particular, the high-$\beta$ block in [6](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-030.md#src-a3aac34377546802) has $q>J$, $g=X^{o(1)}$, and $q/g>J$, so neither [\[eq:tpc30-old-long-region\]](TPC-030.md#ref-700efeae985cd32a) nor [\[eq:tpc30-old-sparse-region\]](TPC-030.md#ref-0518d6712f231cf5) applies. Its large quotient content $e$ nevertheless makes it power-small here.

The result is also distinct from deleting near-diagonal rows. The condition $\mathcal G>Z$ implies that $m-n$ has a large divisor; it does not imply that $|m-n|$ is small. The high-$\beta$ cell is compatible with $|m-n|=X^{\beta+o(1)}$, far from the near-row range.

## The small-content residual

###### ref-b62af0cf2e216a1e

###### ref-58857ab5e041901c

After applying [\[thm:tpc30-common-target-pruning\]](TPC-030.md#ref-39672c3119a6ac34) with a fixed threshold $X^\eta$, what remains satisfies $$\boxed{  \mathcal G_{m,n}(j)\le X^\eta.}  \label{eq:tpc30-small-content-core}$$ The sharp model is the fully coprime target sector $$(N_m(j),N_n(j))=1.  \label{eq:tpc30-coprime-target-core}$$ There $$g=e=x=y=1$$ for every selected factorization. Neither shared content nor a repartition of the same factors supplies a power saving.

###### ref-3ce764c5eee638ca

For a reflected pair, compatibility still gives $$g\mid m-n.$$ The normalized row determinant $$\Delta_0=\frac{m-n}{g}  \label{eq:tpc30-normalized-determinant}$$ is therefore an integer. The next useful decomposition is by the number of allowed values of $\Delta_0$ in translated windows or residue classes, not by another neighborhood of $\Delta_0=0$. Near-row windows have already been removed in the earlier pipeline; the genuinely new question concerns macroscopically separated rows whose normalized determinants occupy a thin additive spectrum.

An absolute estimate for one thin determinant class would not by itself close [\[eq:tpc30-small-content-core\]](TPC-030.md#ref-b62af0cf2e216a1e): summing all residue classes absolutely restores the natural scale. For the present strategy, the next proposed gate is a signed family estimate that recombines these classes while retaining part of the single-class saving. It must also preserve the two mixed channels and the both-ultra channel from the exact cutoff difference. Fixed-form logarithmically averaged Elliott cancellation [`\citep{Tao2016}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-030.md#cite-0bccf4cf69786109) is a useful benchmark, but it supplies neither an effective rate nor the required uniformity in the polynomially growing slopes, divisor families, rows, and masks.

## Staged route after this paper

The resulting route is deliberately modular:

1.  prove determinant-entropy bounds for translated windows and individual normalized-determinant residue cells inside [\[eq:tpc30-small-content-core\]](TPC-030.md#ref-b62af0cf2e216a1e);

2.  seek signed dispersion across those cells, exploiting the factorable row source rather than only an arbitrary bounded mask;

3.  reassemble the mixed and both-ultra channels without separating away the cancellation encoded by the exact cutoff difference;

4.  return to the complete residual and endpoint packets only after the family estimate is uniform on the physical parameter box.

Failure at any one of these stages would still identify a sharp method boundary. None of these future interfaces is assumed in the theorems of the present paper.

## Finite exact certificate

2.  exact-$c$ orbit layers, direct occupancy in finite windows $\mathcal I$ of length $O_{\mathscr D}(J)$, and the fixed-row large-content inequality $$\#\{j\in\mathcal I:(j,h)=1,\ \mathcal G_{m,n}(j)>Z\}  \le\tau(|m-n|)\left(1+\frac JZ\right);$$

3.  the four-factor identity $$(rU,sV)  =(r,s)(U,V)(r/(r,s),V/(U,V))               (s/(r,s),U/(U,V));$$

4.  the explicit finite row-residue inequality $$\mathcal R_c\le K_c\|\gamma^{(1)}\|_2\|\gamma^{(2)}\|_2,$$ where $K_c$ is the largest row occupancy of a residue class;

## Claims deliberately excluded

The following statements are not consequences of this paper:

-   cancellation or closure on every small-target-gcd fiber;

-   closure of the complete ultra-long cutoff difference or the full TPC-18 residual;

-   an effective polynomially uniform Chowla–Elliott theorem;

-   a positive lower bound, a Hardy–Littlewood asymptotic, or infinitely many twin primes;

-   a general violation or weakening of the sieve parity barrier.

The positive conclusion is narrower: within the retained three-channel physical packet, every polynomially large full-target-content sector is power-small, including quotient-rich and cross-rich sectors that are invisible to reflected content alone. The remaining small-target-content residual is not solved; its fully coprime sector is identified as the sharp model.
