## Abstract

We continue the primitive Möbius-tail reduction at the point where canonical determinant cells are individually sparse but their untwisted reassembly remains uncontrolled. Let $Q=X^{\beta+o(1)}$ be the row scale, $J=X^{1-\beta+o(1)}$ the orbit scale, and $N_0=JQ^2\asymp XQ$ the natural two-row mass. The two mixed ultra-cutoff channels and the both-ultra channel admit the pointwise identity $$\mathsf A_{m,T}C_n+C_m\mathsf A_{n,T}+C_mC_n  =\mathsf A_{m,U_0}\mathsf A_{n,U_0}   -\mathsf A_{m,T}\mathsf A_{n,T}.$$ Consequently, their divisor coefficient is the difference of two rank-one tensors and has rank at most two. We retain this matching, the actual prime–Möbius row coefficients, the canonical target content, and the joint physical multiplier in one exact bilinear formula. The row sign and the reflected divisor sign fuse as $$\mu(d)b_R(w)=-\mu(dw)w_R(w)  \qquad\bigl(w\mid \ell dj+h\bigr),$$ while exact content and determinant phase factorize without requiring any coprimality between the content and the auxiliary Fourier modulus.

For the signed amplitude $A_C(n)$ of the matched shell with canonical content at most $C\le J$, $C\ll_{\mathscr D}Q$, and normalized determinant $n$, the preceding singleton-cell estimate gives $$\|A_C\|_1\ll X^\varepsilon N_0,  \qquad  \|A_C\|_\infty\ll X^\varepsilon Q(C+J),  \qquad  \|A_C\|_2^2\ll X^\varepsilon\frac{N_0^2}{Q}.$$ Choosing a prime Fourier modulus larger than the determinant span yields an exact no-wrap Parseval identity. Hence all but $O(X^{2\sigma+\varepsilon})$ frequencies satisfy $|\widehat A_{C,q}(r)|\le N_0X^{-\sigma}$. A classical additive large sieve gives the corresponding Farey-family estimate. At $\beta=267/400$, all but an $O(X^{-1/400+o(1)})$ proportion of determinant frequencies already have every fixed saving below $133/400$.

The remaining hard raw component of the original calibrated residual is the distinguished coefficient $r=0$, not a generic frequency, modulo the already controlled large-content and drift terms. We show exactly that the nontrivial terms of the small-content Möbius projector reassemble to minus the previously bounded large-content shell, so the zero coefficient is equivalent, within the inherited large-content error, to the full raw cutoff shell. If its flatness exponent $\chi$ satisfies $$|\widehat A_{C,q}(0)|^2\le X^{\chi+o(1)}\sum_n|A_C(n)|^2,$$ then, whenever $\chi<\beta$ and $\kappa>0$, the matched shell has every saving below $$\min\left\{1-\beta,\,\kappa,\,\frac{\beta-\chi}{2}\right\},  \qquad C=X^{\kappa+o(1)}.$$ At the high-$\beta$ endpoint with $C=\lfloor J\rfloor$, the precise remaining condition is $\chi\le1/400$. This flatness estimate is not proved here. A sharp finite Fourier model shows that the available support and norm bounds, even combined with perfect cancellation at every nonzero frequency, cannot control $r=0$. A primitive finite witness likewise shows that the content projector retains a coherent row mode. Thus the paper supplies an unconditional almost-all-frequency theorem, an exact conditional transfer, and a sharp interface obstruction; it does not prove a prime-pair asymptotic, twin primes, or a breach of the sieve parity barrier.

# Introduction and route ledger

###### ref-64985e3bfb9e45ee

The primitive Möbius-tail programme has reached a sharply localized analytic question. The preceding reductions first preserved the four prime/model channels long enough to recombine their divisor coefficients, then returned from a selected cutoff to the full physical cutoff, and finally removed target pairs with large common content [`\citep{WangTPC19,WangTPC20,WangTPC25,WangTPC29,WangTPC30}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-032.md#cite-09db42d5184e63ba); [\[WangTPC20\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-032.md#cite-7525f88be3fcfa5f); [\[WangTPC25\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-032.md#cite-39e8dc14c55a2411); [\[WangTPC29\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-032.md#cite-df4241c33f4a0394); [\[WangTPC30\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-032.md#cite-12cd0ee444491001). TPC-31 introduced the factorization-independent coordinates $$N_m(j)=mj+h,  \qquad  c=(N_m(j),N_n(j)),  \qquad  \Delta^\#=\frac{m-n}{c},  \label{eq:tpc32-intro-canonical}$$ and proved fixed-power estimates for individual translated windows and residue cells of $\Delta^\#$ [`\citep{WangTPC31}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-032.md#cite-86fc77251857150f). Absolute reassembly of all cells, however, returns the original shell. Equivalently, the untwisted sum is the zero coefficient of an auxiliary finite Fourier transform.

The purpose of this paper is to determine exactly how much of that Fourier problem follows from the available physical estimates, and exactly what new input is still missing. Three features must remain visible at the same time:

1.  the two mixed cutoff channels and the both-ultra channel have to be matched before absolute values are taken;

2.  the outer Möbius sign on an opened row has to be fused with the reflected divisor sign;

3.  the auxiliary determinant phase may be separated row by row, but the actual pair mask must remain a joint multiplier unless a controlled projective decomposition has been proved.

Keeping these three points produces a rank-two algebraic core and an exact determinant-frequency representation. The resulting mean-square theorem is strong enough on average, and at every frequency outside a power-small exceptional set. What remains is a single distinguished coefficient.

## The unconditional results

Write $$Q=X^{\beta+o(1)},  \qquad  J=X^{1-\beta+o(1)},  \qquad  QJ\asymp X,  \qquad  N_0=JQ^2\asymp XQ.  \label{eq:tpc32-intro-scales}$$ The inherited prefix $\mathsf A_{m,T}$ and ultra increment $C_m$ obey the exact pointwise recombination $$\mathsf A_{m,T}C_n+C_m\mathsf A_{n,T}+C_mC_n  =\mathsf A_{m,U_0}\mathsf A_{n,U_0}   -\mathsf A_{m,T}\mathsf A_{n,T}.  \label{eq:tpc32-intro-shell}$$ Thus the divisor-coefficient matrix is the difference of two rank-one tensors. This low rank is an exact matching statement, not a cancellation estimate: a rank-one tensor may carry the entire coherent mass.

There is also an exact arithmetic factorization. For every $C,q\ge1$ and $r\pmod q$, $$\begin{aligned}  &\mathbf 1_{(N_m,N_n)\le C}  e_q\!\left(-r\frac{m-n}{(N_m,N_n)}\right)  \notag\\  &\qquad=  \sum_{c\le C}\sum_{k\ge1}\mu(k)  \mathbf 1_{ck\mid N_m}\mathbf 1_{ck\mid N_n}  e_{cq}\!\left(-r(m-n)\right).  \label{eq:tpc32-intro-content-factorization}\end{aligned}$$ The sum is finite. Its phase splits as $e_{cq}(-rm)e_{cq}(rn)$, with no assumption that $(c,q)=1$. Together with [\[eq:tpc32-intro-shell\]](TPC-032.md#ref-f9167f6047a98fdc), this gives a factorable row core with the full joint physical multiplier retained.

Let $A_C(n)$ be the signed amplitude of the matched three-channel shell with canonical content at most $C$ and normalized determinant $n$. The TPC-31 singleton-cell estimate and total absolute mass imply, for $C\le J$ and $C\ll_{\mathscr D}Q$, $$\|A_C\|_1\ll X^\varepsilon N_0,  \qquad  \|A_C\|_\infty\ll X^\varepsilon Q(C+J),  \qquad  \|A_C\|_2^2\ll X^\varepsilon\frac{N_0^2}{Q}.  \label{eq:tpc32-intro-norms}$$ A prime modulus larger than the determinant span eliminates residue collisions, so Parseval is an identity for the physical determinant amplitudes, not merely for grouped residue classes. No spectral large sieve or unproved Möbius hypothesis enters these conclusions.

Define $$\lambda_C(t)=\sum_{\substack{c\mid t\\c\le C}}\mu(t/c).  \label{eq:tpc32-intro-projector}$$ Then $$\mathbf 1_{(N_m,N_n)\le C}  =\sum_{t\mid N_m,\ t\mid N_n}\lambda_C(t),  \label{eq:tpc32-intro-projector-identity}$$ while $\lambda_C(1)=1$ and, for $g=(N_m,N_n)$, $$\sum_{\substack{t\mid g\\t>1}}\lambda_C(t)  =-\mathbf 1_{g>C}.  \label{eq:tpc32-intro-projector-reassembly}$$ Thus the unit-divisor term is the untwisted full-content shell, and every nonunit projector term together is exactly minus the large-content correction already treated in TPC-30. The cell decomposition does not center the coefficient needed by the original problem.

To quantify the remaining input, we introduce a zero-frequency flatness exponent $\chi$ through $$|\widehat A_{C,q}(0)|^2  \le X^{\chi+o(1)}\sum_n|A_C(n)|^2.  \label{eq:tpc32-intro-flatness}$$ This is a hypothesis, not a conclusion of the paper. The theorem needed by the remaining hard raw component of the original calibrated residual is the assertion that the special frequency $r=0$ is not in that exceptional set, after the inherited large-content and drift terms are separated.

## Why this is a genuine new gate

The interface obstruction is sharp. A constant signal on one complete residue system has the norm bounds in [\[eq:tpc32-intro-norms\]](TPC-032.md#ref-daa91620d9172287), has zero Fourier transform at every nonzero frequency, and nevertheless has maximal zero coefficient. Likewise, the small-content projector on pairwise coprime targets is the matrix $\mathbf J-I$; it retains the constant-row direction with eigenvalue of order the number of rows. Therefore support length, $\ell^1/\ell^\infty/\ell^2$ control, low rank, and nonzero-frequency cancellation cannot by themselves prove [\[eq:tpc32-intro-one-over-four-hundred\]](TPC-032.md#ref-6375798b5ddcb3d8).

Opening one reflected ultra leg exposes the next arithmetic object. A divisibility condition parametrizes the row variable by one progression, and the two Möbius signs become a correlation of two nonproportional affine forms. Classical one-frequency Möbius estimates do not estimate that correlation, while known logarithmically averaged two-point results do not provide the uniform polynomial-scale physical estimate required here. This identifies a concrete next problem without asserting that an existing theorem closes it.

The conclusions of this paper are therefore deliberately asymmetric. We prove the matched factorization, the almost-all-frequency theorem, the exact zero-frequency transfer, and the sharp no-go models. We do not prove the flatness hypothesis, a full residual estimate, a Hardy–Littlewood main term, positivity of a prime-pair count, the twin-prime conjecture, or a breach of the sieve parity barrier [`\citep{HeathBrown1982Parity}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-032.md#cite-bd33f7374ef1e88f).

# The physical packet and the matched cutoff shell

> **Lemma: Prime–Möbius sign fusion** \[lem:tpc32-sign-fusion\] If $w\mid N_\alpha(j)$ and $b_R(w)\ne0$, then $$\boxed{  \mu(d_\alpha)b_R(w)  =-\mu(d_\alpha w)w_R(w).}  \label{eq:tpc32-sign-fusion}$$

> **Proof** The support of $b_R$ makes $w$ squarefree, while $d_\alpha$ is squarefree by construction. Since $d_\alpha\mid m_\alpha$, target primitivity [\[eq:tpc32-target-divisor-primitivity\]](TPC-032.md#ref-1ce9123290860bb6) gives $(d_\alpha,w)=1$. Hence $\mu(d_\alpha)\mu(w)=\mu(d_\alpha w)$, and [\[eq:tpc32-positive-coefficient-form\]](TPC-032.md#ref-ce4467d4222ddf21) proves the claim.

> **Remark: A fusion which is not available** The Möbius factor introduced later by exact-content inversion may share prime factors with $w$. It therefore cannot be fused with $\mu(w)$ unless an additional coprimality condition has first been proved. No such fusion is used below.

## Three raw channels as one rank–two shell

###### ref-52a98c06c5bf2f39

###### ref-8d4ce09fc6e53c6e

For two rows $m=m_\alpha$ and $n=m_\gamma$, define $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal K^{(1)}_{\alpha,\gamma}(j)  &=\mathsf A_{m,T}(j)C_n(j),  \notag\\  \mathcal K^{(2)}_{\alpha,\gamma}(j)  &=C_m(j)\mathsf A_{n,T}(j),  \label{eq:tpc32-three-raw-kernels}\\  \mathcal K^{(3)}_{\alpha,\gamma}(j)  &=C_m(j)C_n(j).  \notag\end{aligned}$$ Their matched sum is $$\mathcal K^{\mathrm{sh}}_{\alpha,\gamma}(j)  :=\sum_{\nu=1}^3\mathcal K^{(\nu)}_{\alpha,\gamma}(j).  \label{eq:tpc32-matched-shell-definition}$$

###### ref-d0e7139df2839e63

###### ref-b39f44dbe3a5dcbc

###### ref-aa53d164d0f0928f

###### ref-d442ae77242f868f

> **Proposition: Exact cutoff shell** \[prop:tpc32-exact-cutoff-shell\] Pointwise in every physical row pair and orbit point, $$\boxed{  \mathcal K^{\mathrm{sh}}_{\alpha,\gamma}(j)  =\mathsf A_{m,U_0}(j)\mathsf A_{n,U_0}(j)   -\mathsf A_{m,T}(j)\mathsf A_{n,T}(j).}  \label{eq:tpc32-exact-cutoff-shell}$$ Equivalently, after opening both divisor sums, $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal K^{\mathrm{sh}}_{\alpha,\gamma}(j)  ={}&\sum_{u,v\ge1}b_R(u)b_R(v)  \Theta_{T,U_0}(u,v)  \mathbf 1_{u\mid N_\alpha(j)}\mathbf 1_{v\mid N_\gamma(j)},  \label{eq:tpc32-opened-matched-shell}\\  \Theta_{T,U_0}(u,v)  :={}&\mathbf 1_{u\le U_0}\mathbf 1_{v\le U_0}        -\mathbf 1_{u\le T}\mathbf 1_{v\le T}.  \label{eq:tpc32-cutoff-tensor}\end{aligned}$$ In particular $\Theta_{T,U_0}$, viewed as a matrix in $u,v$, has algebraic rank at most two.

> **Proof** Insert [\[eq:tpc32-exact-prefix-increment\]](TPC-032.md#ref-166743e79c06d22d) on both rows and expand the difference of products. This gives the three terms in [\[eq:tpc32-three-raw-kernels\]](TPC-032.md#ref-52a98c06c5bf2f39). Opening the prefixes proves [\[eq:tpc32-opened-matched-shell\]](TPC-032.md#ref-aa53d164d0f0928f). Finally, with $p_Y(u)=\mathbf 1_{u\le Y}$, $$\Theta_{T,U_0}=p_{U_0}\otimes p_{U_0}-p_T\otimes p_T,$$ a difference of two rank-one tensors.

###### ref-e686ec23c7b82965

###### ref-a5bfb3f071848a5b

###### ref-ca2873b5a3aa19b3

###### ref-74a8170ee4b7ee0d

There is a useful symmetric polarization which keeps the same rank bound. Put $$d_{T,U_0}=p_{U_0}-p_T,  \qquad  s_{T,U_0}=\frac{p_{U_0}+p_T}{2}.  \label{eq:tpc32-cutoff-polarization-vectors}$$ Then $$\boxed{  \Theta_{T,U_0}  =d_{T,U_0}\otimes s_{T,U_0}   +s_{T,U_0}\otimes d_{T,U_0}.}  \label{eq:tpc32-cutoff-polarization}$$ At the level of the physical divisor sums, define $$\mathsf H_{m;T,U_0}(j)  :=\frac{\mathsf A_{m,U_0}(j)+\mathsf A_{m,T}(j)}2  =\mathsf A_{m,T}(j)+\frac12C_m(j).  \label{eq:tpc32-half-prefix}$$ Then the exact polarized shell is $$\boxed{  \mathcal K^{\mathrm{sh}}_{\alpha,\gamma}(j)  =C_m(j)\mathsf H_{n;T,U_0}(j)   +\mathsf H_{m;T,U_0}(j)C_n(j).}  \label{eq:tpc32-physical-polarization}$$ Thus three rectangles become two polarized tensors before estimation. This is an algebraic rank statement. It does not make the masked row form positive semidefinite and does not authorize deletion of either polarized term.

## The complete calibrated difference and its drift boundary

###### ref-5cab30789b2a3bc0

###### ref-7d55b521495bc6c3

For $m=\ell d$, let $$\delta_H(m)  =\frac{H}{\varphi(H)}   \frac{d}{\varphi(d)(\ell-1)}  \ll_{\varepsilon,h}\frac{X^\varepsilon}{L},  \label{eq:tpc32-row-drift}$$ and define the calibrated prefix $$P_{m,Y}(j)=\mathsf A_{m,Y}(j)-\delta_H(m).  \label{eq:tpc32-calibrated-prefix}$$

###### ref-7a7c0ee175a727d4

###### ref-0e1c43e122fd69ac

> **Proposition: Exact calibrated cutoff difference** \[prop:tpc32-calibrated-cutoff\] For every physical $m,n,j$, $$\begin{aligned}  &P_{m,U_0}(j)P_{n,U_0}(j)   -P_{m,T}(j)P_{n,T}(j)  \notag\\  &\quad={}  \mathcal K^{\mathrm{sh}}_{\alpha,\gamma}(j)  -\delta_H(m)C_n(j)-\delta_H(n)C_m(j).  \label{eq:tpc32-calibrated-cutoff}\end{aligned}$$ There is no two-drift term: it cancels between the two cutoff levels.

> **Proof** Use $P_{m,U_0}=P_{m,T}+C_m$, which follows from [\[eq:tpc32-exact-prefix-increment\]](TPC-032.md#ref-166743e79c06d22d), and expand. Alternatively, insert [\[eq:tpc32-calibrated-prefix\]](TPC-032.md#ref-7d55b521495bc6c3) into the difference in [\[eq:tpc32-exact-cutoff-shell\]](TPC-032.md#ref-b39f44dbe3a5dcbc); the two copies of $\delta_H(m)\delta_H(n)$ cancel.

Let $\mathcal D^{\mathrm{dr}}(\mathcal E)$ be the contribution of the final two terms in [\[eq:tpc32-calibrated-cutoff\]](TPC-032.md#ref-0e1c43e122fd69ac) to [\[eq:tpc32-physical-functional\]](TPC-032.md#ref-4e1fa64564728745), with any additional event $\mathcal E$. Since an indicator or a unit-modulus determinant phase can only decrease its absolute majorant, the following estimate is uniform over every restriction used later.

###### ref-86d54e497c79183d

###### ref-1d994a117ec0ea0a

> **Proposition: Uniform drift boundary** \[prop:tpc32-drift-boundary\] For every fixed $\varepsilon>0$, $$\boxed{  |\mathcal D^{\mathrm{dr}}(\mathcal E)|  \ll_{\varepsilon,h,\mathscr D}  X^\varepsilon\frac{XQ}{L}.}  \label{eq:tpc32-drift-boundary}$$ Consequently, if $L\ge X^{\lambda_0}$ for fixed $\lambda_0>0$, the drift legs have a fixed-power saving relative to $XQ$, after choosing the soft-loss exponent below $\lambda_0$.

> **Proof** By [\[eq:tpc32-row-l1\]](TPC-032.md#ref-f3cf3b4504dbe2a2) and [\[eq:tpc32-row-drift\]](TPC-032.md#ref-5cab30789b2a3bc0), $$\sum_\alpha|\gamma_\alpha^{(i)}|\delta_H(m_\alpha)  \ll X^\varepsilon Q/L.$$ Use this on the drift row, [\[eq:tpc32-row-l1\]](TPC-032.md#ref-f3cf3b4504dbe2a2) on the other row, [\[eq:tpc32-prefix-envelope\]](TPC-032.md#ref-8e4ebce6d51fabd2) for the ultra increment, and the $O_{\mathscr D}(J)$ orbit length. Each of the two legs is $$\ll X^\varepsilon J(Q/L)Q  \asymp X^\varepsilon XQ/L.$$ Renaming the harmless soft-loss exponent proves the claim.

> **Remark: What is carried into the next sections** The hard raw object is the single matched kernel [\[eq:tpc32-exact-cutoff-shell\]](TPC-032.md#ref-b39f44dbe3a5dcbc), not three unrelated absolute sums. The calibrated packet equals that object plus the rigorously bounded drift contribution [\[eq:tpc32-drift-boundary\]](TPC-032.md#ref-1d994a117ec0ea0a). Every factorization below retains the joint multiplier [\[eq:tpc32-joint-multiplier\]](TPC-032.md#ref-b6454ae92392185f); boundedness alone is not treated as a factorization theorem.

# Canonical-content inversion and the retained bilinear form

We now place the canonical determinant phase inside the exact cutoff shell. The central point is elementary but useful: exact target content can be imposed by a common-divisor Möbius projector, and the normalized determinant character then splits into one character on each row. The fixed residue factor, physical weight, and pair mask are not discarded in this operation.

## Primitive common divisors

###### ref-61cc3369ff254e0b

###### ref-16e5a9f1a236e9cb

For two physical rows $m=m_\alpha$ and $n=m_\gamma$, abbreviate $$N_m=mj+h,\qquad N_n=nj+h,  \qquad G_{m,n}(j)=(N_m,N_n).  \label{eq:tpc32-two-targets-content}$$ When $m\ne n$, the canonical normalized determinant of TPC-31 is $$\Delta^\#_{m,n}(j)=\frac{m-n}{G_{m,n}(j)}.  \label{eq:tpc32-canonical-determinant}$$

###### ref-d69a6e7cf7065a48

###### ref-8fb5727b39b0c6af

###### ref-5956269d93fa543a

###### ref-03655edbeff18c16

> **Lemma: Every common target divisor divides the row difference** \[lem:tpc32-common-divisor-row-difference\] Under [\[eq:tpc32-primitive-target-support\]](TPC-032.md#ref-ea761dbb716217bf), if $$t\mid N_m,\qquad t\mid N_n,  \label{eq:tpc32-common-target-divisor}$$ then $$(t,mnhj)=1,\qquad t\mid m-n.  \label{eq:tpc32-common-divisor-properties}$$ In particular $$G_{m,n}(j)\mid m-n,  \label{eq:tpc32-content-divides-difference}$$ so [\[eq:tpc32-canonical-determinant\]](TPC-032.md#ref-16e5a9f1a236e9cb) is a nonzero integer.

> **Proof** Target primitivity [\[eq:tpc32-target-divisor-primitivity\]](TPC-032.md#ref-1ce9123290860bb6) applied on each row gives $(t,mnH)=1$. If a prime $p$ divided both $t$ and $j$, then $p\mid N_m-mj=h$, hence $p\mid H$, a contradiction. Thus $(t,j)=1$. Finally $$t\mid N_m-N_n=(m-n)j,$$ and $j$ may be cancelled modulo $t$. Taking $t=G_{m,n}(j)$ proves the last assertion. This is the primitive target-gcd geometry used in TPC-30 and TPC-31 [`\citep{WangTPC30,WangTPC31}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-032.md#cite-12cd0ee444491001); [\[WangTPC31\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-032.md#cite-86fc77251857150f).

## Exact-content Möbius projectors

The following formulation is convenient for both cumulative and dyadic content ranges.

###### ref-94bb8a43bac02f68

###### ref-9d6045676ee824de

> **Definition: Content projector** \[def:tpc32-content-projector\] For a finite set $\mathscr C\subset\mathbb N$, define $$\eta_{\mathscr C}(t)  :=\sum_{\substack{c\mid t\\c\in\mathscr C}}  \mu(t/c).  \label{eq:tpc32-content-projector-coefficient}$$

###### ref-f920151fbaa9d76d

###### ref-c653e8b04bb24e5b

###### ref-0fd60f07cf0d8064

> **Proposition: Exact gcd Möbius inversion** \[prop:tpc32-gcd-mobius-inversion\] For all positive integers $N_1,N_2$, with $G=(N_1,N_2)$, $$\boxed{  \mathbf 1_{G\in\mathscr C}  =\sum_{\substack{t\mid N_1\\t\mid N_2}}    \eta_{\mathscr C}(t).}  \label{eq:tpc32-gcd-mobius-inversion}$$ Equivalently, $$\mathbf 1_{G=c}  =\sum_{k\ge1}\mu(k)    \mathbf 1_{ck\mid N_1}\mathbf 1_{ck\mid N_2}  \qquad(c\ge1).  \label{eq:tpc32-exact-content-inversion}$$ All sums in these identities are finite.

> **Proof** Interchanging the divisor sums gives $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_{t\mid G}\eta_{\mathscr C}(t)  &=\sum_{\substack{c\mid G\\c\in\mathscr C}}    \sum_{k\mid G/c}\mu(k).\end{aligned}$$ The inner sum is one exactly when $G/c=1$, and is zero otherwise. This proves [\[eq:tpc32-gcd-mobius-inversion\]](TPC-032.md#ref-c653e8b04bb24e5b). Taking $\mathscr C=\{c\}$ gives [\[eq:tpc32-exact-content-inversion\]](TPC-032.md#ref-0fd60f07cf0d8064).

###### ref-82bba493e749a3f3

> **Remark: Size and sign** The coefficient $\eta_{\mathscr C}$ is signed and need not be multiplicative. The general pointwise estimate $$|\eta_{\mathscr C}(t)|\le\tau(t)  \label{eq:tpc32-projector-trivial-bound}$$ does not by itself give cancellation after summation over $t$. The value of the projector is its exact reassembly, not an individual smallness property of its coefficients.

## Canonical determinant phases

###### ref-410e9cf124453505

Write $$e_q(x)=\exp(2\pi i x/q),\qquad q\ge1.  \label{eq:tpc32-additive-character}$$

###### ref-df29ef29ce717a6a

###### ref-b608d5949f599172

###### ref-7155acae1763e0e4

> **Theorem: Exact canonical phase splitting** \[thm:tpc32-canonical-phase-splitting\] Let $q\ge1$, $r\in\mathbb Z$, let $m\ne n$, and let $\mathscr C\subset\mathbb N$ be finite. Under the hypotheses of [\[lem:tpc32-common-divisor-row-difference\]](TPC-032.md#ref-d69a6e7cf7065a48), $$\begin{aligned}  &\mathbf 1_{G_{m,n}(j)\in\mathscr C}  e_q\!\left(-r\Delta^\#_{m,n}(j)\right)  \notag\\  &\quad={}  \sum_{c\in\mathscr C}\sum_{k\ge1}\mu(k)  \mathbf 1_{ck\mid N_m}\mathbf 1_{ck\mid N_n}  e_{cq}\!\left(-r(m-n)\right).  \label{eq:tpc32-canonical-phase-inversion}\end{aligned}$$ For every summand on the right, $$\boxed{  e_{cq}\!\left(-r(m-n)\right)  =e_{cq}(-rm)e_{cq}(rn).}  \label{eq:tpc32-canonical-phase-split}$$ Neither identity requires $(c,q)=1$.

> **Proof** Fix $c\in\mathscr C$. If $ck$ divides both targets, then [\[lem:tpc32-common-divisor-row-difference\]](TPC-032.md#ref-d69a6e7cf7065a48) gives $c\mid m-n$, so every displayed character is well defined. For the actual content $G=G_{m,n}(j)$, the contribution of this $c$ is zero unless $c\mid G$; when $c\mid G$, it equals $$e_{cq}\!\left(-r(m-n)\right)  \sum_{k\mid G/c}\mu(k).$$ Möbius inversion kills it unless $G=c$. In the surviving case, $$e_{cq}\!\left(-r(m-n)\right)  =e_q\!\left(-r\frac{m-n}{G}\right),$$ which proves [\[eq:tpc32-canonical-phase-inversion\]](TPC-032.md#ref-b608d5949f599172). Equation [\[eq:tpc32-canonical-phase-split\]](TPC-032.md#ref-7155acae1763e0e4) is the additive-character identity obtained by expanding $m-n$. No modular inverse of $c$ has been used, so no coprimality between $c$ and $q$ is present.

> **Remark: Why the denominator is $cq$** It would be incorrect to replace division by $c$ with multiplication by an inverse of $c\pmod q$. The phase is lifted from modulus $q$ to modulus $cq$; this is precisely what makes [\[eq:tpc32-canonical-phase-split\]](TPC-032.md#ref-7155acae1763e0e4) valid even when $(c,q)>1$.

## The complete retained-multiplier factorization

###### ref-e3d050504c93e058

For a finite content family $\mathscr C$, define the matched determinant transform $$\begin{aligned}  \widehat{\mathcal S}^{\mathrm{sh}}_{\mathscr C,q}(r)  :={}&\sum_{\substack{\alpha,\gamma\\m_\alpha\ne m_\gamma}}  \gamma_\alpha^{(1)}\gamma_\gamma^{(2)}  \sum_j\mathfrak A_{\alpha,\gamma}(j)  \mathcal K^{\mathrm{sh}}_{\alpha,\gamma}(j)  \notag\\  &\qquad\times  \mathbf 1_{G_{m_\alpha,m_\gamma}(j)\in\mathscr C}  e_q\!\left(-r\Delta^\#_{m_\alpha,m_\gamma}(j)\right).  \label{eq:tpc32-matched-determinant-transform}\end{aligned}$$ This is the sum of the transforms of all three raw channels, before any triangle inequality.

###### ref-addd03db35ecd12b

###### ref-0db4169f4f5ebaf3

###### ref-a6b60208e5300630

Put $$\epsilon_{U_0}=1,\qquad \epsilon_T=-1.  \label{eq:tpc32-cutoff-signs}$$ For $Y\in\{U_0,T\}$, define the two one-row factors $$\begin{aligned}  X^{(1)}_{\alpha;c,k,Y,r}(j)  :={}&\gamma_\alpha^{(1)}  \mathbf 1_{ck\mid N_\alpha(j)}  \mathsf A_{m_\alpha,Y}(j)  e_{cq}(-rm_\alpha),  \label{eq:tpc32-left-row-factor}\\  X^{(2)}_{\gamma;c,k,Y,r}(j)  :={}&\gamma_\gamma^{(2)}  \mathbf 1_{ck\mid N_\gamma(j)}  \mathsf A_{m_\gamma,Y}(j)  e_{cq}(rm_\gamma).  \label{eq:tpc32-right-row-factor}\end{aligned}$$

###### ref-401683876f3d4226

###### ref-4433fba287989057

> **Theorem: Matched bilinear factorization with joint multiplier** \[thm:tpc32-retained-bilinear-factorization\] Under the physical hypotheses of [2](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-032.md#src-898a7a3f2c3b36f1), for every finite $\mathscr C\subset\mathbb N$, every $q\ge1$, and every $r\in\mathbb Z$, $$\begin{aligned}  \widehat{\mathcal S}^{\mathrm{sh}}_{\mathscr C,q}(r)  ={}&\sum_{c\in\mathscr C}\sum_{k\ge1}\mu(k)  \sum_j\sum_{Y\in\{U_0,T\}}\epsilon_Y  \notag\\  &\times\sum_{\substack{\alpha,\gamma\\m_\alpha\ne m_\gamma}}  \mathfrak A_{\alpha,\gamma}(j)  X^{(1)}_{\alpha;c,k,Y,r}(j)  X^{(2)}_{\gamma;c,k,Y,r}(j).  \label{eq:tpc32-retained-bilinear-factorization}\end{aligned}$$ The sums over $k$ are finite. Formula [\[eq:tpc32-retained-bilinear-factorization\]](TPC-032.md#ref-4433fba287989057) retains the exact rank–two cutoff difference and the complete joint physical multiplier.

> **Proof** Insert the cutoff difference [\[eq:tpc32-exact-cutoff-shell\]](TPC-032.md#ref-b39f44dbe3a5dcbc) into [\[eq:tpc32-matched-determinant-transform\]](TPC-032.md#ref-e3d050504c93e058). Apply [\[thm:tpc32-canonical-phase-splitting\]](TPC-032.md#ref-df29ef29ce717a6a) to the content indicator and phase. The two row characters in [\[eq:tpc32-canonical-phase-split\]](TPC-032.md#ref-7155acae1763e0e4), the two common-divisibility indicators, the two row coefficients, and the two prefixes are exactly the factors in [\[eq:tpc32-left-row-factor\]](TPC-032.md#ref-0db4169f4f5ebaf3)– [\[eq:tpc32-right-row-factor\]](TPC-032.md#ref-a6b60208e5300630). The joint multiplier is never altered. Since $ck$ divides two targets of size $O_{\mathscr D}(X)$, one has $ck\ll_{\mathscr D}X$, proving finiteness.

###### ref-c4d2eb66e152f186

For fixed $c,k,Y,r,j$, let $M_j=(\mathfrak A_{\alpha,\gamma}(j))_{\alpha,\gamma}$. The innermost sum in [\[eq:tpc32-retained-bilinear-factorization\]](TPC-032.md#ref-4433fba287989057) is the bilinear form $$(X^{(1)}_{c,k,Y,r}(j))^{\mathsf T}  M_jX^{(2)}_{c,k,Y,r}(j).  \label{eq:tpc32-matrix-bilinear-form}$$ No complex conjugation is implicit. This notation makes the remaining boundary explicit: entrywise boundedness of $M_j$ does not imply a bounded operator norm or a low-cost projective decomposition.

###### ref-dd8cf8ae03473baf

###### ref-24b58c4458ecb4f3

###### ref-8c9cdfe7e4944e02

> **Corollary: Conditional full product representation** \[cor:tpc32-conditional-projective-factorization\] Suppose, on a particular physical packet, that the retained multiplier has an explicit representation $$\mathfrak A_{\alpha,\gamma}(j)  =\int_\Omega  p_{\omega,j}(\alpha)q_{\omega,j}(\gamma)  \,d\nu(\omega),  \label{eq:tpc32-joint-projective-representation}$$ with absolute convergence sufficient to interchange all finite sums. Then the row-pair sum in [\[eq:tpc32-retained-bilinear-factorization\]](TPC-032.md#ref-4433fba287989057) equals $$\begin{aligned}  \int_\Omega  &\left(\sum_\alpha  p_{\omega,j}(\alpha)  X^{(1)}_{\alpha;c,k,Y,r}(j)\right)  \notag\\  &\times  \left(\sum_\gamma  q_{\omega,j}(\gamma)  X^{(2)}_{\gamma;c,k,Y,r}(j)\right)  \,d\nu(\omega).  \label{eq:tpc32-full-product-representation}\end{aligned}$$

> **Proof** Substitute [\[eq:tpc32-joint-projective-representation\]](TPC-032.md#ref-24b58c4458ecb4f3) and interchange the absolutely convergent integral and sums.

The fixed-residue and physical smooth factors have such bounded-cost representations in the inherited packet [`\citep{WangTPC25}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-032.md#cite-39e8dc14c55a2411). The generic pair mask has not been proved to satisfy one with a sufficiently small projective norm. Thus [\[cor:tpc32-conditional-projective-factorization\]](TPC-032.md#ref-dd8cf8ae03473baf) is an exact conditional interface, not an estimate asserted from [\[eq:tpc32-joint-multiplier-bound\]](TPC-032.md#ref-0a5ebc5df7176b76).

## Dyadic projectors and exact zero-frequency reassembly

###### ref-08897252ee433a08

###### ref-c59e18e7a380d96d

###### ref-24a2ad08c3872fb2

For $C\ge1$, define the cumulative and dyadic coefficients $$\begin{aligned}  \eta_{\le C}(t)  &:=\sum_{\substack{c\mid t\\c\le C}}\mu(t/c),  \label{eq:tpc32-cumulative-projector}\\  \eta_{(C,2C]}(t)  &:=\sum_{\substack{c\mid t\\C<c\le2C}}\mu(t/c).  \label{eq:tpc32-dyadic-projector}\end{aligned}$$ To match the cumulative notation used elsewhere in the paper, we also write $$\lambda_C(t):=\eta_{\le C}(t).  \label{eq:tpc32-lambda-projector-alias}$$

###### ref-f688d4dceda7e1cf

###### ref-840e0a4481007e31

> **Lemma: The first dyadic band** \[lem:tpc32-first-dyadic-projector-band\] For every $C\ge1$, $$\boxed{  \lambda_C(1)=1,\qquad  \lambda_C(t)=0\ (2\le t\le C),\qquad  \lambda_C(t)=-1\ (C<t\le2C).}  \label{eq:tpc32-first-dyadic-projector-band}$$

> **Proof** The first equality is immediate. If $2\le t\le C$, every divisor of $t$ is included in the definition, so $$\lambda_C(t)=\sum_{c\mid t}\mu(t/c)  =\sum_{d\mid t}\mu(d)=0.$$ If $C<t\le2C$, every proper divisor of $t$ is at most $t/2\le C$, whereas $c=t$ is omitted. Therefore $$\lambda_C(t)=\sum_{\substack{c\mid t\\c<t}}\mu(t/c)  =\sum_{\substack{d\mid t\\d>1}}\mu(d)  =-\mu(1)=-1.$$

###### ref-e18720973e1052c4

###### ref-2f8841ff5386ebd3

###### ref-ed078d75baf76ad5

###### ref-d81d4a7c3ab5fd92

> **Corollary: Exact cumulative and dyadic content projectors** \[cor:tpc32-dyadic-content-projector\] For $G=(N_1,N_2)$, $$\begin{aligned}  \mathbf 1_{G\le C}  &=\sum_{t\mid N_1,\,t\mid N_2}\eta_{\le C}(t),  \label{eq:tpc32-cumulative-projector-identity}\\  \mathbf 1_{C<G\le2C}  &=\sum_{t\mid N_1,\,t\mid N_2}\eta_{(C,2C]}(t).  \label{eq:tpc32-dyadic-projector-identity}\end{aligned}$$ Moreover, $$\eta_{(C,2C]}(t)  =\eta_{\le2C}(t)-\eta_{\le C}(t).  \label{eq:tpc32-dyadic-projector-difference}$$ Hence cumulative projectors telescope exactly into dyadic content layers, with an optionally truncated final layer.

> **Proof** Apply [\[prop:tpc32-gcd-mobius-inversion\]](TPC-032.md#ref-f920151fbaa9d76d) to $\mathscr C=[1,C]\cap\mathbb N$ and $\mathscr C=(C,2C]\cap\mathbb N$. The coefficient identity follows directly from the definitions.

###### ref-10edbad84158c9a7

At determinant frequency zero, the exact-content labels may be reassembled into one common-divisor coefficient. Indeed, setting $r=0$ in [\[eq:tpc32-canonical-phase-inversion\]](TPC-032.md#ref-b608d5949f599172) and writing $t=ck$ gives $$\mathbf 1_{G\le C}  =\sum_{t\mid N_1,\,t\mid N_2}\eta_{\le C}(t).$$ The term $t=1$ is distinguished because $$\eta_{\le C}(1)=1.  \label{eq:tpc32-t-one-coefficient}$$

###### ref-f583eb3e4f2fc98a

###### ref-90a7b391e95a1b41

> **Proposition: $t=1$ and large-content reassembly** \[prop:tpc32-t-one-large-reassembly\] For every positive integer $G$ and every $C\ge1$, $$\boxed{  \sum_{\substack{t\mid G\\t>1}}\eta_{\le C}(t)  =-\mathbf 1_{G>C}.}  \label{eq:tpc32-nontrivial-projector-reassembly}$$ Thus the $t=1$ term of the cumulative projector is the unrestricted amplitude, while all $t>1$ terms reassemble exactly to the negative large-content amplitude.

> **Proof** By [\[eq:tpc32-cumulative-projector-identity\]](TPC-032.md#ref-2f8841ff5386ebd3), $\sum_{t\mid G}\eta_{\le C}(t)=\mathbf 1_{G\le C}$. Subtract the $t=1$ value [\[eq:tpc32-t-one-coefficient\]](TPC-032.md#ref-10edbad84158c9a7) and use $\mathbf 1_{G\le C}-1=-\mathbf 1_{G>C}$.

###### ref-426f23182390ad22

###### ref-fed8475312ed4a61

To state the consequence for the physical shell, let $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal S_{\mathrm{full}}^{\mathrm{sh}}  &:={}  \mathcal S[\mathcal K^{\mathrm{sh}};\textup{true}],  \label{eq:tpc32-full-raw-shell}\\  \mathcal S_{>C}^{\mathrm{sh}}  &:={}  \mathcal S[\mathcal K^{\mathrm{sh}};G_{m_\alpha,m_\gamma}(j)>C].  \label{eq:tpc32-large-content-raw-shell}\end{aligned}$$

###### ref-84bb42f4d8f137aa

###### ref-7fb3e03d1606253a

> **Corollary: Exact auxiliary-zero reassembly** \[cor:tpc32-zero-reassembly\] For every auxiliary modulus $q\ge1$, $$\boxed{  \widehat{\mathcal S}^{\mathrm{sh}}_{[1,C],q}(0)  =\mathcal S_{\mathrm{full}}^{\mathrm{sh}}   -\mathcal S_{>C}^{\mathrm{sh}}.}  \label{eq:tpc32-zero-reassembly}$$ More precisely, the $t=1$ term in the common-divisor expansion is $\mathcal S_{\mathrm{full}}^{\mathrm{sh}}$, and the sum of all $t>1$ terms is $-\mathcal S_{>C}^{\mathrm{sh}}$.

> **Proof** At $r=0$, the phase in [\[eq:tpc32-matched-determinant-transform\]](TPC-032.md#ref-e3d050504c93e058) is one. Apply [\[prop:tpc32-t-one-large-reassembly\]](TPC-032.md#ref-f583eb3e4f2fc98a) pointwise to each physical row–orbit triple, then sum with the original signed coefficient and the retained multiplier.

###### ref-306bad20223a8c2c

###### ref-db83c47f936562e1

TPC-30 proves, uniformly for the sum of all three raw channels, $$|\mathcal S_{>C}^{\mathrm{sh}}|  \ll_{\varepsilon,h,\mathscr D}  X^\varepsilon\left(Q^2+\frac{XQ}{C}\right)  \ll_{\mathscr D}X^\varepsilon XQ  \left(\frac1J+\frac1C\right).  \label{eq:tpc32-inherited-large-content-bound}$$ Combining this bound with [\[eq:tpc32-zero-reassembly\]](TPC-032.md#ref-7fb3e03d1606253a) gives $$\left|  \widehat{\mathcal S}^{\mathrm{sh}}_{[1,C],q}(0)  -\mathcal S_{\mathrm{full}}^{\mathrm{sh}}  \right|  \ll X^\varepsilon XQ\left(\frac1J+\frac1C\right).  \label{eq:tpc32-zero-full-equivalence}$$ This is an equivalence up to an already controlled large-content error, not an estimate for the unrestricted shell.

###### ref-3283c064d49f2593

###### ref-a28ac819d5402ab5

###### ref-7241fe35111dcdfc

For the complete calibrated cutoff difference, the drift estimate [\[eq:tpc32-drift-boundary\]](TPC-032.md#ref-1d994a117ec0ea0a) yields the corresponding boundary $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal K^{\mathrm{cal}}_{\alpha,\gamma}(j)  &:=  P_{m_\alpha,U_0}(j)P_{m_\gamma,U_0}(j)  -P_{m_\alpha,T}(j)P_{m_\gamma,T}(j),  \label{eq:tpc32-calibrated-shell-kernel}\\  \mathcal S_{\mathrm{full}}^{\mathrm{cal}}  &:=  \mathcal S[\mathcal K^{\mathrm{cal}};\textup{true}],  \notag\\  \widehat{\mathcal S}^{\mathrm{cal}}_{[1,C],q}(0)  &:=  \mathcal S[\mathcal K^{\mathrm{cal}};G_{m_\alpha,m_\gamma}(j)\le C].  \label{eq:tpc32-calibrated-zero-definition}\end{aligned}$$ The notation retains $q$ to emphasize that this is the zero coefficient of the same auxiliary residue-label transform; its value is independent of $q$. One then has $$\boxed{  \left|  \widehat{\mathcal S}^{\mathrm{cal}}_{[1,C],q}(0)  -\mathcal S_{\mathrm{full}}^{\mathrm{cal}}  \right|  \ll_{\varepsilon,h,\mathscr D}  X^\varepsilon XQ  \left(\frac1J+\frac1C+\frac1L\right).}  \label{eq:tpc32-calibrated-zero-full-equivalence}$$ Here both calibrated quantities are defined using the two-prefix difference in [\[eq:tpc32-calibrated-cutoff\]](TPC-032.md#ref-0e1c43e122fd69ac); the $L^{-1}$ term accounts for the two drift legs uniformly under either the small- or large-content restriction.

> **Remark: The zero-frequency gate is not removed** Equation [\[eq:tpc32-zero-full-equivalence\]](TPC-032.md#ref-db83c47f936562e1) shows that controlling the auxiliary residue-label zero coefficient is, within the inherited large-content margin, the same problem as controlling the unrestricted matched shell. The exact Möbius projector exposes this fact but does not make the $t=1$ contribution oscillatory. Bounds for nonzero determinant frequencies, or termwise absolute bounds for $\eta_{\le C}(t)$, do not imply the missing estimate.

# Prime–Möbius leg fusion and the joint-mask boundary

The factorization of the cutoff shell becomes useful only if the actual opened-row coefficient is retained. We now restore that provenance and separate what is algebraically exact from what would require a new norm estimate.

## The physical row coefficient

###### ref-6a9ce2fe163d49ae

###### ref-dbe105e95300a5b8

###### ref-07555fe1864c3067

For $i\in\{1,2\}$, a row is $\alpha=(\ell,d)$ with $$\ell\asymp L\ \text{prime},  \qquad  d\asymp D\ \text{squarefree},  \qquad  (d,H)=1,  \qquad  m_\alpha=\ell d\asymp Q,  \label{eq:tpc32-row-provenance-fusion}$$ and has coefficient $$\gamma_\alpha^{(i)}  =\mu(d)(\log\ell)\omega_D^{(i)}(d)    \psi_L^{(i)}(\ell/L)\zeta_\alpha^{(i)}.  \label{eq:tpc32-gamma}$$ Here the $\omega_D^{(i)}$ and $\psi_L^{(i)}$ range over fixed bounded smooth families and $\zeta_\alpha^{(i)}$ contains fixed-period characters or phases. The inherited estimates are $$\sum_\alpha|\gamma_\alpha^{(i)}|\ll Q,  \qquad  \sum_\alpha|\gamma_\alpha^{(i)}|^2\ll Q\log(2L).  \label{eq:tpc32-row-masses}$$ These bounds use Chebyshev’s estimate for the prime source and are stronger than replacing every logarithm by $X^\varepsilon$ [`\citep{WangTPC25}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-032.md#cite-39e8dc14c55a2411).

###### ref-81dc769db6f3dfda

###### ref-e31e0abb858a94c9

###### ref-16edda71b049c867

Recall the squarefree divisor coefficient $$b_R(w)=-\mu(w)w_R(w),  \label{eq:tpc32-bR-squarefree}$$ where $$w_R(w)=\log w+\mathbf 1_{w\le R}\frac{w}{\varphi(w)}  \sum_{\substack{a\le R/w\\(a,w)=1}}  \frac{\mu(a)^2}{\varphi(a)}  \label{eq:tpc32-wR-fusion}$$ on squarefree $w$; both sides of [\[eq:tpc32-bR-squarefree\]](TPC-032.md#ref-81dc769db6f3dfda) vanish off the inherited squarefree support. For $w>T\ge R$ this reduces to $$b_R(w)=a(w)=-\mu(w)\log w.  \label{eq:tpc32-ultra-a}$$

###### ref-6370aa441c093efc

###### ref-24efedc9bc4f38fd

###### ref-c6c41c7c3ec663c6

> **Lemma: Primitive one-leg sign fusion** \[lem:tpc32-leg-fusion\] Suppose that $w\mid m_\alpha j+h$ on the primitive physical support. Then $(w,d)=1$, and $$\boxed{  \gamma_\alpha^{(i)}b_R(w)  =-\mu(dw)(\log\ell)\omega_D^{(i)}(d)    \psi_L^{(i)}(\ell/L)\zeta_\alpha^{(i)}w_R(w).}  \label{eq:tpc32-leg-fusion}$$ In the ultra range $w>T$, $$\mu(d)a(w)=-\mu(dw)\log w.  \label{eq:tpc32-ultra-leg-fusion}$$

> **Proof** If a prime $p$ divided both $w$ and $d$, then it would divide $m_\alpha=\ell d$ and $m_\alpha j+h$, hence $p\mid h$. This contradicts $(d,H)=1$, because $H=\operatorname{rad}|h|$. Thus $(w,d)=1$. The divisor coefficient is squarefree-supported, and $d$ is squarefree, so multiplicativity gives $\mu(d)\mu(w)=\mu(dw)$. Substitute [\[eq:tpc32-bR-squarefree\]](TPC-032.md#ref-81dc769db6f3dfda) into [\[eq:tpc32-gamma\]](TPC-032.md#ref-dbe105e95300a5b8); [\[eq:tpc32-ultra-leg-fusion\]](TPC-032.md#ref-c6c41c7c3ec663c6) follows from [\[eq:tpc32-ultra-a\]](TPC-032.md#ref-16edda71b049c867).

###### ref-8313305d15df8730

One may consequently write a cutoff row signal schematically as $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal T_W^{(i)}(j)  ={}&-\sum_{\ell,d}\sum_{\substack{w\le W\\w\mid\ell dj+h}}  \mu(dw)(\log\ell)w_R(w)  \omega_D^{(i)}(d)\psi_L^{(i)}(\ell/L)  \zeta_{\ell,d}^{(i)}\Phi_{\ell,d}^{(i)}(j)  \notag\\  &\quad+\text{the calibrated drift},  \label{eq:tpc32-one-row-signal}\end{aligned}$$ where $\Phi$ retains the actual fixed-residue and smooth physical factors. Changing variables to $n=dw$ makes the outer sign a single $\mu(n)$, but the inner coefficient still contains the factorization $n=dw$, the prime source $\ell$, and the congruence $w\mid\ell dj+h$. Thus [\[eq:tpc32-one-row-signal\]](TPC-032.md#ref-8313305d15df8730) is a prime–Möbius normal form, not a claim that the resulting coefficient is well-factorable in the technical sieve-theoretic sense.

## What the smooth and residue factors permit

Fixed-period masks have a finite Fourier expansion of bounded total mass. The smooth factors have an absolutely convergent Mellin projective representation; after the actual row coefficients have been included, its weighted projective $\ell^1$ mass is $O(Q^2)$ and its energy mass is $O(Q\log(2L))$ [`\citep{WangTPC25}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-032.md#cite-39e8dc14c55a2411). Consequently these specific factors may be separated and reassembled without introducing an $X^\varepsilon$ projective loss.

###### ref-d6bbf3b880243139

###### ref-713580f24da7ef4e

The generic row-pair mask is different. Write the full joint multiplier as $$\mathfrak A_{\alpha,\gamma}(j)  =\mathfrak m(\alpha,\gamma)    \Xi_{\alpha,\gamma}(j)    \mathcal W_{\alpha,\gamma}(j/J).  \label{eq:tpc32-joint-multiplier-boundary}$$ The inherited abstract interface assumes only $$|\mathfrak m(\alpha,\gamma)|\le1,  \qquad  \mathfrak m(\alpha,\gamma)=0  \quad\text{when }m_\alpha=m_\gamma.  \label{eq:tpc32-mask-bound}$$ Entrywise multiplication by such a matrix need not preserve positivity, rank, or spectral norm. Accordingly, the exact formula in [3](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-032.md#src-aa0ead658992fa89) is a factorable row core with [\[eq:tpc32-joint-multiplier\]](TPC-032.md#ref-b6454ae92392185f) retained; it is not an unqualified product of two row sums.

###### ref-8054a2141fa4bd5f

###### ref-961c819a9af3ade4

> **Definition: Controlled projective multiplier** \[def:tpc32-projective-multiplier\] We say that $\mathfrak A$ has projective cost $\mathfrak P$ on a fixed physical packet if it admits a representation $$\mathfrak A_{\alpha,\gamma}(j)  =\int_\Omega p_{\omega,j}(\alpha)q_{\omega,j}(\gamma)  \,d\nu(\omega)  \label{eq:tpc32-projective-decomposition}$$ whose required row norms and orbit seminorms integrate to at most $\mathfrak P$.

Under [\[eq:tpc32-projective-decomposition\]](TPC-032.md#ref-961c819a9af3ade4), each bilinear core may be written as an integral of products of row sums and any estimate uniform in the displayed seminorms may be reassembled with cost $\mathfrak P$. Finite-dimensional singular-value decomposition alone does not give the needed theorem: its projective norm may be as large as the obstacle scale. No controlled decomposition for the generic pair mask is asserted here.

> **Remark: Two different factorization statements** The cutoff coefficient has algebraic rank at most two in the divisor variables. The pair mask is a Schur multiplier in the row variables. These are independent statements. Low divisor rank does not remove an arbitrary joint row multiplier, and bounded entries do not imply bounded Schur or projective norm.

# Determinant coefficients and unconditional frequency dispersion

###### ref-ed120dc7492d4139

We now retain the three cutoff-shell channels in their matched signed combination. Write $$\mathcal S_{\rm sh}(\mathcal E)  =\sum_{\alpha,\gamma,j}    \mathfrak a^{\rm sh}_{\alpha,\gamma,j}    \mathbf 1_{\mathcal E(\alpha,\gamma,j)},  \label{eq:tpc32-matched-physical-sum}$$ where $\mathfrak a^{\rm sh}_{\alpha,\gamma,j}$ is the complete signed physical summand: it contains both opened-row coefficients, the actual row-pair mask, the fixed-period and smooth factors, and the matched kernel $$\mathsf A_{m_\alpha,U_0}(j)\mathsf A_{m_\gamma,U_0}(j)  -\mathsf A_{m_\alpha,T}(j)\mathsf A_{m_\gamma,T}(j).$$ Thus no triangle inequality separates the two mixed channels from the both-ultra channel in the definition below.

###### ref-b7e032e15614743c

###### ref-0985793cafe1812f

Put $$c_{\alpha,\gamma}(j)  =(m_\alpha j+h,m_\gamma j+h),  \qquad  \Delta^\#_{\alpha,\gamma}(j)  =\frac{m_\alpha-m_\gamma}{c_{\alpha,\gamma}(j)}.  \label{eq:tpc32-dispersion-content-determinant}$$ For $1\le C\ll_{\mathscr D}Q$, define the signed normalized-determinant coefficient $$A_C(n)  :=\mathcal S_{\rm sh}  \bigl(c_{\alpha,\gamma}(j)\le C,        \ \Delta^\#_{\alpha,\gamma}(j)=n\bigr),  \qquad n\in\mathbb Z.  \label{eq:tpc32-determinant-coefficient}$$ The off-diagonal row mask makes $A_C(0)=0$. To avoid confusing this coefficient value with a Fourier frequency, we shall always write $\widehat A_{C,q}(0)$ for the latter.

###### ref-b7ed8fd60be7d1db

The physical row interval has length $O_{\mathscr D}(Q)$. Fix a constant $B_{\mathscr D}\ge1$ such that $$\operatorname{supp}A_C  \subset  [-B_{\mathscr D}Q,B_{\mathscr D}Q]\cap  (\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\})  \label{eq:tpc32-determinant-support-interval}$$ for every $C$. Indeed, $|\Delta^\#|=|m_\alpha-m_\gamma|/c\le B_{\mathscr D}Q$. In particular the diameter of this support, rather than merely its cardinality, is $O_{\mathscr D}(Q)$; this is the quantity entering the additive large sieve below.

## From the inherited $\ell^1$ and singleton bounds to energy

###### ref-7a415a72984b6b6b

###### ref-0135e186db571f87

Let $A_C^+(n)$ be the absolute majorant formed by summing the absolute values of the complete matched physical summands in [\[eq:tpc32-determinant-coefficient\]](TPC-032.md#ref-0985793cafe1812f). The physical absolute-mass interface and the singleton version of the determinant-support theorem of TPC-31 give, for every fixed $\varepsilon>0$, $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_n A_C^+(n)  &\ll_{\varepsilon,h,\mathscr D}X^\varepsilon N_0,  \qquad N_0:=JQ^2\asymp XQ,  \label{eq:tpc32-inherited-det-l1}\\  \max_n A_C^+(n)  &\ll_{\varepsilon,h,\mathscr D}  X^\varepsilon Q\log(2L)  \{C+J\log(2C)\}.  \label{eq:tpc32-inherited-det-linfty}\end{aligned}$$ For clarity, the second estimate follows by taking the allowed determinant set at each exact content to be the singleton $\{n\}$: its physical support count is at most one, and $$\sum_{c\le C}\left(1+\frac Jc\right)  \ll C+J\log(2C).$$ Both estimates remain valid for the matched sum because the absolute majorant of the sum of its three expanded channels is bounded by the sum of their TPC-31 majorants [`\citep{WangTPC31}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-032.md#cite-86fc77251857150f).

###### ref-b3364a85349c09fb

###### ref-b0a93c45561fc3c5

###### ref-2ad3aeba73b9954f

###### ref-41155fbfcc6fe379

###### ref-a6c71d0befdc634b

> **Proposition: Determinant coefficient norms** \[prop:tpc32-determinant-norms\] For every $1\le C\ll_{\mathscr D}Q$, $$\begin{aligned}  \|A_C\|_1  &\ll_{\varepsilon,h,\mathscr D}X^\varepsilon N_0,  \label{eq:tpc32-det-l1}\\  \|A_C\|_\infty  &\ll_{\varepsilon,h,\mathscr D}X^\varepsilon Q(C+J),  \label{eq:tpc32-det-linfty}\\  \|A_C\|_2^2  &\ll_{\varepsilon,h,\mathscr D}  X^\varepsilon JQ^3(C+J).  \label{eq:tpc32-det-l2-general}\end{aligned}$$ If $C\le J$, then $$\boxed{  \|A_C\|_2^2  \ll_{\varepsilon,h,\mathscr D}  X^\varepsilon\frac{N_0^2}{Q}.}  \label{eq:tpc32-det-l2-C-below-J}$$

> **Proof** Since $|A_C(n)|\le A_C^+(n)$, [\[eq:tpc32-inherited-det-l1\]](TPC-032.md#ref-7a415a72984b6b6b) gives [\[eq:tpc32-det-l1\]](TPC-032.md#ref-b0a93c45561fc3c5). Equation [\[eq:tpc32-inherited-det-linfty\]](TPC-032.md#ref-0135e186db571f87) gives [\[eq:tpc32-det-linfty\]](TPC-032.md#ref-2ad3aeba73b9954f) after enlarging the soft exponent to absorb $\log(2L)\log(2C)$. Apply both inherited estimates with $\varepsilon/2$ and use $$\sum_n|A_C(n)|^2  \le\sum_n A_C^+(n)^2  \le \bigl(\max_nA_C^+(n)\bigr)        \sum_nA_C^+(n).$$ This proves [\[eq:tpc32-det-l2-general\]](TPC-032.md#ref-41155fbfcc6fe379), after relabelling the soft exponent. If $C\le J$, then $$JQ^3(C+J)\ll J^2Q^3  =\frac{(JQ^2)^2}{Q}=\frac{N_0^2}{Q},$$ which proves the last assertion.

The argument is deliberately sign-blind only at the final norm bound. The coefficient $A_C$ itself was formed after the three physical channels were matched. Thus every future improvement of [\[eq:tpc32-det-l2-C-below-J\]](TPC-032.md#ref-a6c71d0befdc634b) coming from their signed interaction can be inserted without changing the Fourier statements below.

## A no-wrap determinant Fourier transform

###### ref-118930b6da8229b4

###### ref-ad9efed695bf35cd

Choose an integer $$Y>2B_{\mathscr D}Q+2.$$ Bertrand’s postulate supplies, for all sufficiently large $Q$, a prime $q$ with $Y<q<2Y$. We fix $Y$ to be the least integer above the displayed threshold. Consequently $$q\asymp_{\mathscr D}Q,  \qquad  q>\operatorname{diam}(\operatorname{supp}A_C).  \label{eq:tpc32-no-wrap-prime}$$ Define $$\widehat A_{C,q}(r)  :=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}A_C(n)e_q(-rn),  \qquad  e_q(x)=\exp(2\pi ix/q),  \qquad r\pmod q.  \label{eq:tpc32-no-wrap-DFT}$$

###### ref-d2d3c9a9f9fe613c

###### ref-0a766baba64e2680

###### ref-c7747ffda4fc4bfd

> **Theorem: No-wrap Parseval and exceptional frequencies** \[thm:tpc32-no-wrap-parseval\] Let $q$ satisfy [\[eq:tpc32-no-wrap-prime\]](TPC-032.md#ref-118930b6da8229b4). Then $$\boxed{  \frac1q\sum_{r\, (q)}|\widehat A_{C,q}(r)|^2  =\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}|A_C(n)|^2.}  \label{eq:tpc32-no-wrap-parseval}$$ If $C\le J$ and $C\ll_{\mathscr D}Q$, then, for every $\sigma>0$, $$\boxed{  \#\left\{r\pmod q:  |\widehat A_{C,q}(r)|>N_0X^{-\sigma}\right\}  \ll_{\varepsilon,h,\mathscr D}X^{2\sigma+\varepsilon}.}  \label{eq:tpc32-no-wrap-exceptional-count}$$ The exceptional set in [\[eq:tpc32-no-wrap-exceptional-count\]](TPC-032.md#ref-c7747ffda4fc4bfd) is allowed to contain $r=0$.

> **Proof** Ordinary finite Fourier Parseval gives $$\frac1q\sum_{r\, (q)}|\widehat A_{C,q}(r)|^2  =\sum_{a\, (q)}    \left|\sum_{n\equiv a\, (q)}A_C(n)\right|^2.$$ The diameter condition in [\[eq:tpc32-no-wrap-prime\]](TPC-032.md#ref-118930b6da8229b4) makes reduction modulo $q$ injective on $\operatorname{supp}A_C$, so the last expression is exactly $\sum_n|A_C(n)|^2$. If $E$ frequencies exceed the threshold in [\[eq:tpc32-no-wrap-exceptional-count\]](TPC-032.md#ref-c7747ffda4fc4bfd), then $$E N_0^2X^{-2\sigma}  \le\sum_{r\, (q)}|\widehat A_{C,q}(r)|^2  =q\|A_C\|_2^2  \ll X^\varepsilon\frac qQ N_0^2.$$ Now $q\asymp_{\mathscr D}Q$, and the result follows.

The no-wrap choice is useful but not cosmetic. If one merely takes an arbitrary $q\asymp Q$, determinants separated by a multiple of $q$ occupy the same residue cell, and the right side of Parseval is the energy of residue-cell sums rather than the coefficient energy itself.

## Farey-family dispersion

###### ref-22e5e9c3ea590b0b

For a reduced rational frequency $a/v$, put $$\widehat A_C(a/v)  :=\sum_n A_C(n)e_v(-an).  \label{eq:tpc32-rational-det-transform}$$ The classical additive large sieve applies to an arbitrary complex coefficient sequence; no factorability hypothesis is needed here [`\citep{IwaniecKowalski2004}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-032.md#cite-49dfea87eaada156).

###### ref-a84802515531eddc

###### ref-fcbf5533a396cfd1

###### ref-9ef86ea876aac3a4

###### ref-925c400a466f6838

> **Theorem: Additive large sieve for determinant frequencies** \[thm:tpc32-farey-large-sieve\] For $V\ge2$, $C\le J$, and $C\ll_{\mathscr D}Q$, $$\boxed{  \sum_{2\le v\le V}\ \sum_{a\, (v)}^{*}  |\widehat A_C(a/v)|^2  \ll_{\varepsilon,h,\mathscr D}  X^\varepsilon(Q+V^2)\frac{N_0^2}{Q}.}  \label{eq:tpc32-farey-large-sieve}$$ Consequently $$\begin{aligned}  &\#\left\{(a,v):2\le v\le V, (a,v)=1,  |\widehat A_C(a/v)|>N_0X^{-\sigma}\right\}  \notag\\  &\hspace{8em}\ll_{\varepsilon,h,\mathscr D}  X^{2\sigma+\varepsilon}\left(1+\frac{V^2}{Q}\right).  \label{eq:tpc32-farey-exceptional-count}\end{aligned}$$ If $Q=X^{\beta+o(1)}$ and $V=X^{\rho+o(1)}$ with $\rho>0$, the exceptional proportion among the $\asymp V^2$ reduced fractions is power-small for every fixed $$\boxed{\sigma<\min\{\rho,\beta/2\}.}  \label{eq:tpc32-farey-saving-range}$$

> **Proof** Translate the integer support interval in [\[eq:tpc32-determinant-support-interval\]](TPC-032.md#ref-b7ed8fd60be7d1db) into an interval of positive integers. Translation changes each transform by a unit-modulus factor. The interval has length at most $2B_{\mathscr D}Q+1$, so the additive large sieve gives $$\sum_{2\le v\le V}\sum_{a\, (v)}^*  |\widehat A_C(a/v)|^2  \ll_{\mathscr D}(Q+V^2)\|A_C\|_2^2.$$ Insert [\[eq:tpc32-det-l2-C-below-J\]](TPC-032.md#ref-a6c71d0befdc634b). Chebyshev’s inequality gives [\[eq:tpc32-farey-exceptional-count\]](TPC-032.md#ref-9ef86ea876aac3a4).
>
> The number of reduced fractions with denominator at most $V$ is $\asymp V^2$. If $2\rho\le\beta$, division of [\[eq:tpc32-farey-exceptional-count\]](TPC-032.md#ref-9ef86ea876aac3a4) by $V^2$ gives the exponent $2\sigma-2\rho+o(1)$. If $2\rho\ge\beta$, it gives $2\sigma-\beta+o(1)$. Both are negative precisely in the strict range [\[eq:tpc32-farey-saving-range\]](TPC-032.md#ref-925c400a466f6838).

> **Remark: What the large sieve omits** The sum in [\[eq:tpc32-farey-large-sieve\]](TPC-032.md#ref-fcbf5533a396cfd1) begins at $v=2$, so all of its reduced frequencies are nonzero. One may add the single point $0/1$ to the large-sieve family, but the inequality then permits that one value to be as large as the natural scale $N_0$. A density-one conclusion for rational frequencies therefore says nothing about the distinguished coefficient $\widehat A_{C,q}(0)$ required by the untwisted physical reassembly. The next section isolates exactly the extra input needed there.

# The distinguished zero frequency and a conditional transfer theorem

###### ref-fc70fce4566fa397

The notation in this section is connected to the canonical factorization by $$\widehat A_{C,q}(r)  =\widehat{\mathcal S}^{\mathrm{sh}}_{[1,C],q}(r),  \qquad  \mathcal S_{\rm sh}^{\rm all}=\mathcal S_{\mathrm{full}}^{\mathrm{sh}},  \qquad  \mathcal S_{\rm sh}(c>C)=\mathcal S_{>C}^{\mathrm{sh}}.  \label{eq:tpc32-zero-notation-crosswalk}$$ Here $r=0$ is the finite Fourier frequency of the normalized determinant. It is neither the orbit-variable Poisson zero mode nor the centered divisor-kernel zero mode used in earlier parts of the program.

###### ref-b5af876ad9be575f

The frequency $r=0$ in [\[eq:tpc32-no-wrap-DFT\]](TPC-032.md#ref-ad9efed695bf35cd) is independent of the auxiliary modulus: $$\widehat A_{C,q}(0)  =\sum_nA_C(n)  =\mathcal S_{\rm sh}(c_{\alpha,\gamma}(j)\le C).  \label{eq:tpc32-zero-is-small-content}$$ It is therefore the untwisted small-content shell, not a typical oscillatory determinant twist. This section first records its exact relation to the complete raw shell and then states a quantitative additional hypothesis under which the TPC-30 large-content estimate closes the splice.

## Equivalence to the full shell modulo large content

###### ref-f9a5159dca513239

###### ref-cb728eaee889f419

###### ref-072f2fc1c4cfdfe2

Let $$\mathcal S_{\rm sh}^{\rm all}:=\mathcal S_{\rm sh}(\text{all target contents}).  \label{eq:tpc32-full-matched-shell}$$ Since exact contents partition the physical triples, $$\mathcal S_{\rm sh}^{\rm all}  =\widehat A_{C,q}(0)   +\mathcal S_{\rm sh}(c_{\alpha,\gamma}(j)>C).  \label{eq:tpc32-zero-full-partition}$$ TPC-30 proves, separately for the two mixed channels and the both-ultra channel, the large-content estimate $$|\mathcal S_{\rm sh}(c>C)|  \ll_{\varepsilon,h,\mathscr D}  X^\varepsilon\left(Q^2+\frac{XQ}{C}\right)  \ll_{\mathscr D}  X^\varepsilon N_0\left(\frac1J+\frac1C\right),  \label{eq:tpc32-large-content-input}$$ where the matched version follows by adding those three bounds [`\citep{WangTPC30,WangTPC31}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-032.md#cite-12cd0ee444491001); [\[WangTPC31\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-032.md#cite-86fc77251857150f).

###### ref-5884808b1e352fc8

###### ref-847d6abbcb3f629d

> **Proposition: Zero coefficient and full-shell equivalence** \[prop:tpc32-zero-full-equivalence\] For every $1\le C\ll_{\mathscr D}Q$ in the range of the inherited large-content theorem, $$\boxed{  |\mathcal S_{\rm sh}^{\rm all}-\widehat A_{C,q}(0)|  \ll_{\varepsilon,h,\mathscr D}  X^\varepsilon N_0\left(\frac1J+\frac1C\right).}  \label{eq:tpc32-zero-full-splice-bound}$$ Consequently, an estimate for the zero coefficient transfers to the complete matched raw shell with precisely the two displayed losses, and an estimate for the complete raw shell transfers back to the zero coefficient with the same losses.

> **Proof** Subtract [\[eq:tpc32-zero-is-small-content\]](TPC-032.md#ref-b5af876ad9be575f) from [\[eq:tpc32-zero-full-partition\]](TPC-032.md#ref-cb728eaee889f419) and apply [\[eq:tpc32-large-content-input\]](TPC-032.md#ref-072f2fc1c4cfdfe2). The reverse transfer is the same inequality with the two terms interchanged.

The content-projector factorization gives the coefficient-level version of this elementary partition. With $\lambda_C$ defined in [\[eq:tpc32-lambda-projector-alias\]](TPC-032.md#ref-24a2ad08c3872fb2), [\[prop:tpc32-t-one-large-reassembly\]](TPC-032.md#ref-f583eb3e4f2fc98a) states exactly that $$\lambda_C(1)=1,\qquad  \sum_{\substack{t\mid g\\t>1}}\lambda_C(t)=-\mathbf 1_{g>C}.$$ Thus the principal $t=1$ term is the untwisted full-content shell, while all nonprincipal projector terms reassemble exactly to minus the large-content shell. There is no unrecorded cancellation obtained by expanding the small-content indicator.

## Zero-frequency flatness

The no-wrap Parseval identity identifies $\|A_C\|_2$ as the root-mean-square size of its determinant Fourier coefficients. The missing statement is that the distinguished zero coefficient is not much larger than this rms scale.

###### ref-f1cbefb3bace0af2

###### ref-8d8be263baeb3c7a

###### ref-eb5b3bfbb912a071

> **Definition: Zero-frequency flatness exponent** \[def:tpc32-zero-flatness\] Suppose $A_C$ is not identically zero. Its zero-frequency concentration ratio is $$\mathfrak F_0(A_C)  :=\frac{|\sum_nA_C(n)|^2}{\sum_n|A_C(n)|^2}  =\frac{|\widehat A_{C,q}(0)|^2}{\|A_C\|_2^2}.  \label{eq:tpc32-zero-concentration-ratio}$$ We say that the packet has flatness exponent at most $\chi$ if $$\boxed{  \mathfrak F_0(A_C)\le X^{\chi+o(1)}.}  \label{eq:tpc32-flatness-hypothesis}$$ If $A_C\equiv0$, we set $\mathfrak F_0(A_C)=0$.

Condition [\[eq:tpc32-flatness-hypothesis\]](TPC-032.md#ref-eb5b3bfbb912a071) is an additional arithmetic input. It is not proved by Parseval, the additive large sieve, the matched rank-two identity, or any theorem in this paper. The notation “flatness” describes only the relative size of the distinguished Fourier coefficient; it is not a probabilistic assumption.

###### ref-6bb52734223206c5

###### ref-6f9b11815aa11825

###### ref-f835480e9b17ddf9

###### ref-c73f015ebc86a364

###### ref-85cffc2cf75124ec

###### ref-b93bebf86a0fb621

###### ref-c0766196e09dcc3e

> **Theorem: Conditional zero-frequency transfer** \[thm:tpc32-conditional-zero-transfer\] Assume $$Q=X^{\beta+o(1)},  \qquad  J=X^{1-\beta+o(1)},  \qquad 0<\beta<1,  \label{eq:tpc32-zero-transfer-scales}$$ and choose $$C=X^{\kappa+o(1)}\le J,  \qquad C\ll_{\mathscr D}Q  \label{eq:tpc32-zero-transfer-content-scale}$$ for a fixed $\kappa\ge0$. If the actual matched coefficient $A_C$ satisfies [\[eq:tpc32-flatness-hypothesis\]](TPC-032.md#ref-eb5b3bfbb912a071) for a fixed $\chi$, then $$\begin{aligned}  |\widehat A_{C,q}(0)|  &\ll X^{o(1)}N_0X^{-(\beta-\chi)/2},  \label{eq:tpc32-conditional-zero-bound}\\  |\mathcal S_{\rm sh}^{\rm all}|  &\ll X^{o(1)}N_0  \left\{  X^{-(\beta-\chi)/2}  +X^{-(1-\beta)}  +X^{-\kappa}  \right\}.  \label{eq:tpc32-conditional-full-shell-bound}\end{aligned}$$ In particular, if $$\chi<\beta,  \qquad \kappa>0,  \label{eq:tpc32-positive-conditional-margins}$$ then every fixed $$\boxed{  \sigma<  \min\left\{  1-\beta,\ \kappa,\ \frac{\beta-\chi}{2}  \right\}}  \label{eq:tpc32-conditional-saving-range}$$ is a power-saving exponent relative to $N_0\asymp XQ$ for the complete matched raw shell.

> **Proof** By the flatness hypothesis and [\[prop:tpc32-determinant-norms\]](TPC-032.md#ref-b3364a85349c09fb), $$|\widehat A_{C,q}(0)|  \le X^{\chi/2+o(1)}\|A_C\|_2  \ll X^{o(1)}N_0X^{-\beta/2+\chi/2},$$ which is [\[eq:tpc32-conditional-zero-bound\]](TPC-032.md#ref-c73f015ebc86a364). Insert this estimate and the scale relations $J^{-1}=X^{-(1-\beta)+o(1)}$ and $C^{-1}=X^{-\kappa+o(1)}$ into [\[eq:tpc32-zero-full-splice-bound\]](TPC-032.md#ref-847d6abbcb3f629d). This proves [\[eq:tpc32-conditional-full-shell-bound\]](TPC-032.md#ref-85cffc2cf75124ec). The strict range [\[eq:tpc32-conditional-saving-range\]](TPC-032.md#ref-c0766196e09dcc3e) absorbs all $o(1)$ and soft losses.

###### ref-388e9dc36f4a5604

> **Remark: Conditional means conditional** \[rem:tpc32-flatness-not-proved\] The conclusion of [\[thm:tpc32-conditional-zero-transfer\]](TPC-032.md#ref-6bb52734223206c5) is a rigorous implication, but it is not an unconditional estimate for the physical shell. In particular, the fact that all but a power-small proportion of the nonzero frequencies obey the desired bound does not verify [\[eq:tpc32-flatness-hypothesis\]](TPC-032.md#ref-eb5b3bfbb912a071) at $r=0$.

# The high-$\beta$ ledger and the $1/400$ gate

###### ref-72afc866429adad2

We now specialize the preceding unconditional and conditional statements to the high-row-scale packet inherited from TPC-28–TPC-31. ## Exact fraction arithmetic

###### ref-5fb6da2f7b0b0d63

###### ref-70618d85792f6c67

###### ref-a5e123b2334854d2

###### ref-04814fec3742763c

###### ref-5993c82f5853278d

The small numerical gap driving this section is best kept in exact fractions: $$\begin{aligned}  1-\beta  &=\frac{133}{400}=\frac{266}{800},  \label{eq:tpc32-high-large-content-margin}\\  \frac\beta2  &=\frac{267}{800},  \label{eq:tpc32-high-rms-margin}\\  \frac\beta2-(1-\beta)  &=\frac{267}{800}-\frac{266}{800}  =\boxed{\frac1{800}},  \label{eq:tpc32-high-amplitude-slack}\\  3\beta-2  &=\frac{801}{400}-\frac{800}{400}  =\boxed{\frac1{400}},  \label{eq:tpc32-high-energy-slack}\\  2(1-\beta)-\beta  &=\frac{266}{400}-\frac{267}{400}  =-\boxed{\frac1{400}}.  \label{eq:tpc32-high-exception-density-exponent}\end{aligned}$$ The $1/800$ quantity is an amplitude-exponent gap. The $1/400$ quantity is its squared-energy counterpart and also the density exponent for the exceptional frequency set at the large-content target scale.

## An unconditional density-one frequency theorem

###### ref-416594569537c8a6

###### ref-a98b3df7e85dc072

Take $$C\le J  \label{eq:tpc32-high-C-below-J}$$ and choose the no-wrap prime $q\asymp Q$ from [\[thm:tpc32-no-wrap-parseval\]](TPC-032.md#ref-d2d3c9a9f9fe613c). Then $$\left(  \frac1q\sum_{r\, (q)}|\widehat A_{C,q}(r)|^2  \right)^{1/2}  \ll X^{o(1)}N_0X^{-267/800}.  \label{eq:tpc32-high-rms-bound}$$ Thus the rms determinant frequency has a saving larger than the TPC-30 large-content margin by exactly $1/800$ in the exponent.

###### ref-7940e2ffd7512143

###### ref-81c905fe7a7e901e

###### ref-732eb191f23c9517

###### ref-501a90f0bedbd3e4

> **Corollary: High-$\beta$ almost-all-frequency saving** \[cor:tpc32-high-almost-all-frequencies\] Fix $0<\eta<133/400$ and put $$\sigma_\eta=\frac{133}{400}-\eta.  \label{eq:tpc32-high-sigma-eta}$$ For every fixed $\varepsilon>0$, $$\begin{aligned}  &\#\left\{r\pmod q:  |\widehat A_{C,q}(r)|>  N_0X^{-133/400+\eta}\right\}  \notag\\  &\hspace{9em}  \ll_{\varepsilon,h,\mathscr D}  X^{266/400-2\eta+\varepsilon},  \label{eq:tpc32-high-exception-count}\\  &\frac1q  \#\left\{r\pmod q:  |\widehat A_{C,q}(r)|>  N_0X^{-133/400+\eta}\right\}  \notag\\  &\hspace{9em}  \ll_{\varepsilon,h,\mathscr D}  X^{-1/400-2\eta+\varepsilon+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc32-high-exception-proportion}\end{aligned}$$ In particular, after choosing $\varepsilon<\eta$, a proportion $1-O(X^{-1/400-\eta+o(1)})$ of the frequencies has the stated saving. The exceptional set may contain $r=0$.

> **Proof** Insert [\[eq:tpc32-high-sigma-eta\]](TPC-032.md#ref-81c905fe7a7e901e) into [\[eq:tpc32-no-wrap-exceptional-count\]](TPC-032.md#ref-c7747ffda4fc4bfd): $$2\sigma_\eta  =\frac{266}{400}-2\eta.$$ This proves [\[eq:tpc32-high-exception-count\]](TPC-032.md#ref-732eb191f23c9517). Since $q=X^{267/400+o(1)}$, division by $q$ and [\[eq:tpc32-high-exception-density-exponent\]](TPC-032.md#ref-5993c82f5853278d) give [\[eq:tpc32-high-exception-proportion\]](TPC-032.md#ref-501a90f0bedbd3e4).

The conclusion is stronger than merely saying that a generic frequency has some power saving. It says that the precise saving required to meet the $133/400$ large-content floor already holds outside a set of relative density $X^{-1/400+o(1)}$. It does not identify the distinguished zero frequency with the density-one set.

The Farey-family version has the same rms endpoint $\beta/2$. Taking $V=X^{267/800+o(1)}=Q^{1/2+o(1)}$ in [\[thm:tpc32-farey-large-sieve\]](TPC-032.md#ref-a84802515531eddc) gives, outside a power-small proportion of reduced rational frequencies, every fixed saving below $$\min\left\{\frac{267}{800},\frac{267}{800}\right\}  =\frac{267}{800}.  \label{eq:tpc32-high-farey-rms-endpoint}$$ Again, this family contains no copy of the zero rational frequency because its denominators begin at two.

## The exact conditional threshold

###### ref-554eae6b2963eba1

###### ref-737159c130fcef02

For the full-shell splice choose $$C=\lfloor J\rfloor=X^{133/400+o(1)},  \qquad  \kappa=\frac{133}{400}.  \label{eq:tpc32-high-content-choice}$$ The two large-content losses in [\[eq:tpc32-conditional-full-shell-bound\]](TPC-032.md#ref-85cffc2cf75124ec) then both have endpoint $133/400$. Because the conclusion asks for every strict saving below this endpoint, the flatness term reaches it precisely when $$\begin{aligned}  \frac{\beta-\chi}{2}  &\ge\frac{133}{400}  \notag\\  &\Longleftrightarrow  \chi\le\beta-\frac{266}{400}  =\frac{267}{400}-\frac{266}{400}  =\boxed{\frac1{400}}.  \label{eq:tpc32-high-chi-threshold}\end{aligned}$$

###### ref-cd193475c13f2396

###### ref-fd3fbc28b00052c3

> **Corollary: Conditional high-$\beta$ closure of the matched shell** \[cor:tpc32-high-conditional-closure\] Assume that the actual matched determinant coefficient with the choice [\[eq:tpc32-high-content-choice\]](TPC-032.md#ref-554eae6b2963eba1) satisfies $$\mathfrak F_0(A_C)\le X^{\chi+o(1)}  \qquad\text{for some fixed }\chi\le\frac1{400}.  \label{eq:tpc32-high-flatness-input}$$ Then every fixed $$\boxed{\sigma<\frac{133}{400}}  \label{eq:tpc32-high-conditional-saving}$$ is a power-saving exponent relative to $N_0\asymp XQ$ for the complete matched raw cutoff shell.

> **Proof** Under [\[eq:tpc32-high-flatness-input\]](TPC-032.md#ref-cd193475c13f2396), [\[eq:tpc32-high-chi-threshold\]](TPC-032.md#ref-737159c130fcef02) shows that the flatness margin is at least $133/400$. The other two entries in [\[eq:tpc32-conditional-saving-range\]](TPC-032.md#ref-c0766196e09dcc3e) both equal $133/400$ by [\[eq:tpc32-high-content-choice\]](TPC-032.md#ref-554eae6b2963eba1). Apply [\[thm:tpc32-conditional-zero-transfer\]](TPC-032.md#ref-6bb52734223206c5) with a strict final exponent.

###### ref-8e81124370daa79e

###### ref-6e9d8de727ea238e

Condition [\[eq:tpc32-high-flatness-input\]](TPC-032.md#ref-cd193475c13f2396) has a transparent amplitude interpretation. By definition, $$|\widehat A_{C,q}(0)|  \le X^{\chi/2+o(1)}\|A_C\|_2.  \label{eq:tpc32-high-amplitude-flatness}$$ Thus the boundary $\chi\le1/400$ permits the zero coefficient to be larger than the rms determinant coefficient by a factor at most $$X^{1/800+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc32-high-amplitude-inflation}$$ At $\chi=0$, the zero frequency is no larger than the rms scale up to $X^{o(1)}$, and its saving endpoint is $267/800$; the final splice is still limited to $266/800=133/400$ by large content. By contrast, the unconditional Cauchy bound permits $\mathfrak F_0(A_C)\asymp Q=X^{267/400+o(1)}$, which gives no saving at all. Moving from that worst case to [\[eq:tpc32-high-flatness-input\]](TPC-032.md#ref-cd193475c13f2396) is the genuine arithmetic task; the small numerical slack does not make it a soft consequence of the frequency average.

# Sharp interface obstructions and a primitive witness

The preceding mean-square estimate is nearly sufficient numerically, but it cannot select the coefficient $r=0$. We give two exact models showing that this failure is structural at the current interface rather than an artifact of a loose inequality.

## A flat determinant signal

###### ref-4ca120d3c42b9312

###### ref-2931014d98c2c88f

###### ref-7ab0b98a027da21e

###### ref-65813eb2eb6e633d

> **Proposition: Perfect nonzero-frequency cancellation does not control zero** \[prop:tpc32-flat-signal\] Let $q$ be prime and let $I$ consist of $q$ consecutive integers. Define $$A(n)=\frac{N_0}{q}\mathbf 1_{n\in I}.  \label{eq:tpc32-flat-signal}$$ Then $$\|A\|_1=N_0,  \qquad  \|A\|_\infty=\frac{N_0}{q},  \qquad  \|A\|_2^2=\frac{N_0^2}{q},  \label{eq:tpc32-flat-signal-norms}$$ but its $q$-point determinant transform satisfies $$|\widehat A(0)|=N_0,  \qquad  \widehat A(r)=0\quad(1\le r<q).  \label{eq:tpc32-flat-signal-dft}$$

> **Proof** The norm formulas are immediate. The consecutive integers in $I$ form a complete residue system modulo $q$, so $$\widehat A(r)=\frac{N_0}{q}  e_q(-rn_0)\sum_{a=0}^{q-1}e_q(-ra).$$ The geometric sum is $q$ for $r=0$ and zero otherwise.

Taking $q\asymp Q$, [\[eq:tpc32-flat-signal-norms\]](TPC-032.md#ref-7ab0b98a027da21e) saturates the $\ell^1$, $\ell^\infty$, and $\ell^2$ scales available for $A_C$. Thus those three norms, the support length, and even exact vanishing of every nonzero Fourier coefficient cannot imply any positive flatness saving at zero. This is an abstract interface countermodel; it is not a lower bound for the physical Möbius shell.

## The coherent mode of the content projector

###### ref-6dcb6050b6539c4e

###### ref-13c02e304a5618c2

For positive integers $N_1,\ldots,N_M$, define $$P_C(i,j)=\mathbf 1_{(N_i,N_j)\le C}.  \label{eq:tpc32-content-matrix}$$ The content-projector identity gives the exact signed rank-one expansion $$P_C=\sum_{t\ge1}\lambda_C(t)v_tv_t^{\mathsf T},  \qquad  v_t(i)=\mathbf 1_{t\mid N_i}.  \label{eq:tpc32-content-rank-one-expansion}$$ The coefficients are signed, so this is not a positive-semidefinite decomposition.

###### ref-58b6b9f3c5dba653

###### ref-53275bf6d804f2ba

###### ref-cbb077537ce21052

###### ref-6a0e10ca0f2a5714

> **Proposition: A coherent constant-row mode** \[prop:tpc32-coherent-content\] If the $N_i$ are pairwise coprime and $N_i>C$ for every $i$, then $$P_C=\mathbf J_M-I_M.  \label{eq:tpc32-J-minus-I}$$ Its spectrum is $$M-1,\qquad -1\ \text{with multiplicity }M-1,  \label{eq:tpc32-J-minus-I-spectrum}$$ and $$\|P_C\|_{2\to2}=M-1,  \qquad  \operatorname{srank}(P_C)  :=\frac{\|P_C\|_{F}^{2}}{\|P_C\|_{2\to2}^{2}}  =\frac{M}{M-1}.  \label{eq:tpc32-J-minus-I-stable-rank}$$

> **Proof** For $i\ne j$, pairwise coprimality gives $(N_i,N_j)=1\le C$; on the diagonal $(N_i,N_i)=N_i>C$. This proves [\[eq:tpc32-J-minus-I\]](TPC-032.md#ref-53275bf6d804f2ba). The all-ones vector has eigenvalue $M-1$, and its orthogonal complement has eigenvalue $-1$. Finally, $\|P_C\|_F^2=M(M-1)$, giving [\[eq:tpc32-J-minus-I-stable-rank\]](TPC-032.md#ref-6a0e10ca0f2a5714).

The stable rank being close to one is not a gain here: it records that one coherent direction dominates the operator. In particular, restricting to small canonical content is not a row-centering operation.

## A finite primitive realization

###### ref-00e12b280af5dde4

###### ref-370235c6cf7b2b7f

###### ref-bb8ef569c8a4d471

The coherent model occurs inside the actual arithmetic interface. Take $$h=2,  \quad j=1,  \quad d=1,  \quad R=12,  \quad T=50,  \quad U_0=200,  \quad C=30,  \label{eq:tpc32-witness-parameters}$$ and the prime rows $$59,71,101,107,137,149,179,191,197.  \label{eq:tpc32-witness-rows}$$ Their targets $m+2$ are respectively $$61,73,103,109,139,151,181,193,199,  \label{eq:tpc32-witness-targets}$$ which are distinct primes exceeding $C$. Hence the $9\times9$ content matrix is $\mathbf J_9-I_9$, with spectrum $8,-1,\ldots,-1$ and stable rank $9/8$.

###### ref-2d9bfc7b70d97110

###### ref-698d7f1be13dc6c8

###### ref-40fe60552ea4ccb0

###### ref-214ea2ddafaf7881

For completeness, the finite Ramanujan-model divisor coefficient is $$\lambda'_R(u)  =u\sum_{b\le R/u}\frac{\mu(ub)\mu(b)}{\varphi(ub)}  \qquad(u\le R).  \label{eq:tpc32-lambda-prime}$$ At $R=12$, $$\lambda'_{12}(1)  =\sum_{b\le12}\frac{\mu(b)^2}{\varphi(b)}  =\frac{113}{30}.  \label{eq:tpc32-lambda-113}$$ Since each target in [\[eq:tpc32-witness-targets\]](TPC-032.md#ref-bb8ef569c8a4d471) is prime and lies in $(T,U_0]$, its prefix and ultra increment are $$\mathsf A_{m,50}=-\frac{113}{30},  \qquad  C_m=\log(m+2).  \label{eq:tpc32-witness-prefix-tail}$$ For two distinct witness rows with targets $p_i,p_j$, the sum of the three raw channels is therefore the formal prime-log polynomial $$\boxed{  \log p_i\log p_j  -\frac{113}{30}(\log p_i+\log p_j).}  \label{eq:tpc32-witness-polynomial}$$ Its coefficient of $\log p_i\log p_j$ is one, so there is no universal algebraic annihilation among the three channels.

This finite realization certifies the algebraic interface only. Although the displayed rows happen to be members of a finite list of prime pairs at gap two, their presence proves neither a growing lower bound nor any asymptotic statement about twin primes. It also does not prove that the physical shell has a coherent main term; cancellation from the remaining row coefficients and phases is still possible.

## The arbitrary-mask obstruction

###### ref-b43ccd0348351081

Low rank in divisor variables does not survive an uncontrolled conclusion about the row operator. Given any finite kernel $K_{\alpha,\gamma}$, an entrywise bounded mask may be chosen to align its phases, for example $$\mathfrak m(\alpha,\gamma)=  \begin{cases}   \overline{K_{\alpha,\gamma}}/|K_{\alpha,\gamma}|,     &\alpha\ne\gamma\text{ and }K_{\alpha,\gamma}\ne0,\\   0,&\alpha=\gamma\text{ or }K_{\alpha,\gamma}=0.  \end{cases}  \label{eq:tpc32-sign-mask}$$ Then the masked off-diagonal scalar sum is $\sum_{\alpha\ne\gamma}|K_{\alpha,\gamma}|$. Therefore no theorem uniform over all entrywise bounded pair masks can deduce signed cancellation from the matched rank-two identity alone. The actual mask may have additional structure, but that structure must be stated and quantified rather than inferred from [\[eq:tpc32-mask-bound\]](TPC-032.md#ref-713580f24da7ef4e).

# Technology comparison and the next affine–Möbius gate

The exact identities above expose several familiar analytic forms, but none of the cited technologies accepts the complete physical coefficient without an additional argument. We record the boundary to prevent a formal rearrangement from being mistaken for a cancellation theorem.

## One Möbius sign versus two affine signs

###### ref-321475a9a4695b77

Classical uniform bounds of Davenport type estimate a single oscillatory Möbius sum $$\sum_{d\asymp D}\mu(d)\omega(d)e(\theta d)  \label{eq:tpc32-davenport-form}$$ with arbitrary logarithmic saving when $D$ is a polynomial scale and $\omega$ has controlled smoothness; see, for example, [`\citet[Chapter~13]{IwaniecKowalski2004}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-032.md#cite-49dfea87eaada156). Fixed-period characters and Mellin phases can be included without changing the essential one-variable form. This is useful for a bare row coefficient, but opening a reflected ultra leg changes the arithmetic problem.

###### ref-29e818c1408b5ac0

###### ref-2a9a250fd90b5d19

###### ref-c72d19a00fa93ec3

###### ref-0ed191ff8d1f09e0

Fix $\ell,j$ and a reflected divisor $s$ on primitive support. The condition $$s\mid \ell dj+h  \label{eq:tpc32-reflected-divisibility}$$ has a unique solution $d\equiv d_0\pmod s$, because $(s,\ell j)=1$. Write $d=d_0+st$. The complementary quotient is $$U(t)=\frac{\ell j(d_0+st)+h}{s}  =u_\star+\ell jt,  \qquad  u_\star=\frac{\ell jd_0+h}{s},  \label{eq:tpc32-affine-quotient}$$ and the two affine forms satisfy the nondegeneracy identity $$\boxed{su_\star-\ell jd_0=h\ne0.}  \label{eq:tpc32-affine-determinant}$$ After the exact sign fusion, a typical opened coefficient contains $$\mu(d_0+st)\mu(u_\star+\ell jt).  \label{eq:tpc32-affine-mobius-correlation}$$ This is a two-point affine Möbius correlation, not the one-sign sum [\[eq:tpc32-davenport-form\]](TPC-032.md#ref-321475a9a4695b77).

Logarithmically averaged two-point Chowla–Elliott theorems control fixed nonproportional affine forms in an averaged regime [`\citep{Tao2016}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-032.md#cite-0bccf4cf69786109). The physical packet here asks additionally for uniformity as $s,\ell,j$ and the affine coefficients grow polynomially, for dyadic rather than logarithmic averaging, for fixed residue factors, and for reassembly over all rows and cutoffs. We do not claim that the existing theorem supplies this package.

## Spectral and Kloosterman technology

Bilinear and trilinear estimates for Kloosterman fractions can yield strong cancellation once the variables, moduli, smooth weights, and coefficient norms match the hypotheses of the relevant theorem [`\citep{DukeFriedlanderIwaniec1997,BettinChandee2018}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-032.md#cite-63bff44e59766643); [\[BettinChandee2018\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-032.md#cite-20c5dcaa0c8d6228). Modern exceptional spectral large sieves provide further gains in carefully structured families [`\citep{Pascadi2026LargeSieve}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-032.md#cite-362d37d3b467d698). In the present shell, however, the modulus is tied to reflected target divisors, the prime–Möbius coefficient depends on a moving factorization, and the generic pair mask is still joint. No parameter ledger in this paper verifies the hypotheses of one of those stronger estimates. They are possible technologies for a future refined packet, not black boxes used in our theorems.

## A concrete next target

###### ref-e1606343cc9dce7e

The conditional transfer theorem reduces a high-$\beta$ closure of the raw shell to the exact boundary $\chi\le1/400$. A robust route with a fixed margin is to prove, for the actual physical amplitude and $C\asymp J$, a power-saving estimate of the form $$\left|\sum_nA_C(n)\right|^2  \ll X^{1/400-\eta}\sum_n|A_C(n)|^2  \label{eq:tpc32-next-flatness-target}$$ for some fixed $\eta>0$. A viable proof would have to exploit information absent from the abstract norm interface, such as:

1.  cancellation in the affine correlation [\[eq:tpc32-affine-mobius-correlation\]](TPC-032.md#ref-0ed191ff8d1f09e0) uniform over a controlled subfamily;

2.  a projective or operator-norm decomposition of the actual pair mask;

3.  an additional centering identity that annihilates the coherent determinant mode before absolute reassembly; or

4.  a decomposition into spectral/Kloosterman ranges with a complete conductor and norm ledger.

Each option can be tested independently. Failure of one does not refute the others, and a sharp impossibility result for any proposed interface would itself refine the route map.

For the full calibrated cutoff difference, the two drift legs also impose the inherited saving $L^{-1}$; hence any final exponent must be intersected with the fixed prime-source margin. Even a proof of [\[eq:tpc32-next-flatness-target\]](TPC-032.md#ref-e1606343cc9dce7e) would close only this residual packet. It would not by itself supply positivity, undo all earlier reductions, or prove a prime-pair theorem.

## Scope of the advance

The advance in this paper is that the determinant-frequency route no longer ends at the vague instruction “control the zero mode.” It proves that ordinary determinant frequencies already have the required size outside a power-small exceptional set, identifies the remaining hard raw component of the original calibrated residual with one special member of that set, quantifies the exact additional flatness needed, and shows that the current Hilbert-space data cannot force it. The remaining door is narrow, but it is still an open analytic estimate rather than a claimed consequence of the existing large sieve.

## Claim boundary

The unconditional mathematical statements established in this paper are:

1.  the exact matched cutoff shell, its rank-two divisor tensor, and its calibrated drift boundary;

2.  exact canonical-content inversion, determinant-phase splitting, and the retained-multiplier bilinear representation;

3.  the prime–Möbius one-leg fusion and the explicit separation boundary for a generic pair mask;

4.  determinant $\ell^1$, $\ell^\infty$, and $\ell^2$ bounds inherited from the physical singleton-cell theorem;

5.  no-wrap Parseval, exceptional-frequency compression, and the classical Farey large-sieve estimate;

6.  the exact equivalence of the auxiliary zero coefficient and the full matched raw shell modulo the previously bounded large-content contribution;

7.  the high-$\beta$ almost-all-frequency proportion and the two sharp finite interface obstructions.

The flatness transfer theorem is a proved implication whose premise $$|\widehat A_{C,q}(0)|^2  \le X^{\chi+o(1)}\sum_n|A_C(n)|^2$$ is explicitly additional. The paper does not prove this premise, does not show that $r=0$ is a typical determinant frequency, and does not assert that the generic pair mask has bounded projective norm.

Neither the certificate nor the analytic theorems prove:

1.  signed cancellation of the complete Möbius-tail residual;

2.  an improved level of distribution for primes;

3.  a Hardy–Littlewood prime-pair asymptotic or positivity statement;

4.  the twin-prime conjecture; or

5.  a breach of the sieve parity barrier.

The finite prime-row witness is an exact algebraic regression test only. The negative JSON scope flags make any future promotion of that witness, or of the conditional $1/400$ gate, into an unconditional prime-pair claim detectable during review.
