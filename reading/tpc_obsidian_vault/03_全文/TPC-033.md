## Abstract

We continue the analysis of the distinguished zero-frequency coefficient left by a matched prime–Möbius cutoff shell. The earlier reduction identifies the required physical amplitude but leaves its two-point Möbius structure and actual row-pair mask entangled. We first return from the abstract bounded-mask interface to the original mask $$\mathbf 1_{\ell\ne\ell'}  \mathbf 1_{|\ell d-\ell'e|>QX^{-\kappa_0}}  \mathbf 1_{(d,e)\le X^{\kappa_0}}.$$ After fixing the opposite row, its gcd condition has the exact expansion $$\mathbf 1_{(d,e)\le G}  =\sum_{v\mid d,\ v\mid e}\lambda_G(v),  \qquad  \sum_{v\mid e}|\lambda_G(v)|\le3^{\omega(e)}=X^{o(1)}.$$ The near-diagonal deletion cuts the remaining row interval into at most two pieces. Opening one ultra divisor and reflecting its complementary factor therefore decomposes each physical column, with only $X^{o(1)}$ coefficient mass, into primitive affine correlations $$\sum_{r\in I}  \mu(\widetilde d_0+sr)  \mu(\widetilde u_0+\ell jv r)W(r),  \qquad  s\widetilde u_0-\ell jv\widetilde d_0=h,  \qquad  |I|\ll1+\frac{D}{sv}.$$ This is a columnwise theorem for the actual mask, not a low-rank theorem for an arbitrary bounded mask.

For the arithmetic core, every equation $sU-aD=h$ with $(a,s)=1$ and $(as,h)=1$ is put into an integral $SL_2$-normal form $$U_t=hx+at,\qquad D_t=hy+st,\qquad sx-ay=1.$$ It yields the exact identities $$(D_t,U_t)=(D_t,h)=(U_t,h)=(h,t)$$ and converts the primitive Möbius product into the reducible quadratic sequence $\mu(D_tU_t)$, whose discriminant is $h^2$. With a balanced Bézout origin we prove uniformly for $K=\max(a,s)$ that $$\sum_{\substack{T<t\le2T\\(t,h)=1}}  \mu^2(D_t)\mu^2(U_t)  =\mathfrak S_{\rm sf}(a,s;h)T   +O_h\!\left(\frac{T}{\log(2T)}+\sqrt{KT}\right),$$ where the Euler product is explicit and bounded below by a positive constant depending only on $h$. This controls squarefree support, not Möbius signs.

We then prove an exact fixed-determinant projective Plancherel identity. For a prime $q$, $h\ne0\pmod q$, and functions $f,g:\mathbb F_q\to\mathbb C$, let $$\mathcal C_{a,s}^{(h)}(f,g)  =\sum_{D\in\mathbb F_q}f(D)g\bigl(s^{-1}(aD+h)\bigr).$$ Writing $f^\circ=f-\bar f$ and $g^\circ=g-\bar g$, one has $$\sum_{a,s\ne0}  \left|\mathcal C_{a,s}^{(h)}-q\bar f\bar g\right|^2  =q\|f^\circ\|_2^2\|g^\circ\|_2^2  -\sum_{b\ne0}\left|\sum_Df^\circ(D)g^\circ(bD)\right|^2.$$ Consequently the finite Möbius model has $o(q)$ correlation for all but $O(q^{2-2\eta})$ nonzero coefficient pairs $(a,s)$, for every $0<\eta<1/2$. Complete $a=\ell j$ orbits preserve this energy exactly, even if $\ell$ is restricted to primes.

Finally, the two exact polarizations of the physical shell define column energies $E_L,E_R$. We prove the conditional transfer $$E_L+E_R\ll X^{o(1)}Q^3  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad  |\mathcal S_{\rm sh}^{\rm all}|\ll X^{o(1)}Q^2.$$ At $Q=X^{267/400+o(1)}$, this is precisely the $J^{-1}=X^{-133/400+o(1)}$ amplitude saving required by the preceding zero-frequency gate. The $Q^3$ estimate is not proved here. A single-fiber square-root strategy falls short by $49727/210000$, and a single finite-field modulus cannot simultaneously provide integer no-wrap and physical slope coverage. Thus the remaining target is a restricted physical projective-energy estimate, not pointwise two-point Chowla. No prime-pair asymptotic, twin-prime theorem, positivity result, or breach of the parity barrier is claimed.

# Introduction

The purpose of this paper is to turn one exceptional Fourier coefficient into a concrete arithmetic average. TPC-32 proves determinant-frequency dispersion for a matched prime–Möbius cutoff shell, but the physical problem selects the coefficient at frequency zero [`\citep{WangTPC32}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-033.md#cite-49ad82103eb75eb7). Ordinary frequencies are small outside a power-small exceptional set; membership of the distinguished coefficient in that set is not decided by Parseval. The same paper shows that support size, $\ell^1$-mass, $\ell^2$-energy, and even perfect cancellation at every nonzero frequency do not force cancellation at zero. Additional arithmetic structure is required.

There are two logically separate questions. First, what is the exact arithmetic sequence inside a zero-frequency column after the actual row-pair mask is restored? Second, can averaging over the physical family of those sequences replace a pointwise two-point Möbius theorem? The present paper answers the first question and proves a finite-field model for the second. It does not complete the final transfer to the integer physical packet.

## From a bounded mask to the actual column

###### ref-d659e82836b3eaf4

###### ref-0a6812eb3ae42a86

The abstract interface of TPC-25–TPC-32 deliberately retains the row-pair mask as an arbitrary bounded joint multiplier [`\citep{WangTPC25,WangTPC32}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-033.md#cite-39e8dc14c55a2411); [\[WangTPC32\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-033.md#cite-49ad82103eb75eb7). That is the correct interface for the earlier norm estimates, but it is too broad for an affine-column theorem: an arbitrary entrywise bounded matrix can align with any prescribed kernel. We therefore return to the actual mask inherited from TPC-18 and recorded in TPC-21 [`\citep{WangTPC21}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-033.md#cite-8d248780f3293b33), $$\mathfrak m((\ell,d),(\ell',e))  =\mathbf 1_{\ell\ne\ell'}   \mathbf 1_{|\ell d-\ell'e|>QX^{-\kappa_0}}   \mathbf 1_{(d,e)\le X^{\kappa_0}}.  \label{eq:tpc33-intro-actual-mask}$$ Here $d,e$ are squarefree and lie on one opened dyadic scale. For a fixed opposite row $(\ell',e)$, the last indicator has a divisor expansion of total coefficient mass $3^{\omega(e)}=X^{o(1)}$, while the middle indicator merely removes one interval from the $d$-line. After one ultra divisor is reflected, the remaining inner variable lies on $$\widetilde d_r=\widetilde d_0+sr,  \qquad  \widetilde u_r=\widetilde u_0+\ell jv r,  \qquad  s\widetilde u_0-\ell jv\widetilde d_0=h.  \label{eq:tpc33-intro-affine-column}$$ Thus the actual mask has small *columnwise* expansion complexity. This does not say that the entire mask matrix has small projective or Schur norm.

## Exact arithmetic of a fixed-determinant line

###### ref-0768d1a08f812bf7

###### ref-aa860f40ff8fa7ef

The equation $$sU-aD=h  \label{eq:tpc33-intro-determinant-line}$$ is not merely a nonproportional pair of affine forms. Under the physical primitivity conditions $(a,s)=1$ and $(as,h)=1$, it belongs to one unimodular orbit. Choosing $sx-ay=1$ gives $$\binom{U_t}{D_t}  =\begin{pmatrix}x&a\\y&s\end{pmatrix}   \binom{h}{t}.$$ The determinant-one matrix transfers all common divisors back to the two coordinates $(h,t)$. In particular, the primitive part of the two-sign sequence is one Möbius value on the reducible quadratic $$D_tU_t  =as t^2+h(sx+ay)t+h^2xy,  \qquad  \operatorname{disc}(D_tU_t)=h^2.  \label{eq:tpc33-intro-quadratic}$$ This reformulation does not prove signed cancellation. We do prove a uniform squarefree-support theorem for a balanced Bézout origin. Its main term has a positive Euler product uniformly in the growing slopes when $\max(a,s)=o(T)$ by a fixed power.

## Projective averaging at fixed determinant

###### ref-2d8c1a6038c7d9fd

The central unconditional average theorem is finite and exact. Over a prime field, the change of variables $$(a,s)\longmapsto(b,c)=(a/s,h/s)  \label{eq:tpc33-intro-projective-change}$$ is a bijection of $(\mathbb F_q^\times)^2$. Translation Parseval in $c$, followed by summation over the slope $b$, gives an exact second moment for every fixed nonzero determinant $h$. Outside $O(q^{2-2\eta})$ coefficient pairs, it yields an $O(q^{1/2+\eta})$ centered fluctuation and hence $o(q)$ total correlation in the finite Möbius model. Moreover, on a complete residue orbit the restriction $a=\ell j$ loses no energy: multiplication by every fixed nonzero $\ell$, prime or not, permutes $j\in\mathbb F_q^\times$.

This theorem rotates the earlier “almost every determinant frequency” statement into “fixed determinant, almost every nonzero coefficient pair.” The average runs over both $b=a/s$ and the affine offset $c=h/s$; by itself it is not an almost-every-$b$ statement. A direct physical consequence still fails for a precise reason. Integer no-wrap needs a modulus on the ambient determinant scale, approximately $X$, whereas complete coverage of the orbit parameter is available only for moduli at most $J=X^{133/400+o(1)}$. A single modulus cannot satisfy both requirements.

## The exact conditional gate

###### ref-8bc103095488d287

###### ref-45bba13c2596dce5

Let $C_m$ denote the ultra increment and $\mathsf H_m$ the half-prefix from the exact polarization in TPC-32. The full matched shell is $$\mathcal K^{\rm sh}_{\alpha,\gamma}(j)  =C_{m_\alpha}(j)\mathsf H_{m_\gamma}(j)   +\mathsf H_{m_\alpha}(j)C_{m_\gamma}(j).  \label{eq:tpc33-intro-polarization}$$ Keeping the opposite row outside each column defines two nonnegative column energies $E_L,E_R$. The inherited one-row estimate $\|\gamma^{(i)}\|_2^2\ll Q\log(2L)$ gives the rigorous implication $$E_L+E_R\ll X^{o(1)}Q^3  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad  |\mathcal S_{\rm sh}^{\rm all}|\ll X^{o(1)}Q^2.  \label{eq:tpc33-intro-energy-transfer}$$ The left side is a new analytic input, not a consequence of the row-energy bound. Separately estimating the two polarizations is sufficient but possibly stronger than estimating their scalar sum.

###### ref-0c5f3c556f66b95a

At the source-compatible endpoint of TPC-28, $$Q=X^{267/400+o(1)},\qquad  J=X^{133/400+o(1)},\qquad  JQ\asymp X,  \label{eq:tpc33-intro-high-beta}$$ and therefore $Q^2=(JQ^2)J^{-1}$. The right side of [\[eq:tpc33-intro-energy-transfer\]](TPC-033.md#ref-45bba13c2596dce5) is exactly the amplitude budget of the preceding zero-frequency gate. It is important not to restate this as a proved flatness ratio: no matching lower bound for the determinant-cell energy is known.

The new contribution should be read as an exact synthesis tailored to the TPC residual: the structured physical disintegration, the fixed-determinant projective identity with its prime-product lift, and the quantified energy splice. Basic finite Fourier orthogonality and squarefree sieving have a large classical literature, so no priority claim is made for those ingredients in isolation. Nor do the results imply a Hardy–Littlewood main term, positivity of a prime-pair correlation, infinitely many twin primes, or a general resolution of the sieve parity problem.

The paper is organized as follows. fixes the matched shell and the actual mask. proves the integral normal form, and [4](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-033.md#src-3953365364654067) proves the support theorem. performs the physical column disintegration. proves the finite-field identity, and [7](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-033.md#src-23c765b84808f8a6) derives its Möbius and product-slope consequences. gives the $Q^3$ transfer. The final sections record sharp obstructions, the literature boundary, the high-$\beta$ ledger, and the exact computational certificate.

## The $SL_2(\mathbb Z)$ parametrization

> **Theorem: Fixed-determinant normal form** \[thm:tpc33-affine-normal-form\] Every integral solution of $$sU-aD=h  \label{eq:tpc33-fixed-determinant-equation}$$ has a unique representation $$\boxed{  U_t=hx+at,  \qquad  D_t=hy+st,  \qquad t\in\mathbb Z.}  \label{eq:tpc33-canonical-affine-forms}$$ The inverse coordinates are $$\boxed{  \binom ht  =\begin{pmatrix}s&-a\\-y&x\end{pmatrix}   \binom UD.}  \label{eq:tpc33-affine-inverse}$$ For every $t$, with gcds understood positively, $$\boxed{  (D_t,U_t)=(D_t,h)=(U_t,h)=(h,t).}  \label{eq:tpc33-gcd-identities}$$ Moreover, $$(D_t,s)=1,  \qquad  (U_t,a)=1.  \label{eq:tpc33-slope-coprimality}$$

> **Proof** The determinant of $V$ is one. Multiplying $V(h,t)^{\mathsf T}$ gives [\[eq:tpc33-canonical-affine-forms\]](TPC-033.md#ref-acd9338d5cc8da1f), and $sU_t-aD_t=(sx-ay)h=h$. Conversely, the inverse matrix in [\[eq:tpc33-affine-inverse\]](TPC-033.md#ref-2e121d0bb4e2f836) is integral. Its first coordinate is $sU-aD=h$, so every solution has one and only one integral second coordinate $t$.
>
> An integral unimodular transformation preserves the ideal generated by the two coordinates. Hence $(D_t,U_t)=(h,t)$. Also $$(D_t,h)=(st,h)=(t,h),  \qquad  (U_t,h)=(at,h)=(t,h),$$ because $(as,h)=1$. This proves [\[eq:tpc33-gcd-identities\]](TPC-033.md#ref-e6fba581410519f4). Finally, [\[eq:tpc33-bezout\]](TPC-033.md#ref-a704d7a4e0f6330d) implies $(y,s)=(x,a)=1$. Therefore $$(D_t,s)=(hy,s)=1,  \qquad  (U_t,a)=(hx,a)=1,$$ again using $(as,h)=1$.

###### ref-dcdae0ed30f141f4

Every other Bézout pair is $$x'=x+ak,\qquad y'=y+sk,\qquad k\in\mathbb Z.  \label{eq:tpc33-bezout-change}$$ For a fixed physical solution, changing from $(x,y)$ to $(x',y')$ changes its affine coordinate from $t$ to $t-hk$. Thus the residue class $t\pmod h$, and in particular $(t,h)$, is intrinsic even though the integer origin is not.

> **Corollary: Primitive layer** \[cor:tpc33-primitive-layer\] The following conditions are equivalent: $$(D_t,h)=1,\qquad  (U_t,h)=1,\qquad  (D_t,U_t)=1,\qquad  (t,h)=1.  \label{eq:tpc33-primitive-equivalences}$$

> **Proof** All four gcds are equal by [\[eq:tpc33-gcd-identities\]](TPC-033.md#ref-e6fba581410519f4).

## Möbius layers and the quadratic discriminant

###### ref-dccb3472876f8a0d

Let $$g=(h,t)=(D_t,U_t),\qquad  D_t=gD_t',\qquad U_t=gU_t'.  \label{eq:tpc33-common-gcd-layer}$$ Then $(D_t',U_t')=1$.

###### ref-45d5bc8c566ae7af

###### ref-39070a329c8c39eb

###### ref-529ad02b7552e350

> **Proposition: Exact Möbius gcd layer** \[prop:tpc33-mobius-gcd-layer\] Whenever $D_t,U_t>0$, $$\boxed{  \mu(D_t)\mu(U_t)  =\mu^2(g)\mathbf 1_{(g,D_t'U_t')=1}\mu(D_t'U_t').}  \label{eq:tpc33-mobius-gcd-layer}$$ In particular, on the primitive layer $(t,h)=1$, $$\boxed{  \mu(D_t)\mu(U_t)=\mu(D_tU_t).}  \label{eq:tpc33-primitive-mobius-fusion}$$

> **Proof** If $g$ is not squarefree or shares a prime with $D_t'U_t'$, both sides of [\[eq:tpc33-mobius-gcd-layer\]](TPC-033.md#ref-39070a329c8c39eb) vanish. Otherwise $g,D_t',U_t'$ are pairwise coprime and $$\mu(gD_t')\mu(gU_t')  =\mu(g)^2\mu(D_t')\mu(U_t')  =\mu^2(g)\mu(D_t'U_t').$$ The primitive statement follows from $g=1$.

###### ref-cfd41fb46e1a74e5

The fused polynomial is $$F_{a,s,h}(t)  :=D_tU_t  =as t^2+h(sx+ay)t+h^2xy.  \label{eq:tpc33-reducible-quadratic}$$

###### ref-957d47489597d85e

###### ref-fbe2870f935abb91

> **Proposition: Square discriminant** \[prop:tpc33-square-discriminant\] The discriminant of $F_{a,s,h}$ is exactly $$\boxed{\operatorname{disc}F_{a,s,h}=h^2.}  \label{eq:tpc33-square-discriminant}$$

> **Proof** By [\[eq:tpc33-bezout\]](TPC-033.md#ref-a704d7a4e0f6330d), $$\begin{aligned}  \operatorname{disc}F_{a,s,h}  &=h^2\bigl((sx+ay)^2-4asxy\bigr)\\  &=h^2(sx-ay)^2=h^2.\end{aligned}$$

Thus the two affine signs form a reducible quadratic Möbius sequence with a fixed square discriminant. The identity is structural, not a cancellation theorem: the case $a=s=h=1$ contains the open sequence $\mu(t)\mu(t+1)$, as recorded in [9.1](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-033.md#src-78195e29f49c7269).

## The reduced physical origin

###### ref-4b817f47ab9b1e98

###### ref-bf932b60f6f27048

###### ref-47e52950f3dbda49

###### ref-bff3156d9526b28d

For column disintegration it is often more convenient to choose the unique integer $D_0$ with $$1\le D_0\le s,\qquad aD_0+h\equiv0\pmod s,  \label{eq:tpc33-reduced-origin-D}$$ and put $$U_0=\frac{aD_0+h}{s}.  \label{eq:tpc33-reduced-origin-U}$$ Then all solutions are $$D_r=D_0+sr,\qquad U_r=U_0+ar,  \label{eq:tpc33-reduced-origin-forms}$$ with $$|D_0|\le s,\qquad |U_0|\le a+|h|.  \label{eq:tpc33-reduced-intercept-bounds}$$ This origin differs from the canonical one by an integral translation. # A balanced moving-slope squarefree-energy theorem

The normal form separates a support question from a sign question. We now show that the squarefree support has the expected positive density in a genuine growing-slope range. The proof is an elementary squarefree sieve tailored to the Bézout coordinates. The origin must be balanced: an arbitrary transformation $(x,y)\mapsto(x+ak,y+sk)$ can make the heights of both forms arbitrarily large while $a,s$, and the length of the parameter interval remain fixed. For general background on elementary squarefree sieves, see [`\citet{IwaniecKowalski2004}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-033.md#cite-49dfea87eaada156).

## Balanced coordinates and the Euler product

###### ref-1a362bf45dd8e685

###### ref-62b602da8a60be46

###### ref-2a5dc1e0b0376faf

###### ref-578ba2fbf99b060e

Assume [\[eq:tpc33-affine-hypotheses\]](TPC-033.md#ref-7a4e9e3c0a41f84a). If $a\ge2$, choose the unique integer $x$ such that $$sx\equiv1\pmod a,\qquad -\frac a2<x\le\frac a2,  \label{eq:tpc33-balanced-x}$$ and put $y=(sx-1)/a$. If $a=1$, take $x=0$, $y=-1$. Then $$sx-ay=1,\qquad |x|\le\frac a2,\qquad  |y|\le\frac s2+1.  \label{eq:tpc33-balanced-bounds}$$ Use the forms $D_t,U_t$ from [\[eq:tpc33-canonical-affine-forms\]](TPC-033.md#ref-acd9338d5cc8da1f) and put $$K=\max(a,s).  \label{eq:tpc33-K}$$ For $T\ge4(|h|+1)$ and $T<t\le2T$, both forms are positive and $$\max(D_t,U_t)\ll_h KT.  \label{eq:tpc33-affine-height}$$

###### ref-82fa94cd3b48eca2

Define $$E_{a,s,h}(T)  :=\sum_{\substack{T<t\le2T\\(t,h)=1}}  \mu^2(D_t)\mu^2(U_t)  \label{eq:tpc33-squarefree-energy}$$ and $$\boxed{  \mathfrak S_{\rm sf}(a,s;h)  :=\prod_{p\mid h}\left(1-\frac1p\right)  \prod_{\substack{p\nmid h\\p\mid as}}        \left(1-\frac1{p^2}\right)  \prod_{p\nmid ash}\left(1-\frac2{p^2}\right).}  \label{eq:tpc33-squarefree-singular-series}$$

###### ref-76dcac769ee5383d

###### ref-5c1a49f7860945ed

###### ref-6b21271a447472b6

###### ref-6048adb0fae2f386

> **Theorem: Uniform squarefree energy** \[thm:tpc33-uniform-squarefree-energy\] For fixed $h\ne0$, uniformly over all positive $a,s,T$ satisfying [\[eq:tpc33-affine-hypotheses\]](TPC-033.md#ref-7a4e9e3c0a41f84a) and $T\ge4(|h|+1)$, $$\boxed{  E_{a,s,h}(T)  =\mathfrak S_{\rm sf}(a,s;h)T  +O_h\!\left(    \frac{T}{\log(2T)}+\sqrt{KT}   \right).}  \label{eq:tpc33-squarefree-asymptotic}$$ Furthermore, $$\mathfrak S_{\rm sf}(a,s;h)\ge c_h>0  \label{eq:tpc33-squarefree-positive}$$ uniformly in $a,s$. Consequently, for every fixed $\eta>0$, $$K\le T^{1-\eta}  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad  E_{a,s,h}(T)  =\mathfrak S_{\rm sf}(a,s;h)T+o_{h,\eta}(T)  \label{eq:tpc33-squarefree-growing-slope-range}$$ uniformly in that range.

## Local root counts

###### ref-64783b076c6099d7

> **Lemma: The three local factors** \[lem:tpc33-local-squarefree-factors\] The local density of the conditions in [\[eq:tpc33-squarefree-energy\]](TPC-033.md#ref-bfd8d2903734972d) is:
>
> 1.  $1-1/p$ if $p\mid h$;
>
> 2.  $1-1/p^2$ if $p\nmid h$ and $p\mid as$;
>
> 3.  $1-2/p^2$ if $p\nmid ash$.

> **Proof** If $p\mid h$, then $p\nmid as$. The primitive condition excludes $t\equiv0\pmod p$, and $$D_t\equiv st\pmod p,  \qquad  U_t\equiv at\pmod p.$$ Thus neither form is divisible by $p$ on the retained classes, giving $1-1/p$.
>
> Suppose $p\nmid h$ and $p\mid s$. Then $p\nmid a$, and $sx-ay=1$ makes $y\not\equiv0\pmod p$. Hence $D_t\equiv hy\not\equiv0 \pmod p$, while $U_t$ has exactly one root modulo $p^2$. The case $p\mid a$ is symmetric. This proves (ii).
>
> Finally suppose $p\nmid ash$. Each affine form has exactly one root modulo $p^2$. The roots are distinct: a common root would imply $p^2\mid sU_t-aD_t=h$, which is impossible. Thus precisely two classes modulo $p^2$ are removed. The argument includes $p=2$.

## Small primes and the large-square tail

###### ref-b1da17cfbc89fc6f

###### ref-47edd0359a8d9c43

###### ref-0ca7be689f9c6d0f

###### ref-6e35739bd5fc309b

###### ref-7c945b8f20baffc4

> **Proof: Proof of [\[thm:tpc33-uniform-squarefree-energy\]](TPC-033.md#ref-76dcac769ee5383d)** For $z\ge2$, put $$M_z=\operatorname{rad}|h|  \prod_{\substack{p\le z\\p\nmid h}}p^2.  \label{eq:tpc33-small-prime-modulus}$$ Let $N_z(T)$ count $T<t\le2T$ for which $(t,h)=1$ and neither form is divisible by $p^2$ for any $p\le z$, $p\nmid h$. The conditions are periodic modulo $M_z$. By the Chinese remainder theorem and [\[lem:tpc33-local-squarefree-factors\]](TPC-033.md#ref-64783b076c6099d7), $$N_z(T)=\mathfrak S_z(a,s;h)T+O(M_z),  \label{eq:tpc33-truncated-sieve-count}$$ where $\mathfrak S_z$ is the product in [\[eq:tpc33-squarefree-singular-series\]](TPC-033.md#ref-82fa94cd3b48eca2) truncated at primes at most $z$, together with all fixed factors $p\mid h$. Absolute convergence gives, uniformly in $a,s$, $$|\mathfrak S_z(a,s;h)-\mathfrak S_{\rm sf}(a,s;h)|  \ll_h\sum_{p>z}\frac1{p^2}  \ll_h\frac1z.  \label{eq:tpc33-singular-series-tail}$$
>
> It remains to remove integers for which a prime $p>z$ has $p^2\mid D_tU_t$. If $p\mid s$, the congruence $p^2\mid D_t$ has no solution; otherwise it occupies at most one class modulo $p^2$. The analogous statement holds for $U_t$. By [\[eq:tpc33-affine-height\]](TPC-033.md#ref-578ba2fbf99b060e), a relevant prime satisfies $p\ll_h\sqrt{KT}$. Therefore $$\begin{aligned}  |N_z(T)-E_{a,s,h}(T)|  &\ll_h  \sum_{z<p\ll_h\sqrt{KT}}  \left(\frac{T}{p^2}+1\right)  \notag\\  &\ll_h\frac{T}{z}+\sqrt{KT}.  \label{eq:tpc33-large-square-tail}\end{aligned}$$
>
> For all sufficiently large $T$, Chebyshev’s estimate $\sum_{p\le z}\log p\ll z$ permits the choice $z=c\log(2T)$, with a sufficiently small absolute $c>0$, for which $M_z\ll_h T^{1/2}$; the remaining bounded range is absorbed by the implied constant. Combining [\[eq:tpc33-truncated-sieve-count\]](TPC-033.md#ref-47edd0359a8d9c43)– [\[eq:tpc33-large-square-tail\]](TPC-033.md#ref-6e35739bd5fc309b), and absorbing the harmless $T^{1/2}$ term, proves [\[eq:tpc33-squarefree-asymptotic\]](TPC-033.md#ref-5c1a49f7860945ed).
>
> For positivity, replacing a factor $1-2/p^2$ by $1-1/p^2$ can only increase the product. Hence $$\mathfrak S_{\rm sf}(a,s;h)  \ge  \prod_{p\mid h}\left(1-\frac1p\right)  \prod_{p\nmid h}\left(1-\frac2{p^2}\right)  =:c_h>0.  \label{eq:tpc33-squarefree-lower-product}$$ Finally, if $K\le T^{1-\eta}$, the second error in [\[eq:tpc33-squarefree-asymptotic\]](TPC-033.md#ref-5c1a49f7860945ed) is at most $T^{1-\eta/2}$, proving [\[eq:tpc33-squarefree-growing-slope-range\]](TPC-033.md#ref-6048adb0fae2f386).

###### ref-76dc678efa2b566d

> **Remark: What the theorem does not give** \[rem:tpc33-squarefree-not-signed\] The summand in [\[eq:tpc33-squarefree-energy\]](TPC-033.md#ref-bfd8d2903734972d) is nonnegative. The theorem proves that squarefree values persist uniformly in the stated moving-slope window; it gives no cancellation in $\sum\mu(D_t)\mu(U_t)$. In the physical high-$\beta$ columns the slope can be much larger than the affine-fiber length, so even the asymptotic range $K\le T^{1-\eta}$ need not apply.

> **Remark: Why the dyadic origin matters** For a general interval $T_0<t\le T_0+Y$, the same tail argument naturally contains $\sqrt{K(|T_0|+Y)}$, not merely $\sqrt{KY}$. The balanced dyadic formulation is therefore a genuine hypothesis rather than a cosmetic normalization.

# Structured-mask disintegration of the physical columns

We now prove the physical reduction announced in [\[eq:tpc33-intro-affine-column\]](TPC-033.md#ref-0a6812eb3ae42a86). The result uses the actual mask [\[eq:tpc33-actual-mask\]](TPC-033.md#ref-b722cac2ff4274f6). It is false as a statement about every entrywise bounded mask.

## An exact projection of the row-gcd mask

###### ref-4017787a9e93a245

For $G\ge1$, define $$\lambda_G(v)  :=\sum_{\substack{g\mid v\\g\le G}}\mu(v/g).  \label{eq:tpc33-truncated-gcd-coefficient}$$

###### ref-289bb29f0052da53

###### ref-beb727096d71a79c

###### ref-cc8711441e89c345

> **Lemma: Exact row-gcd projector** \[lem:tpc33-row-gcd-projector\] For every $d,e\in\mathbb N$, $$\boxed{  \mathbf 1_{(d,e)\le G}  =\sum_{\substack{v\mid d\\v\mid e}}\lambda_G(v).}  \label{eq:tpc33-row-gcd-projector}$$ If $e$ is squarefree, then $$\boxed{  \sum_{v\mid e}|\lambda_G(v)|  \le3^{\omega(e)}\ll_\varepsilon e^\varepsilon.}  \label{eq:tpc33-row-gcd-projector-mass}$$

> **Proof** Put $n=(d,e)$. Reversing the two divisor sums gives $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_{v\mid n}\lambda_G(v)  &=\sum_{\substack{g\mid n\\g\le G}}    \sum_{w\mid n/g}\mu(w)\\  &=\mathbf 1_{n\le G}.\end{aligned}$$ This proves [\[eq:tpc33-row-gcd-projector\]](TPC-033.md#ref-beb727096d71a79c). If $e$ is squarefree, then $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_{v\mid e}|\lambda_G(v)|  &\le\sum_{v\mid e}\sum_{g\mid v}|\mu(v/g)|  =\sum_{gw\mid e}1=3^{\omega(e)}.\end{aligned}$$ The final bound is the standard divisor estimate.

The key point is the order of use. After the opposite row is fixed, $v$ ranges only over divisors of its squarefree coordinate $e$, so the total coefficient mass is $X^{o(1)}$. Summing [\[eq:tpc33-row-gcd-projector\]](TPC-033.md#ref-beb727096d71a79c) without fixing a column would not by itself prove a small operator norm for the full mask.

> **Remark: Fixed determinant, growing slopes** The two slopes in [\[eq:tpc33-standard-affine-column\]](TPC-033.md#ref-7551a2d430e54d44) are $s$ and $a=\ell jv$. Both are coprime to $h$ and to one another, and their determinant is the fixed integer $h$. They nevertheless grow with $X$, and the interval may contain only $O(1)$ points when $sv>D$. A theorem for one fixed affine pair cannot simply be summed over these layers.

# Fixed-determinant projective Plancherel

We pass to a finite model in which the two-parameter affine coefficient family is complete. The terminology “projective Plancherel” is descriptive: the proof is finite Fourier orthogonality in the coordinates $b=a/s$ and $c=h/s$. Thus the second moment averages both the projective ratio $b$ and the nonzero affine offset $c$; it gives no almost-every-$b$ statement by itself. The useful feature here is its specialization to one nonzero determinant fiber and the explicit defect left by homogeneous dilations.

###### ref-4d2a9bad0f1944f1

###### ref-3ef0e777437ebdee

###### ref-15060f392a9d1795

Let $q$ be prime, let $h\in\mathbb F_q^\times$, and let $f,g:\mathbb F_q\to\mathbb C$. We use the unnormalized conventions $$\begin{aligned}  \bar f&=\frac1q\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_q}f(x),  &f^\circ&=f-\bar f,  \label{eq:tpc33-finite-centering}\\  \|f\|_2^2&=\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_q}|f(x)|^2,  &\widehat f(r)&=\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_q}f(x)e_q(-rx),  \label{eq:tpc33-finite-normalization}\end{aligned}$$ where $e_q(z)=\exp(2\pi iz/q)$. For $a,s\in\mathbb F_q^\times$, define $$\mathcal C_{a,s}^{(h)}(f,g)  :=\sum_{D\in\mathbb F_q}  f(D)g\!\left(s^{-1}(aD+h)\right).  \label{eq:tpc33-projective-correlation}$$ This is the correlation along the affine line $sU-aD=h$.

###### ref-599d48981f696349

###### ref-13f26a1f476d6be2

###### ref-03dce2a4462e973c

> **Theorem: Exact determinant-fiber second moment** \[thm:tpc33-projective-plancherel\] With the preceding conventions, $$\begin{aligned}  &\boxed{  \sum_{a,s\in\mathbb F_q^\times}  \left|   \mathcal C_{a,s}^{(h)}(f,g)-q\bar f\bar g  \right|^2}  \notag\\[-0.2em]  &\boxed{\qquad  =q\|f^\circ\|_2^2\|g^\circ\|_2^2  -\sum_{b\in\mathbb F_q^\times}   \left|\sum_{D\in\mathbb F_q}f^\circ(D)g^\circ(bD)\right|^2.}  \label{eq:tpc33-projective-plancherel}\end{aligned}$$ In particular, $$\boxed{  \sum_{a,s\in\mathbb F_q^\times}  \left|   \mathcal C_{a,s}^{(h)}(f,g)-q\bar f\bar g  \right|^2  \le q\|f^\circ\|_2^2\|g^\circ\|_2^2.}  \label{eq:tpc33-projective-upper-bound}$$

###### ref-1cc7352e316ed2be

###### ref-8c610de5573262f4

###### ref-76836681b947c9ac

###### ref-c1b8488f01ffcd01

> **Proof** Put $$b=a/s,\qquad c=h/s.  \label{eq:tpc33-projective-coordinates}$$ Because $h\ne0$, the map $(a,s)\mapsto(b,c)$ is a bijection of $(\mathbb F_q^\times)^2$: its inverse is $s=h/c$, $a=bs$. Centering both functions gives $$\mathcal C_{a,s}^{(h)}(f,g)-q\bar f\bar g  =R_{b,c},  \qquad  R_{b,c}:=\sum_Df^\circ(D)g^\circ(bD+c).  \label{eq:tpc33-centered-affine-correlation}$$ The mixed term containing $g^\circ$ vanishes because an affine permutation preserves its zero sum, while the other vanishes because $\sum_D f^\circ(D)=0$.
>
> Temporarily include $c=0$. Fourier transformation in $c$ gives $$\widehat R_b(r)  :=\sum_{c\in\mathbb F_q}R_{b,c}e_q(-rc)  =\widehat f^\circ(-br)\widehat g^\circ(r).  \label{eq:tpc33-R-fourier}$$ Parseval and $\widehat f^\circ(0)=\widehat g^\circ(0)=0$ imply $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_{b\ne0}\sum_{c\in\mathbb F_q}|R_{b,c}|^2  &=\frac1q\sum_{r\ne0}|\widehat g^\circ(r)|^2    \sum_{b\ne0}|\widehat f^\circ(-br)|^2  \notag\\  &=\frac1q    \left(\sum_{\rho\ne0}|\widehat f^\circ(\rho)|^2\right)    \left(\sum_{r\ne0}|\widehat g^\circ(r)|^2\right)  \notag\\  &=q\|f^\circ\|_2^2\|g^\circ\|_2^2.  \label{eq:tpc33-complete-affine-energy}\end{aligned}$$ The determinant-$h$ family has $c\ne0$. Subtracting its omitted $c=0$ energy, $$\sum_{b\ne0}|R_{b,0}|^2  =\sum_{b\ne0}    \left|\sum_Df^\circ(D)g^\circ(bD)\right|^2,$$ proves [\[eq:tpc33-projective-plancherel\]](TPC-033.md#ref-13f26a1f476d6be2). Dropping this nonnegative term proves [\[eq:tpc33-projective-upper-bound\]](TPC-033.md#ref-03dce2a4462e973c).

> **Remark: The dilation-alignment defect** The subtracted term is the complete energy of the homogeneous lines $U=bD$. Thus the identity can be strictly smaller than its upper bound when $f^\circ$ and a multiplicative dilation of $g^\circ$ align. No randomness assumption is made.

###### ref-5cc2d7c0b4bba791

###### ref-87288af5db38e08b

> **Corollary: Exceptional determinant-fiber pairs** \[cor:tpc33-exceptional-projective-directions\] For every $\Lambda>0$, $$\begin{aligned}  &\#\left\{(a,s)\in(\mathbb F_q^\times)^2:  \left|\mathcal C_{a,s}^{(h)}(f,g)-q\bar f\bar g\right|>\Lambda  \right\}  \notag\\  &\qquad\le  \frac{q\|f^\circ\|_2^2\|g^\circ\|_2^2}{\Lambda^2}.  \label{eq:tpc33-projective-exception-count}\end{aligned}$$

> **Proof** Apply Chebyshev’s inequality to [\[eq:tpc33-projective-upper-bound\]](TPC-033.md#ref-03dce2a4462e973c).

The corollary is an average over the two-dimensional coefficient family $(a,s)$, equivalently over $(b,c)$. It gives no pointwise cancellation for one prescribed physical pair $(a,s)$, nor an almost-every-$b$ conclusion, unless an additional argument controls the remaining coordinate.

# Finite Möbius slopes and prime-product lifts

We derive two consequences of the exact second moment. The first is an unconditional almost-all-pair theorem for a finite Möbius model. The second verifies that a complete product slope $a=\ell j$ does not lose projective coverage merely because $\ell$ is restricted.

## A finite Möbius model

###### ref-55160dcfc537f219

###### ref-ba1b30a81b731e1e

###### ref-715d3f79a698a66e

For a prime $q$, define $\mu_q:\mathbb F_q\to\mathbb R$ by $$\mu_q(0)=0,\qquad  \mu_q(n)=\mu(n),\qquad 1\le n<q,  \label{eq:tpc33-finite-mobius-model}$$ using the standard representatives. Let $$M(x)=\sum_{n\le x}\mu(n).  \label{eq:tpc33-Mertens-function}$$ Then $$\overline{\mu_q}=\frac{M(q-1)}q,  \qquad  \|\mu_q^\circ\|_2^2\le q.  \label{eq:tpc33-mobius-mean-energy}$$

###### ref-1787d0ad0818815b

###### ref-116bba47f8ad596f

###### ref-d75b368cab7a0925

###### ref-65c545fff01169f6

> **Theorem: Almost every finite determinant-fiber Möbius pair** \[thm:tpc33-mobius-almost-all-slopes\] Fix $0<\eta<1/2$. For every prime $q$ and every $h\in\mathbb F_q^\times$, outside a set $\mathcal E_{q,h,\eta}\subset(\mathbb F_q^\times)^2$ satisfying $$|\mathcal E_{q,h,\eta}|\le q^{2-2\eta},  \label{eq:tpc33-mobius-exception-set}$$ one has $$\boxed{  \mathcal C_{a,s}^{(h)}(\mu_q,\mu_q)  =\frac{M(q-1)^2}{q}+O(q^{1/2+\eta}).}  \label{eq:tpc33-mobius-almost-all-formula}$$ Consequently, $$\mathcal C_{a,s}^{(h)}(\mu_q,\mu_q)=o(q)  \label{eq:tpc33-mobius-almost-all-cancellation}$$ for a proportion $1-o(1)$ of coefficient pairs $(a,s)$ as $q\to\infty$.

> **Proof** Apply [\[cor:tpc33-exceptional-projective-directions\]](TPC-033.md#ref-5cc2d7c0b4bba791) with $f=g=\mu_q$ and $\Lambda=q^{1/2+\eta}$. By [\[eq:tpc33-mobius-mean-energy\]](TPC-033.md#ref-715d3f79a698a66e), the total variance is at most $q^3$, so the exceptional count is at most $q^3/q^{1+2\eta}=q^{2-2\eta}$. The centered main term is $$q\overline{\mu_q}^{\,2}=\frac{M(q-1)^2}{q},$$ which proves [\[eq:tpc33-mobius-almost-all-formula\]](TPC-033.md#ref-d75b368cab7a0925). The prime number theorem is equivalent to $M(x)=o(x)$, so this main term is $o(q)$; the error is also $o(q)$ because $\eta<1/2$.

> **Remark: Model boundary** The map $n\mapsto\mu_q(n)$ is not a periodic multiplicative function on the integers; it is the finite vector obtained by truncating the ordinary Möbius function before $q$. The theorem is therefore a modular model for determinant-fiber coefficient averaging, not a theorem about one integer affine fiber.

## Complete and incomplete product slopes

Let $\mathcal L\subset\mathbb F_q^\times$ be arbitrary. For a fixed $\ell\in\mathcal L$, multiplication by $\ell$ permutes $\mathbb F_q^\times$.

###### ref-c8cee4b8025278d8

###### ref-04e131353715feb8

> **Proposition: Exact product-slope lift** \[prop:tpc33-product-slope-lift\] For every $f,g:\mathbb F_q\to\mathbb C$, $$\begin{aligned}  &\boxed{  \sum_{\ell\in\mathcal L}  \sum_{j,s\in\mathbb F_q^\times}  \left|   \mathcal C_{\ell j,s}^{(h)}(f,g)-q\bar f\bar g  \right|^2}  \notag\\[-0.2em]  &\boxed{\qquad  =|\mathcal L|  \sum_{a,s\in\mathbb F_q^\times}  \left|   \mathcal C_{a,s}^{(h)}(f,g)-q\bar f\bar g  \right|^2.}  \label{eq:tpc33-product-slope-lift}\end{aligned}$$ In particular, restricting $\mathcal L$ to prime residue classes causes no coverage loss when $j$ runs through a complete nonzero residue system.

> **Proof** For each fixed $\ell\ne0$, the substitution $a=\ell j$ is a bijection of $\mathbb F_q^\times$. Apply it inside the $j$-sum and then sum over $\ell$.

###### ref-4ca741a2415e6502

For an incomplete integer set $\mathcal J$, let $$\nu_{\mathcal J}(r)=\#\{j\in\mathcal J:j\equiv r\pmod q\},  \qquad  \nu_{\mathcal J}^*=\max_{r\in\mathbb F_q^\times}\nu_{\mathcal J}(r),  \label{eq:tpc33-residue-multiplicity}$$ and define $\nu_{\mathcal S}^*$ similarly. We restrict to elements nonzero modulo $q$.

###### ref-c3251ae392c41d6c

###### ref-b9aa88eb1861ac73

###### ref-dbbc396eb157274a

###### ref-1c26eaba4120bd8d

> **Corollary: Incomplete product-slope energy** \[cor:tpc33-incomplete-product-slope\] For finite integer sets $\mathcal J,\mathcal S$ with no multiples of $q$, $$\begin{aligned}  &\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal L}\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}  \left|\mathcal C_{\ell j,s}^{(h)}(f,g)-q\bar f\bar g\right|^2  \notag\\  &\qquad\le  |\mathcal L|\nu_{\mathcal J}^*\nu_{\mathcal S}^*  q\|f^\circ\|_2^2\|g^\circ\|_2^2.  \label{eq:tpc33-incomplete-product-slope-energy}\end{aligned}$$ If $\mathcal J,\mathcal S$ are intervals of respective lengths $J,S$, then $$\nu_{\mathcal J}^*\le J/q+1,  \qquad  \nu_{\mathcal S}^*\le S/q+1.  \label{eq:tpc33-interval-residue-multiplicity}$$ After division by $|\mathcal L|JS$, the rms loss relative to complete residue averaging is at most $$\boxed{  \left(1+\frac qJ\right)^{1/2}  \left(1+\frac qS\right)^{1/2}.}  \label{eq:tpc33-incomplete-rms-loss}$$

> **Proof** Group $j,s$ by their nonzero residues. Each pair is repeated at most $\nu_{\mathcal J}^*\nu_{\mathcal S}^*$ times. Apply [\[prop:tpc33-product-slope-lift\]](TPC-033.md#ref-c8cee4b8025278d8) and then [\[eq:tpc33-projective-upper-bound\]](TPC-033.md#ref-03dce2a4462e973c). The interval bounds are elementary. Dividing the resulting mean square by the complete-residue mean-square normalization introduces an additional factor $(q-1)/q\le1$; discarding it gives [\[eq:tpc33-incomplete-rms-loss\]](TPC-033.md#ref-1c26eaba4120bd8d).

The exact lift is useful only after the orbit variable covers residue classes economically. When $q\gg J$, the first factor in [\[eq:tpc33-incomplete-rms-loss\]](TPC-033.md#ref-1c26eaba4120bd8d) is large; this is the coverage side of the single-modulus obstruction proved later.

# The physical $Q^3$ column-energy gate

The affine disintegration identifies the correct inner sequences. We now state exactly how much collective cancellation is needed from them. The outer row coefficient must not be included twice in the column energy.

## The two polarized column energies

###### ref-ad238042d1148e27

###### ref-a972c49902061cfc

###### ref-0cf30d456899af02

###### ref-a0a91f4a7237f662

###### ref-85928a56df28e7c7

###### ref-b92970af974ca00e

For a fixed right row $\gamma$, define the left-ultra column $$B_\gamma^L  :=\sum_\alpha\gamma_\alpha^{(1)}  \sum_j\mathfrak A_{\alpha,\gamma}^{\rm act}(j)  C_{m_\alpha}(j)\mathsf H_{m_\gamma}(j),  \label{eq:tpc33-left-column}$$ and its energy $$E_L:=\sum_\gamma|B_\gamma^L|^2.  \label{eq:tpc33-left-column-energy}$$ The coefficient $\gamma_\gamma^{(2)}$ is outside $B_\gamma^L$. Symmetrically, for a fixed left row $\alpha$, put $$\begin{aligned}  B_\alpha^R  &:=\sum_\gamma\gamma_\gamma^{(2)}  \sum_j\mathfrak A_{\alpha,\gamma}^{\rm act}(j)  \mathsf H_{m_\alpha}(j)C_{m_\gamma}(j),  \label{eq:tpc33-right-column}\\  E_R&:=\sum_\alpha|B_\alpha^R|^2.  \label{eq:tpc33-right-column-energy}\end{aligned}$$ Here $\gamma_\alpha^{(1)}$ is outside $B_\alpha^R$. Define the two scalar polarizations $$\mathcal S_L=\sum_\gamma\gamma_\gamma^{(2)}B_\gamma^L,  \qquad  \mathcal S_R=\sum_\alpha\gamma_\alpha^{(1)}B_\alpha^R.  \label{eq:tpc33-scalar-polarizations}$$ The exact identity [\[eq:tpc33-matched-polarization\]](TPC-033.md#ref-0cc34bed74faa0e9) gives $$\mathcal S_{\rm sh}^{\rm all}=\mathcal S_L+\mathcal S_R.  \label{eq:tpc33-shell-as-column-sum}$$

###### ref-9ab26231f1f04009

###### ref-93b689ba666a5583

###### ref-1aeee9bcc4a6115c

###### ref-6df34d26cdf991d9

> **Theorem: Matched polarized column-energy transfer** \[thm:tpc33-column-energy-transfer\] On the physical packet of [2](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-033.md#src-939b2e5093d4b77a), $$\boxed{  |\mathcal S_{\rm sh}^{\rm all}|  \ll_{\mathscr D}  \sqrt{Q\log(2L)}  \left(\sqrt{E_L}+\sqrt{E_R}\right).}  \label{eq:tpc33-exact-energy-transfer}$$ Consequently, the additional estimate $$\boxed{  E_L+E_R\ll_{\varepsilon,h,\mathscr D}X^\varepsilon Q^3}  \label{eq:tpc33-Q3-energy-input}$$ for every fixed $\varepsilon>0$ implies $$\boxed{  |\mathcal S_{\rm sh}^{\rm all}|  \ll_{\varepsilon,h,\mathscr D}X^\varepsilon Q^2.}  \label{eq:tpc33-Q2-shell-output}$$

###### ref-c54892ff18763e87

###### ref-9690307f26758a7e

> **Proof** By [\[eq:tpc33-scalar-polarizations\]](TPC-033.md#ref-85928a56df28e7c7), Cauchy–Schwarz, and the inherited row energy [\[eq:tpc33-row-l2\]](TPC-033.md#ref-2f0c3efa1cce8517), $$\begin{aligned}  |\mathcal S_L|  &\le  \left(\sum_\gamma|\gamma_\gamma^{(2)}|^2\right)^{1/2}  \left(\sum_\gamma|B_\gamma^L|^2\right)^{1/2}  \notag\\  &\ll_{\mathscr D}\sqrt{Q\log(2L)}\sqrt{E_L},  \label{eq:tpc33-left-CS}\end{aligned}$$ and similarly $$|\mathcal S_R|  \ll_{\mathscr D}\sqrt{Q\log(2L)}\sqrt{E_R}.  \label{eq:tpc33-right-CS}$$ Use [\[eq:tpc33-shell-as-column-sum\]](TPC-033.md#ref-b92970af974ca00e) and the triangle inequality to obtain [\[eq:tpc33-exact-energy-transfer\]](TPC-033.md#ref-93b689ba666a5583). If [\[eq:tpc33-Q3-energy-input\]](TPC-033.md#ref-1aeee9bcc4a6115c) holds, then $$\sqrt{E_L}+\sqrt{E_R}  \le\sqrt{2(E_L+E_R)}  \ll X^{\varepsilon/2}Q^{3/2}.$$ Applying the input with a smaller soft exponent and absorbing $\log(2L)$ proves [\[eq:tpc33-Q2-shell-output\]](TPC-033.md#ref-6df34d26cdf991d9).

> **Remark: Sufficient, not equivalent** The estimate [\[eq:tpc33-Q3-energy-input\]](TPC-033.md#ref-1aeee9bcc4a6115c) controls the two polarizations separately. It is a clean sufficient condition for the matched scalar shell, but cancellation between $\mathcal S_L$ and $\mathcal S_R$ could make the scalar sum smaller even when one energy is large. No necessity or equivalence is asserted.

> **Corollary: Conditional zero-frequency closure** \[cor:tpc33-zero-frequency-closure\] Suppose $J\ll_{\mathscr D}Q$. # Sharp obstructions and the existing-theorem boundary

The preceding theorems create an average route around pointwise affine cancellation. They do not make a prescribed direction small. We record three exact obstructions before comparing the physical requirement with existing Chowla–Elliott technology.

## Three sharp interface obstructions

###### ref-077ee6d84d6cac66

###### ref-ac93cc2a9849718f

###### ref-a0c617ceac9a62f2

> **Proposition: A fixed direction contains two-point Chowla** \[prop:tpc33-chowla-subcase\] The family $sU-aD=h$ contains the shift-one Möbius correlation. In particular, take $$a=s=h=1,\qquad x=1,\qquad y=0.  \label{eq:tpc33-shift-one-parameters}$$ Then $$D_t=t,\qquad U_t=t+1,\qquad  \mu(D_t)\mu(U_t)=\mu(t)\mu(t+1).  \label{eq:tpc33-shift-one-correlation}$$ Thus an unweighted $o(T)$ theorem uniform over every individual affine direction would imply the still open two-point Möbius Chowla statement.

> **Proof** The Bézout relation is $sx-ay=1$. Substitution in [\[eq:tpc33-canonical-affine-forms\]](TPC-033.md#ref-acd9338d5cc8da1f) gives [\[eq:tpc33-shift-one-correlation\]](TPC-033.md#ref-a0c617ceac9a62f2).

The next example shows why separate Fourier pseudorandomness of the two legs cannot control a prescribed pairing.

###### ref-d39be45f1a6d1a1d

###### ref-a7af928dabcc62c7

###### ref-589cf58588cc9615

> **Proposition: Fourier-flat legs with a coherent line** \[prop:tpc33-coherent-line-counterexample\] Let $q$ be an odd prime, fix $a_0,s_0,h\in\mathbb F_q^\times$, and let $\chi$ be the quadratic character with $\chi(0)=0$. Define $$f(D)=\chi(D),  \qquad  g(U)=\chi\!\left(a_0^{-1}(s_0U-h)\right).  \label{eq:tpc33-legendre-coherent-functions}$$ Then $\bar f=\bar g=0$, and every nonzero additive Fourier coefficient of either function has modulus $\sqrt q$. Nevertheless, $$\boxed{  \mathcal C_{a_0,s_0}^{(h)}(f,g)=q-1.}  \label{eq:tpc33-legendre-coherent-line}$$

> **Proof** Both functions are affine transforms of the quadratic character, so their means vanish and their nonzero Fourier coefficients are quadratic Gauss sums of modulus $\sqrt q$. On the selected line $U=s_0^{-1}(a_0D+h)$, $$g(U)=\chi\!\left(a_0^{-1}(a_0D+h-h)\right)=\chi(D)=f(D).$$ Hence the correlation is $\sum_D\chi(D)^2=q-1$.

This counterexample concerns an abstract two-leg Fourier interface. It does not assert that the Möbius function possesses the same coherent structure; it proves that one-leg Fourier bounds alone cannot rule it out.

###### ref-646d54e53cacd55f

> **Proposition: The single-modulus no-wrap conflict** \[prop:tpc33-single-modulus-conflict\] On the high-$\beta$ physical packet, a single prime modulus cannot both turn the modular determinant condition into an exact integer equation on the ambient affine box and provide complete orbit-slope coverage.

###### ref-3e6151e713ec8fff

> **Proof** On a projected physical layer, the slopes are $s$ and $a=\ell jv$, while the $\widetilde d$-interval has ambient length $\asymp D/v$. Therefore the determinant expression varies on the scale $$a\frac Dv=\ell jD\asymp QJ\asymp X.  \label{eq:tpc33-ambient-determinant-span}$$ An elementary no-wrap lift from $sU-aD\equiv h\pmod q$ to equality throughout this ambient box requires $q\gg X$. In contrast, complete coverage by the orbit variable $j$ requires $q\lesssim J$; otherwise the rms factor in [\[eq:tpc33-incomplete-rms-loss\]](TPC-033.md#ref-1c26eaba4120bd8d) contains $(q/J)^{1/2}$. At the physical endpoint $J=X^{133/400+o(1)}\ll X$, these requirements are incompatible.

The proposition rules out only the most direct one-modulus transplantation of [\[thm:tpc33-projective-plancherel\]](TPC-033.md#ref-599d48981f696349). It does not rule out a multi-modulus circle method, a delta symbol, a spectral decomposition, or a direct integer large-sieve inequality for the restricted slope set.

Two further nearby boundaries are worth recording. Teräväinen proves a fixed gap from the trivial bound for certain roots-of-unity-valued multiplicative functions on fixed nonproportional affine forms [`\citep[Thm.~2.6]{Teravainen2024PolynomialArguments}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-033.md#cite-e60cdd94ef6016b7); this is not a mean-zero theorem, and the roots-of-unity hypothesis does not directly permit $\mu$, which also takes the value zero. Pilatte proves a quantitative logarithmic shift-one result for the Liouville function [`\citep[Thm.~1.1]{Pilatte2025LogChowla}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-033.md#cite-fda6ddbc776fa413); it is a preprint result for a particular fixed pair, not a general growing-coefficient dyadic Möbius theorem.

In summary, no cited theorem simultaneously supplies:

1.  polynomially growing affine coefficients;

2.  cancellation on every fixed dyadic shell;

3.  averaging along the fixed-determinant constrained family;

4.  the actual joint row mask and physical weights; and

5.  a power ledger strong enough for [\[eq:tpc33-Q3-energy-input\]](TPC-033.md#ref-1aeee9bcc4a6115c).

These requirements remain an additional analytic input. The projective identity in this paper identifies one viable averaged geometry, but does not claim that existing Chowla–Elliott theorems already close it. The distinction between sign-sensitive prime detection and sign-blind sieve information is the classical parity boundary [`\citep{HeathBrown1982Parity}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-033.md#cite-bd33f7374ef1e88f).

# The high-$\beta$ ledger and the next gate

We now specialize the exact interfaces to the source-compatible endpoint of TPC-28 [`\citep{WangTPC28}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-033.md#cite-1f714b6620a45b76). The purpose is to measure what one-fiber cancellation can and cannot accomplish, and to state the next target without implying that it has been proved.

## Physical exponents

###### ref-a60c62858a91af7a

###### ref-8be1f70f1c37dd0a

###### ref-b1be8464ba54a316

###### ref-15071fbaa2c6bd47

###### ref-32028ffa308dbd44

###### ref-f6c6dfaa0f76605d

###### ref-660a903e73bf469b

###### ref-5fba13104d2b5a97

The row, orbit, prime-source, and opened-divisor exponents are $$\begin{aligned}  \beta&=\frac{267}{400},  &Q&=X^{267/400+o(1)},  &J&=X^{133/400+o(1)},  \label{eq:tpc33-high-QJ}\\  \lambda&=\frac{99979}{210000},  &\ell&=X^{\lambda+o(1)},  &d_*&=\frac{10049}{52500},  \label{eq:tpc33-high-LD}\\  D&=X^{d_*+o(1)},  &T&=X^{193/500+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc33-high-DT}\end{aligned}$$ The exponent $\lambda$ here is unrelated to the finite projector coefficient $\lambda_G(v)$ from [5](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-033.md#src-b914df2280b552da). Here $Q=LD$ and $QJ\asymp X$. In an unprojected affine column, $$\ell j  =X^{\lambda+133/400+o(1)}  =X^{42451/52500+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc33-high-ellj}$$ The reflected complement satisfies $$s\ll X/T=X^{307/500+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc33-high-s-range}$$ After the row-gcd projection, the second slope is $$a=\ell jv,\qquad v\mid e,\qquad  a\le X^{1+o(1)},  \label{eq:tpc33-high-projected-slope}$$ because $$\frac{42451}{52500}+\frac{10049}{52500}=1.  \label{eq:tpc33-high-slope-sum}$$ The corresponding affine fiber has length $$|I|\ll1+X^{d_*+o(1)}/(sv).  \label{eq:tpc33-high-fiber-length}$$ Thus many slope layers are arithmetically nondegenerate but contain only $O(1)$ points. Nonzero determinant alone does not create a long average.

## Why square root on every long fiber is still insufficient

###### ref-c2209f23cc63f275

###### ref-a162e667030a8900

###### ref-feafda4d88b7f0af

The longest possible affine fiber has length $D$, attained only when the product $sv$ is bounded. Suppose, optimistically, that every such fiber could be bounded with square-root cancellation while all outer variables were reassembled absolutely. The largest relative saving visible to that strategy would be $$D^{-1/2}  =X^{-10049/105000+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc33-single-fiber-sqrt-saving}$$ Indeed, if the fiber lengths are $n_i\le D$, then after absolute reassembly the ratio of square-root bounds to trivial masses is a weighted average of $n_i^{-1/2}$, and hence cannot improve on $D^{-1/2}$. The required amplitude saving is $$J^{-1}=X^{-133/400+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc33-required-J-saving}$$ The exact missing exponent is $$\boxed{  \frac{133}{400}-\frac{10049}{105000}  =\frac{49727}{210000}  =0.236795\ldots.}  \label{eq:tpc33-single-fiber-gap}$$

###### ref-af92de9bdcb92547

This is a limitation of a specified proof strategy, not a lower bound for the actual Möbius sum. It proves that even a pointwise square-root theorem for each longest fiber cannot close the endpoint if every remaining index is treated by the triangle inequality. The missing gain must use collective averaging across some of $$\ell,\quad j,\quad s,\quad v,\quad  \text{and the opposite row}.  \label{eq:tpc33-outer-average-variables}$$

## The restricted physical projective-energy problem

###### ref-dfbe1e07ac0c0932

The next analytic target is exactly $$\boxed{  E_L+E_R\ll_{\varepsilon,h,\mathscr D}X^\varepsilon Q^3,}  \label{eq:tpc33-next-projective-energy}$$ with $E_L,E_R$ defined in [\[eq:tpc33-left-column-energy\]](TPC-033.md#ref-a972c49902061cfc) and [\[eq:tpc33-right-column-energy\]](TPC-033.md#ref-a0a91f4a7237f662), and with each inner column expanded as in [\[thm:tpc33-actual-mask-column\]](TPC-033.md#ref-c7e81a715d7b0aca). The estimate must retain:

1.  both exact polarizations;

2.  the actual row coefficients and row-gcd projection;

3.  the fixed-period and smooth orbit factors;

4.  the $+1$ occupancy in [\[eq:tpc33-high-fiber-length\]](TPC-033.md#ref-5fba13104d2b5a97); and

5.  the exceptional coefficient pairs allowed by determinant-fiber averaging.

There are several nonexclusive routes to [\[eq:tpc33-next-projective-energy\]](TPC-033.md#ref-dfbe1e07ac0c0932):

1.  prove an integer restricted-projective large sieve directly for $a=\ell jv$ and the physical $s$-range;

2.  use a multi-modulus delta/circle decomposition to separate no-wrap from slope coverage;

3.  expose a genuine second long variable and apply a correlation theorem in two variables before taking absolute values; or

4.  split long and sparse fibers and combine a $TT^*$ estimate on the long range with an incidence-energy estimate on the $O(1)$-point range.

Each route requires a new norm and conductor ledger. The present paper does not select one as already successful.

## Endpoint consequences if the gate is proved

###### ref-5a4ce693d3254c45

At [\[eq:tpc33-high-QJ\]](TPC-033.md#ref-a60c62858a91af7a), $$Q^2=N_0J^{-1}  =N_0X^{-133/400+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc33-high-Q2-saving}$$ Thus [\[eq:tpc33-next-projective-energy\]](TPC-033.md#ref-dfbe1e07ac0c0932), together with [\[thm:tpc33-column-energy-transfer\]](TPC-033.md#ref-9ab26231f1f04009), gives every strict saving below $133/400$ for the matched raw shell. The large-content correction at $C=J$ has the same $Q^2$ size.

###### ref-433518ecf51a3899

The drift term is smaller still. From [\[eq:tpc33-high-QJ\]](TPC-033.md#ref-a60c62858a91af7a)–[\[eq:tpc33-high-LD\]](TPC-033.md#ref-8be1f70f1c37dd0a), $$\frac{XQ/L}{Q^2}  =\frac JL  =X^{-15077/105000+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc33-high-drift-ratio}$$ Consequently the calibrated shell would also be $O(X^{o(1)}Q^2)$.

Even that conditional endpoint closes only the residual packet inherited from the preceding reductions. It does not supply the independent positivity and main-term reconstruction needed for a prime-pair theorem. The next gate is therefore substantial but sharply localized: it is a restricted projective $L^2$ estimate, not a disguised assertion that the twin-prime conjecture has already been proved.

## What is not certified

Finite enumeration verifies algebra and exact bookkeeping, not an asymptotic analytic estimate. In particular, the certificate does not:

1.  certify the error term in [\[eq:tpc33-squarefree-asymptotic\]](TPC-033.md#ref-5c1a49f7860945ed); that term is proved in the text by a CRT sieve and a large-square tail;

2.  prove signed cancellation in any growing affine Möbius family;

3.  prove the physical $Q^3$ input [\[eq:tpc33-Q3-energy-input\]](TPC-033.md#ref-1aeee9bcc4a6115c);

4.  prove the zero-frequency flatness ratio of TPC-32;

5.  close every residual packet in the earlier prime-pair reduction;

6.  prove positivity or a Hardy–Littlewood asymptotic;

7.  prove infinitely many twin primes; or

8.  break the sieve parity barrier.

The proved statements are the integral normal form, its squarefree-support theorem, the actual-mask column disintegration, the finite projective second moment and its corollaries, the exact conditional $Q^3$-to-$Q^2$ transfer, and the sharp obstructions. The remaining mathematical task is [\[eq:tpc33-next-projective-energy\]](TPC-033.md#ref-dfbe1e07ac0c0932), together with all later positivity and reconstruction stages. This delineates the proved, conditional, and open parts of the argument.
