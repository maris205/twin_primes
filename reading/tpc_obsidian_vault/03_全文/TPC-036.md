## Abstract

The preceding orbit–sliced analysis leaves a concrete interface question: can the actual row-pair multiplier be reassembled into separable layers at only $X^{o(1)}$ cost, without destroying the product slope that carries the multiplicative spectrum? We answer the static part affirmatively and locate the next obstruction exactly.

For a finite kernel $K$, let $\pi_{\infty}(K)$ be its bounded projective norm. The physical row mask is $$\mathfrak m_{\rm act}(\alpha,\gamma)  =\mathbf 1_{\ell_\alpha\ne\ell_\gamma}   \mathbf 1_{|m_\alpha-m_\gamma|>\Delta}   \mathbf 1_{(d_\alpha,d_\gamma)\le G}.$$ The same-source deletion has projective cost at most $2$. The near-row deletion is a finite interval kernel and has cost $O(\log(2Q))$. For the gcd threshold, an orthogonal random-sign compression of the exact divisor projector gives $$\pi_{\infty}\!\left(\mathbf 1_{(d,e)\le G}\right)  \le(3+2\sqrt2)^{\omega_*},  \qquad  \omega_*:=\max_{\text{physical }d}\omega(d).$$ Consequently $$\boxed{  \pi_{\infty}(\mathfrak m_{\rm act})  \ll\log(2Q)(3+2\sqrt2)^{\omega_*}=X^{o(1)}.}$$ Together with the previously proved finite-residue and Mellin separations, this closes the static controlled-multiplier boundary in the retained TPC-32 factorization. It is a projective-complexity theorem, not an operator-norm bound for the physical Gram matrix.

Fixing the opposite row gives a stronger product-ready form. The gcd and same-source conditions become unary weights, while the near-row cutoff is a difference of two Ferrers kernels. After splitting the $O_h(1)$ fixed orbit residue cells, finite Fourier inversion therefore decomposes each physical column exactly into $$b_\rho(\ell)f_\rho(d)\eta_\rho(j)$$ with $O(D)$ discrete layers but total signed projective and orbit-seminorm cost $X^{o(1)}$; in particular $b_\rho$ is independent of $j$ and of the complementary factor $s$. The logarithmic cost is optimal: for $T_n=(\mathbf 1_{k\le r})$, $$\pi_{\infty}(T_n)\asymp\log(2n),  \qquad  \pi_{+}(T_n)=n.$$ Thus signed Fourier reassembly is essential, whereas positive or raw column-by-column rectangle splitting is polynomially expensive.

Termwise additive-frequency separation of the product phase is costly. For $A_r(x,y)=e_q(rxy)$ on $\mathbb F_q^\times$, $r\ne0$, $$\boxed{  \pi_{\infty}(A_r)=\frac{(q-2)\sqrt q+1}{q-1}\asymp\sqrt q.}$$ By contrast the multiplication kernel $\mathbf 1_{xy=a}$ has projective cost exactly $1$. Expanding that kernel additively and reassembling its frequencies absolutely manufactures a $\sqrt q$ amplitude loss, which at $q\asymp J$ is precisely one factor $J$ in energy.

Finally, we diagonalize the coherent complementary-factor sum. For the TPC-35 product–slope operator $T$ and an arbitrary weight $w$, $$\sum_j\left|\sum_sw(s)(Tb)(j,s)\right|^2  =\frac1{q-1}\sum_\chi|\widehat b(\bar\chi)|^2  \left|\sum_sw(s)\widehat R_s(\chi)\right|^2.$$ If $f,g$ are additively centered and $w=1$, then $$\sum_sR_s(a)=qg(0)f(-h/a),$$ so complete $s$-coherence collapses to a residue-zero boundary trace. Nevertheless the factor $q-1$ from Cauchy in $s$ is sharp in the abstract centered nonprincipal class. The remaining physical gate is therefore not static-mask separation: it is the joint control of incomplete $s$-weights, integer aliases, and the actual Möbius coefficient mass in the resulting character spectrum. We prove no coefficient-specific orbit gate, affine Chowla theorem, parity breach, Hardy–Littlewood asymptotic, or twin–prime result.

## The static multiplier is softly projective

###### ref-35d9f2978d595d56

###### ref-ba50f131febdf1da

For a kernel $K$ on two finite sets, write $\pi_{\infty}(K)$ for its bounded projective norm; see [3](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-036.md#src-75757ab6246176d6). The physical static mask has three factors: $$\mathfrak m_{\rm act}(\alpha,\gamma)  =\mathbf 1_{\ell_\alpha\ne\ell_\gamma}   \mathbf 1_{|m_\alpha-m_\gamma|>\Delta}   \mathbf 1_{(d_\alpha,d_\gamma)\le G},  \qquad  \Delta=QX^{-\kappa_0},\quad G=X^{\kappa_0}.  \label{eq:tpc36-static-mask-intro}$$ The same-source factor is compressed by source orthogonality. The near-row factor is a one-dimensional interval kernel. The new step is a global orthogonal compression of the row-gcd projector. If $\omega_*=\max_\alpha\omega(d_\alpha)$, then $$\boxed{  \pi_{\infty}(\mathfrak m_{\rm act})  \ll\log(2Q)(3+2\sqrt2)^{\omega_*}=X^{o(1)}.}  \label{eq:tpc36-global-mask-intro}$$ The equality $X^{o(1)}$ uses only the standard fixed-base divisor estimate $C^{\omega(n)}\ll_{C,\varepsilon}n^\varepsilon$. It is uniform in the pruning threshold $G$.

The distinction between layer count and projective mass is essential. The exact finite representation may contain polynomially many layers, but their normalized total variation is soft. Every estimate uniform in the bounded factors may therefore be integrated without paying for the raw number of layers.

## Columnwise separation and its optimal cost

###### ref-5da4c94be5fb85dd

The orbit energy first fixes the opposite row. For $\gamma=(\ell_\gamma,e)$, the gcd cutoff in [\[eq:tpc36-static-mask-intro\]](TPC-036.md#ref-35d9f2978d595d56) is then a unary $d$-weight. Ordering the physical $d$-values turns the near-row condition into the difference of two prefix, or Ferrers, kernels. After splitting the fixed $O_h(1)$ orbit residue cells, finite Fourier inversion gives the exact representation $$\gamma_{(\ell,d)}^{(1)}  \mathfrak A^{\rm act}_{(\ell,d),\gamma}(j)  =\sum_{r\bmod H_0}\mathbf 1_{j\equiv r\!\!\!\pmod{H_0}}  \int\sum_\rho  c_{\rho,\omega,\gamma,r}  b_{\rho,\omega,\gamma,r}(\ell)  f_{\rho,\omega,\gamma,r}(d)  \eta_{\rho,\omega,\gamma,r}(j/J)\,d\nu_r(\omega),  \label{eq:tpc36-product-ready-intro}$$ with $O(D)$ discrete layers and $X^{o(1)}$ total signed orbit-seminorm cost. In particular $b(\ell)$ is independent of $j$ and of the reflected complementary factor $s$. This proves the static product-ready shape asked for in TPC-35.

## The cost of absolute additive-frequency separation

###### ref-9eba648b7626d96c

###### ref-f446dc5143398477

The product slope is not another harmless smooth factor. For prime $q$ and $r\ne0$, put $$A_r(x,y)=e_q(rxy),\qquad x,y\in\mathbb F_q^\times.  \label{eq:tpc36-product-phase-intro}$$ Multiplicative characters give a flat singular-vector basis and the exact projective norm $$\boxed{  \pi_{\infty}(A_r)=\frac{(q-2)\sqrt q+1}{q-1}.}  \label{eq:tpc36-product-phase-cost-intro}$$ On the other hand, $K_a(x,y)=\mathbf 1_{xy=a}$ is a permutation kernel and $\pi_{\infty}(K_a)=1$. The additive delta expansion of $K_a$, followed by termwise absolute reassembly of its nonzero frequencies, costs $\asymp\sqrt q$. At $q\asymp J$, this is exactly a factor $J$ after squaring. Thus the additive-frequency expansion cannot be estimated at the critical scale by summing its frequency costs separately. The multiplicative-character reconstruction keeps projective cost one.

## The coherent complementary-factor spectrum

###### ref-5b1a89852de0a9d2

TPC-35 diagonalizes the complete square energy $\sum_{j,s}|(Tb)(j,s)|^2$. The physical column first sums over the complementary factor $s$ and then squares. We prove the corresponding weighted identity. For $G_q=\mathbb F_q^\times$, any $w:G_q\to\mathbb C$, and $$S_w(j)=\sum_{s\in G_q}w(s)(Tb)(j,s),  \label{eq:tpc36-weighted-trace-intro}$$ one has $$\boxed{  \sum_j|S_w(j)|^2  =\frac1{q-1}\sum_\chi  |\widehat b(\bar\chi)|^2  \left|\sum_sw(s)\widehat R_s(\chi)\right|^2.}  \label{eq:tpc36-weighted-spectrum-intro}$$ For the complete weight $w=1$, additive centering gives the exact trace $$\sum_{s\in G_q}R_s(a)=qg(0)f(-h/a).  \label{eq:tpc36-complete-trace-intro}$$ Thus full $s$-coherence collapses to a residue-zero boundary term. # Bounded projective norms and finite Fourier compression

The relevant complexity is the normalized mass of a signed rank-one representation, not its number of summands.

## The bounded projective norm

###### ref-f7a94858aa97012a

###### ref-bfbcafb9af675368

###### ref-5b9c20d40eb0f339

> **Definition: Bounded projective norm** \[def:tpc36-projective-norm\] For finite sets $\mathcal X,\mathcal Y$ and a kernel $K:\mathcal X\times\mathcal Y\to\mathbb C$, define $$\pi_{\infty}(K)  :=\inf\int_\Omega  \|p_\omega\|_{\ell^\infty(\mathcal X)}  \|q_\omega\|_{\ell^\infty(\mathcal Y)}\,d|\nu|(\omega),  \label{eq:tpc36-projective-norm}$$ where the infimum runs over all finite signed or complex measures and representations $$K(x,y)=\int_\Omega p_\omega(x)q_\omega(y)\,d\nu(\omega).  \label{eq:tpc36-projective-representation}$$ Finite sums suffice in finite dimensions; probability-space notation is convenient for orthogonal compression.

The normalization permits the scalar coefficient of a layer to be placed in either factor or in the measure. The following properties will be used repeatedly.

###### ref-7dc57d5e78c03e91

###### ref-f88b9afba5f79dc6

###### ref-c7c138e8e7950fa4

###### ref-56f40775216a1e8c

###### ref-06f52908ce3e41c0

> **Lemma: Elementary projective calculus** \[lem:tpc36-projective-calculus\] For finite kernels $K,L$ on the same product set, $$\begin{aligned}  \|K\|_\infty&\le\pi_{\infty}(K),  \label{eq:tpc36-entry-lower}\\  \pi_{\infty}(K+L)&\le\pi_{\infty}(K)+\pi_{\infty}(L),  \label{eq:tpc36-triangle}\\  \pi_{\infty}(K\circ L)&\le\pi_{\infty}(K)\pi_{\infty}(L),  \label{eq:tpc36-schur-submultiplicativity}\end{aligned}$$ where $\circ$ is entrywise multiplication. If $K$ is viewed as an $m\times n$ matrix, then $$\boxed{  \pi_{\infty}(K)\ge\frac{\|K\|_*}{\sqrt{mn}},}  \label{eq:tpc36-nuclear-lower}$$ where $\|K\|_*$ is its nuclear norm.

> **Proof** The first two assertions follow directly from [\[eq:tpc36-projective-representation\]](TPC-036.md#ref-5b9c20d40eb0f339). Multiplying two such representations over the product measure proves [\[eq:tpc36-schur-submultiplicativity\]](TPC-036.md#ref-56f40775216a1e8c). A rank-one matrix $pq^{\mathsf T}$ has nuclear norm $\|p\|_2\|q\|_2$, which is at most $\sqrt{mn}\|p\|_\infty\|q\|_\infty$. Integrate and take the infimum.

> **Remark: Projective complexity is not a Gram estimate** The norm $\pi_{\infty}$ controls the cost of passing a bilinear estimate through a Schur multiplier. It does not imply that the multiplier matrix, or the off-diagonal Gram matrix obtained after inserting it, has small operator norm. In particular, [\[eq:tpc36-nuclear-lower\]](TPC-036.md#ref-06f52908ce3e41c0) is used below only for finite-model optimality statements.

## A discrete interval lemma

###### ref-795173b14ad5f3cc

###### ref-c03ccfa83edae62e

Write $e_M(t)=\exp(2\pi i t/M)$. For $1\le N<M/2$, define $$\kappa_N(t)=\mathbf 1_{\{0,1,\ldots,N-1\}}(t),  \qquad t\in\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z,  \label{eq:tpc36-cyclic-interval}$$ and use the normalized Fourier transform $$\widehat\kappa_N(a)  =\frac1M\sum_{t=0}^{N-1}e_M(-at).  \label{eq:tpc36-interval-Fourier}$$

###### ref-12cfe96cc77f0c0b

###### ref-43e9c840423ec63c

###### ref-64fb49222d781580

###### ref-130d991234e96421

###### ref-5595ea5bd45afb12

> **Lemma: Discrete interval Fourier mass** \[lem:tpc36-interval-Fourier\] Uniformly for $1\le N<M/2$, $$\sum_{a\bmod M}|\widehat\kappa_N(a)|  \ll1+\log(2N).  \label{eq:tpc36-interval-l1}$$ Consequently the prefix kernel $$H_N(k,r)=\mathbf 1_{1\le r\le k},  \qquad 0\le k\le N,\quad 1\le r\le N,  \label{eq:tpc36-prefix-kernel}$$ has the exact representation $$H_N(k,r)=\sum_{a\bmod M}  \widehat\kappa_N(a)e_M(ak)e_M(-ar)  \label{eq:tpc36-prefix-Fourier}$$ and satisfies $$\pi_{\infty}(H_N)\ll1+\log(2N).  \label{eq:tpc36-prefix-projective-upper}$$

###### ref-e586d3bd3348c8e4

> **Proof** For $a\ne0$, the geometric sum gives $$|\widehat\kappa_N(a)|  \le\min\left\{\frac NM,  \frac1{2\min(a,M-a)}\right\},  \label{eq:tpc36-Dirichlet-bound}$$ after taking $1\le a\le M-1$. The terms with $\min(a,M-a)\le M/N$ contribute $O(1)$; the remaining harmonic sum is $O(1+\log N)$. Fourier inversion proves [\[eq:tpc36-prefix-Fourier\]](TPC-036.md#ref-130d991234e96421), because $r\le k$ is equivalent to $k-r\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}$ when $M\ge2N+1$. The factors in that representation have modulus one, proving [\[eq:tpc36-prefix-projective-upper\]](TPC-036.md#ref-5595ea5bd45afb12).

## Finite orthogonality as compression

###### ref-4327fdc74c7d37dd

If a finite set $\mathcal I$ is injected into $\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z$, then $$\frac1M\sum_{t\bmod M}  e_M\bigl(t(\iota(v)-\iota(w))\bigr)=\mathbf 1_{v=w}.  \label{eq:tpc36-finite-orthogonality}$$ This identity will compress a sum over common divisors. The number $M$ of formal phases can be large; the normalized measure $M^{-1}\sum_t$ has mass one. Replacing [\[eq:tpc36-finite-orthogonality\]](TPC-036.md#ref-4327fdc74c7d37dd) by independent Rademacher signs gives the same projective bound.

## Same-source deletion

###### ref-15b4218efb65ef07

###### ref-e79d265cc189919d

## Orthogonal compression of the row-gcd projector

###### ref-0319d12c5cdcd88e

TPC-33 proves the exact identity $$\mathbf 1_{(d,e)\le G}  =\sum_{\substack{v\mid d\\v\mid e}}\lambda_G(v),  \qquad  \lambda_G(v)=\sum_{\substack{g\mid v\\g\le G}}\mu(v/g).  \label{eq:tpc36-gcd-projector}$$ We upgrade its fixed-column absolute complexity to a global signed projective representation.

###### ref-0f710214b5d528db

###### ref-8b035ba6d87c17f0

Let $\mathcal I_G$ be the finite set of $v$ dividing at least one physical squarefree row coordinate, and inject $\mathcal I_G$ into $\mathbb Z/M_G\mathbb Z$. Put $$\begin{aligned}  P_t(d)  &:=\sum_{v\mid d}  \operatorname{sgn}(\lambda_G(v))  \sqrt{|\lambda_G(v)|}\,e_{M_G}(t\iota(v)),  \label{eq:tpc36-gcd-P}\\  Q_t(e)  &:=\sum_{v\mid e}  \sqrt{|\lambda_G(v)|}\,e_{M_G}(-t\iota(v)),  \label{eq:tpc36-gcd-Q}\end{aligned}$$ with $\operatorname{sgn}(0)=0$.

###### ref-64fc26631cd08804

###### ref-84b87e85f87172ac

###### ref-b5a8e4f06d1c3adb

> **Theorem: Global orthogonal gcd compression** \[thm:tpc36-global-gcd\] For physical squarefree $d,e$, $$\boxed{  \mathbf 1_{(d,e)\le G}  =\frac1{M_G}\sum_{t\bmod M_G}P_t(d)Q_t(e).}  \label{eq:tpc36-global-gcd-factorization}$$ If $\omega_*=\max\{\omega(d):d\text{ occurs in the packet}\}$, then $$\boxed{  \pi_{\infty}\!\left(\mathbf 1_{(d,e)\le G}\right)  \le(3+2\sqrt2)^{\omega_*}=X^{o(1)}.}  \label{eq:tpc36-global-gcd-cost}$$

###### ref-9ffe825e8e4479d9

###### ref-4f95a94225508c45

###### ref-71dfb051b844348b

> **Proof** Insert [\[eq:tpc36-gcd-P\]](TPC-036.md#ref-0f710214b5d528db)–[\[eq:tpc36-gcd-Q\]](TPC-036.md#ref-8b035ba6d87c17f0), average over $t$, and apply [\[eq:tpc36-finite-orthogonality\]](TPC-036.md#ref-4327fdc74c7d37dd). Only equal divisors survive, giving $\sum_{v\mid d,e}\lambda_G(v)$, which is [\[eq:tpc36-gcd-projector\]](TPC-036.md#ref-0319d12c5cdcd88e).
>
> For squarefree $v$, $$|\lambda_G(v)|  \le\sum_{g\mid v}|\mu(v/g)|  =2^{\omega(v)}.  \label{eq:tpc36-lambda-bound}$$ Therefore, uniformly in $t$, $$\begin{aligned}  |P_t(d)|  &\le\sum_{v\mid d}2^{\omega(v)/2}  =(1+\sqrt2)^{\omega(d)},  \label{eq:tpc36-P-bound}\\  |Q_t(e)|  &\le(1+\sqrt2)^{\omega(e)}.  \label{eq:tpc36-Q-bound}\end{aligned}$$ The normalized counting measure in [\[eq:tpc36-global-gcd-factorization\]](TPC-036.md#ref-84b87e85f87172ac) has total mass one. Multiplying the two suprema gives $(1+\sqrt2)^{2\omega_*}=(3+2\sqrt2)^{\omega_*}$. Finally, $C^{\omega(n)}\ll_{C,\varepsilon}n^\varepsilon$ for each fixed $C>1$, and $d,e\ll X^{O(1)}$.

> **Remark: Why absolute divisor expansion looked expensive** The raw set $\mathcal I_G$ can have polynomial size. Summing one rank-one term per $v$ in absolute value ignores orthogonality between divisor coordinates. The average in [\[eq:tpc36-global-gcd-factorization\]](TPC-036.md#ref-84b87e85f87172ac) has the same number of formal phases but normalized mass one; only the divisor suprema remain.

# Fixed-column product-ready reassembly

The global theorem separates the two row labels, but a global factor may still depend jointly on the source $\ell$ and divisor coordinate $d$ through $m=\ell d$. The product–slope operator needs one further separation. Orbit slicing provides the correct order: fix the opposite row first.

## The exact Ferrers formula

###### ref-b8cd08a5ff608866

###### ref-31bfdfb79cb2a1ab

###### ref-eeb1aff3a7e2a8f9

Fix $$\gamma=(\ell_\gamma,e),\qquad n=m_\gamma=\ell_\gamma e.  \label{eq:tpc36-fixed-column}$$ Let $\mathcal D=\{d_1<\cdots<d_{N_D}\}$ be an ambient integer set containing the physical $d$-support. One may take the full dyadic integer interval, so $$N_D\ll D.  \label{eq:tpc36-divisor-count}$$ Squarefreeness, coprimality, and smooth support are retained later as unary $d$-weights. Write $r(d_k)=k$. For every source $\ell$, put $$\begin{aligned}  k_-^\gamma(\ell)  &:=\#\{d\in\mathcal D:\ell d<n-\Delta\},  \label{eq:tpc36-column-lower-rank}\\  k_+^\gamma(\ell)  &:=\#\{d\in\mathcal D:\ell d\le n+\Delta\}.  \label{eq:tpc36-column-upper-rank}\end{aligned}$$ Then $$\mathbf 1_{|\ell d-n|>\Delta}  =1+H_{N_D}(k_-^\gamma(\ell),r(d))     -H_{N_D}(k_+^\gamma(\ell),r(d)).  \label{eq:tpc36-column-prefix}$$

###### ref-18d012ba0af95d76

> **Theorem: Exact fixed-column source–divisor separation** \[thm:tpc36-fixed-column-separation\] For every fixed opposite row $\gamma$, there are coefficients and bounded functions such that $$\mathfrak m_{\rm act}((\ell,d),\gamma)  =\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal R_\gamma}  c_{\rho,\gamma}b_{\rho,\gamma}(\ell)  f_{\rho,\gamma}(d),  \label{eq:tpc36-column-separation}$$ with $$\begin{aligned}  |\mathcal R_\gamma|&\ll D,  \label{eq:tpc36-column-layer-count}\\  \sum_{\rho\in\mathcal R_\gamma}|c_{\rho,\gamma}|  \|b_{\rho,\gamma}\|_\infty  \|f_{\rho,\gamma}\|_\infty  &\ll1+\log(2D)=X^{o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc36-column-projective-cost}\end{aligned}$$ The estimate is uniform in $\gamma$.

> **Proof** Use the constant term and the $M_D$ Fourier terms displayed in [\[eq:tpc36-exact-column-Fourier\]](TPC-036.md#ref-58c8bb5f9b98536e). The source factor and the two threshold phases belong to $b$; the gcd cutoff and rank phase belong to $f$. Their sup norms are bounded by $2$ and $1$, respectively. The layer count is $1+M_D=O(D)$, while [\[eq:tpc36-interval-l1\]](TPC-036.md#ref-43e9c840423ec63c) proves the signed mass bound. ## Including the physical row and orbit weights

The coefficient [\[eq:tpc36-row-coefficients\]](TPC-036.md#ref-08b187b2d8dab2e4) is already a product of a source weight and a $d$-weight, apart from a fixed-period factor. First split the $O_h(1)$ orbit cells $j\bmod H_0$; finite Fourier inversion in the remaining row residue variables has bounded total mass. On each cell, Mellin inversion separates every smooth factor $W(\ell dj/X)$ into $\ell^{it}d^{it}j^{it}$ and has Schwartz total variation [`\citep{WangTPC25}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-036.md#cite-39e8dc14c55a2411). Multiplying these inherited representations by [\[eq:tpc36-column-separation\]](TPC-036.md#ref-18d012ba0af95d76), and enlarging $\mathcal R_\gamma$ by only an $O_h(1)$ factor, proves the physical form below.

> **Theorem: Physical product-ready column** \[thm:tpc36-physical-product-ready\] For each fixed $\gamma$, the actual left-column multiplier including its left row coefficient has the exact residue-cell representation $$\gamma_{(\ell,d)}^{(1)}  \mathfrak A_{(\ell,d),\gamma}^{\rm act}(j)  =\sum_{r\bmod H_0}\mathbf 1_{j\equiv r\!\!\!\pmod{H_0}}  \int_{\Omega_r}\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal R_\gamma}  c_{\rho,\omega,\gamma,r}  b_{\rho,\omega,\gamma,r}(\ell)  f_{\rho,\omega,\gamma,r}(d)  \eta_{\rho,\omega,\gamma,r}(j/J)\,d\nu_r(\omega).  \label{eq:tpc36-physical-product-ready}$$ For every fixed $B\ge0$, its normalized mass satisfies $$\sum_{r\bmod H_0}\int_{\Omega_r}\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal R_\gamma}  |c_{\rho,\omega,\gamma,r}|  \|b_{\rho,\omega,\gamma,r}\|_\infty  \|f_{\rho,\omega,\gamma,r}\|_\infty  \|\eta_{\rho,\omega,\gamma,r}\|_{C^B}  \,d|\nu_r|(\omega)  \ll_B X^{o(1)},  \label{eq:tpc36-product-ready-cost}$$ after the fixed logarithmic source weight is included in the soft factor. # Sharp reassembly and order-of-operations boundaries

The preceding constructions use cancellation between signed layers. We now show that their logarithmic behavior is sharp and that the product phase belongs to a different complexity class.

## The Ferrers logarithm is optimal

###### ref-480d59c3636670e6

###### ref-039f7b7b0254ad1c

###### ref-ccec8b1ac7447684

###### ref-9320d021f8763003

> **Proof** The case $n=1$ is immediate. Assume $n\ge2$. The upper bound is [\[eq:tpc36-prefix-projective-upper\]](TPC-036.md#ref-5595ea5bd45afb12). For the lower bound, the inverse of $T_n$ is the lower bidiagonal first-difference matrix. Direct substitution of the discrete sine vectors diagonalizes $(T_n^{-1})^*T_n^{-1}$ and gives eigenvalues $$4\sin^2\frac{(2k-1)\pi}{4n+2},  \qquad1\le k\le n,  \label{eq:tpc36-difference-eigenvalues}$$ which proves [\[eq:tpc36-Ferrers-singular-values\]](TPC-036.md#ref-e4bae7d9c7821160). Since $\sin x\le x$, the first $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ singular values give $$\|T_n\|_*  \gg n\sum_{k\le n/2}\frac1{2k-1}  \gg n\log(2n).  \label{eq:tpc36-Ferrers-nuclear-lower}$$ Apply [\[eq:tpc36-nuclear-lower\]](TPC-036.md#ref-06f52908ce3e41c0) with $m=n$.

Thus the $O(\log D)$ cost in [\[thm:tpc36-fixed-column-separation\]](TPC-036.md#ref-86322365ddd27b4e) cannot be replaced uniformly by a constant for arbitrary threshold profiles.

## Positive layers have linear cost

###### ref-a3ad503aedf4d1e3

###### ref-c74717d6ae7f382d

Define the nonnegative projective cost $$\pi_{+}(K)  :=\inf\sum_\rho c_\rho,  \label{eq:tpc36-positive-projective-norm}$$ where the infimum is over exact decompositions $$K=\sum_\rho c_\rho x_\rho y_\rho^{\mathsf T},  \qquad  c_\rho\ge0,\quad  0\le x_\rho,y_\rho\le1  \label{eq:tpc36-positive-decomposition}$$ entrywise.

###### ref-7fee15732e659564

###### ref-4d346241d888b14e

> **Proposition: Exact nonnegative Ferrers cost** \[prop:tpc36-positive-Ferrers\] For every $n\ge1$, $$\boxed{\pi_{+}(T_n)=n.}  \label{eq:tpc36-positive-Ferrers-cost}$$

###### ref-526a72f18acfebac

> **Proof** An exact nonnegative rank-one layer supported in the lower triangle can meet at most one diagonal entry. Indeed, if it is positive at both $(i,i)$ and $(j,j)$ with $i<j$, then its $(i,j)$ entry is also positive, contradicting $(T_n)_{i,j}=0$. Since every diagonal entry of $T_n$ equals one and the factors in [\[eq:tpc36-positive-decomposition\]](TPC-036.md#ref-c74717d6ae7f382d) are at most one, summing the diagonal identities gives $\sum_\rho c_\rho\ge n$. Conversely, $$T_n=\sum_{k=1}^n  \mathbf 1_{\{r:r\ge k\}}e_k^{\mathsf T}  \label{eq:tpc36-positive-column-decomposition}$$ has cost $n$.

This is the exact penalty for insisting that every intermediate layer be entrywise nonnegative. A raw column splitting or nonnegative rectangle reassembly can therefore lose $D$, whereas the signed proof loses only $X^{o(1)}$.

## The product phase has square-root projective cost

###### ref-ae4bb9a4e620cd22

Let $q>2$ be prime, put $G_q=\mathbb F_q^\times$, and for $r\in\mathbb F_q^\times$ define the $(q-1)\times(q-1)$ matrix $$A_r(x,y)=e_q(rxy),\qquad x,y\in G_q.  \label{eq:tpc36-product-phase-matrix}$$

###### ref-2301c436be82aa66

###### ref-f0632b9797a8cc2c

###### ref-4e85df297eb9f457

> **Theorem: Exact product-phase projective norm** \[thm:tpc36-product-phase-norm\] For every $r\ne0$, $$A_rA_r^*=qI_{q-1}-J_{q-1},  \label{eq:tpc36-product-phase-Gram}$$ and $$\boxed{  \pi_{\infty}(A_r)  =\frac{(q-2)\sqrt q+1}{q-1}.}  \label{eq:tpc36-product-phase-exact-cost}$$

###### ref-31392667d4a57fc8

> **Proof** For $x,x'\in G_q$, additive orthogonality gives $$\sum_{y\in G_q}e_q(r(x-x')y)  =\begin{cases}  q-1,&x=x',\\  -1,&x\ne x'.  \end{cases}  \label{eq:tpc36-product-phase-row-inner-product}$$ This is [\[eq:tpc36-product-phase-Gram\]](TPC-036.md#ref-f0632b9797a8cc2c). Hence $A_r$ has singular value $\sqrt q$ with multiplicity $q-2$ and singular value $1$ with multiplicity one. The nuclear lower bound [\[eq:tpc36-nuclear-lower\]](TPC-036.md#ref-06f52908ce3e41c0) gives the right side of [\[eq:tpc36-product-phase-exact-cost\]](TPC-036.md#ref-4e85df297eb9f457).
>
> For equality, use the multiplicative characters of $G_q$ as an orthonormal basis. Every normalized character vector has all coordinates of modulus $(q-1)^{-1/2}$. The matrix $A_r$ maps each such vector to a scalar multiple of the conjugate-character vector; the scalar is a Gauss sum of modulus $\sqrt q$ for nonprincipal characters and modulus $1$ for the principal character. Its singular-value decomposition therefore has flat left and right singular vectors. Rescaling each vector to supremum one makes the total projective mass exactly $\|A_r\|_* /(q-1)$.

## Absolute additive-frequency reassembly is costly

###### ref-83dda7173060d883

For $a\in G_q$, let $$K_a(x,y)=\mathbf 1_{xy=a}.  \label{eq:tpc36-product-permutation-kernel}$$

###### ref-0f1917c65387c9b3

###### ref-fb20cb3b9c5136f5

###### ref-fd1748436d2ed73a

###### ref-162ae9a9854fb18d

> **Proposition: A cost-one product kernel** \[prop:tpc36-product-kernel-cost\] One has $$\boxed{\pi_{\infty}(K_a)=1.}  \label{eq:tpc36-product-kernel-projective-cost}$$ But its additive orthogonality expansion $$K_a(x,y)=\frac1q\sum_{r\in\mathbb F_q}e_q(-ra)A_r(x,y)  \label{eq:tpc36-additive-product-delta}$$ has termwise absolute projective cost $$\frac1q\left[  1+(q-1)\frac{(q-2)\sqrt q+1}{q-1}  \right]  \asymp\sqrt q.  \label{eq:tpc36-additive-reassembly-cost}$$

###### ref-e705ffc760bc1302

> **Proof** Multiplicative orthogonality gives $$K_a(x,y)=\frac1{q-1}\sum_\chi  \overline{\chi(a)}\chi(x)\chi(y),  \label{eq:tpc36-multiplicative-product-delta}$$ whose projective mass is one. Since $K_a$ has an entry equal to one, [\[eq:tpc36-entry-lower\]](TPC-036.md#ref-f88b9afba5f79dc6) gives equality. Formula [\[eq:tpc36-additive-product-delta\]](TPC-036.md#ref-fd1748436d2ed73a) is ordinary additive orthogonality; insert [\[thm:tpc36-product-phase-norm\]](TPC-036.md#ref-2301c436be82aa66), noting that $A_0=J_{q-1}$ has cost one.

###### ref-d1eb81dcc8b71d77

> **Corollary: Cost of absolute additive-frequency separation** \[cor:tpc36-retain-product-slope\] At $q\asymp J$, separating each nonzero additive frequency of $e_q(r\ell j)$ and then reassembling absolutely introduces a sharp amplitude cost $\asymp J^{1/2}$, hence an energy cost $\asymp J$. Thus this additive-frequency expansion cannot be bounded at the critical scale by summing its nonzero-frequency costs separately. The static mask may still be separated by [\[thm:tpc36-global-static-mask\]](TPC-036.md#ref-57f8ebb39a277a68), while the multiplicative-character reconstruction [\[eq:tpc36-multiplicative-product-delta\]](TPC-036.md#ref-e705ffc760bc1302) avoids the loss.

This is a method-specific order-of-operations obstruction. It is not a lower bound for the physical orbit energy: the cost-one identity [\[eq:tpc36-multiplicative-product-delta\]](TPC-036.md#ref-e705ffc760bc1302) demonstrates the cancellation that termwise additive separation discards.

# Exact spectra for coherent complementary-factor sums

The fixed-column theorem reaches the product-slope shape, but the topology of the physical orbit slice is not the square function in $s$ treated in TPC-35. We now diagonalize the operation that sums in $s$ first.

## The complete product-slope operator

###### ref-00314992b35e3072

###### ref-6043072a77332b61

###### ref-17ccd2dc26a217a3

###### ref-a17103b7c0695997

###### ref-6357e1778d39227e

Let $q>2$ be prime, put $G_q=\mathbb F_q^\times$, and fix $h\in G_q$. Let $f,g:\mathbb F_q\to\mathbb C$ be additively centered: $$\sum_{D\in\mathbb F_q}f(D)=0,  \qquad  \sum_{U\in\mathbb F_q}g(U)=0.  \label{eq:tpc36-centered-fg}$$ Define $$\begin{aligned}  R_s(a)  &:=\sum_{D\in\mathbb F_q}f(D)  g\!\left(s^{-1}(aD+h)\right),  \qquad a,s\in G_q,  \label{eq:tpc36-Rsa}\\  (Tb)(j,s)  &:=\sum_{\ell\in G_q}b(\ell)R_s(\ell j),  \qquad j,s\in G_q.  \label{eq:tpc36-Tb}\end{aligned}$$ For a multiplicative character $\chi$ of $G_q$, use $$\widehat b(\chi)=\sum_{x\in G_q}b(x)\overline{\chi(x)},  \qquad  \widehat R_s(\chi)=\sum_{a\in G_q}R_s(a)\overline{\chi(a)}.  \label{eq:tpc36-multiplicative-transforms}$$ TPC-35 proves the square-function identity $$\sum_{j,s}|(Tb)(j,s)|^2  =\frac1{q-1}\sum_\chi  |\widehat b(\bar\chi)|^2  \sum_s|\widehat R_s(\chi)|^2.  \label{eq:tpc36-TPC35-square-spectrum}$$

## A weighted coherent spectrum

###### ref-3e668c30aa825a6b

For an arbitrary weight $w:G_q\to\mathbb C$, put $$S_w(j)=\sum_{s\in G_q}w(s)(Tb)(j,s).  \label{eq:tpc36-Sw}$$

###### ref-88c29ad1a97e832a

###### ref-6830c62534deab36

> **Theorem: Weighted coherent product-slope spectrum** \[thm:tpc36-weighted-coherent-spectrum\] With the preceding conventions, $$\boxed{  \sum_{j\in G_q}|S_w(j)|^2  =\frac1{q-1}\sum_\chi  |\widehat b(\bar\chi)|^2  \left|\sum_{s\in G_q}w(s)\widehat R_s(\chi)\right|^2.}  \label{eq:tpc36-weighted-coherent-spectrum}$$

###### ref-0a2c2b514d767097

> **Proof** For fixed $s$, the substitution $a=\ell j$ gives $$\sum_{j\in G_q}(Tb)(j,s)\overline{\chi(j)}  =\widehat b(\bar\chi)\widehat R_s(\chi).  \label{eq:tpc36-fixed-s-transform}$$ Multiply by $w(s)$, sum over $s$, and apply multiplicative Parseval in $j$.

###### ref-f44c19ea4a9c3b55

The square function [\[eq:tpc36-TPC35-square-spectrum\]](TPC-036.md#ref-6357e1778d39227e) contains $\sum_s|\widehat R_s(\chi)|^2$. The coherent topology contains instead $$\left|\sum_sw(s)\widehat R_s(\chi)\right|^2.  \label{eq:tpc36-coherent-character-gate}$$ This is the exact character-level quantity that a physical theorem must control after static reassembly.

## The complete complementary-factor trace

The complete weight $w=1$ has an unexpectedly simple closed form.

###### ref-b453ea39e53d8110

###### ref-8d0b4a39bdd0377d

###### ref-810ac8638ece8b85

> **Theorem: Residue-zero boundary trace** \[thm:tpc36-complete-s-trace\] For every $a\in G_q$, $$\boxed{  \sum_{s\in G_q}R_s(a)=qg(0)f(-h/a).}  \label{eq:tpc36-complete-s-trace}$$ Consequently $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_{j\in G_q}\left|\sum_{s\in G_q}(Tb)(j,s)\right|^2  ={}&\frac{q^2|g(0)|^2}{q-1}  \sum_\chi|\widehat b(\bar\chi)|^2  \left|\sum_{D\ne0}f(D)\chi(D)\right|^2.  \label{eq:tpc36-complete-s-energy}\end{aligned}$$

###### ref-d3b43e04b05d38ba

###### ref-9fdc3fcc44a35436

###### ref-3ade477e0b975c4f

> **Proof** Fix $a$ and let $D_0=-h/a$. For $D=D_0$, the argument of $g$ is zero for every $s$, so its contribution to the $s$-sum is $(q-1)f(D_0)g(0)$. For $D\ne D_0$, the element $aD+h$ is nonzero and $s^{-1}(aD+h)$ runs once through $G_q$. Additive centering gives $$\sum_{U\in G_q}g(U)=-g(0).  \label{eq:tpc36-nonzero-g-sum}$$ Also $\sum_{D\ne D_0}f(D)=-f(D_0)$. The remaining contribution is therefore $f(D_0)g(0)$, proving [\[eq:tpc36-complete-s-trace\]](TPC-036.md#ref-8d0b4a39bdd0377d).
>
> Now $$\sum_s(Tb)(j,s)  =qg(0)\sum_{\ell\in G_q}  b(\ell)f\!\left(-\frac h{\ell j}\right).  \label{eq:tpc36-complete-T-trace}$$ Taking the multiplicative transform in $j$, the substitution $D=-h/(\ell j)$ gives, up to the unit phase $\overline{\chi(-h)}$, $$\widehat b(\bar\chi)  \sum_{D\ne0}f(D)\chi(D).  \label{eq:tpc36-trace-transform}$$ Parseval proves [\[eq:tpc36-complete-s-energy\]](TPC-036.md#ref-810ac8638ece8b85).

###### ref-754ca9c801c873e4

###### ref-a4855bb2ae3778c0

> **Corollary: Exact complete cancellation when $g(0)=0$** \[cor:tpc36-gzero-cancellation\] If $g(0)=0$, then $$\sum_{s\in G_q}(Tb)(j,s)=0  \label{eq:tpc36-gzero-cancellation}$$ for every $b$ and every $j$.

Ordinary additive centering does not force $g(0)=0$. If an uncentered physical $g$ is supported away from zero and is then centered by subtracting its mean, its new value at zero is generally the negative of that mean. The boundary trace must therefore be extracted, cancelled, or estimated; it cannot be deleted by notation.

## Sharpness of complementary-factor Cauchy

The exact trace does not imply a uniform gain for all centered data.

###### ref-d0de9218c2672a61

###### ref-529ac2d6fda7e17a

> **Theorem: Sharp coherent-$s$ saturation** \[thm:tpc36-s-Cauchy-sharp\] For every prime $q>2$, there are nonzero centered $f,g$ and $b$, with $f(0)=0$, such that $$\boxed{  \sum_j\left|\sum_s(Tb)(j,s)\right|^2  =(q-1)\sum_{j,s}|(Tb)(j,s)|^2.}  \label{eq:tpc36-s-Cauchy-equality}$$ Thus the Cauchy factor $\#G_q=q-1$ is sharp even after the principal energy arising from $f(0)$ has been removed.

###### ref-32875529bbe4ffab

###### ref-e751d0d3bea8b5f9

###### ref-ded17448e636cd8f

###### ref-09515eb7c2c36493

> **Proof** Choose a nonprincipal multiplicative character $\chi$ and extend it by zero. Put $$f(D)=\overline{\chi(D)}\ (D\ne0),\qquad f(0)=0,  \label{eq:tpc36-sharp-f}$$ $$g(U)=\mathbf 1_{\{0\}}(U)-\frac1q,  \qquad  b(\ell)=\overline{\chi(\ell)}.  \label{eq:tpc36-sharp-gb}$$ Both $f$ and $g$ are additively centered. The constant part of $g$ contributes zero to $R_s(a)$ by character orthogonality. The remaining delta selects $D=-h/a$, so $$R_s(a)=\overline{\chi(-h)}\chi(a),  \label{eq:tpc36-sharp-R}$$ independently of $s$. Hence $$(Tb)(j,s)  =(q-1)\overline{\chi(-h)}\chi(j),  \label{eq:tpc36-sharp-T}$$ also independently of $s$. Equality in Cauchy follows pointwise in $j$, and summing proves [\[eq:tpc36-s-Cauchy-equality\]](TPC-036.md#ref-529ac2d6fda7e17a).

> **Remark: Two independent critical $J$-losses** At $q\asymp J$, termwise additive separation of the product phase loses $J$ in energy by [\[cor:tpc36-retain-product-slope\]](TPC-036.md#ref-d1eb81dcc8b71d77). Uniform Cauchy in the complete $s$-variable can also lose $J$ by [\[thm:tpc36-s-Cauchy-sharp\]](TPC-036.md#ref-d0de9218c2672a61). The mechanisms are distinct. The first is avoided by retaining product slopes; the second requires a bound for the actual weighted trace [\[eq:tpc36-coherent-character-gate\]](TPC-036.md#ref-f44c19ea4a9c3b55).

# Endpoint ledger and the relocated coefficient gate

The static multiplier question is now resolved, but the physical energy still requires integer-to-finite-field completion in the correct coherent topology. This section records exactly what the new theorems save and what they do not.

## Complementary dyadic localization and a large-modulus realization

###### ref-378bfcd9add5703f

###### ref-6589b21e8cbc7838

###### ref-e6f1ff8a886c870d

###### ref-9a4ea56dbee94b51

By [\[eq:tpc36-target\]](TPC-036.md#ref-92d704d15d20c2bb), there are fixed constants $0<c_{\mathscr D}<C_{\mathscr D}$ such that $$c_{\mathscr D}X  \le N_{\ell d}(j):=\ell dj+h  \le C_{\mathscr D}X  \label{eq:tpc36-target-support-constants}$$ throughout the packet. Partition $T<u\le U_0$ into disjoint half-open dyadic blocks $$\mathscr U:=\{2^kT:k\ge0,\ 2^kT<U_0\},  \qquad  \mathcal U_U:=(U,2U]\cap(T,U_0],  \qquad |\mathscr U|=O(\log X),  \label{eq:tpc36-u-dyadic-blocks}$$ and attach to each block the complementary interval $$\mathcal S_U:=  \left\{s\in\mathbb N:  \frac{c_{\mathscr D}X}{2U}\le s  \le\frac{C_{\mathscr D}X}{U}\right\}.  \label{eq:tpc36-s-complementary-block}$$ On an equality solution $su=N_{\ell d}(j)$ with $u\in\mathcal U_U$, the indicator of $\mathcal S_U$ is identically one. Hence the exact kernel identity $$\mathbf 1_{T<u\le U_0}\mathbf 1_{su=N_{\ell d}(j)}  =  \sum_{U\in\mathscr U}  \mathbf 1_{u\in\mathcal U_U}\mathbf 1_{s\in\mathcal S_U}  \mathbf 1_{su=N_{\ell d}(j)}  \label{eq:tpc36-reciprocal-dyadic-identity}$$ holds pointwise. The new cutoffs are unary in $u$ and $s$, and summing the $O(\log X)$ blocks costs only $X^{o(1)}$.

###### ref-ed97f60caf44b1b1

###### ref-92e2b0bc3f0d6085

###### ref-a550169940cde63e

###### ref-224ada993005f5a9

Now fix one such block and put $$F=s u-N_{\ell d}(j)=s u-\ell jd-h.  \label{eq:tpc36-affine-determinant}$$ On the full Cartesian support $u\in\mathcal U_U$, $s\in\mathcal S_U$, one has $$su\le2C_{\mathscr D}X,  \qquad  |F|\le3C_{\mathscr D}X.  \label{eq:tpc36-block-determinant-bound}$$ Enlarge a fixed constant $C_0$ so that $C_0\ge3C_{\mathscr D}$ and every individual physical variable is smaller than $C_0X$. By Bertrand’s postulate, for large $X$ one may choose a prime $$2C_0X<q<4C_0X,  \label{eq:tpc36-large-modulus}$$ and then $$F\equiv0\pmod q\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad F=0.  \label{eq:tpc36-large-modulus-no-alias}$$ Thus, block by block, the modular product-slope layer is pointwise identical to the integer layer after the physical variables are zero-extended to $\mathbb F_q$. Summing the dyadic blocks preserves this exact identity at soft cost.

###### ref-2557fee253cf8a91

This is an unconditional algebraic realization, not an efficient analytic one. In the standard completion route, the $J$-long orbit occupies only a proportion $$\frac Jq\asymp\frac JX\asymp\frac1Q  \label{eq:tpc36-large-modulus-coverage}$$ of the large modulus. Any argument that forgets this localization before using a complete normalized estimate may pay the coverage scale $Q$. This is method-specific bookkeeping, not a universal lower bound for every large-modulus method.

## Orbit-scale moduli and aliases

###### ref-3c85804f463e3d87

###### ref-c0ea2289624ddb91

###### ref-441940bf4cbf433a

On each reciprocal dyadic block, at the useful scale $q\asymp J$, the congruence $F\equiv0\pmod q$ admits $$O(1+X/q)=O(Q)  \label{eq:tpc36-one-modulus-aliases}$$ integer aliases. The $O(\log X)$ blocks are absorbed into $X^{o(1)}$. TPC-35 proves the exact joint ladder: for pairwise coprime $q_i\asymp J$, $1\le i\le r$, the alias count is $$O\!\left(1+\frac X{J^r}\right).  \label{eq:tpc36-CRT-alias-ladder}$$ In particular, $$\frac X{J^3}=\frac Q{J^2}=X^{1/400+o(1)},  \label{eq:tpc36-three-modulus-margin}$$ while four moduli give strict no-wrap. Equality [\[eq:tpc36-three-modulus-margin\]](TPC-036.md#ref-441940bf4cbf433a) is only an exponent-compatible ledger. Reassembling every three-modulus alias at the diagonal scale and controlling all proper-coordinate character faces remain unproved.

###### ref-7daff1d5711b50ed

###### ref-8d0971f071f8c589

On every block, the complementary factor has length at most $$S_0\ll X/T.  \label{eq:tpc36-complementary-length}$$ Reducing a bare interval of this length modulo $q\asymp J$ gives maximum residue multiplicity $$\ll1+\frac{S_0}{q}  \ll1+\frac Q{T}  =X^{563/2000+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc36-s-residue-multiplicity}$$ The actual weight is more structured than a bare interval, but [\[eq:tpc36-s-residue-multiplicity\]](TPC-036.md#ref-8d0971f071f8c589) shows that completeness in $s$ cannot be treated as cost-free.

Two formerly conflated questions are now separate. The first asks whether the mask can be put into signed product-ready form; the answer is yes. ## A coefficient-specific coherent target

###### ref-24d78f800e609fd6

For one completed finite-field layer, define $$\mathcal G_q(b,f,g;w)  :=\frac1{q-1}\sum_\chi  |\widehat b(\bar\chi)|^2  \left|\sum_sw(s)\widehat R_s(\chi)\right|^2.  \label{eq:tpc36-coherent-gate-functional}$$ By [\[thm:tpc36-weighted-coherent-spectrum\]](TPC-036.md#ref-88c29ad1a97e832a), this is exactly $\|S_w\|_2^2$, not a majorant. The next physical theorem can therefore be pursued through either of two nonexclusive routes:

1.  prove an aggregate bound for [\[eq:tpc36-coherent-gate-functional\]](TPC-036.md#ref-24d78f800e609fd6) over the actual reassembly layers and aliases; or

2.  prove a quantitative avoidance statement showing that the actual $b,f,g,w$ have negligible overlap with the character pairs on which [\[eq:tpc36-coherent-character-gate\]](TPC-036.md#ref-f44c19ea4a9c3b55) is large.

Neither route asks for a maximum over all abstract coefficients. Conversely, [\[thm:tpc36-s-Cauchy-sharp\]](TPC-036.md#ref-d0de9218c2672a61) shows that centeredness and $f(0)=0$ alone cannot prove the desired aggregate estimate.

## Route menu for the next layer

The following routes are nonexclusive.

Route A: residue-zero boundary extraction.

:   Split the complete $s$-trace [\[eq:tpc36-complete-s-trace\]](TPC-036.md#ref-8d0b4a39bdd0377d) from the incomplete boundary weight. Determine whether the actual centered ultra coefficient has a small or diagonally absorbable $g(0)$ component before estimating the remainder.

Route B: weighted two-character dispersion.

:   Expand the physical $s$-weight multiplicatively and estimate $\sum_sw(s)\widehat R_s(\chi)$ by the actual Möbius character support, rather than by Cauchy. This is the closest continuation of the spectral route.

Route C: coherent three-modulus CRT.

:   Keep three orbit-scale moduli separate, center each coordinate face, and prove diagonal-scale reassembly simultaneously for principal, singleton, double, and full-coordinate modes. Static mask complexity no longer adds a polynomial loss.

Route D: a scoped negative theorem.

:   If the physical coefficient family can be shown to concentrate on the saturating $s$-coherent mode, or if a proper-coordinate alias survives every admissible centering, that is a precise obstruction to this route. It would not be a lower bound for twin-prime correlations themselves.

The first two routes now have exact finite-model targets; the third has the critical exponent budget. Failure of any one route leaves the others available without changing the results proved in this paper.

## Relation to the parity boundary

The factor $q\asymp J$ appears twice: as the energy cost of splitting a product phase additively, and as the sharp uniform Cauchy cost in the coherent $s$-variable. Avoiding the first is an algebraic order-of-operations theorem. Avoiding the second requires arithmetic information about the actual coefficient family. No estimate here supplies that information.

Accordingly, the soft mask theorem is a genuine advance in the proof program, but it is not a parity-breaking theorem in the sense of classical sieve theory [`\citep{HeathBrown1982Parity,IwaniecKowalski2004}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-036.md#cite-bd33f7374ef1e88f); [\[IwaniecKowalski2004\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-036.md#cite-49dfea87eaada156). It removes one candidate obstruction and exposes the next one in an exact norm.

## What is proved

The unconditional new results of this paper are:

1.  the exact global orthogonal compression [\[eq:tpc36-global-gcd-factorization\]](TPC-036.md#ref-84b87e85f87172ac) and soft gcd projective bound;

2.  the global actual-mask theorem [\[eq:tpc36-global-static-mask-bound\]](TPC-036.md#ref-9eed4c6a8e4d885d);

3.  the fixed-column source–divisor representation [\[eq:tpc36-exact-column-Fourier\]](TPC-036.md#ref-58c8bb5f9b98536e) with logarithmic signed mass;

4.  the exact Ferrers singular values, the optimal signed logarithmic cost, and the exact nonnegative cost $n$;

5.  the product-phase Gram and exact projective norm [\[eq:tpc36-product-phase-exact-cost\]](TPC-036.md#ref-4e85df297eb9f457);

6.  the cost-one multiplication kernel and the sharp loss for its termwise additive-frequency reassembly;

7.  the weighted coherent spectrum [\[eq:tpc36-weighted-coherent-spectrum\]](TPC-036.md#ref-6830c62534deab36); and

8.  the complete $s$-trace, its residue-zero energy formula, and the sharp centered nonprincipal Cauchy example.

The physical product-ready column theorem also uses the fixed residue-cell split and Mellin separation already proved in TPC-25. Its dependence on that prior result is explicit rather than re-proved here.

## What is not proved

Finite enumeration certifies algebra on the tested families; it does not prove asymptotic analytic estimates. Neither the manuscript nor the certificate proves:

1.  an operator or Hilbert–Schmidt bound for the actual orbit-Gram off-diagonal;

2.  the coefficient-specific physical gate $V_L+V_R\ll X^{o(1)}Q^3/J$;

3.  a complete integer-to-modular reassembly at $q\asymp J$;

4.  control of one-modulus or three-modulus alias fibers at diagonal scale;

5.  a bound for the actual weighted coherent functional [\[eq:tpc36-coherent-gate-functional\]](TPC-036.md#ref-24d78f800e609fd6);

6.  vanishing of the actual centered residue-zero boundary $g(0)$;

7.  cancellation of the terminal/proper-divisor cross terms;

8.  an affine Chowla estimate or Möbius disjointness theorem;

9.  a breach of sieve parity, positivity, or a Hardy–Littlewood prime-pair asymptotic; or

10. infinitely many twin primes.

The precise advance is narrower and reusable: static physical-mask complexity is no longer an unresolved polynomial obstruction. Signed reassembly places the real next gate at weighted coherent spectral overlap and joint alias control. A positive or negative theorem about that new functional would be the next logically meaningful layer.
