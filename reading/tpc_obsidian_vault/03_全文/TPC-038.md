## Abstract

TPC–37 proposed a target–coupled shifted–fiber estimate as one possible continuation of an orbit–sliced prime–Möbius energy program. We resolve the coefficient–free part of that proposal exactly and identify an additional equality–recovery step required by its physical application.

Let $q_1,q_2,q_3$ be distinct orbit–scale primes, $M=q_1q_2q_3$, $\Phi=\varphi(M)$, and let $N$ be a unit modulo $M$. If $F=su-N$, every coordinate face has the exact expansion $$K_{S,N}(F)=\frac1\Phi  \sum_{\chi\in\mathfrak X_S}  \chi(s)\chi(u)\overline{\chi(N)},$$ where the local component of $\chi$ is nonprincipal exactly on $S$. Zero extension makes this expansion valid even when $N+F$ is a nonunit; then every face vanishes in a killed local coordinate. On the regular support $N+F\in(\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z)^\times$, the finite CRT target dependence is diagonalized by the unit–group ratio $$r=(N+F)N^{-1}\pmod M.$$ The full and proper face families are complementary character projectors. Their recombination is the target–independent identity $$C_N(F)+P_N(F)=\mathbf 1_{M\mid F}.$$

On this regular support we prove a Hilbert–valued ratio Parseval identity. For arbitrary vectors $z_{N,F}$, character orthogonality controls the sums of the coherent ratio buckets, not the individual pairs $(N,F)$. For fixed $F$, the ratio map has exact maximum multiplicity $$d_M(F)=\prod_{q_i\mid F}(q_i-1).$$ When $q_i\asymp J$, the target collision scales are therefore $1,J,J^2,J^3$, according as $F$ is divisible by zero, one, two, or three auxiliary primes. Generic determinants are injective; the obstruction is concentrated on the coordinate divisibility strata.

We also compute the complete one–period target–synthesis spectrum. The full–face operator has squared norm $$\prod_{i=1}^3\frac{q_i(q_i-2)^2}{(q_i-1)^2}\asymp\Phi,$$ whereas, for $q_i\asymp J$, the proper operator has squared norm $\asymp\Phi/J$. Both scalar spectra have a dominant global principal target mode; fixed–target Parseval does not imply target–synthesis orthogonality.

After full/proper recombination, every nonjoint ghost cancels exactly and the completed output is the joint–alias sum over $F=kM$. At the endpoint $$Q=X^{267/400+o(1)},\qquad J=X^{133/400+o(1)},\qquad  M=X^{399/400+o(1)},$$ their endpoint order is $$\mathcal K_X:=1+X/M=X^{1/400+o(1)}.$$ All multiplicative character phases equal $1$ on this identity bucket. For the alias–completed sum, diagonal energy plus Cauchy loses the exact finite alias count; at the endpoint this has order $\mathcal K_X$. A joint–alias Gram estimate removes that loss.

Crucially, the alias–completed sum is not the original equality output, which is its $k=0$ column. There is no coefficient–free recovery: two opposite alias columns make both fixed full/proper field outputs vanish while leaving the equality column arbitrary. Thus the shifted–fiber estimate proposed in TPC–37 controls a congruence completion but, by itself, does not imply the physical equality gate. A full alias–Gram bound implies the equality bound directly through its $k=0$ diagonal entry, at the stronger diagonal scale. The terminal identity bucket expands into Möbius autocorrelations at shifts $rM$, and the physical row expansion contains the affine four–Möbius boundary found earlier.

Finally, we distinguish two prescribed face–field estimates from an all–field operator estimate of the same normalization that is stable under restrictions. The latter would be strictly stronger: its $F=0,s=1$ restriction implies a diagonal–scale estimate for the terminal affine–Möbius/four–Möbius operator. We prove no positive physical equality–recovery estimate and no Möbius estimate. The results are exact reductions, a correction of the logical gate, and method obstructions—not a Möbius lower bound, a parity breach, a Hardy–Littlewood asymptotic, or a twin–prime theorem.

# Introduction

The orbit–sliced program in TPC–34–TPC–37 reduces a scalar prime–pair remainder to a coefficient–specific energy estimate for a reflected Möbius divisor sum [`\citep{WangTPC34,WangTPC35,WangTPC36,WangTPC37}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-038.md#cite-21637783bbd2f0dd); [\[WangTPC35\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-038.md#cite-3c1024de2f5c4031); [\[WangTPC36\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-038.md#cite-7286aa6023fa493f); [\[WangTPC37\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-038.md#cite-cb3177b3d328eeab). The physical row mask is now known to have subpolynomial signed projective complexity, the zero residue faces have been removed at the target scale, and all seven proper faces of a three–modulus completion close on the exact equality. The full face remains unsuppressed there.

###### ref-81a385523e9afc7c

TPC–37 recorded two possible continuations. The first works after finite–field completion and asks for an actual weighted Mellin–overlap estimate, including its principal boundary. The second keeps the integer determinant $$F=su-N  \label{eq:tpc38-intro-determinant}$$ visible and asks for a target–coupled shifted–fiber estimate. This paper audits the second continuation. The question is not whether one may write another formal Hilbert–space norm. It is whether the moving target and the CRT faces create genuine orthogonality at the physical normalization.

## What the target coupling really is

###### ref-f55c38bdaa67ad6e

Let $q_1,q_2,q_3$ be distinct odd primes, put $$M=q_1q_2q_3,\qquad  \Phi=\varphi(M)=\prod_{i=1}^3(q_i-1),  \label{eq:tpc38-intro-M-Phi}$$ and let $N$ be a unit modulo $M$. TPC–37 defines, in each local coordinate, a principal face $m_{i,N}(F)$ and a nonprincipal aggregate $c_{i,N}(F)$. Their tensor products give eight faces. The full face $C_N$ uses three nonprincipal coordinates; the proper union $P_N$ contains the other seven.

The first result of this paper is the exact identity $$K_{S,N}(F)  =\frac1\Phi\sum_{\chi\in\mathfrak X_S}  \chi(N+F)\overline{\chi(N)},  \label{eq:tpc38-intro-character-face}$$ where the local component of $\chi$ is nonprincipal exactly for $i\in S$. Since $N+F=su$, the phase factors further as $$\chi(N+F)\overline{\chi(N)}  =\chi(s)\chi(u)\overline{\chi(N)}.  \label{eq:tpc38-intro-product-factorization}$$ Zero–extended characters make this valid without a hidden correction when $s$ or $u$ is nonunit; then every face vanishes in a killed local coordinate. On the regular support $N+F\in(\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z)^\times$, the finite CRT target dependence is diagonalized exactly by the unit–group ratio $$r=\frac{N+F}{N}\pmod M.  \label{eq:tpc38-intro-ratio}$$

For fixed $F$, on its regular domain this ratio map is injective in every local coordinate in which $q_i\nmid F$, and is constant in every coordinate in which $q_i\mid F$. Its exact largest fiber is $$d_M(F)=\prod_{q_i\mid F}(q_i-1).  \label{eq:tpc38-intro-collision-multiplicity}$$ When $q_i\asymp J$, this gives the target collision scale ladder $$1,\qquad J,\qquad J^2,\qquad J^3,  \label{eq:tpc38-intro-collision-ladder}$$ according as $F$ is divisible by zero, one, two, or three orbit–scale primes. The underlying multiplicity formula is exact; the displayed $J$-powers are asymptotic scales, not a large–sieve heuristic.

## Fixed target orthogonality does not survive target synthesis

###### ref-ec3620589eb35624

###### ref-c8273a1b3b45ff40

###### ref-ea9395a101eef5bb

For one fixed $N$, the functions $$e_{\chi,N}(F)=\chi(N+F)\overline{\chi(N)}  \label{eq:tpc38-intro-echi}$$ are orthogonal over $F\bmod M$. This recovers the one–period masses $$\sum_{F\bmod M}|C_N(F)|^2=A,\qquad  \sum_{F\bmod M}|P_N(F)|^2=1-A,  \label{eq:tpc38-intro-fixed-target-masses}$$ where $$A=\prod_{i=1}^3\left(1-\frac1{q_i-1}\right).  \label{eq:tpc38-intro-A}$$ When $N$ moves, the cross Gram becomes a product of shifted Jacobi sums. More directly, the full face rows satisfy $$\langle C_N,C_{N'}\rangle  =\prod_{i=1}^3  \begin{cases}   1-p_i,&N\equiv N'\pmod{q_i},\\   1-p_i-p_i^2,&N\not\equiv N'\pmod{q_i},  \end{cases}  \label{eq:tpc38-intro-full-row-Gram}$$ with $p_i=(q_i-1)^{-1}$. Distinct target rows therefore have inner product $1-O(J^{-1})$ when they are distinct in all coordinates. They are almost parallel, not almost orthogonal.

###### ref-8babafc621c08f96

We compute the entire scalar target–synthesis spectrum. For $$(T_Ca)(F)=\sum_{N\bmod M}^{*}a_NC_N(F),  \label{eq:tpc38-intro-TC}$$ the squared singular values are indexed by target Mellin modes. The largest is $$\|T_C\|_{\operatorname{op}}^2  =\prod_{i=1}^3\frac{q_i(q_i-2)^2}{(q_i-1)^2}  \asymp\Phi\asymp J^3.  \label{eq:tpc38-intro-TC-norm}$$ For the proper union, the principal squared singular value is $\asymp\Phi/J\asymp J^2$; all its nonprincipal target modes are $O(1)$. The small fixed–row norm $1-A\asymp J^{-1}$ therefore does not imply a small synthesis norm.

## Recombination and the identity bucket

###### ref-955e759210255020

The full and proper character families are complementary. Summing them before estimating gives $$\boxed{C_N(F)+P_N(F)=\mathbf 1_{M\mid F}.}  \label{eq:tpc38-intro-recombination}$$ Every nonjoint ghost cancels. Separate estimates for $C_N$ and $P_N$ are consequently sufficient but strictly stronger than an estimate for their recombined congruence completion.

###### ref-29284db27637bda2

In the physical determinant interval, [\[eq:tpc38-intro-recombination\]](TPC-038.md#ref-955e759210255020) leaves only $$F=kM,\qquad |k|\ll X/M.  \label{eq:tpc38-intro-joint-aliases}$$ At the endpoint $$Q=X^{267/400+o(1)},\qquad  J=X^{133/400+o(1)},\qquad  M=X^{399/400+o(1)},  \label{eq:tpc38-intro-endpoint}$$ the endpoint alias order is $$\mathcal K_X:=1+\frac XM  =\frac Q{J^2}X^{o(1)}  =X^{1/400+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc38-intro-KX}$$ On $F=kM$, the ratio in [\[eq:tpc38-intro-ratio\]](TPC-038.md#ref-27623a98559f0d3a) is $1$ and every character phase in [\[eq:tpc38-intro-character-face\]](TPC-038.md#ref-c82d2280ae5757b5) equals one. Multiplicative character orthogonality is therefore exactly blind on the remaining bucket.

###### ref-2c2629345250adc0

###### ref-0ad41e7fce1c8b09

###### ref-ae0cb6b7f61a48b0

Write $\mathscr Z_k$ for the raw shifted output on $su=N+kM$. The recombined completed output is $$\left\|\sum_k\mathscr Z_k\right\|_2^2  =\sum_{k,k'}\langle\mathscr Z_k,\mathscr Z_{k'}\rangle.  \label{eq:tpc38-intro-alias-Gram}$$ If its alias columns satisfy the diagonal trace bound $$\sum_k\|\mathscr Z_k\|_2^2  \ll X^{o(1)}Q^2J\mathcal K_X,  \label{eq:tpc38-intro-diagonal-input}$$ Cauchy gives only $X^{o(1)}Q^2J\mathcal K_X^2$. The desired scale is $$Q^2J\mathcal K_X=\frac{Q^3}{J}X^{o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc38-intro-target-scale}$$ The exact finite loss is the alias count $K_B$; at the endpoint it is of order $\mathcal K_X$, and a rank–one family of alias columns attains it.

There is a more basic logical distinction. The original physical output is $\mathscr Z_0$, not the alias sum in [\[eq:tpc38-intro-alias-Gram\]](TPC-038.md#ref-2c2629345250adc0). No norm bound for the latter recovers the former: for two aliases, the abstract choice $\mathscr Z_0=v$, $\mathscr Z_1=-v$ makes the alias sum and both fixed full/proper field outputs vanish while $\mathscr Z_0$ is arbitrary. Thus the shifted–fiber estimate proposed in TPC–37 controls the congruence–completed output but needs an additional equality–recovery input before it can imply the original orbit gate. A uniform column bound at scale $Q^2J$ would recover $\mathscr Z_0$, but at $k=0$ it is already stronger than the desired $Q^2J\mathcal K_X$ bound. A full alias–Gram bound is stronger still and automatically contains this column bound.

## Arithmetic meaning and claim boundary

###### ref-7fde08aedc7ce0a3

###### ref-c526ae8ad8f6f223

On the terminal submodel $s=1$, a joint alias contains $$a_N(k)=-\mu(N+kM)\log(N+kM)\Phi_0(N+kM).  \label{eq:tpc38-intro-terminal-sequence}$$ Its squared alias sum has cross terms $$\mu(N+kM)\mu(N+(k+r)M),  \label{eq:tpc38-intro-Mobius-correlation}$$ and restoring the physical rows gives the affine four–Möbius Gram identified in TPC–34. This is an averaged, highly structured correlation. It is not logically equivalent to pointwise Chowla in each short progression.

There is also a necessary distinction in terminology. Bounds for the two prescribed fields $C$ and $P$ do not constitute an all–field operator estimate. An estimate of the same normalization for every coefficient field, stable under fiber restriction, is strictly stronger. Since $C_N(0)=A=1-O(J^{-1})$, that hereditary all–field estimate restricted to $F=0,s=1$ would imply a diagonal–scale estimate for the original terminal operator, stronger than the inherited target by the factor $\mathcal K_X$. We prove no hereditary all–field theorem, positive physical equality–recovery estimate, or Möbius correlation estimate.

The unconditional conclusions of the paper are the exact character projectors, shifted Jacobi Grams, ratio Parseval theorem, exact collision multiplicity and its scale ladder, target–synthesis spectrum, face recombination, equality–nonrecoverability, and sharp abstract coherence examples. They locate the remaining gates but do not prove the physical orbit estimate, affine Chowla, sieve–parity failure, a Hardy–Littlewood asymptotic, or infinitely many twin primes.

## Fixed target Parseval and shifted target Gram

###### ref-0c3d923834ca2183

For a fixed unit $N$, put $$e_{\chi,N}(F)  =\chi(N+F)\overline{\chi(N)}.  \label{eq:tpc38-echiN}$$

> **Proposition: Fixed target Parseval** \[prop:tpc38-fixed-target-Parseval\] For characters $\chi,\psi\bmod M$, $$\sum_{F\bmod M}e_{\chi,N}(F)  \overline{e_{\psi,N}(F)}  =\Phi\mathbf 1_{\chi=\psi}.  \label{eq:tpc38-fixed-target-orthogonality}$$ Consequently, $$\boxed{  \sum_{F\bmod M}|C_N(F)|^2=A,\qquad  \sum_{F\bmod M}|P_N(F)|^2=1-A.}  \label{eq:tpc38-fixed-target-face-norms}$$

> **Proof** The translation $F\mapsto N+F$ runs through every residue modulo $M$. Character orthogonality on the unit group proves [\[eq:tpc38-fixed-target-orthogonality\]](TPC-038.md#ref-1246e58c9dffa726). Then $N'+F=x+\Delta$, and the CRT factors the sum in [\[eq:tpc38-shifted-Gram-definition\]](TPC-038.md#ref-1ae8965ab9793786) into [\[eq:tpc38-local-shifted-sum\]](TPC-038.md#ref-3d8fec5ebcaf806e). # Hilbert–valued ratio Parseval and determinant strata

The correct large–sieve variable is the ratio, not $F$ or $N$ separately. This section gives the exact Hilbert–valued identity and its sharp collision multiplicities. The general large sieve may be compared with [`\citep{Bombieri1965,IwaniecKowalski2004}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-038.md#cite-072be3f1fcf63a7d); [\[IwaniecKowalski2004\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-038.md#cite-49dfea87eaada156); no asymptotic large–sieve input is needed here.

## The ratio bucket identity

###### ref-b8c7826d9767e989

###### ref-5ed1ff036821223c

###### ref-d914490591280fb1

Let $\mathcal I$ be a finite multiset of pairs $(N,F)$ such that both $N$ and $N+F$ are units modulo $M$. Attach vectors $z_{N,F}\in\mathcal H$, where $\mathcal H$ is a complex Hilbert space, and put $$r(N,F)=(N+F)N^{-1}\in(\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z)^\times.  \label{eq:tpc38-ratio-map}$$ For every ratio $r$, define its coherent bucket $$V_r=\sum_{\substack{(N,F)\in\mathcal I\\r(N,F)=r}}z_{N,F}.  \label{eq:tpc38-ratio-bucket}$$ For a character $\chi\bmod M$, put $$Y_\chi=\sum_{(N,F)\in\mathcal I}  \chi(N+F)\overline{\chi(N)}z_{N,F}.  \label{eq:tpc38-Ychi}$$

###### ref-bdc82948538dd12c

###### ref-0443e3566e589c36

###### ref-8c0b985119b6db1e

###### ref-4c4e0c21684d26e9

###### ref-fadc21963323987a

###### ref-eecf5ec4dbbf9cc4

> **Theorem: Hilbert–valued ratio Parseval** \[thm:tpc38-ratio-Parseval\] One has the exact identity $$\boxed{  \sum_{\chi\bmod M}\|Y_\chi\|_{\mathcal H}^2  =\Phi\sum_{r\in(\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z)^\times}\|V_r\|_{\mathcal H}^2.}  \label{eq:tpc38-ratio-Parseval}$$ For any character family $\mathfrak A$, define $$K_{\mathfrak A,N}(F)  =\frac1\Phi\sum_{\chi\in\mathfrak A}  \chi(N+F)\overline{\chi(N)}.  \label{eq:tpc38-family-kernel}$$ Then $$\boxed{  \left\|\sum_{(N,F)\in\mathcal I}  K_{\mathfrak A,N}(F)z_{N,F}\right\|_{\mathcal H}^2  \le\frac{|\mathfrak A|}{\Phi}  \sum_r\|V_r\|_{\mathcal H}^2.}  \label{eq:tpc38-family-ratio-bound}$$ If $$\nu_{\rm rat}  =\max_r\#\{(N,F)\in\mathcal I:r(N,F)=r\},  \label{eq:tpc38-ratio-multiplicity}$$ then $$\left\|\sum_{(N,F)\in\mathcal I}  K_{\mathfrak A,N}(F)z_{N,F}\right\|_{\mathcal H}^2  \le\frac{|\mathfrak A|}{\Phi}\nu_{\rm rat}  \sum_{(N,F)\in\mathcal I}\|z_{N,F}\|_{\mathcal H}^2.  \label{eq:tpc38-family-multiplicity-bound}$$

> **Proof** Expand the left side of [\[eq:tpc38-ratio-Parseval\]](TPC-038.md#ref-0443e3566e589c36). Character orthogonality gives $\Phi$ when two pairs have the same ratio and zero otherwise. Regrouping the surviving inner products yields the bucket norms in [\[eq:tpc38-ratio-bucket\]](TPC-038.md#ref-5ed1ff036821223c). Next, $$\sum_{N,F}K_{\mathfrak A,N}(F)z_{N,F}  =\frac1\Phi\sum_{\chi\in\mathfrak A}Y_\chi.$$ Cauchy in the character variable and [\[eq:tpc38-ratio-Parseval\]](TPC-038.md#ref-0443e3566e589c36) prove [\[eq:tpc38-family-ratio-bound\]](TPC-038.md#ref-4c4e0c21684d26e9). Cauchy inside each ratio bucket gives $\|V_r\|^2\le\nu_{\rm rat}\sum_{r(N,F)=r}\|z_{N,F}\|^2$, proving the last estimate.

###### ref-a350b79f422f40ac

For the full and proper families, the prefactors in [\[eq:tpc38-family-ratio-bound\]](TPC-038.md#ref-4c4e0c21684d26e9) are respectively $$\frac{|\mathfrak X_{\rm full}|}{\Phi}=A=1-O(J^{-1}),  \qquad  \frac{|\mathfrak X_{\rm prop}|}{\Phi}=1-A\asymp J^{-1}.  \label{eq:tpc38-family-density-factors}$$ The theorem gains the family density but leaves every coherent collision inside $V_r$ untouched.

## Exact fixed–determinant multiplicity

###### ref-1733e8c5c14d5d51

For fixed $F\bmod M$, let $$\mathcal D_F=\{N\in(\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z)^\times:N+F\in(\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z)^\times\}  \label{eq:tpc38-DF}$$ and define $r_F(N)=1+FN^{-1}$ on $\mathcal D_F$.

###### ref-dfc9e3c4b9c28ff9

###### ref-7d82e811b7d666cc

###### ref-f42612b216dcdaae

> **Theorem: Exact target collision multiplicity** \[thm:tpc38-fixed-F-collision\] The largest fiber of $r_F$ has cardinality $$\boxed{d_M(F)=\prod_{q_i\mid F}(q_i-1).}  \label{eq:tpc38-dMF}$$ Consequently, for arbitrary vectors $z_N\in\mathcal H$, $$\sum_{\chi\bmod M}  \left\|\sum_{N\in\mathcal D_F}z_N  \chi(N+F)\overline{\chi(N)}\right\|_{\mathcal H}^2  \le\Phi d_M(F)\sum_{N\in\mathcal D_F}\|z_N\|_{\mathcal H}^2.  \label{eq:tpc38-fixed-F-large-sieve}$$ The multiplicity and the inequality are sharp in the unrestricted Hilbert coefficient class.

> **Proof** Work first modulo $q_i$. If $F=0\pmod{q_i}$, then $r_F(N)=1$ for all $n_i$ unit values of $N$. If $F\ne0\pmod{q_i}$, the domain is $G_i\setminus\{-F\}$, and $$N\longmapsto 1+F/N$$ is a bijection onto $G_i\setminus\{1\}$, with inverse $N=F/(r-1)$. Tensoring through the CRT gives [\[eq:tpc38-dMF\]](TPC-038.md#ref-7d82e811b7d666cc). Apply [\[thm:tpc38-ratio-Parseval\]](TPC-038.md#ref-bdc82948538dd12c) with fixed $F$ to obtain [\[eq:tpc38-fixed-F-large-sieve\]](TPC-038.md#ref-f42612b216dcdaae). Equality in the bucket Cauchy step is obtained by taking equal vectors on a largest fiber.

###### ref-d9b26765d1e765e4

###### ref-dace3bd9a29b4108

If the primes satisfy $q_i\asymp J$, then $$d_M(F)\asymp J^{|Z(F)|},  \qquad  Z(F)=\{i:q_i\mid F\}.  \label{eq:tpc38-collision-strata}$$ Thus the four determinant strata have collision scales $$1,\qquad J,\qquad J^2,\qquad J^3.  \label{eq:tpc38-collision-scales}$$ Generic $F$ is the best possible case: the target ratio map is injective. The joint aliases $M\mid F$ are the worst possible case: every target is sent to $r=1$.

## Fixed–determinant face mass

The collision multiplicity may also be seen directly in the target mass of a face.

###### ref-ea998ccb507dd890

###### ref-3a1f414b1e6f5118

###### ref-ab91f996263d2abe

###### ref-7c23249cff842ccd

> **Proposition: Exact target mass on a determinant stratum** \[prop:tpc38-fixed-F-face-mass\] For $Z(F)=\{i:q_i\mid F\}$, $$\boxed{  \sum_{N\bmod M}^{*}|C_N(F)|^2  =\prod_{i\in Z(F)}n_i(1-p_i)^2   \prod_{i\notin Z(F)}(n_i-1)p_i^2.}  \label{eq:tpc38-fixed-F-full-mass}$$ If $M\nmid F$, then $P_N(F)=-C_N(F)$, so the same formula holds for the proper union. If $M\mid F$, then $$\sum_{N\bmod M}^{*}|P_N(F)|^2  =\Phi(1-A)^2.  \label{eq:tpc38-fixed-F-proper-joint-mass}$$ For $q_i\asymp J$, the full–face scales in [\[eq:tpc38-fixed-F-full-mass\]](TPC-038.md#ref-3a1f414b1e6f5118) are $$J^{-3},\qquad J^{-1},\qquad J,\qquad J^3  \label{eq:tpc38-fixed-F-mass-ladder}$$ as $|Z(F)|=0,1,2,3$, while the joint proper mass is $\asymp J$.

> **Proof** If $q_i\mid F$, the local full factor is $1-p_i$ for all $n_i$ unit targets. If $q_i\nmid F$, the target $N=-F$ is killed and the remaining $n_i-1$ targets have local value $-p_i$. Tensor the local squared masses. The proper identities follow from [\[eq:tpc38-offjoint-cancellation\]](TPC-038.md#ref-c2e2a79f6cd5af7c) and [\[eq:tpc38-face-values-zero\]](TPC-038.md#ref-352c2a1a18984006).

Summing [\[eq:tpc38-fixed-F-full-mass\]](TPC-038.md#ref-3a1f414b1e6f5118) over one $F$-period gives $\Phi A$, and summing the proper mass gives $\Phi(1-A)$, as required by fixed–target Parseval and Fubini. Over an integer determinant interval of length $O(X)$, these traces are at most $X^{1+o(1)}$ and $X^{1+o(1)}/J$, respectively. This trace bookkeeping does not control coherent row synthesis; the next section computes that synthesis exactly.

# Exact target–synthesis spectrum

Fixed–target Parseval concerns the norm of one row. A target–coupled Bessel estimate concerns coherent combinations of many such rows. The distinction can be quantified without an inequality.

## The local full–face Gram

###### ref-27bc9bc701f37b65

Fix one odd prime $q$, put $n=q-1$, $p=n^{-1}$, and let $$c_N(F)=\mathbf 1_{F=0}-p\mathbf 1_{F\ne-N},  \qquad N\in\mathbb F_q^\times,\quad F\in\mathbb F_q.  \label{eq:tpc38-local-full-row}$$

###### ref-b1e6c6968173b3f3

###### ref-e2dc6ba69f3fc36b

###### ref-b5847e54cbd4b889

###### ref-a979b7cf93778e53

> **Lemma: Exact local target Gram** \[lem:tpc38-local-target-Gram\] For $N,N'\in\mathbb F_q^\times$, $$\langle c_N,c_{N'}\rangle  =\begin{cases}   1-p,&N=N',\\   1-p-p^2,&N\ne N'.  \end{cases}  \label{eq:tpc38-local-target-Gram}$$ Equivalently, the $n\times n$ Gram matrix is $$G_q=p^2I_n+(1-p-p^2)\mathbf1\mathbf1^*.  \label{eq:tpc38-local-Gram-matrix}$$ Its squared singular values are $$\lambda_q(\eta)=  \begin{cases}   \displaystyle\frac{q(q-2)^2}{(q-1)^2},   &\eta=\mathbf1,\\[0.9em]   \displaystyle\frac1{(q-1)^2},   &\eta\ne\mathbf1,  \end{cases}  \label{eq:tpc38-local-singular-values}$$ where $\eta$ ranges over target Mellin modes.

> **Proof** For $N=N'$, the distinguished value $F=0$ contributes $(1-p)^2$, the killed value $F=-N$ contributes zero, and the remaining $q-2=n-1$ values contribute $p^2$. Their sum is $1-p$.
>
> For $N\ne N'$, the values $0,-N,-N'$ are distinct. The first contributes $(1-p)^2$, the next two contribute zero, and the remaining $q-3=n-2$ values contribute $p^2$. The total is $1-p-p^2$. This proves [\[eq:tpc38-local-Gram-matrix\]](TPC-038.md#ref-b5847e54cbd4b889). On the orthogonal complement of $\mathbf1$, its eigenvalue is $p^2$. On $\mathbf1$, the eigenvalue is $$p^2+n(1-p-p^2)  =\frac{q(q-2)^2}{(q-1)^2}.$$

The off–diagonal entry in [\[eq:tpc38-local-target-Gram\]](TPC-038.md#ref-e2dc6ba69f3fc36b) differs from the row norm by only $p^2$. The local full rows are nearly parallel.

## The CRT tensor spectrum

###### ref-96465560616c6f68

###### ref-7529bebda2b08eb5

Return to three primes. Define the full target–synthesis operator $$T_C:\ell^2((\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z)^\times)\longrightarrow\ell^2(\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z),  \qquad  (T_Ca)(F)=\sum_{N\bmod M}^{*}a_NC_N(F).  \label{eq:tpc38-TC-definition}$$ For a target Mellin mode $\eta=\eta_1\otimes\eta_2\otimes\eta_3$, put $$\lambda_C(\eta)=\prod_{i=1}^3\lambda_{q_i}(\eta_i),  \label{eq:tpc38-lambda-C}$$ with the local factors in [\[eq:tpc38-local-singular-values\]](TPC-038.md#ref-a979b7cf93778e53).

###### ref-09358340f0e44cc0

###### ref-96045f13395a502b

###### ref-113abeb4bf83693c

> **Theorem: Complete full–face target spectrum** \[thm:tpc38-full-target-spectrum\] The target Mellin modes diagonalize $T_C^*T_C$, and their squared singular values are exactly $\lambda_C(\eta)$. In particular, $$\boxed{  \|T_C\|_{\operatorname{op}}^2  =\Lambda_C  :=\prod_{i=1}^3  \frac{q_i(q_i-2)^2}{(q_i-1)^2}.}  \label{eq:tpc38-TC-norm}$$ If $q_i\asymp J$, then $$\Lambda_C\asymp\Phi\asymp J^3.  \label{eq:tpc38-TC-endpoint}$$ Under the same hypothesis $q_i\asymp J$, every nonprincipal global target mode has squared singular value $O(1)$.

> **Proof** The CRT identifies $T_C$ with the tensor product of the three local matrices in [\[eq:tpc38-local-full-row\]](TPC-038.md#ref-27bc9bc701f37b65). Tensor [\[lem:tpc38-local-target-Gram\]](TPC-038.md#ref-b1e6c6968173b3f3). The largest local eigenvalue is the principal one. If a global mode has a nonprincipal component at $i$, then that local factor is $O(J^{-2})$, while the two remaining principal factors are $O(J)$. Their product is $O(1)$.

The direct Gram formula is also useful. For $p_i=(q_i-1)^{-1}$, $$\boxed{  \langle C_N,C_{N'}\rangle  =\prod_{i=1}^3  \left[  (1-p_i)\mathbf 1_{N=N'\ (\bmod q_i)}  +(1-p_i-p_i^2)\mathbf 1_{N\ne N'\ (\bmod q_i)}  \right].}  \label{eq:tpc38-global-full-row-Gram}$$ If $N$ and $N'$ differ in every coordinate, the right side is $1-O(J^{-1})$.

###### ref-24332e5dfc238865

> **Theorem: Complete proper–face target spectrum** \[thm:tpc38-proper-target-spectrum\] Every nonprincipal global target Mellin mode has squared singular value $\lambda_C(\eta)$. The global principal mode has squared singular value $$\boxed{  \lambda_P(\mathbf1)  =\Lambda_C+\Phi(1-2A).}  \label{eq:tpc38-proper-principal-eigenvalue}$$ For $q_i\asymp J$, $$\lambda_P(\mathbf1)\asymp\frac\Phi J\asymp J^2,  \qquad  \|T_P\|_{\operatorname{op}}^2\asymp J^2.  \label{eq:tpc38-TP-endpoint}$$ Moreover, $$\langle P_N,P_{N'}\rangle  =1-2A+\langle C_N,C_{N'}\rangle,  \label{eq:tpc38-proper-row-Gram}$$ and $$\langle C_N,P_{N'}\rangle  =A-\langle C_N,C_{N'}\rangle.  \label{eq:tpc38-cross-row-Gram}$$

###### ref-8d6fb8836dd3fc48

###### ref-9e29bb5533a55789

> **Proof** Equation [\[eq:tpc38-proper-Gram-rank-one\]](TPC-038.md#ref-2301bac9fda62163) follows by expanding $\langle\delta_0-C_N,\delta_0-C_{N'}\rangle$. The rank–one correction vanishes on every nonprincipal target mode and adds $\Phi(1-2A)$ on the principal mode. This proves the exact spectrum.
>
> For the asymptotic, divide [\[eq:tpc38-proper-principal-eigenvalue\]](TPC-038.md#ref-82a875383fdb9397) by $\Phi$. One has $$\frac{\Lambda_C}{\Phi}  =\prod_{i=1}^3  \left(1-p_i-p_i^2+p_i^3\right),  \label{eq:tpc38-Lambda-density}$$ while $A=\prod_i(1-p_i)$. Expanding the fixed products shows $$\frac{\lambda_P(\mathbf1)}\Phi  =\sum_{i=1}^3p_i+O(J^{-2})\asymp J^{-1}.  \label{eq:tpc38-proper-principal-density}$$ The row Gram identities follow from the same expansion.

## Trace is not synthesis

###### ref-975abca799c5e7b4

The fixed–target row norms are $$\|C_N\|_2^2=A=1-O(J^{-1}),  \qquad  \|P_N\|_2^2=1-A\asymp J^{-1}.  \label{eq:tpc38-row-norms-versus-synthesis}$$ By contrast, the synthesis norms are of orders $J^3$ and $J^2$. Thus a diagonal or trace estimate over $N$ cannot be upgraded to a target–coupled operator estimate without controlling the principal target direction. This is a precise finite–dimensional obstruction; it does not assert that the actual Möbius row vector occupies that direction.

###### ref-24c5a88e1fa9ede6

###### ref-ccb705a30c17e8c4

###### ref-87d63d91d01432e0

> **Corollary: Coefficient–blind target Bessel obstruction** \[cor:tpc38-target-Bessel-obstruction\] Any coefficient–blind inequalities $$\|T_Ca\|_2^2\le B_C\|a\|_2^2,  \qquad  \|T_Pa\|_2^2\le B_P\|a\|_2^2  \label{eq:tpc38-coefficient-blind-Bessel-bounds}$$ valid for every scalar target vector $a$ must satisfy $$B_C\ge\Lambda_C\asymp J^3,  \qquad  B_P\ge\lambda_P(\mathbf1)\asymp J^2.  \label{eq:tpc38-coefficient-blind-lower-bounds}$$ Thus the fixed–row norms alone cannot furnish smaller synthesis constants.

> **Remark: Scope** The theorem concerns unrestricted scalar target coefficients on one CRT period. Physical coefficients are far more structured. The result rules out a coefficient–blind frame argument; it is neither a lower bound for $V_L+V_R$ nor evidence that the actual Möbius vector is extremal.

# Full/proper recombination and the joint–alias Gram

The preceding sections show why estimating the two face families separately is expensive. The CRT congruence–completed kernel contains their exact sum, so one should recombine before applying an inequality.

## Strict face–splitting obstruction

###### ref-c38489d09bbf55fb

Let $E\subset\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z$ be a nonempty set of nonjoint residues on which $C_N(F)\ne0$. Fix a unit vector $v$ in a Hilbert space and set $$z_F=\frac{\overline{C_N(F)}}{|C_N(F)|}v,  \qquad F\in E.  \label{eq:tpc38-offjoint-witness}$$

###### ref-1f59da864537c0c4

###### ref-99db9758c5115e8c

###### ref-799acaa1df82b48c

> **Proposition: Separate faces are strictly stronger** \[prop:tpc38-face-splitting-obstruction\] For the family [\[eq:tpc38-offjoint-witness\]](TPC-038.md#ref-c38489d09bbf55fb), $$\sum_{F\in E}C_N(F)z_F  =\left(\sum_{F\in E}|C_N(F)|\right)v,  \label{eq:tpc38-large-full-output}$$ and $$\sum_{F\in E}P_N(F)z_F  =-\left(\sum_{F\in E}|C_N(F)|\right)v.  \label{eq:tpc38-large-proper-output}$$ Their recombined output is zero. Hence no abstract estimate can recover separate full and proper outputs from their sum.

> **Proof** Use $P_N(F)=-C_N(F)$ for $M\nmid F$. The definition of $z_F$ aligns every full–face summand.

This proposition is not a counterexample to either fixed physical field estimate in [\[eq:tpc38-interface-two-field-input\]](TPC-038.md#ref-b5eed4607e144ffd): its vectors were chosen adversarially. It proves a logical point. Separate–face control is sufficient, but it is not necessary for the recombined physical kernel and may destroy an exact cancellation before arithmetic coefficients are used.

## The identity bucket

###### ref-49c6c430cadd3dd3

###### ref-eb4438a015208d65

###### ref-30c5c64bb4ee6915

###### ref-5fd2328429107a61

###### ref-d8caaf3b804d7e80

###### ref-4f68276abca54dbb

Let $B\ll X$ bound the determinant range and put $$\mathcal I_B=\{k\in\mathbb Z:|kM|\le B\},  \qquad K_B=|\mathcal I_B|=2\left\lfloor\frac BM\right\rfloor+1.  \label{eq:tpc38-alias-index-set}$$ Let $$\mathscr Z_k:=\mathscr R_{kM}\in\mathcal H_{\rm phys},  \qquad k\in\mathcal I_B,  \label{eq:tpc38-Zk}$$ be the raw shifted columns associated with $$su=N+kM.  \label{eq:tpc38-joint-shifted-equality}$$ Define the two completed face outputs by $$\mathscr Y_C:=\sum_{|F|\le B}\mathscr Z_F[C],  \qquad  \mathscr Y_P:=\sum_{|F|\le B}\mathscr Z_F[P].  \label{eq:tpc38-completed-face-outputs}$$ Linearity and [\[eq:tpc38-face-recombination\]](TPC-038.md#ref-7a9db697c9a3a897) give the exact identity $$\boxed{  \mathscr Y_C+\mathscr Y_P  =\sum_{k\in\mathcal I_B}\mathscr Z_k  =:\mathscr Y_{\rm alias}.}  \label{eq:tpc38-joint-output}$$ By contrast, the original equality output of the retained regular subpacket is $$\boxed{\mathscr Y_{\rm phys}=\mathscr Z_0=\mathscr R_0.}  \label{eq:tpc38-original-versus-alias-output}$$ Thus face recombination produces an exact congruence completion of the original equality; it does not identify the completion with its zero column.

###### ref-534c0eb1b2210257

###### ref-220b179b4fd70026

> **Proposition: Character blindness on the identity bucket** \[prop:tpc38-character-blindness\] For every unit target $N$, every $k\in\mathcal I_B$, and every character $\chi\bmod M$, $$\chi(N+kM)\overline{\chi(N)}=1.  \label{eq:tpc38-character-phase-one}$$ Thus every joint alias belongs to the single ratio bucket $r=1$, and the multiplicative character Gram on that bucket is rank one.

> **Proof** Modulo $M$, one has $N+kM=N$, and $N$ is a unit.

###### ref-3bba1bf0a8de8dea

No additional multiplicative character decomposition can separate the vectors in [\[eq:tpc38-joint-output\]](TPC-038.md#ref-d8caaf3b804d7e80). The completion is governed by their ordinary Hilbert Gram matrix $$\mathsf G_B=(\langle\mathscr Z_k,\mathscr Z_{k'}\rangle)_{k,k'\in\mathcal I_B}.  \label{eq:tpc38-alias-Gram-matrix}$$

## Alias completion does not recover exact equality

###### ref-9996f0eabaacb2ef

###### ref-b443e8154bd33514

> **Theorem: Sharp equality–recovery obstruction** \[thm:tpc38-equality-recovery-obstruction\] Assume $K_B>1$. In the unrestricted Hilbert column class, neither $\mathscr Y_{\rm alias}$ nor the pair $(\mathscr Y_C,\mathscr Y_P)$ controls $\mathscr Z_0$. More precisely, for every $T>0$ there are columns for which $$\mathscr Y_C=\mathscr Y_P=\mathscr Y_{\rm alias}=0,  \qquad  \|\mathscr Z_0\|_2=T.  \label{eq:tpc38-equality-recovery-witness-output}$$

###### ref-8be7a7f19b6ae4f7

> **Proof** Choose $k_1\in\mathcal I_B\setminus\{0\}$ and a unit vector $v$. Set $$\mathscr Z_0=Tv,  \qquad  \mathscr Z_{k_1}=-Tv,  \qquad  \mathscr Z_k=0\quad(k\ne0,k_1),  \label{eq:tpc38-equality-recovery-witness}$$ and set every nonjoint target atom in every shifted output to zero. On a joint alias, $C_N(kM)=A$ and $P_N(kM)=1-A$, independently of $N$. Hence $\mathscr Y_C=A(\mathscr Z_0+\mathscr Z_{k_1})=0$ and $\mathscr Y_P=(1-A)(\mathscr Z_0+\mathscr Z_{k_1})=0$, while $\|\mathscr Z_0\|_2=T$.

The witness is coefficient–blind and artificial. It does not assert that the physical Möbius columns cancel in this way. It proves that an estimate for the two completed fixed fields needs a new physical recovery argument before it can bound [\[eq:tpc38-interface-gate\]](TPC-038.md#ref-467e3b5b597a5268).

## Localized columns, the alias Gram, and the sharp Cauchy loss

###### ref-f83a990ef6921518

Put $$B_0=Q^2J.  \label{eq:tpc38-B0}$$ The following strong localized input is useful for calibrating possible positive theorems.

###### ref-48aa288a258c6e91

###### ref-a3103ade218db117

> **Assumption: Uniform full alias–column bound** \[ass:tpc38-moving-shift-diagonal\] Uniformly for $k\in\mathcal I_B$, $$\|\mathscr Z_k\|_2^2\ll X^{o(1)}B_0.  \label{eq:tpc38-moving-shift-diagonal}$$

###### ref-27e02e67aee6c6fc

At $k=0$, [\[ass:tpc38-moving-shift-diagonal\]](TPC-038.md#ref-48aa288a258c6e91) already gives $$\|\mathscr Y_{\rm phys}\|_2^2  \ll X^{o(1)}Q^2J,  \label{eq:tpc38-moving-diagonal-already-strong}$$ which is stronger than the inherited target $X^{o(1)}Q^3/J$, since $Q^2J\mathcal K_X=X^{o(1)}Q^3/J$. It is therefore not a preliminary consequence of the proved input–copy diagonal; it is itself a stronger coefficient theorem for the raw output.

###### ref-6f592e6f46293f4c

###### ref-2fde392cac1a0576

> **Assumption: Joint–alias Gram almost–orthogonality** \[ass:tpc38-joint-alias-Gram\] The Gram matrix in [\[eq:tpc38-alias-Gram-matrix\]](TPC-038.md#ref-3bba1bf0a8de8dea) satisfies $$\boxed{\|\mathsf G_B\|_{\operatorname{op}}\ll X^{o(1)}B_0.}  \label{eq:tpc38-joint-alias-Gram-gate}$$

###### ref-9add9ddf38b29a02

###### ref-4a28a843c75ae8b4

###### ref-c7777624b5c90051

###### ref-2af18936207c4e8f

> **Proposition: What the strong Gram gate implies** \[prop:tpc38-reduced-gate-suffices\] Under [\[ass:tpc38-joint-alias-Gram\]](TPC-038.md#ref-6f592e6f46293f4c), $$\left\|\sum_{k\in\mathcal I_B}\mathscr Z_k\right\|_2^2  \ll X^{o(1)}B_0K_B.  \label{eq:tpc38-reduced-gate-output}$$ At the physical endpoint $B\asymp X$, the right side has scale $$X^{o(1)}B_0K_B  \asymp X^{o(1)}Q^2J\mathcal K_X  =X^{o(1)}\frac{Q^3}{J}.  \label{eq:tpc38-reduced-gate-endpoint}$$ Moreover, $$\|\mathscr Z_k\|_2^2  \le \|\mathsf G_B\|_{\operatorname{op}}  \ll X^{o(1)}B_0  \qquad(k\in\mathcal I_B).  \label{eq:tpc38-Gram-implies-columns}$$ In particular, the Gram gate implies [\[ass:tpc38-moving-shift-diagonal\]](TPC-038.md#ref-48aa288a258c6e91) and the stronger equality bound [\[eq:tpc38-moving-diagonal-already-strong\]](TPC-038.md#ref-27e02e67aee6c6fc).

> **Proof** The left side of [\[eq:tpc38-reduced-gate-output\]](TPC-038.md#ref-4a28a843c75ae8b4) is $\langle\mathsf G_B\mathbf1,\mathbf1\rangle$. Bound it by $\|\mathsf G_B\|_{\operatorname{op}}\|\mathbf1\|_2^2$. The diagonal entries of a positive semidefinite Gram matrix are bounded by its operator norm, proving [\[eq:tpc38-Gram-implies-columns\]](TPC-038.md#ref-2af18936207c4e8f).

###### ref-c567aed175e4d159

For the alias–completed vector alone, the operator–norm gate is stronger than necessary. Given the diagonal trace bound, a weaker sufficient one–vector condition is $$\operatorname{Re}\sum_{k\ne k'}  \langle\mathscr Z_k,\mathscr Z_{k'}\rangle  \ll X^{o(1)}B_0K_B,  \label{eq:tpc38-one-vector-offdiagonal-gate}$$ together with the diagonal trace of the same size. We retain [\[eq:tpc38-joint-alias-Gram-gate\]](TPC-038.md#ref-2fde392cac1a0576) because it is the genuine Bessel form.

###### ref-8500de555b4af2ce

###### ref-dbff43f08aef2fb8

###### ref-d663e87bcf9361b6

> **Proposition: Sharp coefficient–free alias loss** \[prop:tpc38-sharp-alias-loss\] Suppose only the diagonal trace estimate $$\sum_{k\in\mathcal I_B}\|\mathscr Z_k\|_2^2  \ll X^{o(1)}B_0K_B.  \label{eq:tpc38-alias-diagonal-trace}$$ Then Cauchy gives $$\left\|\sum_{k\in\mathcal I_B}\mathscr Z_k\right\|_2^2  \ll X^{o(1)}B_0K_B^2.  \label{eq:tpc38-Cauchy-alias-loss}$$ The factor $K_B$ relative to [\[eq:tpc38-reduced-gate-output\]](TPC-038.md#ref-4a28a843c75ae8b4) is optimal for the abstract Hilbert column class.

###### ref-2fe8c9f8aeffde09

> **Proof** Cauchy proves [\[eq:tpc38-Cauchy-alias-loss\]](TPC-038.md#ref-d663e87bcf9361b6). For sharpness, let $v$ be a unit vector and take $$\mathscr Z_k=\sqrt{B_0}\,v  \qquad(k\in\mathcal I_B).  \label{eq:tpc38-rank-one-alias-witness}$$ Then the diagonal trace is $B_0K_B$, while the squared sum is $B_0K_B^2$.

###### ref-9758bef857d176df

At $B\asymp X$, the exact finite loss in [\[prop:tpc38-sharp-alias-loss\]](TPC-038.md#ref-8500de555b4af2ce) is $K_B$, and $$\boxed{  K_B\asymp\mathcal K_X:=1+\frac XM  =\frac X{J^3}X^{o(1)}  =\frac Q{J^2}X^{o(1)}  =X^{1/400+o(1)}.}  \label{eq:tpc38-missing-KX}$$ This is much smaller than the earlier $J$-scale face losses, but it is a fixed power and cannot be hidden in $X^{o(1)}$.

###### ref-261ff1cfb8d75351

> **Remark: The old diagonal is a different object** \[rem:tpc38-moving-diagonal-not-inherited\] Equation [\[eq:tpc38-interface-diagonal\]](TPC-038.md#ref-df12a6e10497cd2c) proves the input–copy diagonal, not $\|\mathscr R_0\|_2^2$. For $k\ne0$, the affine shift is $h+kM$, which may be of size $X$, and the fixed–$h$ primitivity and incidence hypotheses are not automatically uniform. Thus [\[ass:tpc38-moving-shift-diagonal\]](TPC-038.md#ref-48aa288a258c6e91) is both arithmetically new and, at $k=0$, already stronger than the inherited physical target.

## A target–scale recovery formulation

The original gate can be recovered without a full Gram theorem if one can control two particular vectors rather than every alias coefficient.

###### ref-3aaab44f4e79ed04

###### ref-c7d2b3ba72dc2511

###### ref-7ec0cc96821108da

###### ref-b2b77b414696f26a

> **Proposition: Two–sum equality recovery** \[prop:tpc38-two-sum-recovery\] Suppose $$\begin{aligned}  \|\mathscr Y_{\rm alias}\|_2^2  &\ll X^{o(1)}B_0K_B,  \label{eq:tpc38-alias-total-target-bound}\\  \left\|\sum_{\substack{k\in\mathcal I_B\\k\ne0}}\mathscr Z_k\right\|_2^2  &\ll X^{o(1)}B_0K_B.  \label{eq:tpc38-nonzero-alias-recovery-gate}\end{aligned}$$ Then $$\|\mathscr Z_0\|_2^2\ll X^{o(1)}B_0K_B.  \label{eq:tpc38-two-sum-recovered-output}$$ At the endpoint, this is the inherited scale $X^{o(1)}Q^3/J$.

> **Proof** Subtract the nonzero–alias sum from $\mathscr Y_{\rm alias}$ and use $\|x-y\|^2\le2\|x\|^2+2\|y\|^2$.

If the two fixed–field estimates [\[eq:tpc38-interface-two-field-input\]](TPC-038.md#ref-b5eed4607e144ffd) were proved, their period ledgers and [\[eq:tpc38-joint-output\]](TPC-038.md#ref-d8caaf3b804d7e80) would imply [\[eq:tpc38-alias-total-target-bound\]](TPC-038.md#ref-c7d2b3ba72dc2511). They do not imply [\[eq:tpc38-nonzero-alias-recovery-gate\]](TPC-038.md#ref-7ec0cc96821108da), because the fixed fields are not assumed to be stable under deleting $F=0$. This is the precise additional recovery gate missing from the earlier sufficient–input claim.

## A fourth modulus removes aliases but not the equality gate

###### ref-5aac0d9c471390de

###### ref-0fff0f33db778901

Choose four pairwise coprime primes $q_i\asymp J$ and put $M_4=\prod_{i=1}^4q_i$. Then $M_4>X$ at the endpoint, so $$\mathbf 1_{M_4\mid F}=\mathbf 1_{F=0}  \qquad(|F|\ll X).  \label{eq:tpc38-four-modulus-no-wrap}$$ This removes every nonzero joint alias. However, on $F=0$ the four–coordinate full face has scalar value $$A_4=\prod_{i=1}^4\left(1-\frac1{q_i-1}\right)  =1-O(J^{-1}).  \label{eq:tpc38-four-modulus-full-value}$$

###### ref-fddda80d484d2f17

> **Corollary: Four–modulus loopback** \[cor:tpc38-four-modulus-loopback\] Adding a fourth orbit–scale modulus eliminates the alias Gram but returns the full face, up to the scalar $A_4$, to the original equality operator. It supplies no coefficient–free power saving for the full physical gate.

This is not a negative result about the physical coefficient. It shows that the alias problem and the equality coefficient problem are distinct: more CRT coordinates solve the former and leave the latter unchanged.

# Terminal embeddings and sparse Möbius progressions

The identity bucket has an explicit arithmetic submodel. We record it to calibrate the strength of possible operator formulations, not to assert a new Möbius estimate.

## Terminal aliases

###### ref-eb88e1572791a77a

###### ref-5659778735fee2ef

###### ref-43799e5e0b45e6e9

###### ref-f7ed832b8a16cc51

On the terminal factorization $s=1$, the $k$-th joint alias has $$u=N+kM.  \label{eq:tpc38-terminal-alias}$$ For a smooth terminal cutoff $\Phi_0$, define $$a_N(k)  =-\mu(N+kM)\log(N+kM)\Phi_0(N+kM).  \label{eq:tpc38-terminal-alias-sequence}$$ For every finite interval $I$ of admissible $k$’s, $$\begin{aligned}  \left|\sum_{k\in I}a_N(k)\right|^2  &=\sum_{k\in I}|a_N(k)|^2  \notag\\  &\quad+2\operatorname{Re}  \sum_{r\ge1}  \sum_{\substack{k,k+r\in I}}  a_N(k+r)\overline{a_N(k)}.  \label{eq:tpc38-terminal-autocorrelation}\end{aligned}$$ The off–diagonal terms contain $$\mu(N+kM)\mu(N+(k+r)M)  \label{eq:tpc38-M-spaced-Mobius}$$ at shifts $rM$. The progression has modulus $M=X^{399/400+o(1)}$ and length only $\mathcal K_X=X^{1/400+o(1)}$.

###### ref-974743ea9ab05e76

###### ref-026fa65569ce0e9f

> **Proposition: Exact arithmetic content of the terminal alias Gram** \[prop:tpc38-terminal-alias-content\] The same–target part of the joint–alias Gram contains the autocorrelation [\[eq:tpc38-terminal-autocorrelation\]](TPC-038.md#ref-43799e5e0b45e6e9). Restoring the physical row $\alpha=(\ell,d)$ and target $N_\alpha(j)=\ell dj+h$, the cross–row terms contain weights of the form $$\begin{aligned}  &\mu(d_\alpha)\mu(d_\beta)  (\log\ell_\alpha)(\log\ell_\beta)  \notag\\[-0.2em]  &\qquad\times  \mu(N_\alpha(j)+kM)  \mu(N_\beta(j)+k'M),  \label{eq:tpc38-four-Mobius-row-kernel}\end{aligned}$$ multiplied by the actual masks and smooth factors.

> **Proof** Insert [\[eq:tpc38-terminal-alias-sequence\]](TPC-038.md#ref-5659778735fee2ef) into the Gram expansion. The physical row coefficient contains $\mu(d)\log\ell$; pairing two rows gives [\[eq:tpc38-four-Mobius-row-kernel\]](TPC-038.md#ref-026fa65569ce0e9f).

Equation [\[eq:tpc38-four-Mobius-row-kernel\]](TPC-038.md#ref-026fa65569ce0e9f) is the shifted version of the affine four–Möbius terminal boundary in TPC–34. The required physical theorem would average it over rows, orbit times, opposite rows, aliases, and projective layers. It is therefore not logically equivalent to proving $\sum_{k<K}\mu(N+kM)=o(K)$ for every $N$. Conversely, it cannot be deduced from character orthogonality because all joint aliases have ratio one.

## Prescribed fields versus a hereditary all–field estimate

###### ref-6a50e36ddac5ed08

###### ref-e4b323c7a0ce0fe0

Let $\mathcal K$ be a coefficient Hilbert space containing the two prescribed fields $C,P$, and let $\mathfrak T:\mathcal K\to\mathcal H_{\rm phys}$ be the linear synthesis map $$\boldsymbol\kappa\longmapsto  \sum_F\mathscr Z_F[\boldsymbol\kappa].  \label{eq:tpc38-formal-synthesis-map}$$ The TPC–37 continuation asks only for bounds at the two fields $C$ and $P$. An all–field estimate of the same normalization would assert $$\left\|\sum_F\mathscr Z_F[\boldsymbol\kappa]\right\|_2^2  \ll X^{o(1)}B_0  \sup_N\sum_F|\kappa_N(F)|^2  \label{eq:tpc38-all-field-estimate}$$ for every admissible field $\boldsymbol\kappa$. We call it hereditary if the same bound remains valid after multiplying the field by indicators of determinant fibers or factorization layers. This norm is the one appearing in the earlier prescribed–field input; no Hilbert structure for the right–hand side is being asserted.

###### ref-e1c76d5f2d3232cd

> **Proposition: Two tests do not determine an operator norm** \[prop:tpc38-two-test-dichotomy\] Bounds for $\mathfrak T C$ and $\mathfrak T P$ alone give no bound for $\|\mathfrak T\|_{\operatorname{op}}$ on the orthogonal complement of their span in $\mathcal K$. Moreover, a small recombined value $\mathfrak T(C+P)$ gives no bound for either separate value.

> **Proof** The first statement is elementary finite–dimensional linear algebra: an operator may vanish on two prescribed vectors and have arbitrary norm on their orthogonal complement. For the second, take $\mathfrak T C=v$ and $\mathfrak T P=-v$, or use the explicit offjoint construction in [\[prop:tpc38-face-splitting-obstruction\]](TPC-038.md#ref-1f59da864537c0c4).

###### ref-4238113847606263

In addition, [\[thm:tpc38-equality-recovery-obstruction\]](TPC-038.md#ref-9996f0eabaacb2ef) shows that the two completed test values do not recover their $F=0$ column. This is logically distinct from their failure to determine the operator norm. The distinction becomes arithmetic on the equality face. Heredity under $F=0$ would in particular give the explicit restricted input $$\|\mathscr Z_0[\boldsymbol\kappa]\|_2^2  \ll X^{o(1)}B_0\sup_N|\kappa_N(0)|^2  \label{eq:tpc38-hereditary-F0-input}$$ for every field supported on $F=0$.

###### ref-f1c49c48f39012f9

###### ref-ac93b4d273f8c5cd

###### ref-a20856b15afb441b

> **Theorem: Equality–face embedding** \[thm:tpc38-equality-face-embedding\] Assume [\[eq:tpc38-hereditary-F0-input\]](TPC-038.md#ref-4238113847606263), and let $\mathscr R_0:=\mathscr Z_0[\mathbf1]$ be the raw regular–subpacket output on $F=0$. Then $$\boxed{  \mathscr Z_0[C]=A\mathscr R_0,  \qquad  \mathscr Z_0[P]=(1-A)\mathscr R_0.}  \label{eq:tpc38-equality-face-embedding}$$ Consequently, the hereditary all–field estimate at normalization $B_0=Q^2J$ implies $$\|\mathscr R_0\|_2^2\ll X^{o(1)}Q^2J.  \label{eq:tpc38-hereditary-equality-bound}$$ This is stronger than the inherited target $Q^3/J$ by a factor of order $\mathcal K_X=(Q/J^2)X^{o(1)}$.

> **Proof** The identities follow from $C_N(0)=A$ and $P_N(0)=1-A$, which are independent of $N$. Apply the hereditary estimate to $C_N(F)\mathbf 1_{F=0}$ and divide by $A^2=1-O(J^{-1})$. Finally, $Q^2J\mathcal K_X=X^{o(1)}Q^3/J$ by [\[eq:tpc38-interface-key-ledger\]](TPC-038.md#ref-ba728cc4c5c46461).

If the hereditary all–field estimate is also stable under the terminal restriction $s=1$, then [\[eq:tpc38-hereditary-equality-bound\]](TPC-038.md#ref-a20856b15afb441b) applies to the affine terminal operator whose square expands into [\[eq:tpc38-four-Mobius-row-kernel\]](TPC-038.md#ref-026fa65569ce0e9f) with $k=k'=0$.

###### ref-405bdd3a4b8fa067

> **Corollary: Terminal hereditary all–field implication** \[cor:tpc38-terminal-hereditary-implication\] An all–field estimate hereditary under $F=0,s=1$ implies a diagonal–scale bound for the terminal affine–Möbius operator isolated in TPC–34.

This corollary does not say that the two prescribed–field inputs [\[eq:tpc38-interface-two-field-input\]](TPC-038.md#ref-b5eed4607e144ffd) implies affine Chowla. By itself that input does not even recover the equality column from its alias completion, and it may also use cancellation between equality, proper divisors, terminal atoms, and nonzero aliases. The implication applies only to the stronger, localizable operator theorem.

## What the sharp examples do not prove

The rank–one witness [\[eq:tpc38-rank-one-alias-witness\]](TPC-038.md#ref-2fe8c9f8aeffde09), the offjoint witness [\[eq:tpc38-offjoint-witness\]](TPC-038.md#ref-c38489d09bbf55fb), and the principal target mode in [5](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-038.md#src-99d0480ff83b9ca2) are artificial coefficient choices. They establish optimal losses in broad abstract classes. They do not prove that the actual Möbius vectors align, that [\[ass:tpc38-joint-alias-Gram\]](TPC-038.md#ref-6f592e6f46293f4c) is false, or that a Möbius autocorrelation is large. Their role is to identify precisely which new arithmetic information a positive theorem must use.

# The corrected route tree: completion and equality recovery

The target–coupled continuation separates into exact CRT algebra, a congruence–completed analytic output, and an equality–recovery step. The last step cannot be omitted. This section records the corrected route tree.

## What the exact algebra removes

The unconditional reductions prove the following.

1.  The dependence on a moving target $N$ is exactly the ratio phase $\chi(N+F)\overline{\chi(N)}$; no unidentified affine shift remains in the finite CRT algebra.

2.  Fixed–target character orthogonality, moving–target shifted Jacobi Grams, and every zero–extended character convention are explicit.

3.  For fixed $F$, all coefficient–free target collisions are localized by the exact multiplicity $d_M(F)$. Generic determinant strata have no target collision.

4.  Full/proper face splitting may be reversed before estimating. Every nonjoint ghost then cancels identically, and the completed output is the joint–alias sum $\mathscr Y_{\rm alias}$.

5.  The completion has $O(\mathcal K_X)$ columns $F=kM$, with $\mathcal K_X=X^{1/400+o(1)}$ at the endpoint. On them, every multiplicative character phase is one.

6.  Diagonal trace plus coefficient–blind Cauchy loses exactly the finite alias count $K_B$ on the completed alias sum; at the endpoint this has order $\mathcal K_X$, and the loss is sharp in the abstract Hilbert column class.

7.  The original physical output is the single column $\mathscr Z_0$, not $\mathscr Y_{\rm alias}$. The completed fixed fields do not recover this column in the abstract class.

###### ref-334fa7425a01f208

The corrected implication ledger is therefore $$\left.  \begin{array}{c}   \text{two fixed face--field estimates}\\   \text{plus the exact period ledgers}  \end{array}  \right\}  \Longrightarrow  \|\mathscr Y_{\rm alias}\|_2^2  \ll X^{o(1)}Q^2J\mathcal K_X,  \qquad  \mathscr Y_{\rm alias}\not\Rightarrow\mathscr Z_0.  \label{eq:tpc38-corrected-implication-ledger}$$ The final nonimplication is witnessed by [\[thm:tpc38-equality-recovery-obstruction\]](TPC-038.md#ref-9996f0eabaacb2ef).

## Route A: target–scale two–sum recovery

###### ref-1f79d009a0b9804d

The weakest explicit completion–based recovery route isolated here is to prove the completed bound in [\[eq:tpc38-alias-total-target-bound\]](TPC-038.md#ref-c7d2b3ba72dc2511) and, independently, the nonzero–alias aggregate bound $$\left\|\sum_{\substack{k\in\mathcal I_B\\k\ne0}}\mathscr Z_k\right\|_2^2  \ll X^{o(1)}Q^2J K_B.  \label{eq:tpc38-next-nonzero-alias}$$ Their difference is $\mathscr Z_0$, so [\[prop:tpc38-two-sum-recovery\]](TPC-038.md#ref-3aaab44f4e79ed04) gives the inherited target $X^{o(1)}Q^3/J$.

This route does not ask for uniform control of every alias column or for an operator estimate under arbitrary alias weights. It asks only for one additional physical vector. Its expansion contains the averaged $M$-spaced Möbius correlations in [\[prop:tpc38-terminal-alias-content\]](TPC-038.md#ref-974743ea9ab05e76); hence it still requires actual coefficient information, but it is weaker than pointwise Chowla and weaker than a hereditary all–field theorem.

## Route B: strong localized or Gram theorems

###### ref-899a0e713afe6ecb

###### ref-6c0b80bc7075f969

One may instead prove the uniform raw–column estimate $$\|\mathscr Z_k\|_2^2\ll X^{o(1)}Q^2J  \qquad(k\in\mathcal I_B).  \label{eq:tpc38-next-strong-column}$$ This is a meaningful moving–shift theorem, because the shifts $$h_k=h+kM  \label{eq:tpc38-next-moving-h}$$ have new primitivity and incidence behavior. It is not merely a uniform version of the old input–copy diagonal: at $k=0$, [\[eq:tpc38-next-strong-column\]](TPC-038.md#ref-899a0e713afe6ecb) already proves the original physical output at the stronger $Q^2J$ scale.

###### ref-4b12c541d9d51a6d

The still stronger operator route is $$\|\mathsf G_B\|_{\operatorname{op}}\ll X^{o(1)}Q^2J.  \label{eq:tpc38-next-strong-Gram}$$ Since a Gram operator controls its diagonal entries, [\[eq:tpc38-next-strong-Gram\]](TPC-038.md#ref-4b12c541d9d51a6d) implies [\[eq:tpc38-next-strong-column\]](TPC-038.md#ref-899a0e713afe6ecb). These are therefore nested alternatives, not two independent sequential gates. The Gram theorem is reusable under arbitrary alias weights; the localized theorem is already sufficient for the original output.

## Route C: exact recovery by several completions

###### ref-19a01fe883897d95

A different possibility is to construct several CRT completions with moduli $M_d$ and weights $w_d$ such that, throughout the determinant window, $$\sum_d w_d\mathbf 1_{M_d\mid F}=\mathbf 1_{F=0}.  \label{eq:tpc38-multi-completion-recovery}$$ If each weighted completion can be controlled without an excessive synthesis norm, this identity recovers equality algebraically rather than by subtracting a nonzero–alias sum. No such well–conditioned family is constructed here.

The four–modulus case is the simplest exact recovery: $M_4>X$ makes $\mathbf 1_{M_4\mid F}=\mathbf 1_{F=0}$. However, its full face has coefficient $1-O(J^{-1})$ at equality, so the method loops back to the unsuppressed original coefficient. More moduli solve recovery but do not by themselves solve the arithmetic energy estimate.

## Three viable outcomes for the next layer

Positive aggregate recovery theorem.

:   Prove [\[eq:tpc38-next-nonzero-alias\]](TPC-038.md#ref-1f79d009a0b9804d) for the actual rows and combine it with a completed fixed–field estimate. This is the most economical route identified by the present audit.

Positive strong theorem.

:   Prove the localized column estimate or the alias–Gram estimate. Either would be independently publishable; the latter is strictly stronger and includes the former.

Scoped obstruction or recovery no–go.

:   Within a precisely stated physical coefficient, shift, or completion class, prove that no target–scale recovery functional exists, or that a principal alias direction is unavoidable. Such a result would rule out that architecture at the stated normalization, not prove a lower bound for the prime–pair remainder.

## Relation to the parity boundary

The exact rank–one directions and terminal Möbius correlations are consistent with the classical parity obstruction for sieve methods [`\citep{HeathBrown1982Parity,IwaniecKowalski2004}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-038.md#cite-bd33f7374ef1e88f); [\[IwaniecKowalski2004\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-038.md#cite-49dfea87eaada156). This paper neither reformulates nor resolves that obstruction. A future positive recovery theorem would have to exploit coefficient information beyond the finite character identities proved here. Even then, it would first establish the inherited energy gate; further work would still be required before any prime–pair asymptotic.

## Unconditional conclusions

Under the hypotheses stated in the corresponding results (and using the inherited notation only when physical scales are mentioned), this paper proves:

1.  the exact target–coupled character expansion of every CRT face for unit targets;

2.  full/proper recombination and the elimination of all nonjoint ghosts in the regular CRT completion before estimation;

3.  fixed–target Parseval and the shifted Jacobi cross–target Gram;

4.  Hilbert–valued ratio Parseval and the exact determinant multiplicity $d_M(F)$, hence the scale ladder $1,J,J^2,J^3$ when $q_i\asymp J$;

5.  exact fixed–determinant target masses;

6.  the complete scalar target–synthesis spectra of the full and proper fields;

7.  character blindness on the joint–alias identity bucket;

8.  the sharp coefficient–free $K_B$ Cauchy loss for coherent alias columns;

9.  nonrecoverability of the exact equality column from the completed alias sum or the two completed fixed face fields;

10. strict non–equivalence between separate face estimates and their recombined output;

11. the equality–face embedding and the strength of a hereditary all–field operator estimate; and

12. the exact terminal alias autocorrelation identity and the displayed four–Möbius factor contained in the physical row kernel.

## What is not proved

Neither the manuscript nor the certificate proves:

1.  the uniform full alias–column bound [\[eq:tpc38-moving-shift-diagonal\]](TPC-038.md#ref-a3103ade218db117);

2.  the joint–alias Gram estimate [\[eq:tpc38-joint-alias-Gram-gate\]](TPC-038.md#ref-2fde392cac1a0576) or its one–vector weakening;

3.  the two fixed physical face–field estimates [\[eq:tpc38-interface-two-field-input\]](TPC-038.md#ref-b5eed4607e144ffd);

4.  the nonzero–alias recovery estimate [\[eq:tpc38-nonzero-alias-recovery-gate\]](TPC-038.md#ref-7ec0cc96821108da), or any other recovery of $\mathscr Z_0$ from the completed face outputs;

5.  a hereditary target–coupled all–field operator theorem;

6.  the full physical orbit gate $V_L+V_R\ll X^{o(1)}Q^3/J$;

7.  cancellation in the Möbius progression $N+kM$, pointwise or on average;

8.  an affine Chowla theorem or a new Möbius–disjointness theorem;

9.  failure of sieve parity or positivity;

10. a Hardy–Littlewood prime–pair asymptotic; or

11. infinitely many twin primes.

The advance is an exact reduction and a corrected logical gate. The finite unit–target CRT dependence and regular nonjoint face geometry have been resolved; the remaining physical identity bucket has a target–scale recovery route, a nested stronger alias–Gram route, and a sharp coefficient–free loss.
