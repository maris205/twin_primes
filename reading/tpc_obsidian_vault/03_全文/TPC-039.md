## Abstract

TPC–38 showed that a three–modulus multiplicative completion identifies the recombined output with the joint–alias sum, not its original equality column. Multiplicative characters cannot distinguish those aliases because every one lies in the same ratio bucket. We construct the missing coefficient–free recovery map on the additive quotient of that bucket.

Let $M$ be the three–modulus CRT product, let $|F|\le B$, and put $$L=\left\lfloor\frac BM\right\rfloor,  \qquad q=L+1,  \qquad \omega=\exp(2\pi i 1/q).$$ Then $qM>B$, and on the full determinant window $$\mathbf 1_{M\mid F}\frac1q\sum_{\nu=0}^{q-1}  \exp(2\pi i \nu F/(qM))  =\mathbf 1_{qM\mid F}  =\mathbf 1_{F=0}.$$ Full/proper face recombination is essential in this identity: the additive average alone does not remove determinants with $M\nmid F$. For arbitrary Hilbert columns $\mathscr Z_k$, $-L\le k\le L$, define $$\mathscr Y_\nu=\sum_{k=-L}^{L}\omega^{\nu k}\mathscr Z_k.$$ The quotient projector gives the exact recovery formula with unit normalized amplification $$\mathscr Z_0=\frac1q\sum_{\nu=0}^{q-1}\mathscr Y_\nu,  \qquad  \|\mathscr Z_0\|^2\le  \frac1q\sum_{\nu=0}^{q-1}\|\mathscr Y_\nu\|^2.$$

We prove the Hilbert–valued folded Parseval identity $$\frac1q\sum_{\nu=0}^{q-1}\|\mathscr Y_\nu\|^2  =\|\mathscr Z_0\|^2+  \sum_{r=1}^{L}\|\mathscr Z_r+\mathscr Z_{r-q}\|^2.$$ The normalized scalar alias–index tomography Gram has eigenvalues $2,1,0$, with multiplicities $L,1,L$, respectively. Its kernel consists precisely of antisymmetric folded alias pairs and does not intersect the equality column. Thus the minimal bank is weaker than a raw alias trace or a full Gram estimate while still recovering the one column required by the physical problem.

The number $q=L+1$ is optimal. Even allowing arbitrary nonzero phase nodes and complex recovery weights, no bank of at most $L$ phase measurements recovers the central coefficient of every Laurent polynomial supported on $[-L,L]$. The regular $q$-gon attains this lower bound and also minimizes the normalized noise amplification. Under positive weights, every minimally sampled optimal rule is a rotation of this regular polygon with equal weights. Deleting any one twist from the minimal bank destroys coefficient–free exact recovery.

For the orbit–sliced prime–Möbius interface, the twist is $\exp(2\pi i \nu(su-N)/(qM))$. At the endpoint, $$q\asymp 1+X/M=X^{1/400+o(1)},  \qquad  Q^2Jq=X^{o(1)}Q^3/J.$$ Consequently, a square–average bound of this target size for the recombined twisted outputs would close the inherited equality gate with no additional alias loss. The corresponding full/proper fields have exactly the same coefficient masses as their untwisted versions. However, folded Parseval retains the original equality energy as a positive summand, and the terminal expansion retains affine four–Möbius correlations.

We therefore prove an exact, minimally sampled equality projector and a new twist–stable conditional route, not the required Möbius energy estimate. No affine Chowla theorem, parity breach, Hardy–Littlewood asymptotic, or twin–prime theorem is claimed.

## The finite quotient projector

###### ref-d536a7b71e6cbe41

###### ref-d53684d0c8e68770

The physical form of [\[eq:tpc39-intro-exact-recovery\]](TPC-039.md#ref-e2d85d9a64ed13e0) is the identity $$\boxed{  \mathbf 1_{M\mid F}\frac1q\sum_{\nu=0}^{q-1}  \exp(2\pi i \nu F/(qM))  =\mathbf 1_{F=0}  \qquad(|F|\le B).}  \label{eq:tpc39-intro-quotient-projector}$$ Indeed, the left side is $\mathbf 1_{qM\mid F}$, and $qM>B$. The factor $\mathbf 1_{M\mid F}$ is supplied by the exact full/proper recombination $$C_N(F)+P_N(F)=\mathbf 1_{M\mid F}  \label{eq:tpc39-intro-face-recombination}$$ proved in TPC–38. It cannot be omitted: the additive average alone is generally nonzero when $M\nmid F$. Only after full/proper recombination restricts the determinant to $F=kM$ does the quotient average select the multiples of $qM$.

###### ref-3759006971048452

Thus [\[eq:tpc39-intro-quotient-projector\]](TPC-039.md#ref-d536a7b71e6cbe41) is an additive projector on the quotient $$M\mathbb Z/qM\mathbb Z\cong\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z.  \label{eq:tpc39-intro-quotient-group}$$ It converts the congruence completion into exact equality without adding a fourth multiplicative character coordinate.

## Folded Parseval and the exact spectrum

###### ref-e316ffd845b808ed

###### ref-a6abc6a117d9c591

###### ref-662170d232e61bba

For Hilbert–valued columns, character orthogonality on [\[eq:tpc39-intro-quotient-group\]](TPC-039.md#ref-3759006971048452) gives $$\boxed{  \frac1q\sum_{\nu=0}^{q-1}\|\mathscr Y_\nu\|^2  =\|\mathscr Z_0\|^2+  \sum_{r=1}^{L}\|\mathscr Z_r+\mathscr Z_{r-q}\|^2.}  \label{eq:tpc39-intro-folded-Parseval}$$ The associated normalized tomography Gram has spectrum $$2\quad(L\text{ times}),  \qquad  1\quad(1\text{ time}),  \qquad  0\quad(L\text{ times}).  \label{eq:tpc39-intro-spectrum}$$ The eigenvalue–one direction is the zero alias. The kernel consists of antisymmetric pairs $$\mathscr Z_r=-\mathscr Z_{r-q},  \qquad 1\le r\le L,  \label{eq:tpc39-intro-kernel}$$ which do not affect $\mathscr Z_0$. Hence this bank is strictly weaker than a full alias trace and much weaker than an alias–Gram operator bound, but it is sufficient for the required column.

## Minimal sampling and optimal recovery amplification

###### ref-4e9d19b459bad42e

The sample count in [\[eq:tpc39-intro-minimal-q\]](TPC-039.md#ref-778e5915780d06f0) is best possible. Suppose phase nodes $z_1,\ldots,z_m\in\mathbb C^\times$ and complex recovery weights are required to recover the central coefficient of every Laurent polynomial supported on $[-L,L]$. If $m\le L$, the polynomial $$Q(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{m}(z-z_j)  \label{eq:tpc39-intro-annihilator}$$ has degree at most $L$, vanishes at every measurement node, and has nonzero constant coefficient. Exact recovery is therefore impossible. The regular $(L+1)$-gon attains the lower bound.

###### ref-f4f62740a2822dff

If $a$ is the recovery vector for $m$ measurements, its normalized noise amplification is $$\mathcal A=m\|a\|_2^2.  \label{eq:tpc39-intro-amplification}$$ The constant moment forces $\mathcal A\ge1$. Equal weights on the regular polygon give $\mathcal A=1$. Under positive weights, the minimally sampled rule is rigid: the weights are equal and the nodes are a rotation of the regular polygon.

## The new physical gate

###### ref-ac9a5b4f9be30403

###### ref-3b5ae6dbe0d5beeb

###### ref-52f71f101fe496d2

For the physical determinant $F=su-N$, define the twisted face fields $$C_{\nu,N}(F)=\exp(2\pi i \nu F/(qM))C_N(F),  \qquad  P_{\nu,N}(F)=\exp(2\pi i \nu F/(qM))P_N(F).  \label{eq:tpc39-intro-twisted-fields}$$ Their coefficient masses are exactly those of the untwisted fields. After recombination, their outputs are the vectors in [\[eq:tpc39-intro-twisted-bank\]](TPC-039.md#ref-fdc663f0843e235d). Therefore the explicit conditional input $$\frac1q\sum_{\nu=0}^{q-1}\|\mathscr Y_\nu\|^2  \ll X^{o(1)}Q^2J(2L+1)  \label{eq:tpc39-intro-twisted-gate}$$ implies the physical equality gate. At the endpoint, $$2L+1\asymp q\asymp X^{1/400+o(1)},  \qquad  Q^2J(2L+1)=X^{o(1)}Q^3/J.  \label{eq:tpc39-intro-endpoint}$$

Equation [\[eq:tpc39-intro-twisted-gate\]](TPC-039.md#ref-3b5ae6dbe0d5beeb) is not proved here. Folded Parseval shows that it retains $\|\mathscr Z_0\|^2$ as a positive summand. In the terminal factorization $s=1$, the twists create a short Fourier transform along the progression $N+kM$, but the square still contains the original affine four–Möbius term and long–gap correlations. Additive tomography repairs the logical recovery gap; it does not manufacture arithmetic cancellation.

## Organization and claim boundary

states the inherited physical interface. proves the quotient projector and exact Hilbert recovery. proves folded Parseval and the complete spectrum. proves sample minimality, recovery–amplification optimality, and positive–rule rigidity. compares phase recovery with pure divisibility completions. formulates the twist–stable physical gate. records its terminal Möbius content. treats partial and random banks, and gives the corrected route tree.

All quotient, Fourier, spectrum, minimality, and finite divisibility results are unconditional. The paper proves no twist–stable physical energy estimate, no new Möbius autocorrelation estimate, no affine Chowla theorem, no failure of sieve parity, no Hardy–Littlewood prime–pair asymptotic, and no twin–prime theorem.

# The exact additive quotient projector

The multiplicative CRT coordinates have already done their job once [\[eq:tpc39-interface-recombination\]](TPC-039.md#ref-6618388e545fb2cd) is reached. The surviving problem is finite additive recovery on the alias index.

## The minimal no–wrap quotient

###### ref-6729ea152ac48706

Retain the notation in [\[eq:tpc39-interface-alias-ledger\]](TPC-039.md#ref-4b99e7b60ca28f82) and put $$q=L+1,  \qquad  \omega=\exp(2\pi i 1/q).  \label{eq:tpc39-projector-q}$$ The integer $q$ need not be prime.

###### ref-765a3ecbbe7e7f6c

###### ref-30602dfa3561dc0e

> **Lemma: Minimal quotient size** \[lem:tpc39-qM-window\] One has $$B<qM\le B+M.  \label{eq:tpc39-qM-window}$$ Moreover, the residue class $0\pmod q$ meets $\{-L,\ldots,L\}$ only at $0$.

> **Proof** Write $B=LM+r$, where $0\le r<M$. Then $(L+1)M=B+(M-r)$, proving [\[eq:tpc39-qM-window\]](TPC-039.md#ref-30602dfa3561dc0e). Since $q=L+1$, every nonzero $k$ in the alias interval satisfies $0<|k|<q$.

###### ref-55d1ae2dbc1943e6

###### ref-56979d3e08be864a

> **Theorem: Additive quotient identity** \[thm:tpc39-additive-quotient-identity\] For every integer $F$ with $|F|\le B$, $$\boxed{  \mathbf 1_{M\mid F}\frac1q\sum_{\nu=0}^{q-1}  \exp(2\pi i \nu F/(qM))  =\mathbf 1_{qM\mid F}  =\mathbf 1_{F=0}.}  \label{eq:tpc39-additive-quotient-identity}$$

> **Proof** If $M\nmid F$, the first member is zero. If $F=kM$, additive orthogonality gives $$\frac1q\sum_{\nu=0}^{q-1}\omega^{\nu k}  =\mathbf 1_{q\mid k}.$$ Thus the first member is $\mathbf 1_{qM\mid F}$. By [\[lem:tpc39-qM-window\]](TPC-039.md#ref-765a3ecbbe7e7f6c), the only such determinant in the window is $F=0$.

The first factor in [\[eq:tpc39-additive-quotient-identity\]](TPC-039.md#ref-56979d3e08be864a) is essential in the physical construction. The additive average by itself is generally nonzero when $M\nmid F$, so it is not a divisibility indicator on the full determinant window. The product with $\mathbf 1_{M\mid F}$ is the indicator $\mathbf 1_{qM\mid F}$. The available completed objects are the separate full and proper face outputs; their nonjoint terms disappear only after using [\[eq:tpc39-interface-recombination\]](TPC-039.md#ref-6618388e545fb2cd) with the same twist on both fields.

> **Proposition: Recombined twist formula** \[prop:tpc39-recombined-twist\] For every $\nu$, $$\boxed{  \mathscr Y_\nu  =\sum_{k=-L}^{L}\omega^{\nu k}\mathscr Z_k.}  \label{eq:tpc39-recombined-twist}$$ In particular, all nonjoint face atoms cancel before the alias bank is formed.

> **Proof** Linearity and [\[eq:tpc39-interface-recombination\]](TPC-039.md#ref-6618388e545fb2cd) give $$\mathscr Y_\nu  =\sum_{|F|\le B}  \exp(2\pi i \nu F/(qM))\mathbf 1_{M\mid F}\mathscr R_F.$$ Write $F=kM$ and use [\[eq:tpc39-interface-Zk\]](TPC-039.md#ref-d96e09584a5f247b).

> **Theorem: Exact Hilbert equality recovery** \[thm:tpc39-exact-Hilbert-recovery\] For arbitrary columns $\mathscr Z_k\in\mathcal H_{\rm phys}$, $$\boxed{  \mathscr Z_0  =\frac1q\sum_{\nu=0}^{q-1}\mathscr Y_\nu.}  \label{eq:tpc39-exact-Hilbert-recovery}$$ Consequently, $$\boxed{  \|\mathscr Z_0\|^2  \le\frac1q\sum_{\nu=0}^{q-1}\|\mathscr Y_\nu\|^2.}  \label{eq:tpc39-condition-one-recovery}$$

> **Proof** Average [\[eq:tpc39-recombined-twist\]](TPC-039.md#ref-88113e62c30e791b) over $\nu$. By [\[lem:tpc39-qM-window\]](TPC-039.md#ref-765a3ecbbe7e7f6c), only $k=0$ survives. Jensen’s inequality gives [\[eq:tpc39-condition-one-recovery\]](TPC-039.md#ref-3fd89da0c001755e).

The later minimality theorem shows that this sample count and normalized recovery amplification are simultaneously optimal. This is not a condition-number assertion for the full tomography map, which has a nontrivial kernel.

Equation [\[eq:tpc39-condition-one-recovery\]](TPC-039.md#ref-3fd89da0c001755e) converts a twist–stable energy theorem into the desired physical estimate; it is not itself such an energy theorem.

# Hilbert–valued folded Parseval and tomography spectrum

The minimal quotient deliberately identifies some positive and negative aliases. This section computes that folding exactly.

## General quotient sizes

###### ref-1c9d23e80620a53f

###### ref-cdfcf48d77e8145c

###### ref-90b5b4d6d1912353

Let $$L<r\le2L+1  \label{eq:tpc39-general-r-range}$$ and put $\zeta_r=\exp(2\pi i 1/r)$. For Hilbert columns $Z_{-L},\ldots,Z_L\in\mathcal H$, define $$\begin{aligned}  Y_\nu^{(r)}  &:=\sum_{k=-L}^{L}\zeta_r^{\nu k}Z_k,  \qquad 0\le\nu<r,  \label{eq:tpc39-general-twists}\\  W_a^{(r)}  &:=\sum_{\substack{-L\le k\le L\\k\equiv a\pmod r}}Z_k,  \qquad a\in\mathbb Z/r\mathbb Z.  \label{eq:tpc39-residue-buckets}\end{aligned}$$

###### ref-24422f5f3f2fa037

###### ref-96aec9c6b4f7d6d0

###### ref-82b05432969cb305

> **Theorem: Hilbert–valued folded Parseval** \[thm:tpc39-folded-Parseval\] For every $r$ in [\[eq:tpc39-general-r-range\]](TPC-039.md#ref-1c9d23e80620a53f), $$\boxed{  \frac1r\sum_{\nu=0}^{r-1}\|Y_\nu^{(r)}\|^2  =\sum_{a\bmod r}\|W_a^{(r)}\|^2.}  \label{eq:tpc39-folded-Parseval}$$ At the minimal value $r=q=L+1$, $$\boxed{  \frac1q\sum_{\nu=0}^{q-1}\|Y_\nu^{(q)}\|^2  =\|Z_0\|^2+  \sum_{a=1}^{L}\|Z_a+Z_{a-q}\|^2.}  \label{eq:tpc39-minimal-folded-Parseval}$$

> **Proof** Expand the squared norms and average the phase $\zeta_r^{\nu(k-k')}$. Additive orthogonality retains precisely the pairs $k\equiv k'\pmod r$, proving [\[eq:tpc39-folded-Parseval\]](TPC-039.md#ref-96aec9c6b4f7d6d0). If $r=L+1$, the zero residue class is $\{0\}$, while residue $a$, $1\le a\le L$, contains the pair $\{a,a-q\}$.

## Complete singular spectrum

###### ref-1044dba2f33b1197

###### ref-b5fa90ffbe8fd824

Define the normalized tomography operator $$\mathcal T_r:\mathcal H^{2L+1}\longrightarrow\mathcal H^r,  \qquad  (\mathcal T_rZ)_\nu  =r^{-1/2}\sum_{k=-L}^{L}\zeta_r^{\nu k}Z_k.  \label{eq:tpc39-tomography-operator}$$ Its scalar Gram matrix is $$(\mathcal T_r^*\mathcal T_r)_{k,k'}  =\mathbf 1_{k\equiv k'\pmod r}.  \label{eq:tpc39-tomography-Gram}$$

###### ref-e310b568000ea411

###### ref-bff67e6c82a4784e

###### ref-2ef38b5253f17d8d

###### ref-7b01e58d4615c5ea

> **Theorem: Alias–index tomography spectrum** \[thm:tpc39-tomography-spectrum\] For $L<r\le2L+1$, the spectrum of the scalar alias–index Gram matrix in [\[eq:tpc39-tomography-Gram\]](TPC-039.md#ref-b5fa90ffbe8fd824) is $$\boxed{  \begin{array}{c|c}  \text{eigenvalue}&\text{multiplicity}\\ \hline  2&2L+1-r\\  1&2r-(2L+1)\\  0&2L+1-r.  \end{array}}  \label{eq:tpc39-general-spectrum}$$ At $r=q=L+1$, this becomes $$2\ (L\text{ times}),  \qquad1\ (1\text{ time}),  \qquad0\ (L\text{ times}).  \label{eq:tpc39-minimal-spectrum}$$ At the minimal value $r=q$, the eigenvalue–one subspace is the isolated zero column. The kernel is $$Z_0=0,  \qquad  Z_a=-Z_{a-q},  \quad1\le a\le L.  \label{eq:tpc39-minimal-kernel}$$ As an operator on $\mathcal H^{2L+1}$, one has $\mathcal T_r^*\mathcal T_r=G_r\otimes I_{\mathcal H}$, where $G_r$ is this scalar matrix. Thus for finite $\dim\mathcal H=d$, every displayed multiplicity is multiplied by $d$; the table records the intrinsic alias–index multiplicities.

> **Proof** Because $r>L$, every residue bucket in [\[eq:tpc39-residue-buckets\]](TPC-039.md#ref-90b5b4d6d1912353) has size one or two. There are $2L+1-r$ doubletons and $2r-(2L+1)$ singletons. On a bucket of size $s$, the Gram block in [\[eq:tpc39-tomography-Gram\]](TPC-039.md#ref-b5fa90ffbe8fd824) is the all–ones matrix $J_s$, whose eigenvalues are $s$ once and zero $s-1$ times. This proves [\[eq:tpc39-general-spectrum\]](TPC-039.md#ref-bff67e6c82a4784e). The minimal specialization follows from the explicit buckets in the proof of [\[thm:tpc39-folded-Parseval\]](TPC-039.md#ref-24422f5f3f2fa037).

###### ref-5ac8d4712ff5a33f

At the other endpoint $r=2L+1$, every bucket is a singleton and $$\frac1{2L+1}\sum_{\nu=0}^{2L}\|Y_\nu^{(2L+1)}\|^2  =\sum_{k=-L}^{L}\|Z_k\|^2.  \label{eq:tpc39-full-DFT-Parseval}$$ Thus $L+1$ twists are sufficient to recover the zero column, whereas $2L+1$ twists are required to separate every alias column by this cyclic construction.

## Exact position between trace and Gram gates

###### ref-713762b11b4cb920

###### ref-2b935d4fce21a10e

###### ref-1992baf79f51b30e

###### ref-b36c3a4180389b2a

###### ref-c34e177c93aade56

Put $$\begin{aligned}  E_{\rm tr}(Z)  &:=\sum_{k=-L}^{L}\|Z_k\|^2,  \label{eq:tpc39-trace-energy}\\  E_{\rm fold}(Z)  &:=\|Z_0\|^2+  \sum_{a=1}^{L}\|Z_a+Z_{a-q}\|^2.  \label{eq:tpc39-fold-energy}\end{aligned}$$ Then $$\|Z_0\|^2\le E_{\rm fold}(Z)\le2E_{\rm tr}(Z).  \label{eq:tpc39-fold-trace-comparison}$$ If $\mathsf G=(\langle Z_k,Z_{k'}\rangle)$, then $$E_{\rm tr}(Z)=\operatorname{tr}\mathsf G  \le(2L+1)\|\mathsf G\|_{\operatorname{op}}.  \label{eq:tpc39-Gram-trace-comparison}$$ Consequently, $$\text{alias Gram}  \Longrightarrow  \text{raw trace}  \Longrightarrow  \text{minimal folded energy}  \Longrightarrow  \text{zero--column energy}.  \label{eq:tpc39-gate-hierarchy}$$

The reverse implications fail in the unrestricted Hilbert class. Taking $Z_a=Tv$, $Z_{a-q}=-Tv$, and all other columns zero makes $E_{\rm fold}=0$ while the trace and Gram norm grow like $T^2$. Taking the same pair with equal signs and $Z_0=0$ makes the zero–column energy vanish while $E_{\rm fold}$ is arbitrarily large. Hence the minimal tomography bank discards exactly a large collection of directions that are irrelevant to equality recovery; it is not a disguised full trace theorem.

# Minimal sampling, optimal recovery amplification, and rigidity

The root–of–unity construction uses only half as many measurements as a full Fourier inversion. We now show that no phase bank can use fewer.

## Universal phase measurements

###### ref-adfd04214fc9c744

###### ref-832dd9d1df0506df

###### ref-ffa41522e12a6472

Let $z\in\mathbb C^\times$. For a Hilbert column family $Z=(Z_{-L},\ldots,Z_L)$, define the phase measurement $$\mathcal Y_Z(z)=\sum_{k=-L}^{L}z^kZ_k.  \label{eq:tpc39-general-phase-measurement}$$ Given nodes $z_1,\ldots,z_m$ and coefficients $a_1,\ldots,a_m$, the rule $$Z_0=\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_j\mathcal Y_Z(z_j)  \label{eq:tpc39-general-recovery-rule}$$ holds for every Hilbert space and every column family if and only if $$\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_jz_j^k=\mathbf 1_{k=0}  \qquad(-L\le k\le L).  \label{eq:tpc39-moment-system}$$

###### ref-0cfaaae7feb05bd0

###### ref-d7385a2a39eebeb0

> **Theorem: Minimal phase count** \[thm:tpc39-minimal-phase-count\] If [\[eq:tpc39-general-recovery-rule\]](TPC-039.md#ref-832dd9d1df0506df) holds universally, then $$\boxed{m\ge L+1.}  \label{eq:tpc39-minimal-phase-count}$$ This remains true with arbitrary nonzero complex nodes and arbitrary complex recovery weights.

###### ref-147232fe1edfdbb8

> **Proof** Suppose $m\le L$ and form $$Q(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{m}(z-z_j)  =\sum_{k=0}^{m}c_kz^k.  \label{eq:tpc39-annihilating-polynomial}$$ Its constant coefficient is $c_0=(-1)^m\prod_jz_j\ne0$. Take scalar columns $Z_k=c_k$ for $0\le k\le m$ and zero otherwise. Then every measurement in [\[eq:tpc39-general-recovery-rule\]](TPC-039.md#ref-832dd9d1df0506df) is $Q(z_j)=0$, whereas $Z_0=c_0\ne0$, a contradiction.

###### ref-9b75d3f08fa25640

###### ref-7fdf551b4069a2c9

> **Corollary: Optimality of the quotient bank** \[cor:tpc39-regular-polygon-minimal\] The nodes $$z_\nu=\omega^\nu,  \qquad0\le\nu<q=L+1,  \label{eq:tpc39-regular-polygon-nodes}$$ with weights $a_\nu=1/q$ attain the lower bound in [\[thm:tpc39-minimal-phase-count\]](TPC-039.md#ref-0cfaaae7feb05bd0).

> **Proof** The moment system [\[eq:tpc39-moment-system\]](TPC-039.md#ref-ffa41522e12a6472) is additive orthogonality modulo $q$, and $q>|k|$ for every nonzero alias index.

The lower bound concerns recovery of only $Z_0$. Recovering every one of the $2L+1$ arbitrary columns requires a measurement matrix of rank $2L+1$, and therefore at least $2L+1$ samples. The full DFT in [\[eq:tpc39-full-DFT-Parseval\]](TPC-039.md#ref-5ac8d4712ff5a33f) attains that different lower bound.

## Noise amplification

###### ref-f33c8fdb5adaac5f

###### ref-b612cd3c7cf69f05

For a recovery rule satisfying [\[eq:tpc39-moment-system\]](TPC-039.md#ref-ffa41522e12a6472), define $$\mathcal A(a):=m\|a\|_2^2.  \label{eq:tpc39-noise-amplification}$$ The Cauchy bound associated with the rule is $$\|Z_0\|^2  \le\|a\|_2^2\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|\mathcal Y_Z(z_j)\|^2.  \label{eq:tpc39-general-recovery-Cauchy}$$

> **Proposition: Optimal normalized amplification** \[prop:tpc39-optimal-conditioning\] Every universal recovery rule satisfies $$\boxed{\mathcal A(a)\ge1.}  \label{eq:tpc39-amplification-lower-bound}$$ The regular polygon rule in [\[cor:tpc39-regular-polygon-minimal\]](TPC-039.md#ref-9b75d3f08fa25640) has $\mathcal A=1$.

## Rigidity of positive minimal rules

The regular polygon is not merely one positive optimal construction.

> **Theorem: Positive–rule rigidity** \[thm:tpc39-positive-rule-rigidity\] Let $m=q=L+1$, let $z_1,\ldots,z_q\in\mathbb T$, and let $w_1,\ldots,w_q>0$. ###### ref-4b00a85f4e689ea0

> **Proof** Form the square matrix $$U_{j,r}=\sqrt{w_j}\,z_j^r,  \qquad1\le j\le q,  \quad0\le r\le q-1.  \label{eq:tpc39-positive-rule-matrix}$$ By [\[eq:tpc39-positive-moments\]](TPC-039.md#ref-6fb3fc228b784fd2), $(U^*U)_{r,s}=\delta_{r,s}$, since $|s-r|\le q-1=L$. The $q$ distinct nodes are one rotation of all $q$-th roots.

# Divisibility completions and the alias–synthesis trade

The quotient projector can be compared with the multi–completion route proposed in TPC–38. The comparison explains both why a superwindow modulus is unavoidable and why additive phases are a distinct implementation of it.

## Exact rigidity of linear divisibility recovery

###### ref-6f9154660ff2791e

For a positive integer $m$, put $$d_m(F)=\mathbf 1_{m\mid F},  \qquad |F|\le B.  \label{eq:tpc39-divisibility-column}$$ Repeated moduli in a linear combination are combined into one aggregate coefficient $a_m$.

###### ref-f08cb2a2d8f41f51

###### ref-87137ec8a8b8dc0d

###### ref-2fdc35d7300f1ddb

> **Theorem: Identity–bucket divisibility rigidity** \[thm:tpc39-divisibility-rigidity\] Suppose a finite family satisfies $$\sum_m a_m d_m(F)=\mathbf 1_{F=0}  \qquad(|F|\le B).  \label{eq:tpc39-divisibility-recovery}$$ Then $$a_m=0\quad(1\le m\le B),  \qquad  \sum_{m>B}a_m=1.  \label{eq:tpc39-divisibility-rigidity}$$ Conversely, [\[eq:tpc39-divisibility-rigidity\]](TPC-039.md#ref-2fdc35d7300f1ddb) is sufficient.

###### ref-30da7b3dc8e86681

> **Proof** Evaluate [\[eq:tpc39-divisibility-recovery\]](TPC-039.md#ref-87137ec8a8b8dc0d) at each $1\le n\le B$: $$0=\sum_{m\mid n}a_m.  \label{eq:tpc39-divisibility-triangular}$$ At $n=1$ this gives $a_1=0$. Increasing induction on $n$ then gives $a_n=0$, since every proper divisor coefficient has already vanished. At $F=0$, all divisibility columns equal one, so the remaining coefficients sum to one. If $m>B$, then $d_m=\mathbf 1_{F=0}$ on the window, proving the converse.

Thus no linear combination of window–internal completion indicators can recover exact equality. Every effective coefficient in an exact rule is already attached to a no–wrap modulus. This is a no–go for that particular coefficient class, not for phase measurements or for relations among the actual physical columns.

## How quotient tomography implements no–wrap recovery

###### ref-8f0d999d1b6b64a7

The projector in [\[thm:tpc39-additive-quotient-identity\]](TPC-039.md#ref-55d1ae2dbc1943e6) may be written as $$\mathbf 1_{M\mid F}\frac1q\sum_{\nu\bmod q}  \exp(2\pi i \nu F/(qM))  =\mathbf 1_{qM\mid F}.  \label{eq:tpc39-phase-implements-superwindow}$$ Therefore it does not contradict [\[thm:tpc39-divisibility-rigidity\]](TPC-039.md#ref-f08cb2a2d8f41f51): the effective modulus $qM$ is larger than $B$. What is new is its implementation. Instead of introducing $qM$ as an additional multiplicative CRT face modulus, we retain the three–prime recombination at $M$ and resolve its quotient by $q$ additive phase measurements. Each phase has unit modulus, so it does not change the scalar face masses.

## A finite alias–synthesis conservation law

###### ref-cdc9ea22d8eb0798

###### ref-e2ed2a76c479e990

###### ref-79f4850dee325a36

For comparison, let $$R=\prod_{i=1}^{s}p_i  \label{eq:tpc39-general-CRT-R}$$ be a squarefree CRT modulus with a fixed number $s$ of odd prime factors. The full target–synthesis calculation of TPC–38 gives the exact largest squared singular value $$\Lambda_C(R)  =\prod_{i=1}^{s}  \frac{p_i(p_i-2)^2}{(p_i-1)^2}  =R\prod_{i=1}^{s}  \left(1-\frac1{p_i-1}\right)^2.  \label{eq:tpc39-full-synthesis-R}$$ The joint alias count in the window is $$K_R=1+2\left\lfloor\frac BR\right\rfloor.  \label{eq:tpc39-alias-count-R}$$

###### ref-0fa4290f0bc63308

###### ref-600a9947198636b5

> **Proposition: Alias–synthesis conservation** \[prop:tpc39-alias-synthesis-conservation\] If $s$ is fixed and $\min_i p_i\to\infty$, then $$\boxed{  \Lambda_C(R)K_R\asymp R+B.}  \label{eq:tpc39-alias-synthesis-conservation}$$ In particular, coefficient–blind full synthesis cannot make both the modulus cost and the joint alias count smaller than the determinant scale.

> **Proof** The product in [\[eq:tpc39-full-synthesis-R\]](TPC-039.md#ref-e2ed2a76c479e990) is bounded above and below by positive constants and tends to one. Also $RK_R\asymp R+B$.

###### ref-135f20e731a16263

This is a finite spectral trade, not a lower bound for the Möbius physical output. Directly increasing $R$ reduces aliases in inverse proportion to the dominant full synthesis scale. Additive quotient tomography keeps $R=M$ in the multiplicative face calculus and pays instead for a bank of $$q=L+1\asymp1+B/M  \label{eq:tpc39-phase-bank-size}$$ structured outputs. The recovery functional has unit normalized amplification, but an arithmetic theorem uniform or square–averaged over this bank is still required. Neither implementation is coefficient–free evidence of a power saving.

# Twisted face fields and the physical recovery gate

We now return the quotient algebra to the inherited physical interface. Every statement that estimates a physical output is isolated as a conditional input.

## Why this is smaller than an all–field theorem

An all–field Bessel estimate stable under arbitrary determinant restrictions would control every field $\kappa_N(F)$, hence in particular all $C_{\nu,N},P_{\nu,N}$. The minimal twist bank asks only for the $2q$ explicit fields in [\[eq:tpc39-twisted-C-field\]](TPC-039.md#ref-806bfbf96b128e69)–[\[eq:tpc39-twisted-P-field\]](TPC-039.md#ref-3bb13bbc61dd1251), or more weakly for their $q$ recombinations. It discards the $L$-dimensional antisymmetric kernel in [\[eq:tpc39-minimal-kernel\]](TPC-039.md#ref-7b01e58d4615c5ea). It is therefore a genuinely smaller operator test than the hereditary all–field theorem isolated in TPC–38.

It is not known whether the actual product–slope and projective reassembly methods are stable under this bank. Establishing precisely that stability is the new physical gate.

# Terminal injectivity and the Möbius boundary

The terminal factorization $s=1$ displays the arithmetic content that the twist bank must estimate. We first remove a possible combinatorial ambiguity: distinct row–alias atoms do not represent the same integer.

## Exact terminal injectivity

###### ref-9542d4832235e24f

For fixed $j\in\mathcal J$, define $$n_{\alpha,j,k}  :=m_\alpha j+h+kM,  \qquad -L\le k\le L.  \label{eq:tpc39-terminal-target}$$

###### ref-f4a55b05d971cacf

###### ref-b116b8f4e120e58c

###### ref-20656c23db59c9eb

> **Theorem: Terminal row–alias injectivity** \[thm:tpc39-terminal-injectivity\] Assume [\[eq:tpc39-interface-prime-range\]](TPC-039.md#ref-92725e702dd1a0c3) and, as at the endpoint, $$\max_{\alpha,\beta}|m_\alpha-m_\beta|<M.  \label{eq:tpc39-row-difference-less-M}$$ For every fixed $j$, the map $$(\alpha,k)\longmapsto n_{\alpha,j,k}  \label{eq:tpc39-terminal-injective-map}$$ is injective.

###### ref-b42b058441c6734e

> **Proof** If $n_{\alpha,j,k}=n_{\beta,j,k'}$, then $$(m_\alpha-m_\beta)j=(k'-k)M.  \label{eq:tpc39-terminal-collision-equation}$$ Every prime factor $q_i$ of $M$ is larger than the positive integer $j$, so $(j,M)=1$. Thus $M\mid m_\alpha-m_\beta$. By [\[eq:tpc39-row-difference-less-M\]](TPC-039.md#ref-b116b8f4e120e58c), the two row integers are equal. Source separation gives $\alpha=\beta$, and then [\[eq:tpc39-terminal-collision-equation\]](TPC-039.md#ref-b42b058441c6734e) gives $k=k'$.

At the endpoint, $m_\alpha\asymp Q$ and $M/Q\to\infty$, so [\[eq:tpc39-row-difference-less-M\]](TPC-039.md#ref-b116b8f4e120e58c) is automatic after the dyadic constants are fixed. The theorem says that no hidden target multiplicity is responsible for the terminal difficulty.

## The atomic diagonal is already at the target scale

###### ref-a8b9bed5cf0fed16

###### ref-89821d301b14e2fc

###### ref-048c92e9979d0837

###### ref-4ee8da387d9638de

With a terminal cutoff $\Phi_0$, put $$b_{\alpha,j,k}  :=-\mu(n_{\alpha,j,k})\log(n_{\alpha,j,k})  \Phi_0(n_{\alpha,j,k}),  \label{eq:tpc39-terminal-b}$$ interpreting the term as zero outside the positive terminal support. A left terminal slice, including the equality alias $k=0$, has the form $$\mathcal T_{\gamma,j}^{L}  =\sum_\alpha\sum_{k\in\mathcal I_B}  c_{\alpha,\gamma,j}^{L}b_{\alpha,j,k},  \label{eq:tpc39-terminal-left-slice}$$ where $$c_{\alpha,\gamma,j}^{L}  =\gamma_\alpha^{(1)}  \mathfrak A_{\alpha,\gamma}^{\rm act}(j).  \label{eq:tpc39-terminal-left-coefficient}$$ The right slice is symmetric. The shift changes the terminal atom $b$, not the inherited row–time multiplier, and the deterministic multiplier ledger gives $$\sup_{\alpha,\gamma,j}  \left|\mathfrak A_{\alpha,\gamma}^{\rm act}(j)\right|  \ll X^{o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc39-terminal-multiplier-ledger}$$ This is only a pointwise mask, residue, and smooth–weight bound; it is not the unproved moving–shift Hilbert output estimate. All cutoffs and logarithms are absorbed into $X^{o(1)}$.

For the $\nu$-th twist–bank member, each summand additionally carries $\omega^{\nu k}$. This factor has modulus one and disappears on the atomic diagonal, so the same diagonal bound holds after averaging over $\nu$.

###### ref-511cdbe376e71098

Expanding the two squared energies gives $$\|\mathcal T^{L}\|_2^2+\|\mathcal T^{R}\|_2^2  =\mathcal D_{\rm term}+\mathcal O_{\rm term},  \label{eq:tpc39-terminal-diagonal-offdiagonal}$$ where $\mathcal D_{\rm term}$ contains only $(\alpha,k)=(\beta,k')$, and $\mathcal O_{\rm term}$ contains every other pair.

###### ref-4cea4c56a30f0c60

###### ref-8ac425080b7083e1

###### ref-314ca28a9b1d01ac

> **Proposition: Terminal diagonal closure** \[prop:tpc39-terminal-diagonal-closure\] The atomic diagonal satisfies $$\boxed{  \mathcal D_{\rm term}  \ll X^{o(1)}Q^2JK_B.}  \label{eq:tpc39-terminal-diagonal-bound}$$ For $B\asymp X$, $$\mathcal D_{\rm term}  \ll X^{o(1)}\frac{Q^3}{J}.  \label{eq:tpc39-terminal-diagonal-endpoint}$$

> **Proof** There are $O(X^{o(1)}Q)$ opposite rows and $O(J)$ orbit times. For each of the $K_B$ aliases, use $\sum_\alpha|\gamma_\alpha^{(i)}|^2\ll X^{o(1)}Q$ and the bounded multiplier. This proves [\[eq:tpc39-terminal-diagonal-bound\]](TPC-039.md#ref-8ac425080b7083e1) for both sides. Finally, [\[eq:tpc39-interface-key-ledger\]](TPC-039.md#ref-0cf72678525ad0ae) gives [\[eq:tpc39-terminal-diagonal-endpoint\]](TPC-039.md#ref-314ca28a9b1d01ac).

Together with [\[thm:tpc39-terminal-injectivity\]](TPC-039.md#ref-f4a55b05d971cacf), this closes the terminal collision and atomic diagonal ledger. The remaining term $\mathcal O_{\rm term}$ is genuine coefficient correlation:

###### ref-84983e0105cd6692

1.  if $\alpha=\beta$ and $k\ne k'$, it contains $$\mu(n_{\alpha,j,k})\mu(n_{\alpha,j,k'}),   \qquad   n_{\alpha,j,k}-n_{\alpha,j,k'}=(k-k')M;   \label{eq:tpc39-terminal-two-Mobius}$$

2.  if $\alpha\ne\beta$, the physical row provenance contributes the additional factors $\mu(d_\alpha)\mu(d_\beta)$, producing the affine four–Möbius quadratic form isolated in TPC–34 and TPC–38.

## What minimal folding retains

###### ref-6ba17778b1140219

###### ref-9616290f6d7a47f9

###### ref-b9830bba22a97dab

Let $$C_{k,k'}=\langle\mathscr Z_k,\mathscr Z_{k'}\rangle.  \label{eq:tpc39-alias-covariance}$$ The minimal twisted energy is exactly $$\begin{aligned}  E_{\rm fold}  &=C_{0,0}  \notag\\  &\quad+  \sum_{r=1}^{L}  \bigl(  C_{r,r}+C_{r-q,r-q}  +2\operatorname{Re}C_{r,r-q}  \bigr).  \label{eq:tpc39-terminal-folded-covariance}\end{aligned}$$ The diagonal entries in this formula still contain cross–row terms at a fixed alias. In addition, each folded pair has $$r-(r-q)=q,  \qquad qM\in(B,B+M],  \label{eq:tpc39-terminal-long-gap}$$ so its cross term contains a long–gap Möbius correlation.

###### ref-b571c242f8e7d394

The twist $$\exp(2\pi i \nu(su-N_\alpha(j))/(qM))  =\omega^{\nu k}  \label{eq:tpc39-terminal-physical-twist}$$ is constant across all rows at fixed $k$. It therefore does not oscillate the fixed–alias cross–row form. The two members of a folded pair have the same phase modulo $q$, so that correlation also survives. Most importantly, $C_{0,0}=\|\mathscr R_0\|^2$ remains in [\[eq:tpc39-terminal-folded-covariance\]](TPC-039.md#ref-9616290f6d7a47f9) without alteration.

## Comparison with short progression variance

###### ref-da9bb4ff49e2e555

###### ref-52c6f9829a10735e

The closest general theorem for scalar multiplicative functions in this range is the variance estimate of [`\citet[Theorem~1.5]{KlurmanMangerelTeravainen2023}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-039.md#cite-116881a603c7c4db). After subtraction of one most–pretentious character, it gives for almost every suitable modulus $m=o(x)$ a bound of schematic size $$\varepsilon\,\varphi(m)\left(\frac xm\right)^2  \label{eq:tpc39-KMT-scale}$$ over the reduced residue classes; under GRH the exceptional–modulus restriction is removed for typical moduli. Specializing only the scale to $m=M$, $x\asymp X$, and $K=X/M\asymp K_B$, and using $\varphi(M)\asymp M$, the right side has order $$\varepsilon MK^2,  \label{eq:tpc39-KMT-endpoint-scale}$$ whereas the scalar atomic diagonal has order $MK$. Thus this black box would need $\varepsilon\ll K^{-1}X^{o(1)}$ to reach the diagonal scale. At the TPC endpoint, $K=X^{1/400+o(1)}$, while the available savings are logarithmic rather than this fixed power. The Möbius–specific adaptation discussed by those authors likewise gives a logarithmic saving, not $K^{-1}$.

This comparison is deliberately limited. The cited theorem averages reduced residue classes, removes a rank–one pretentious component, and is unconditional for almost all moduli. It does not contain the physical row mask, the row synthesis, or the four–Möbius form in $\mathcal O_{\rm term}$. GRH changes the exceptional–modulus statement, not the missing factor $K$. Hence no existing scalar progression theorem is imported here as a proof of [\[ass:tpc39-minimal-twist-energy\]](TPC-039.md#ref-2c9b9355fbac1985).

# Partial, redundant, and random twist banks

The minimal regular polygon is exact but has no redundant measurement. This section records what is lost under partial sampling and what random sampling can provide.

## The nonzero bank restores the alias loss

###### ref-016e6bffeeb1f026

###### ref-a94c6e9f729a8900

###### ref-c84cb3723d85a8d0

Remove the zero column and define $$\mathscr Y_\nu^*  :=\sum_{\substack{-L\le k\le L\\k\ne0}}  \omega^{\nu k}\mathscr Z_k.  \label{eq:tpc39-nonzero-twisted-bank}$$ The untwisted nonzero aggregate required by the two–sum route of TPC–38 is $$\mathscr Y_0^*  =\sum_{k\ne0}\mathscr Z_k.  \label{eq:tpc39-nonzero-untwisted-sum}$$ Since $\nu=0$ is one member of the bank, $$\boxed{  \|\mathscr Y_0^*\|^2  \le (q-1)\left(  \frac1q\sum_{\nu=0}^{q-1}\|\mathscr Y_\nu^*\|^2  \right).}  \label{eq:tpc39-nonzero-bank-qminusone-loss}$$ Indeed, additive orthogonality gives $\sum_{\nu=0}^{q-1}\mathscr Y_\nu^*=0$. Cauchy applied to the other $q-1$ values then yields $q\|\mathscr Y_0^*\|^2\le(q-1)\sum_\nu\|\mathscr Y_\nu^*\|^2$, which is the displayed bound. The factor $q-1\asymp K_B$ is sharp: the nonzero residue coefficients parametrize every zero–mean bank list. Thus applying Parseval only to the nonzero aliases and then returning to the one untwisted aggregate recreates the same endpoint order as the finite alias loss. The gain in [\[thm:tpc39-exact-Hilbert-recovery\]](TPC-039.md#ref-27a4f8f60d81d4dd) comes from averaging the *completed outputs themselves*, with the zero column included and isolated by the quotient projector.

## Redundant cyclic banks

###### ref-054d83cdbf360b31

Every integer $r>L$ gives the exact recovery formula $$Z_0=\frac1r\sum_{\nu=0}^{r-1}Y_\nu^{(r)},  \label{eq:tpc39-redundant-recovery}$$ because the zero residue class remains a singleton. The spectrum in [\[thm:tpc39-tomography-spectrum\]](TPC-039.md#ref-e310b568000ea411) quantifies the redundancy for $L<r\le2L+1$. At $r=2L+1$, all aliases are separated and the bank energy is the raw trace in [\[eq:tpc39-full-DFT-Parseval\]](TPC-039.md#ref-5ac8d4712ff5a33f); this is strictly stronger than necessary for equality recovery.

Using $r>L+1$ may be helpful if an analytic argument has a small number of exceptional twists. Exact recovery from an arbitrary retained subset is still governed by the moment system [\[eq:tpc39-moment-system\]](TPC-039.md#ref-ffa41522e12a6472); sample count alone does not control its conditioning. Clustered nodes may make the recovery weights large, while the complete regular polygon has the optimal amplification proved in [\[prop:tpc39-optimal-conditioning\]](TPC-039.md#ref-0aea9b1cceee1d50).

## Random phase averaging

###### ref-fc37f3e40c3d83af

Let $\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_m$ be independent and uniform on $[0,1)$, and define $$\widehat Z_0  :=\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^{m}  \sum_{k=-L}^{L}\exp(2\pi i k\theta_j)Z_k.  \label{eq:tpc39-random-estimator}$$

###### ref-eddc85d62bf952ff

###### ref-2f8663ca0c45b296

###### ref-ad1908584cbb96f0

> **Proposition: Exact random mean–square error** \[prop:tpc39-random-MSE\] For every finite Hilbert column family, $$\begin{aligned}  \mathbb E\widehat Z_0&=Z_0,  \label{eq:tpc39-random-unbiased}\\  \mathbb E\|\widehat Z_0-Z_0\|^2  &=\frac1m\sum_{\substack{-L\le k\le L\\k\ne0}}  \|Z_k\|^2.  \label{eq:tpc39-random-MSE}\end{aligned}$$

> **Proof** The expectation of $\exp(2\pi i k\theta)$ is $\mathbf 1_{k=0}$, proving [\[eq:tpc39-random-unbiased\]](TPC-039.md#ref-2f8663ca0c45b296). Independence reduces the variance of the sample mean by $m$, and orthogonality in one sample removes all terms with distinct nonzero frequencies, proving [\[eq:tpc39-random-MSE\]](TPC-039.md#ref-ad1908584cbb96f0).

Random sampling is always unbiased. It becomes quantitatively useful only when a nonzero–alias trace is available. Without that input, it gives no coefficient–free deterministic recovery. The structured regular polygon is exceptional: it obtains zero error with only $L+1$ samples by exact algebra rather than probabilistic concentration.

## Partial control is not enough

The deletion no–go in [\[cor:tpc39-deletion-no-go\]](TPC-039.md#ref-f4467957131bb698) has an analytic consequence. Bounds for all but one member of the minimal bank, no matter how strong, do not control $Z_0$ in the abstract column class. A proof of the physical route must therefore do one of the following:

1.  control the complete minimal bank in square average;

2.  add redundancy and solve the resulting stable moment system after deleting the exceptional twists; or

3.  estimate each exceptional output separately.

This finite issue should be settled before any exceptional–frequency argument is used in the Möbius analysis.

# The corrected continuation after quotient tomography

The equality–recovery problem is now algebraically closed. The surviving question is whether the physical coefficient system admits the required twist–stable energy estimate.

## What is unconditional

The paper proves, without a Möbius hypothesis:

1.  the exact quotient identity [\[eq:tpc39-additive-quotient-identity\]](TPC-039.md#ref-56979d3e08be864a);

2.  exact recovery of $\mathscr Z_0$ from $L+1$ recombined twists with unit normalized recovery amplification;

3.  Hilbert–valued folded Parseval and the complete tomography spectrum for every $L<r\le2L+1$;

4.  minimality of the $L+1$ sample count among arbitrary phase nodes and complex weights;

5.  optimal noise amplification and rigidity of positive minimal rules;

6.  the deletion no–go, the random estimator identity, and the exact relation to raw trace and alias Gram gates;

7.  rigidity of exact recovery by pure divisibility indicators;

8.  the finite alias–synthesis conservation law;

9.  terminal row–alias injectivity and, under the inherited deterministic multiplier ledger, closure of its complete atomic diagonal at the target scale.

These results repair the logical gap in TPC–38: there is now an explicit linear functional that converts a structured family of congruence completions into exact equality.

## Route T: the minimal recombined bank

###### ref-c1f9d1281537f3ac

The canonical minimal–bank continuation isolated here is precisely [\[ass:tpc39-minimal-twist-energy\]](TPC-039.md#ref-2c9b9355fbac1985): $$\frac1q\sum_{\nu\bmod q}\|\mathscr Y_\nu\|^2  \ll X^{o(1)}B_0K_B.  \label{eq:tpc39-next-route-T}$$ This tests only the $q=L+1$ Fourier directions needed for equality. It allows arbitrary energy in the antisymmetric folded kernel [\[eq:tpc39-minimal-kernel\]](TPC-039.md#ref-7b01e58d4615c5ea). It is therefore the smallest exact tomographic gate constructed in this paper.

The terminal analysis shows what must be proved: the atomic diagonal is already acceptable, while fixed–alias cross–row terms, the equality column, and $qM$-gap folded correlations remain. A useful proof would have to exploit the actual Möbius/source coefficients, not only additive orthogonality in the alias index.

## Route F: twist–stable full/proper fields

The stronger face formulation [\[eq:tpc39-twist-stable-C\]](TPC-039.md#ref-b4e9d1083293619d)–[\[eq:tpc39-twist-stable-P\]](TPC-039.md#ref-502c15a436e54f66) is closer to the finite CRT architecture. The twist leaves every coefficient mass unchanged, so no new period factor appears on the input side. What is unknown is whether the coherent physical synthesis respects those scalar ledgers uniformly or in square average over $\nu$.

A theorem on all admissible coefficient fields would imply Route F, but would be unnecessarily strong. The present target is only a single regular polygon orbit of the two prescribed fields.

## Route S: direct superwindow completion

The divisibility rigidity theorem says that exact recovery by completion indicators requires a modulus larger than $B$. One may therefore enlarge the multiplicative CRT modulus itself until there are no aliases. Within the fixed–factor–count, large–prime regime of the conservation law [\[eq:tpc39-alias-synthesis-conservation\]](TPC-039.md#ref-600a9947198636b5), the dominant full synthesis scale then grows to the determinant scale. This is an alternative physical architecture, not a consequence of the additive route. Optimizing the factorization of such a superwindow modulus and its zero–face range is left to a subsequent layer.

## Viable outcomes for the next paper

Positive twist–stable theorem.

:   Prove [\[eq:tpc39-next-route-T\]](TPC-039.md#ref-c1f9d1281537f3ac), or the stronger face estimates, for the actual packet. This would close the equality–recovery stage at the inherited energy scale; it would still feed into the remaining scalar prime–pair reductions rather than by itself prove a prime–pair asymptotic.

Partial arithmetic theorem.

:   Prove the required estimate for a terminal sublayer, a restricted family of rows, or an averaged choice of the auxiliary triple. The exact injectivity and diagonal ledger make any genuine off–diagonal saving separately visible and publishable.

Scoped twist obstruction.

:   Show that a declared class of additive dispersion, large–sieve, or positivity–preserving estimates cannot reach [\[eq:tpc39-next-route-T\]](TPC-039.md#ref-c1f9d1281537f3ac). Such a theorem would rule out that method class, not prove that the physical Möbius form is large.

## Parity boundary

The surviving terminal form contains two– and four–Möbius correlations. This is compatible with the classical parity obstruction for sieve methods [`\citep{HeathBrown1982Parity,IwaniecKowalski2004}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-039.md#cite-bd33f7374ef1e88f); [\[IwaniecKowalski2004\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-039.md#cite-49dfea87eaada156). Quotient tomography neither proves nor disproves a parity–breaking estimate. It proves that equality recovery itself is no longer an undefined step: the remaining statement is the explicit twist–stable physical energy theorem in [\[eq:tpc39-next-route-T\]](TPC-039.md#ref-c1f9d1281537f3ac).
