## Abstract

TPC–42 reduced the surviving triple–prime endpoint to a coherent orbit–slope energy containing one source and one target Möbius value per row. We deform all of those values by one common prime–sign field, rather than attaching independent row phases. For the zero alias, the source divisor and target affine value fuse into a squarefree integer label. The inherited scale separation $D=o(J)$ makes these labels injective across physical rows, and prime–sign orthogonality then makes the complete left–plus–right Hilbert energy equal its atomic diagonal in expectation, with no projective separation. For the full alias trace, the corresponding squarefree–kernel fibers have only $X^{o(1)}$ multiplicity; hence the annealed trace is still at the endpoint diagonal scale. Boolean hypercontractivity gives fixed high moments, and, for each fixed prescribed dyadic packet sequence, almost every global random multiplicative field closes every sufficiently large packet. At the distinguished all–minus prime corner the random model is exactly the Möbius function. We give an exact Walsh expansion there: every four–Möbius cross row collapses to one Möbius value of a squarefree symmetric–difference kernel. A sharp Walsh example shows that annealed diagonalization alone cannot control this specified corner. Thus the declared randomized multiplicative terminal model is closed, while the remaining deterministic problem is an explicit corner de–randomization theorem, not a claim of four–Möbius cancellation or a breach of sieve parity.

# Introduction

###### ref-1e252f27b74b25d8

The terminal TPC packet has reached a coefficient–specific arithmetic gate. TPC–41 closed the complete same–row alias sector and isolated a cross–row form whose literal summands contain $$\mu(d_\alpha)\mu(d_\beta)  \mu(m_\alpha j+h+kM)\mu(m_\beta j+h+k'M).  \label{eq:tpc43-intro-four-mobius}$$ TPC–42 then proved that integers within one residue fiber are already orthogonal in the physical Hilbert coordinates. The remaining operation is coherent synthesis across distinct residue fibers, and projective mask separation reduces each layer to a weighted orbit–slope energy. Neither a scalar progression theorem nor a small localization boundary controls that synthesis [`\citep{WangTPC41,WangTPC42}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-043.md#cite-a0275ac77ab3e348); [\[WangTPC42\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-043.md#cite-cc53a3263a6b378e).

The present paper asks a narrower question before attempting the deterministic Möbius estimate:

> Does the endpoint fail for generic coefficient fields that preserve the same squarefree support and the same prime–level multiplicative coupling as the two physical Möbius occurrences?

###### ref-0d0d4dcf45e8815c

The answer is no, in a strong exact sense. Choose one independent sign $\varepsilon_p\in\{-1,1\}$ at every relevant prime and put $$F_\varepsilon(n)=\mu^2(n)\prod_{p\mid n}\varepsilon_p.  \label{eq:tpc43-intro-rmf}$$ We replace both $\mu(d_\alpha)$ and $\mu(m_\alpha j+h+kM)$ by this same function. This is not independent row noise. All occurrences sharing a prime share its sign, and the actual Möbius function is exactly the point $\varepsilon_p=-1$ for every prime.

## Main results

###### ref-8845f7b80aea7adf

###### ref-3b519ff1842a89d3

The first result is a product–fiber theorem. For a nonzero atom, let $$n_{\alpha,j,k}=\ell_\alpha d_\alpha j+h+kM,  \qquad  g_{\alpha,j,k}=(d_\alpha,n_{\alpha,j,k}),  \label{eq:tpc43-intro-ng}$$ and attach the squarefree Walsh label $$s_{\alpha,j,k}  =\frac{d_\alpha n_{\alpha,j,k}}{g_{\alpha,j,k}^2}.  \label{eq:tpc43-intro-label}$$ At fixed $(j,k,s)$, choosing $g\mid h+kM$ and $d\mid sg^2$ determines both $n=sg^2/d$ and $$\ell=\frac{n-h-kM}{dj}.  \label{eq:tpc43-intro-row-recovery}$$ Thus the fiber has divisor–type multiplicity $X^{o(1)}$. Walsh orthogonality, followed by Cauchy–Schwarz only inside these fibers, proves $$\mathbb E_\varepsilon  \sum_k\left(  \|\mathscr T_{k,\varepsilon}^L\|^2  +\|\mathscr T_{k,\varepsilon}^R\|^2  \right)  \ll X^{o(1)}\mathcal D_{\rm tr}  \ll X^{o(1)}Q^2JK_B.  \label{eq:tpc43-intro-trace-theorem}$$ This is the full physical trace endpoint in the annealed multiplicative model. It retains all deterministic masks and bypasses projective separation altogether.

The equality alias is exactly diagonal. There $(d_\alpha,n_{\alpha,j,0})=1$, so the label is the product $$u_{\alpha,j}  =d_\alpha(\ell_\alpha d_\alpha j+h).  \label{eq:tpc43-intro-fused-label}$$ If two rows share that product, then $$j(\ell_\alpha d_\alpha^2-\ell_\beta d_\beta^2)  =h(d_\beta-d_\alpha).  \label{eq:tpc43-intro-rigidity-equation}$$ The left side is either zero or has magnitude $\gg J$, whereas the right side is $O_h(D_0)=o(J)$. Hence the rows coincide. It follows that $$\boxed{  \mathbb E_\varepsilon\left(  \|\mathscr T_{0,\varepsilon}^L\|^2  +\|\mathscr T_{0,\varepsilon}^R\|^2  \right)=\mathcal D_0.}  \label{eq:tpc43-intro-exact-zero}$$ Since $K_B=X^{1/400+o(1)}$, all but a proportion $X^{-1/400+o(1)}$ of prime–sign environments meet the sufficient equality endpoint. Fixed Bonami–Beckner moments strengthen this to $$\mathbb P_\varepsilon(Z_0>K_B\mathcal D_0)  \le X^{-q/400+o_q(1)}  \label{eq:tpc43-intro-high-tail}$$ for every fixed $q$. Coupling the signs across scales and applying Borel–Cantelli gives almost–sure eventual closure along any one fixed prescribed dyadic packet sequence.

The third result identifies exactly what annealing removes. For two zero–alias labels, put $$\kappa_{\alpha,\beta,j}  =\frac{u_{\alpha,j}u_{\beta,j}}  {(u_{\alpha,j},u_{\beta,j})^2}.  \label{eq:tpc43-intro-kernel}$$ After grouping all deterministic masks and row representations into a coefficient $C(\kappa)$, the physical energy is $$\boxed{  \|\mathscr T_0^L\|^2+\|\mathscr T_0^R\|^2  =\mathcal D_0+\sum_{\kappa>1}\mu(\kappa)C(\kappa).}  \label{eq:tpc43-intro-corner-spectrum}$$ This uses the literal identity $$\mu(d_\alpha)\mu(n_\alpha)  \mu(d_\beta)\mu(n_\beta)  =\mu(\kappa_{\alpha,\beta,j}).  \label{eq:tpc43-intro-four-to-one}$$ Thus the four signs have been compressed to one Möbius value on a squarefree symmetric–difference spectrum. Randomizing only primes up to $z$ deletes exactly those terms whose kernel has a prime factor at most $z$, leaving an exact rough–kernel filtration.

Finally, the annealed–to–corner distinction is sharp. On the divisor Walsh system of a squarefree product with $R$ divisors, one may have mean energy $R$ and all–minus energy $R^2$, with the polynomial vanishing at every other corner. Hence a small exceptional set can be concentrated entirely at the physical phase. No measure selection can replace a deterministic theorem for [\[eq:tpc43-intro-corner-spectrum\]](TPC-043.md#ref-d78339029fd8ac46).

## What has and has not moved

The results close the generic multiplicative geometry, not the physical Möbius point. In particular, they prove that the following features do not by themselves force the $J^2$ coherent loss:

1.  squarefree support;

2.  one globally coupled multiplicative sign field at source and target;

3.  the actual row masks and opposite–row Hilbert coordinates;

4.  the complete alias trace; and

5.  the endpoint aspect ratios of the triple–prime packet.

What remains is a quenched theorem excluding the all–minus prime point from a small, potentially structured exceptional set. We do not call [\[eq:tpc43-intro-trace-theorem\]](TPC-043.md#ref-64baf7fc5a038631) a Möbius correlation estimate: for generic prime signs, $F_\varepsilon*\mathbf 1=\delta_1$ is false, and the actual point cannot be selected probabilistically.

# One multiplicative prime–sign field

###### ref-d4a65849d1e0b1fd

###### ref-7ba68b0eee29ad5a

Let $(\varepsilon_p)_{p\in\mathcal P}$ be independent signs, uniformly distributed on $\{-1,1\}$, for every prime occurring in the finite packet. Extend the prime signs to the completely multiplicative phase $\varepsilon(n)=\prod_p\varepsilon_p^{v_p(n)}$, and define the squarefree–supported random multiplicative function $$F_\varepsilon(n)  :=\mu^2(n)\prod_{p\mid n}\varepsilon_p.  \label{eq:tpc43-rademacher-multiplicative}$$ Thus $F_\varepsilon$ is multiplicative on coprime inputs and has the same zero set and absolute values as $\mu$. It is not called completely multiplicative because its value is zero on nonsquarefree integers. At the distinguished prime point $$\varepsilon_p=-1\quad(p\in\mathcal P),  \label{eq:tpc43-all-minus-point}$$ one has $$F_\varepsilon(n)=\mu(n)  \label{eq:tpc43-all-minus-is-mobius}$$ for every relevant integer.

###### ref-cacaf6b1734e50cb

The deformation is applied consistently to the two Möbius occurrences in each physical row. Define $$_{\gamma,j}  :=\sum_\alpha  a^L_{\alpha,\gamma,j,k}  F_\varepsilon(d_\alpha)  F_\varepsilon(n_{\alpha,j,k}),  \label{eq:tpc43-random-left-column}$$ and define $\mathscr T_{k,\varepsilon}^R$ symmetrically. In particular, $$\mathscr T_{k,-\mathbf 1}^{L/R}=\mathscr T_k^{L/R}.  \label{eq:tpc43-physical-corner}$$ This is not an independent row randomization: the same prime sign appears at every source and target integer divisible by that prime.

> **Lemma: Divisor control of one kernel fiber** \[lem:tpc43-kernel-fiber-bound\] For fixed $(j,k,s)$, the number $r_{j,k}(s)$ of physical rows satisfying $s_{\alpha,j,k}=s$ obeys $$r_{j,k}(s)  \le  \sum_{g\mid |c_k|}\tau(sg^2).  \label{eq:tpc43-kernel-fiber-divisor-bound}$$ Consequently there is a constant $C_1$, depending only on the fixed support exponents, such that $$\rho_X:=\max_{j,k,s}r_{j,k}(s)  \le  \exp\!\left(C_1\frac{\log X}{\log\log X}\right)  =X^{o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc43-rho-bound}$$

###### ref-d9015bcaf452e638

> **Proof** If a row belongs to the $s$-fiber and $g=g_{\alpha,j,k}$, then $$g\mid c_k,  \qquad  d_\alpha n_{\alpha,j,k}=sg^2.  \label{eq:tpc43-fiber-product}$$ Fix such a divisor $g$ and put $R=sg^2$. A choice $d_\alpha\mid R$ determines $$n_{\alpha,j,k}=R/d_\alpha,  \qquad  \ell_\alpha  =\frac{R/d_\alpha-c_k}{d_\alpha j}.  \label{eq:tpc43-row-recovery}$$ There is at most one row for that divisor; integrality, primality, dyadic support, and the physical masks can only delete choices. Hence there are at most $\tau(sg^2)$ rows for fixed $g$, proving [\[eq:tpc43-kernel-fiber-divisor-bound\]](TPC-043.md#ref-83528842ee7b6aaa).

Absorbing the two constants proves [\[eq:tpc43-rho-bound\]](TPC-043.md#ref-daa0abfc71cf1a3d).

> **Theorem: Full trace at the annealed diagonal scale** \[thm:tpc43-annealed-trace\] For the complete physical alias family, $$\begin{aligned}  &\mathbb E_\varepsilon  \sum_{k\in\mathcal I_B}  \bigl(   \|\mathscr T_{k,\varepsilon}^L\|^2   +\|\mathscr T_{k,\varepsilon}^R\|^2  \bigr)  \notag\\  &\qquad\le \rho_X\mathcal D_{\rm tr}  \ll X^{o(1)}Q^2JK_B.  \label{eq:tpc43-annealed-trace-bound}\end{aligned}$$ The estimate retains the actual masks, orbit coordinates, source weights, terminal cutoffs, and all aliases. It does not use the projective layer decomposition.

###### ref-517fef5298f028fc

###### ref-9d6c40c878f1e6cb

> **Proof** Walsh orthogonality gives $$\mathbb E_\varepsilon  |[\mathscr T_{k,\varepsilon}^L]_{\gamma,j}|^2  =\sum_s|A^L_{\gamma,j,k}(s)|^2.  \label{eq:tpc43-one-coordinate-expectation}$$ For one fiber, Cauchy–Schwarz and [\[lem:tpc43-kernel-fiber-bound\]](TPC-043.md#ref-ebd16355461c5036) give $$\begin{aligned}  |A^L_{\gamma,j,k}(s)|^2  &\le r_{j,k}(s)  \sum_{\alpha:s_{\alpha,j,k}=s}  |a^L_{\alpha,\gamma,j,k}|^2  \mu^2(n_{\alpha,j,k})  \notag\\  &\le\rho_X  \sum_{\alpha:s_{\alpha,j,k}=s}  |a^L_{\alpha,\gamma,j,k}|^2  \mu^2(n_{\alpha,j,k}).  \label{eq:tpc43-fiber-Cauchy}\end{aligned}$$ Sum over $s,\gamma,j,k$ to obtain the left contribution $\rho_X\sum_k\mathcal D_{k,L}$. The same argument gives the right contribution. Now use [\[eq:tpc43-rho-bound\]](TPC-043.md#ref-daa0abfc71cf1a3d) and the inherited diagonal bound [\[eq:tpc43-inherited-trace-diagonal\]](TPC-043.md#ref-82f0f0e3b637bc5f).

###### ref-efabc3b4a6051b0b

Within the annealed multiplicative model, the theorem removes the full factor $$\frac{Q}{K_B}=X^{o(1)}J^2  \label{eq:tpc43-annealed-closes-J2}$$ that separates the deterministic overlap estimate from the sufficient equality endpoint in TPC–42. The statement is annealed: it concerns a single globally multiplicative prime–sign field averaged over its prime coordinates, not the distinguished all–minus point.

###### ref-cd8d1c28098683d9

The same geometry acts directly on the minimal folded bank. Let $\mathcal K_0=\{0\}$, and for $1\le a\le L_B$ let $\mathcal K_a=\{a,a-(L_B+1)\}$. Define $$_{\gamma,j}  :=\sum_\alpha\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_a}  a^L_{\alpha,\gamma,j,k}  F_\varepsilon(d_\alpha)F_\varepsilon(n_{\alpha,j,k}),  \label{eq:tpc43-random-folded-column}$$ and define the right side symmetrically.

###### ref-2f2ed6a7abbd884d

###### ref-c7722f7dd42861a8

> **Corollary: Annealed closure of the actual minimal bank** \[cor:tpc43-annealed-folded\] One has $$\mathbb E_\varepsilon\sum_{a=0}^{L_B}  \bigl(   \|\mathscr U_{a,\varepsilon}^L\|^2   +\|\mathscr U_{a,\varepsilon}^R\|^2  \bigr)  \le2\rho_X\mathcal D_{\rm tr}  \ll X^{o(1)}Q^2JK_B.  \label{eq:tpc43-annealed-folded-bound}$$

> **Proof** At fixed $(\gamma,j,a)$, expand the inner sum into atoms $(\alpha,k)$ with $k\in\mathcal K_a$. For each Walsh label there are at most two alias choices, and for each alias there are at most $\rho_X$ rows. Walsh orthogonality and fiberwise Cauchy–Schwarz therefore cost at most $2\rho_X$. Summing the raw atomic coefficients over the disjoint buckets gives exactly $\mathcal D_{\rm tr}$.

###### ref-dd19d3bb801650e0

> **Corollary: One–scale typical trace closure** \[cor:tpc43-one-scale-trace\] Let $L_X\to\infty$ be any function. Then $$\begin{aligned}  &\mathbb P_\varepsilon\left(  \sum_k  \bigl(  \|\mathscr T_{k,\varepsilon}^L\|^2  +\|\mathscr T_{k,\varepsilon}^R\|^2  \bigr)  >L_X\rho_X\mathcal D_{\rm tr}  \right)  \le L_X^{-1}.  \label{eq:tpc43-trace-Markov}\end{aligned}$$ In particular, choosing any slowly growing $L_X\to\infty$ with $L_X=X^{o(1)}$ yields the trace endpoint for all but an $o(1)$ proportion of prime–sign environments.

> **Proof** If $\mathcal D_{\rm tr}=0$, the annealed bound forces the trace energy to vanish almost surely. Otherwise apply Markov’s inequality to that nonnegative energy and use [\[thm:tpc43-annealed-trace\]](TPC-043.md#ref-92df03be3ad03dc6).

> **Remark: A finite exact ensemble** \[rem:tpc43-finite-ensemble\] Only primes dividing the finitely many source and terminal integers enter the packet. Thus every expectation above is a normalized sum over a finite cube. No limiting probability space or independence between different values of $F_\varepsilon(n)$ is being assumed.

# Exact fused–label rigidity at the equality alias

###### ref-af24876e0298796b

###### ref-03c8888ac577c577

At $k=0$, the intercept is the fixed integer $h$. Hence $$(d_\alpha,n_{\alpha,j,0})  =(d_\alpha,h)=1,  \label{eq:tpc43-zero-coprimality}$$ and every nonzero random atom is indexed by the squarefree product $$u_{\alpha,j}  :=d_\alpha n_{\alpha,j,0}  =d_\alpha(\ell_\alpha d_\alpha j+h).  \label{eq:tpc43-fused-zero-label}$$

###### ref-2a6ec1df9065a077

###### ref-1bccec8696d6b2f0

> **Lemma: Fused–label injectivity** \[lem:tpc43-fused-label-injectivity\] For all sufficiently large $X$, fixed $j\in\mathcal J$, and two physical rows $\alpha,\beta$, $$u_{\alpha,j}=u_{\beta,j}  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad  \alpha=\beta.  \label{eq:tpc43-fused-label-injective}$$

###### ref-029bd4b274704c27

###### ref-3329d414855d5307

> **Proof** Equality of the two labels gives $$j(\ell_\alpha d_\alpha^2-\ell_\beta d_\beta^2)  =h(d_\beta-d_\alpha).  \label{eq:tpc43-fused-collision-equation}$$ If the integer in parentheses on the left is nonzero, the left side has absolute value at least $j$, whereas $$|h(d_\beta-d_\alpha)|  \le |h|\max_\rho d_\rho<j  \label{eq:tpc43-fused-size-contradiction}$$ by [\[eq:tpc43-rigidity-size\]](TPC-043.md#ref-340b251511abfb5f). This is impossible. Thus the parenthesized integer vanishes, and [\[eq:tpc43-fused-collision-equation\]](TPC-043.md#ref-029bd4b274704c27), together with $h\ne0$, forces $d_\alpha=d_\beta$. It then forces $\ell_\alpha=\ell_\beta$, so the rows coincide.

The use of $j\asymp J$ is essential. Merely knowing $|\mathcal J|=O(J)$ would not prove the strict inequality in [\[eq:tpc43-fused-size-contradiction\]](TPC-043.md#ref-3329d414855d5307).

###### ref-4701d673b6bd4cb4

###### ref-0c101d581ba1c5ba

###### ref-0e0d99e4edb1c76c

###### ref-daf3229f50115c33

> **Theorem: Exact equality–column diagonalization** \[thm:tpc43-exact-zero-diagonalization\] Put $$Z_0(\varepsilon)  :=\|\mathscr T_{0,\varepsilon}^L\|^2    +\|\mathscr T_{0,\varepsilon}^R\|^2.  \label{eq:tpc43-zero-random-energy}$$ Then $$\boxed{\mathbb E_\varepsilon Z_0(\varepsilon)=\mathcal D_0.}  \label{eq:tpc43-exact-zero-expectation}$$ Consequently, for every $\lambda>0$, $$\mathbb P_\varepsilon\bigl(Z_0(\varepsilon)>\lambda\mathcal D_0\bigr)  \le\lambda^{-1}.  \label{eq:tpc43-zero-Markov}$$ At the inherited endpoint, $$\mathbb P_\varepsilon\bigl(  Z_0(\varepsilon)>K_B\mathcal D_0  \bigr)  \le K_B^{-1}=X^{-1/400+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc43-zero-endpoint-probability}$$ Thus all but at most a proportion $X^{-1/400+o(1)}$ of the multiplicative prime–sign environments satisfy the random–model equality benchmark at the same numerical scale as [\[eq:tpc43-physical-zero-target\]](TPC-043.md#ref-c1618198e6275ab1). This statement does not select the physical all–minus environment.

###### ref-87794d977866fa69

> **Proof** For fixed $(\gamma,j)$, the nonzero row atoms have distinct Walsh labels $u_{\alpha,j}$ by [\[lem:tpc43-fused-label-injectivity\]](TPC-043.md#ref-2a6ec1df9065a077). Equation [\[eq:tpc43-Walsh-orthogonality\]](TPC-043.md#ref-3ca61b2d0a57d40a) therefore deletes every unequal row pair and leaves $$\mathbb E_\varepsilon  |[\mathscr T_{0,\varepsilon}^L]_{\gamma,j}|^2  =\sum_\alpha  |a^L_{\alpha,\gamma,j,0}|^2  \mu^2(n_{\alpha,j,0}).  \label{eq:tpc43-zero-coordinate-diagonal}$$ Sum over the left coordinates and repeat on the right to obtain [\[eq:tpc43-exact-zero-expectation\]](TPC-043.md#ref-0c101d581ba1c5ba). The probability statements are immediate if $\mathcal D_0=0$, since then the nonnegative variable $Z_0$ vanishes almost surely. Otherwise they are Markov’s inequality, followed by [\[eq:tpc43-alias-ledger\]](TPC-043.md#ref-9ce151c9cac3c0c1) and the inherited diagonal bound.

# Hypercontractive moments and almost–sure dyadic closure

The first moment is enough for the endpoint statements above. Fixed higher moments give stronger tail bounds for a random prime–sign environment, while still leaving open exceptional behavior at the specified all–minus corner. We use the standard Bonami–Beckner inequality on the discrete cube [`\citep{Bonami1970,Beckner1975}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-043.md#cite-bdd2e8cd9d92b09f); [\[Beckner1975\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-043.md#cite-0a65b61346d27dbb); the weighted random multiplicative form needed below is also recorded explicitly by [`\citet[Probability Result~2.3]{Harper2019HighMoments}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-043.md#cite-34ab01111188e823).

###### ref-e561200215308f5f

###### ref-b260db18692993d8

###### ref-62d3ef8cd61662fb

> **Lemma: One coordinate** \[lem:tpc43-one-coordinate-moment\] Let $$S(\varepsilon)=\sum_s A_s\chi_s(\varepsilon)  \label{eq:tpc43-one-coordinate-polynomial}$$ be any coordinate polynomial produced by the physical packet. For each fixed integer $q\ge1$, $$\|S\|_{L^{2q}}^2  \le  \sum_s(2q-1)^{\omega(s)}|A_s|^2  \le X^{o_q(1)}\sum_s|A_s|^2.  \label{eq:tpc43-hypercontractive-coordinate}$$

###### ref-cdf2e41430af388b

> **Proof** The first inequality is Bonami–Beckner hypercontractivity, decomposed by Walsh degree. Every label in the packet is at most $X^{O(1)}$, so the standard maximal-order bound gives $$\max_s\omega(s)\ll\frac{\log X}{\log\log X}.  \label{eq:tpc43-max-omega}$$ For fixed $q$, the factor $(2q-1)^{\omega(s)}$ is therefore $X^{o_q(1)}$.

###### ref-4df7b1ad1a8cbbbd

###### ref-62f51be18431e282

###### ref-eb2fba53483a5ed9

###### ref-90e21d436524cb8d

###### ref-98b705066fa7e6a0

> **Theorem: Fixed moments of the physical energies** \[thm:tpc43-fixed-moments\] For every fixed integer $q\ge1$, $$\begin{aligned}  \mathbb E_\varepsilon Z_0(\varepsilon)^q  &\le X^{o_q(1)}\mathcal D_0^q,  \label{eq:tpc43-zero-q-moment}\\  \mathbb E_\varepsilon Z_{\rm tr}(\varepsilon)^q  &\le X^{o_q(1)}\mathcal D_{\rm tr}^q,  \label{eq:tpc43-trace-q-moment}\end{aligned}$$ where $$Z_{\rm tr}(\varepsilon)  :=\sum_k  \bigl(   \|\mathscr T_{k,\varepsilon}^L\|^2   +\|\mathscr T_{k,\varepsilon}^R\|^2  \bigr).  \label{eq:tpc43-trace-random-energy}$$ In particular, $$\mathbb P_\varepsilon\bigl(  Z_0(\varepsilon)>K_B\mathcal D_0  \bigr)  \le X^{-q/400+o_q(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc43-zero-high-moment-tail}$$

###### ref-bf10fdcd2cf06db8

###### ref-05fa8f55bb1a87e9

###### ref-6dd94fff21c4a523

> **Proof** For each output coordinate $i$, write $S_i=\sum_sA_{i,s}\chi_s$ and let $D_i$ be its atomic diagonal. The fiber Cauchy estimate [\[eq:tpc43-fiber-Cauchy\]](TPC-043.md#ref-9d6c40c878f1e6cb) gives $$\sum_s|A_{i,s}|^2\le\rho_XD_i.  \label{eq:tpc43-coordinate-coefficient-l2}$$ For $k=0$, one may replace $\rho_X$ by $1$. By [\[lem:tpc43-one-coordinate-moment\]](TPC-043.md#ref-e561200215308f5f), $$\bigl(\mathbb E|S_i|^{2q}\bigr)^{1/q}  =\|S_i\|_{2q}^2  \le X^{o_q(1)}D_i.  \label{eq:tpc43-coordinate-Lq-square}$$ Minkowski’s inequality in $L^q$ now gives $$\begin{aligned}  \bigl(\mathbb EZ^q\bigr)^{1/q}  &=\left\|\sum_i|S_i|^2\right\|_{L^q}  \le\sum_i\||S_i|^2\|_{L^q}  \le X^{o_q(1)}\sum_iD_i.  \label{eq:tpc43-Minkowski-moment}\end{aligned}$$ This proves both moment estimates. Apply Markov with threshold $K_B^q\mathcal D_0^q$ when $\mathcal D_0>0$; if $\mathcal D_0=0$, the first moment already gives $Z_0=0$ almost surely. Finally use $K_B=X^{1/400+o(1)}$ to obtain [\[eq:tpc43-zero-high-moment-tail\]](TPC-043.md#ref-98b705066fa7e6a0).

The field can be coupled across scales by choosing one sign for every prime once and for all.

###### ref-bd9c7c6dad6fc018

###### ref-80c7cf807f513d0f

###### ref-d1f72ba09a35f0ad

> **Corollary: Almost–sure closure on prescribed dyadic packets** \[cor:tpc43-almost-sure-dyadic\] Let $X_r=2^r$, and for each $r$ fix one deterministic inherited TPC packet. For almost every global prime–sign field, all sufficiently large $r$ satisfy $$\begin{aligned}  Z_0(X_r,\varepsilon)&\le K_B(X_r)\mathcal D_0(X_r),  \label{eq:tpc43-as-zero}\\  Z_{\rm tr}(X_r,\varepsilon)&\le X_r^{o(1)}\mathcal D_{\rm tr}(X_r).  \label{eq:tpc43-as-trace}\end{aligned}$$ Consequently both random–model endpoint estimates hold eventually on that dyadic packet sequence.

###### ref-d7236e19f68fa5e2

###### ref-ff5f124fc67c12e8

> **Proof** The failure probabilities in [\[eq:tpc43-zero-endpoint-probability\]](TPC-043.md#ref-daf3229f50115c33) are summable at $X_r=2^r$. For the trace, use [\[eq:tpc43-rho-bound\]](TPC-043.md#ref-daa0abfc71cf1a3d) and set $$L_X=\exp\!\left(\frac{\log X}{\sqrt{\log\log X}}\right)=X^{o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc43-summable-envelope}$$ For sufficiently large $X$, Markov and [\[thm:tpc43-annealed-trace\]](TPC-043.md#ref-92df03be3ad03dc6) give a failure probability at most $$\mathbb P_\varepsilon\bigl(Z_{\rm tr}>L_X\mathcal D_{\rm tr}\bigr)  \le  \frac{\rho_X}{L_X}  \le\exp\!\left(-c\frac{\log X}{\sqrt{\log\log X}}\right),  \label{eq:tpc43-summable-trace-tail}$$ which is summable on the dyadic sequence. The first Borel–Cantelli lemma proves both assertions; no independence between scales is needed.

The packet sequence in [\[cor:tpc43-almost-sure-dyadic\]](TPC-043.md#ref-bd9c7c6dad6fc018) is prescribed. Uniformity over an additional family of packets requires an explicit entropy or cardinality ledger and is not asserted.

# The deterministic Möbius corner as one Walsh spectrum

###### ref-4ae360049fb1974f

###### ref-db76f66b4c404639

The equality alias admits a sharper deterministic description than the annealed theorem. For nonzero atoms, let $u_{\alpha,j}$ be the squarefree fused label in [\[eq:tpc43-fused-zero-label\]](TPC-043.md#ref-03c8888ac577c577). For two rows at the same orbit time, define their squarefree symmetric–difference kernel $$\kappa_{\alpha,\beta,j}  :=\frac{u_{\alpha,j}u_{\beta,j}}  {(u_{\alpha,j},u_{\beta,j})^2}.  \label{eq:tpc43-pair-kernel}$$ It is squarefree and $$\chi_{u_{\alpha,j}}(\varepsilon)  \chi_{u_{\beta,j}}(\varepsilon)  =\chi_{\kappa_{\alpha,\beta,j}}(\varepsilon).  \label{eq:tpc43-pair-character}$$

###### ref-f1da8a0d52cadd6d

For $\kappa>1$, let $C(\kappa)$ be the sum of all ordered unequal row coefficients, on both physical sides, whose pair kernel is $\kappa$: $$\begin{aligned}  C(\kappa)  :={}&\sum_{\gamma,j}  \sum_{\substack{\alpha\ne\beta\\  \kappa_{\alpha,\beta,j}=\kappa}}  a^L_{\alpha,\gamma,j,0}  \overline{a^L_{\beta,\gamma,j,0}}  \mu^2(n_{\alpha,j,0})\mu^2(n_{\beta,j,0})  \notag\\  &+\text{the symmetric right contribution}.  \label{eq:tpc43-Walsh-spectrum-coefficient}\end{aligned}$$ Pairing $(\alpha,\beta)$ with $(\beta,\alpha)$ shows that $C(\kappa)\in\mathbb R$.

###### ref-ffe678e673399b37

###### ref-08d8759f4d249934

###### ref-0402233f3bbd7f8a

###### ref-3fc6e4a4cd8a1d2c

> **Theorem: Exact four–to–one Möbius compression** \[thm:tpc43-four-to-one-compression\] The complete equality energy has the Walsh expansion $$\boxed{  Z_0(\varepsilon)  =\mathcal D_0+   \sum_{\kappa>1}C(\kappa)\chi_\kappa(\varepsilon).}  \label{eq:tpc43-exact-Walsh-expansion}$$ At the physical all–minus point, $$\boxed{  \|\mathscr T_0^L\|^2+\|\mathscr T_0^R\|^2  =\mathcal D_0+   \sum_{\kappa>1}\mu(\kappa)C(\kappa).}  \label{eq:tpc43-physical-kernel-spectrum}$$ Moreover, $$\mathbb E_\varepsilon  |Z_0(\varepsilon)-\mathcal D_0|^2  =\sum_{\kappa>1}|C(\kappa)|^2.  \label{eq:tpc43-Walsh-Parseval}$$

###### ref-fef1bd86f323ba36

###### ref-6212498a6ac49288

> **Proof** Expand the two squared Hilbert norms defining $Z_0$. By [\[eq:tpc43-pair-character\]](TPC-043.md#ref-db76f66b4c404639), every ordered row pair contributes the Walsh character indexed by its symmetric–difference kernel. The constant character occurs exactly when $u_{\alpha,j}=u_{\beta,j}$, which by [\[lem:tpc43-fused-label-injectivity\]](TPC-043.md#ref-2a6ec1df9065a077) means $\alpha=\beta$. Its coefficient is therefore $\mathcal D_0$, proving [\[eq:tpc43-exact-Walsh-expansion\]](TPC-043.md#ref-08d8759f4d249934).
>
> At $\varepsilon_p=-1$, $$\chi_\kappa(-\mathbf 1)=(-1)^{\omega(\kappa)}=\mu(\kappa).  \label{eq:tpc43-character-at-minus}$$ For a contributing pair, this also gives the literal identity $$\begin{aligned}  &\mu(d_\alpha)\mu(n_{\alpha,j,0})   \mu(d_\beta)\mu(n_{\beta,j,0})  \notag\\  &\qquad  =\mu(u_{\alpha,j})\mu(u_{\beta,j})  =\mu(\kappa_{\alpha,\beta,j}).  \label{eq:tpc43-literal-four-to-one}\end{aligned}$$ Thus [\[eq:tpc43-physical-kernel-spectrum\]](TPC-043.md#ref-0402233f3bbd7f8a) is precisely the physical four–Möbius expansion, not a surrogate. Finally, Walsh orthogonality and the absence of a nonzero constant term give [\[eq:tpc43-Walsh-Parseval\]](TPC-043.md#ref-3fc6e4a4cd8a1d2c).

The theorem replaces a row–pair family of four Möbius signs by one signed spectral sum over squarefree kernels. The coefficient $C(\kappa)$ still contains all masks and all representations of the same kernel; no cancellation estimate for that sum is asserted.

There is an exact small–prime filtration. Fix $z\ge2$, average the signs $\varepsilon_p$ for $p\le z$, and set $\varepsilon_p=-1$ for $p>z$. Let $P^-(n)$ denote the least prime factor of $n>1$.

###### ref-b84a14a8708e84d2

###### ref-99bc56fd29ac658a

> **Corollary: Rough–kernel filtration** \[cor:tpc43-rough-kernel-filtration\] One has $$\boxed{  \mathbb E_{\varepsilon_p:\,p\le z}  Z_0(\varepsilon_{\le z},-\mathbf 1_{>z})  =\mathcal D_0+   \sum_{\substack{\kappa>1\\P^-(\kappa)>z}}   \mu(\kappa)C(\kappa).}  \label{eq:tpc43-rough-filtration}$$

> **Proof** The average of $\chi_\kappa$ is zero if any prime $p\le z$ divides $\kappa$. Otherwise every prime in $\kappa$ keeps the value $-1$, and the character equals $\mu(\kappa)$. Insert this into [\[eq:tpc43-exact-Walsh-expansion\]](TPC-043.md#ref-08d8759f4d249934).

Before any relevant prime is averaged one has the physical corner; after every relevant prime has been averaged, only $\mathcal D_0$ remains. A future de–randomization may therefore attack the successive prime increments or the final rough spectrum. The identity itself does not bound either.

# Sharp annealed–to–corner boundaries

The exceptional set in an annealed theorem can contain the specified all–minus point. This is not a technical possibility: Walsh systems admit exact extremizers.

###### ref-9344e2c82f42b00d

###### ref-32fc17d99f374192

###### ref-5a760cdb2448fd60

###### ref-937561c282be19bd

###### ref-4c8ec1606280d39b

> **Theorem: A one–corner extremizer** \[thm:tpc43-one-corner-extremizer\] Let $P=p_1\cdots p_s$ be squarefree and let $\mathcal U=\{u:u\mid P\}$, so $R:=|\mathcal U|=2^s$. Define $$Y(\varepsilon)  :=\sum_{u\mid P}\mu(u)\chi_u(\varepsilon).  \label{eq:tpc43-extremizer-sum}$$ Then the labels are distinct and orthogonal, $$\begin{aligned}  Y(\varepsilon)  &=\prod_{p\mid P}(1-\varepsilon_p),  \label{eq:tpc43-extremizer-product}\\  \mathbb E|Y|^2&=R,  \label{eq:tpc43-extremizer-mean}\\  |Y(-\mathbf 1)|^2&=R^2,  \label{eq:tpc43-extremizer-corner}\end{aligned}$$ and $Y$ vanishes at every other point of the $s$-dimensional sign cube. Thus the corner–to–annealed energy ratio is exactly $R$, and Markov’s inequality is sharp on this multiplicative Walsh class.

> **Proof** Expanding $\prod_{p\mid P}(1-\varepsilon_p)$ gives [\[eq:tpc43-extremizer-sum\]](TPC-043.md#ref-32fc17d99f374192). The orthogonality of its $R$ Walsh characters gives $\mathbb E|Y|^2=R$. At the all–minus point every factor is two, so $Y(-\mathbf 1)=2^s=R$. At any other point one factor vanishes.

The same ratio is obtained from any $R$ pairwise distinct squarefree labels of the same Möbius parity, with equal unit coefficients in one coherent output coordinate. In particular, the fused–label injectivity of [\[lem:tpc43-fused-label-injectivity\]](TPC-043.md#ref-2a6ec1df9065a077) does not by itself control the physical corner; it is exactly the property that makes the annealed constant term diagonal.

###### ref-69fb3f95b1e32a5c

> **Remark: No universal row theorem** \[rem:tpc43-no-universal-row-theorem\] The physical orbit matrix has only $O(J)$ output times and polynomially many source rows. A Bessel inequality required for every row coefficient direction must therefore pay the usual rank–trace lower bound. This is the dimension obstruction already isolated in TPC–40, and is why TPC–42 restricted its proposed orbit–slope theorem to the actual projective directions. The present random theorem goes further in a different direction: it retains the complete physical coefficient field and proves that almost every consistent multiplicative prime environment avoids the coherent mode.

###### ref-ba4db13839a36b46

> **Remark: What the model does not preserve** \[rem:tpc43-random-convolution-boundary\] For generic $\varepsilon$, one has $F_\varepsilon*\mathbf 1\ne\delta_1$; only the distinguished all–minus point recovers $F_{-\mathbf 1}=\mu$ and hence the Möbius inversion identity $\mu*\mathbf 1=\delta_1$. Therefore [\[thm:tpc43-annealed-trace\]](TPC-043.md#ref-92df03be3ad03dc6) is a theorem for the randomized terminal interface, not a randomized proof about primes. It preserves the exact squarefree support, multiplicative coupling, masks, aliases, and Hilbert geometry that occur at the final gate; it deliberately does not preserve every special algebraic identity of the distinguished Möbius point.

# Literature comparison and method boundary

###### ref-d9adf00574cadfb2

Random multiplicative functions have a substantial literature. In the standard Rademacher model the prime values are independent signs and the function is supported on squarefree integers; in the Steinhaus model the prime values are independent Haar phases. Their partial sums and moments exhibit behavior very different from sums of independent integer–indexed variables; see, for example, [`\citet{HeapLindqvist2016,Harper2019HighMoments,Harper2020LowMoments}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-043.md#cite-fee90b954a2155ed); [\[Harper2019HighMoments\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-043.md#cite-34ab01111188e823); [\[Harper2020LowMoments\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-043.md#cite-8d8ddf2466d65b09). The present theorem is not a new general moment theorem for those models. Its new input is the TPC–specific product–fiber calculation in [\[lem:tpc43-kernel-fiber-bound\]](TPC-043.md#ref-ebd16355461c5036), which permits one globally coupled prime field to pass through the physical source and target simultaneously. For integral $q$, Probability Result 2.3 of [`\citet{Harper2019HighMoments}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-043.md#cite-34ab01111188e823) gives the exact weighted inequality $$\mathbb E\left|\sum_{n\le N}a_nF_\varepsilon(n)\right|^{2q}  \le\left(   \sum_{n\le N}|a_n|^2d_{2q-1}(n)  \right)^q.  \label{eq:tpc43-Harper-weighted-moment}$$ Here $d_r$ denotes the $r$-fold divisor function. On a squarefree kernel $s$, $d_{2q-1}(s)=(2q-1)^{\omega(s)}$, so this is exactly the coordinate estimate used in [\[lem:tpc43-one-coordinate-moment\]](TPC-043.md#ref-e561200215308f5f). Its underlying cube inequality goes back to [`\citet{Bonami1970,Beckner1975}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-043.md#cite-bdd2e8cd9d92b09f); [\[Beckner1975\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-043.md#cite-0a65b61346d27dbb). The subsequent Hilbert–energy estimate is packet specific and follows by fiberwise Cauchy–Schwarz and Minkowski; no asymptotic theorem for unweighted random partial sums is imported.

## Why this is not another external phase bank

TPC–39 used additive alias phases to recover the equality bucket, and TPC–40 proved a sharp recovery–decorrelation uncertainty principle for external row and alias frames [`\citep{WangTPC40}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-043.md#cite-6d295569703974c1). An independent sign on each row, or a complete Dirichlet–character average separating distinct row residues, would give an immediate diagonal second moment. Such a calculation is generic linear algebra and cannot recover the prescribed untwisted column at low amplification.

Here the deformation instead replaces the two actual Möbius occurrences by the same squarefree–supported multiplicative field. Prime factors shared by different source and target integers therefore create genuine dependencies. The divisor fibers in [\[eq:tpc43-kernel-fiber-divisor-bound\]](TPC-043.md#ref-83528842ee7b6aaa), and their exact collapse at $k=0$, are what make the annealed theorem work. No recovery map is applied, and $F_{-\mathbf 1}=\mu$ is one member of the coefficient family. This places the result on the coefficient–specific side of the boundary left open by TPC–40.

## Adjacent deterministic multiplicative correlations

There are powerful deterministic results for averaged or fixed–complexity systems of multiplicative functions. The theorem of [`\citet{MatomakiRadziwillTao2015}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-043.md#cite-b5a8969fcb0fe4a8) averages fixed–order correlations over their additive shifts, including the two–point case. Tao proved the logarithmically averaged two–point Chowla and Elliott statements for fixed affine forms [`\citep{Tao2016}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-043.md#cite-0bccf4cf69786109). Higher uniformity in short intervals on average and applications to averaged polynomial patterns were established by [`\citet{MRTTZ2023}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-043.md#cite-94b49f28087d1aad). More recently, [`\citet{MatthiesenTeravainenWang2026}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-043.md#cite-7ea5e3a73d05efd6) obtained arbitrary logarithmic savings for a specified class of fixed polynomial systems satisfying explicit degree–separation hypotheses, and [`\citet{BanksShparlinski2026}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-043.md#cite-e9cc6a4bbed1dfcb) obtained logarithmic savings for sums with the factor $\mu(n_1n_2n_3)$ constrained by one equation $f(n_1)+g(n_2)+\wp(n_3)=M$, under their injectivity, bounded–weight, and scale hypotheses.

###### ref-bf903c91013a3ed4

None of these theorems directly supplies the physical TPC estimate. The averaged Chowla theorem does not select the fixed shift $h$. Tao’s two–point theorem does allow fixed affine forms, but its orbit average is logarithmically weighted and its coefficients are fixed as the asymptotic parameter tends to infinity; it supplies no uniformity for the $X$-dependent slopes $m_\alpha,m_\beta$, nor for their coherent weighted summation. In the equality column those slopes satisfy $$m_\alpha\asymp Q  =J^{267/133+o(1)},  \label{eq:tpc43-moving-slope-size}$$ and the target is a specific weighted quadratic form over many rows. The results on polynomial patterns use fixed systems and average a common translation. The Banks–Shparlinski theorem has one separable additive constraint and does not contain the two shared-$j$ product slopes in [\[eq:tpc43-intro-four-mobius\]](TPC-043.md#ref-1e252f27b74b25d8). A theorem for each fixed row pair would also not automatically survive the coherent row summation at the required endpoint.

The exact kernel compression [\[eq:tpc43-physical-kernel-spectrum\]](TPC-043.md#ref-0402233f3bbd7f8a) suggests a different comparison: one now seeks Möbius cancellation against the packet–generated coefficient $C(\kappa)$. That coefficient is not presently known to belong to any coefficient class controlled by the cited theorems—for example, a fixed polynomial/nilsequence family or a family admitting a bounded–complexity separable additive representation. We therefore use the cited results only to mark the current boundary, not as black boxes proving [\[eq:tpc43-remaining-kernel-bound\]](TPC-043.md#ref-49264558c1ac576f).

## Scope of the probabilistic language

“Annealed” refers to expectation over the prime signs while keeping the entire TPC packet fixed. “Almost surely along a prescribed dyadic sequence” refers to one global prime field and one fixed deterministic packet at each scale. No statement is made uniformly over all possible packets or about the distinguished all–minus point. The one–corner extremizer in [\[thm:tpc43-one-corner-extremizer\]](TPC-043.md#ref-9344e2c82f42b00d) shows that these qualifications cannot be dropped by measure theory alone.

# The annealed–to–quenched gate

The preceding theorems separate two statements that were previously mixed together. The packet geometry is generically compatible with the endpoint: a single multiplicative prime field diagonalizes the equality alias exactly and controls the remaining aliases through subpolynomial product fibers, so almost every such field closes the full trace. The physical Möbius function is nevertheless one specified point of that field, not a random draw that may be selected after the energy is measured.

###### ref-49264558c1ac576f

By [\[thm:tpc43-four-to-one-compression\]](TPC-043.md#ref-ffe678e673399b37), the exact remaining equality theorem is the one–sided bound $$\sum_{\kappa>1}\mu(\kappa)C(\kappa)  \le X^{o(1)}K_BQ^2J,  \label{eq:tpc43-remaining-kernel-bound}$$ with $C(\kappa)$ defined by the actual masks and row pairs in [\[eq:tpc43-Walsh-spectrum-coefficient\]](TPC-043.md#ref-f1da8a0d52cadd6d). The already controlled diagonal $\mathcal D_0$ is harmless at this scale. Formula [\[eq:tpc43-remaining-kernel-bound\]](TPC-043.md#ref-49264558c1ac576f) is a compression of the physical four–Möbius gate; it is not a known Möbius orthogonality theorem, because the coefficients aggregate growing affine slopes and may be highly correlated with $\kappa$.

Three precise continuations remain.

1.  **Rough–kernel descent.** Use [\[cor:tpc43-rough-kernel-filtration\]](TPC-043.md#ref-b84a14a8708e84d2) to estimate the spectrum with $P^-(\kappa)>z$, then control the successive small–prime increments without paying their number absolutely.

2.  **Walsh spectral flatness.** Prove a coefficient estimate for $C(\kappa)$, such as a sufficiently strong weighted $\ell^2$ or divisor–fiber bound, and combine it with a Möbius estimate adapted to the resulting kernel support. Parseval [\[eq:tpc43-Walsh-Parseval\]](TPC-043.md#ref-3fc6e4a4cd8a1d2c) measures the exact annealed variance, but by [\[thm:tpc43-one-corner-extremizer\]](TPC-043.md#ref-9344e2c82f42b00d) variance alone is insufficient.

3.  **Prime–phase de–randomization.** Prove directly that the all–minus phase cannot lie in the coherent exceptional set for the actual TPC coefficient system. Such a result must exploit arithmetic information beyond multiplicativity and squarefree support; an abstract selection from a measure–one set cannot identify a prescribed phase point.

The first route is now the most concrete next layer: it turns a four–sign row interaction into a single squarefree–kernel spectral sum and exhibits an exact prime filtration.

## Claim boundary

This paper does not prove [\[eq:tpc43-remaining-kernel-bound\]](TPC-043.md#ref-49264558c1ac576f) at the physical point. It proves no fixed–shift Chowla estimate, no deterministic orbit–slope Bessel theorem for the Möbius coefficients, no parity breach, no Hardy–Littlewood prime–pair asymptotic, and no infinitude of twin primes. “Almost every prime–sign field” never means “the Möbius field”: the latter is the distinguished all–minus point, and [\[thm:tpc43-one-corner-extremizer\]](TPC-043.md#ref-9344e2c82f42b00d) proves that this logical distinction is sharp.
