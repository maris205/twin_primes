## Abstract

TPC–43 represented the surviving equality energy at the four–Möbius gate as a nonnegative Walsh polynomial whose all–minus prime corner is the physical Möbius value. Its annealed theorem controlled almost every prime–sign environment but did not select that corner. We prove a partial de–randomization. Split every squarefree row label at a cutoff $z$, and let $\nu_z$ be the number of distinct small–prime parts occurring in the actual packet. A Hilbert–space grouping and one Cauchy–Schwarz inequality give $$\mathbb P_{>z}\!\left(   \sup_{\sigma_{p\le z}}Z_0(\sigma,\varepsilon_{>z})   >L\nu_z\mathcal D_0\right)\le L^{-1}.$$ Thus one high–prime environment controls every small–prime face simultaneously. At the inherited endpoint, a Rankin bound for the projected labels gives $\nu_z\le X^{62549/26302500+o(1)}$ for $z=(\log X)^{501/500}$, strictly below the available $K_B=X^{1/400+o(1)}$ budget. Consequently a density $1-o(1)$ set of high–prime environments remains admissible after all signs $p\le z$ are overwritten arbitrarily, in particular by the physical all–minus values. We also give two exact prime–by–prime transports. The coefficient restriction energy satisfies $$|Z_0(-\mathbf 1)-\mathcal D_0|^2  =\operatorname{Var}(Z_0)    \prod_p(1-\theta_p),  \qquad -1\le\theta_p\le1,$$ while conditional face energies give an exact likelihood product in an energy–biased measure. A degree–two, entirely $z$-rough Gram example has vanishing relative variance, vanishing normalized influences, and vanishing prime–toggle quadratic variation, yet its all–minus amplification diverges. Hence variance, fixed moments, rough support, or small local defects cannot finish the deterministic endpoint. What remains is a coefficient–specific high–prime cumulative correlation estimate, naturally of bilinear/Type–II type; no parity breach or prime–pair theorem is claimed.

# Introduction and main results

###### ref-5ff39d8348851c7d

The terminal obstruction isolated in TPC–42 is a coherent Hilbert energy with one source and one target Möbius value in each row [`\citep{WangTPC42}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-044.md#cite-cc53a3263a6b378e). TPC–43 replaced $\mu$ by one globally consistent Rademacher multiplicative field and proved exact annealed diagonalization at the equality alias [`\citep{WangTPC43}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-044.md#cite-26882076da806b18). The same paper compressed every unequal row pair into a squarefree Walsh kernel: $$Z_0(\varepsilon)  =\mathcal D_0+\sum_{\kappa>1}C_X(\kappa)\chi_\kappa(\varepsilon),  \qquad  Z_0(-\mathbf 1)  =\mathcal D_0+\sum_{\kappa>1}\mu(\kappa)C_X(\kappa).  \label{eq:tpc44-intro-walsh}$$ Here $Z_0\ge0$, $\mathbb EZ_0=\mathcal D_0$, and $Z_0(-\mathbf 1)$ is the literal physical equality energy. The subscript $X$ is suppressed below.

The difficulty is now extremely specific. Annealed estimates describe most vertices of the prime cube, whereas the arithmetic problem asks for one prescribed vertex. A small exceptional set may contain that vertex at every scale. The present paper does not try to hide this quantifier gap. Instead it asks how many prime coordinates can be made adversarial before the annealed endpoint is lost.

## Uniform control of an entire small–prime face

###### ref-3ff491ca9e15dcd4

###### ref-c57cb42767ba3209

Every nonzero equality atom has a squarefree label $u$. Split it at $z\ge2$ as $$u=u_{\le z}u_{>z},  \qquad  P^{+}(u_{\le z})\le z<P^{-}(u_{>z}),  \label{eq:tpc44-intro-label-split}$$ with the usual conventions for the factor $1$. Let $$\nu_z:=\#\{u_{\le z}:u\text{ occurs as an equality label}\}.  \label{eq:tpc44-intro-nu}$$ This is an actual packet complexity, not the cardinality $2^{\pi(z)}$ of the entire small–prime cube.

The first main theorem is the following robust face estimate; its abstract version is proved in [4](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-044.md#src-94940f2710d87ccd).

###### ref-1fb400af2a551c57

###### ref-56149e70c62d9b28

###### ref-e777bfa611bb08fa

> **Theorem: Robust small–prime face** \[thm:tpc44-intro-robust-face\] For every $z\ge2$ and $L\ge1$, $$\mathbb P_{\varepsilon_p:p>z}\!\left(   \sup_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{\{p\le z\}}}   Z_0(\sigma,\varepsilon_{>z})   >L\nu_z\mathcal D_0  \right)  \le L^{-1}.  \label{eq:tpc44-intro-face-probability}$$ In particular, some high–prime environment satisfies $$\sup_{\sigma}Z_0(\sigma,\varepsilon_{>z})\le\nu_z\mathcal D_0.  \label{eq:tpc44-intro-face-existence}$$

The supremum is inside the probability. Thus the theorem first selects one high–prime environment and then allows every small–prime sign to be changed, jointly and adversarially. It is stronger than proving a tail bound for each fixed small–prime assignment separately.

###### ref-9e5d20af14e6d95a

At the TPC endpoint the labels obey $$u\le X^{\lambda_0+o(1)},  \qquad  \lambda_0=1+\frac{10049}{52500}  =\frac{62549}{52500}.  \label{eq:tpc44-intro-lambda}$$ A Rankin count of possible projected labels yields the quantitative advance.

###### ref-cdf21b7a93f41af7

###### ref-35f35fcf8ee11607

###### ref-43b4401a64797369

###### ref-133daf6c59cb06df

> **Theorem: A mildly superlogarithmic frozen block** \[thm:tpc44-intro-quantitative-face\] Put $$z_X=(\log X)^{501/500},  \qquad  L_X=\exp\!\left(\frac{\log X}{\sqrt{\log\log X}}\right).  \label{eq:tpc44-intro-z-L}$$ Then $$\nu_{z_X}  \le X^{62549/26302500+o(1)}  \label{eq:tpc44-intro-nu-bound}$$ and, for all sufficiently large $X$, $$L_X\nu_{z_X}\le K_B,  \qquad K_B=X^{1/400+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc44-intro-budget}$$ Consequently $$\mathbb P_{>z_X}\!\left(   \sup_{\sigma_{p\le z_X}}   Z_0(\sigma,\varepsilon_{>z_X})   >K_B\mathcal D_0  \right)  \le  \exp\!\left(-\frac{\log X}{\sqrt{\log\log X}}\right).  \label{eq:tpc44-intro-quantitative-probability}$$

In particular, the signs at all primes $p\le z_X$ may be overwritten by $-1$, exactly as required by $\mu$. The theorem does not set the remaining high–prime signs to $-1$.

## Exact descents and the remaining gate

###### ref-3b0a3ca252c10446

###### ref-49d247062d62cb38

###### ref-3a3d9e984fad78ba

###### ref-f918d9823c507b52

Let $F=Z_0-\mathcal D_0$, extend $C(\kappa)$ by zero, and list the active primes as $p_1<\cdots<p_R$. For squarefree $m$, define $$C_r(m):=\sum_{a\mid p_1\cdots p_r}\mu(a)C(am),  \qquad (m,p_1\cdots p_r)=1.  \label{eq:tpc44-intro-Cr}$$ Then setting the first $r$ signs to $-1$ is exactly $$F(-\mathbf 1_{\le p_r},\varepsilon_{>p_r})  =\sum_{(m,p_1\cdots p_r)=1}C_r(m)\chi_m(\varepsilon).  \label{eq:tpc44-intro-restricted-F}$$ If $E_r$ is the sum of $|C_r(m)|^2$ over squarefree $m$ coprime to $p_1\cdots p_r$, coefficient pairing gives $$E_r=(1-\theta_r)E_{r-1},  \qquad -1\le\theta_r\le1,  \label{eq:tpc44-intro-energy-step}$$ and hence the exact identity $$\boxed{  |F(-\mathbf 1)|^2  =\operatorname{Var}(Z_0)   \prod_{r=1}^{R}(1-\theta_r).}  \label{eq:tpc44-intro-coefficient-product}$$ This is a restriction identity, not a probabilistic estimate.

###### ref-3c6fb3c063008b94

###### ref-08a74268e5790137

There is also a nonnegative face transport. Average the first $r$ prime signs while keeping every later one equal to $-1$, and call the result $A_r$. When $Z_0(-\mathbf 1)>0$, $$\frac{Z_0(-\mathbf 1)}{\mathcal D_0}  =\prod_{r=1}^{R}(1+\delta_r),  \qquad -1\le\delta_r\le1.  \label{eq:tpc44-intro-face-product}$$ Under the energy–biased probability $d\mathbb Q_Z=(Z_0/\mathcal D_0)dU$, each factor is a conditional likelihood: $$1+\delta_r  =2\mathbb Q_Z\!\left(   \varepsilon_{p_r}=-1   \mid   \varepsilon_{p_{r+1}}=\cdots=\varepsilon_{p_R}=-1  \right).  \label{eq:tpc44-intro-likelihood}$$ If $Z_0(-\mathbf 1)=0$, the desired upper endpoint is automatic. Thus the nontrivial endpoint selection is exactly a cumulative boundary–path problem.

###### ref-ed642b7caf92ade3

The distinction between local and cumulative control is essential. In [8](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-044.md#src-cc33fd43da6c78e4) we construct a degree–two Gram energy with all nonconstant kernels $z$-rough for which $$\frac{\operatorname{Var}Z}{(\mathbb EZ)^2}\longrightarrow0,  \qquad  \max_r|\delta_r|\longrightarrow0,  \qquad  \sum_r\delta_r^2\longrightarrow0,  \label{eq:tpc44-intro-small-local}$$ but $Z(-\mathbf 1)/\mathbb EZ\to\infty$. Therefore neither a second moment nor small primewise defects can finish the physical corner. The remaining positive target is a one–sided cumulative estimate for the actual $C_X(\kappa)$, after the small primes have been folded exactly.

## Claim boundary and organization

The paper proves a partial de–randomization: inside a density–one set of high–prime environments, a growing mildly superlogarithmic block can be overwritten by the physical signs. It does not prove that the all–minus high–prime environment is typical, that the product in [\[eq:tpc44-intro-coefficient-product\]](TPC-044.md#ref-f918d9823c507b52) is $X^{o(1)}$, or that the rough tail cancels. In particular, it proves no fixed–shift Chowla estimate, no breach of the sieve parity barrier, no Hardy–Littlewood prime–pair asymptotic, and no infinitude of twin primes.

records the inherited interface. gives the exact Walsh martingales. prove the uniform face theorem and its endpoint exponent. The two exact descents are developed in \[[sec:tpc44-coefficient-descent](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-044.md#src-0d89389a322ea45a),[sec:tpc44-face-transport](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-044.md#src-b285ae93f5326699)\]. gives the sharp obstruction. compare the result with the literature and state the next coefficient–specific gate. The finite certificate is described in [11](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-044.md#src-aeae6c38b4877ae2).

# The inherited equality–Walsh interface

###### ref-5e89147c2c99d665

###### ref-dbc18ac998441111

We isolate exactly the part of TPC–43 used here. The left and right output coordinates are combined into one finite index set $i$. For each $i$, $$S_i(\varepsilon)=\sum_{u\in\mathcal U_i}b_{i,u}\chi_u(\varepsilon),  \qquad  Z_0(\varepsilon)=\sum_i|S_i(\varepsilon)|^2,  \label{eq:tpc44-coordinate-packet}$$ where every $u$ is squarefree and the nonzero labels in a fixed $\mathcal U_i$ are pairwise distinct. The characters are $$\chi_u(\varepsilon)=\prod_{p\mid u}\varepsilon_p.  \label{eq:tpc44-character}$$ All coefficients and label sets are deterministic after the inherited TPC packet has been fixed.

###### ref-a59166d58756b784

###### ref-f1f46f39b04e400d

The atomic diagonal is $$\mathcal D_0:=\sum_i\sum_{u\in\mathcal U_i}|b_{i,u}|^2.  \label{eq:tpc44-diagonal}$$ Pairwise label distinctness within each coordinate and Walsh orthogonality give $$\mathbb EZ_0=\mathcal D_0.  \label{eq:tpc44-mean-diagonal}$$ If $\mathcal D_0=0$, every coefficient vanishes and all statements below are trivial. We henceforth suppose $\mathcal D_0>0$.

###### ref-e5a8054a608ad9ca

###### ref-1c07dd3dd479d66b

###### ref-972bd619b081995a

###### ref-7b70d273eccb7f3d

Expanding the squared norms and grouping equal squarefree symmetric–difference kernels gives real coefficients $C(\kappa)$ such that $$\begin{aligned}  Z_0(\varepsilon)  &=\mathcal D_0+F(\varepsilon),  \label{eq:tpc44-Z-F}\\  F(\varepsilon)  &=\sum_{\kappa>1}C(\kappa)\chi_\kappa(\varepsilon),  \label{eq:tpc44-F-spectrum}\\  Z_0(-\mathbf 1)  &=\mathcal D_0+\sum_{\kappa>1}\mu(\kappa)C(\kappa),  \label{eq:tpc44-physical-corner}\\  V:=\|F\|_2^2  &=\operatorname{Var}(Z_0)  =\sum_{\kappa>1}|C(\kappa)|^2.  \label{eq:tpc44-variance-parseval}\end{aligned}$$ Every prime in these formulas belongs to the finite support of the labels. We extend $C$ by zero to all other squarefree integers and set $C(1)=0$.

At the physical equality alias the labels have the more specific form $$u_{\alpha,j}  =d_\alpha n_{\alpha,j,0}  =d_\alpha(\ell_\alpha d_\alpha j+h),  \label{eq:tpc44-physical-label}$$ where $h\ne0$ is fixed, $$\begin{aligned}  \ell_\alpha d_\alpha&\asymp Q,  &Q&=X^{267/400+o(1)},  \notag\\  j&\asymp J,  &J&=X^{133/400+o(1)},  \label{eq:tpc44-endpoint-scales}\\  d_\alpha&\asymp D_0,  &D_0&=X^{10049/52500+o(1)}.  \notag\end{aligned}$$ The notation $D_0$ for the divisor scale is distinct from the calligraphic energy $\mathcal D_0$. Since $QJ\asymp X$, $$u_{\alpha,j}  \le X^{\lambda_0+o(1)},  \qquad  \lambda_0=1+\frac{10049}{52500}  =\frac{62549}{52500}.  \label{eq:tpc44-label-size}$$ The equality endpoint budget and inherited diagonal estimate are $$K_B=X^{1/400+o(1)},  \qquad  \mathcal D_0\ll X^{o(1)}Q^2J,  \label{eq:tpc44-endpoint-budget}$$ and the desired physical conclusion would be $$Z_0(-\mathbf 1)\ll X^{o(1)}K_BQ^2J.  \label{eq:tpc44-physical-target}$$

# Forward and reverse prime martingales

###### ref-eb31d52c0dfdbbda

###### ref-0cc2d0643019240f

List the primes that occur in some nonzero Walsh coefficient as $$p_1<p_2<\cdots<p_R.  \label{eq:tpc44-active-primes}$$ For endpoint conventions, put $p_0:=1$; thus the set of prime coordinates at most $p_0$ is empty. For any cutoff $z$, write $$r(z):=\max\bigl(\{r:p_r\le z\}\cup\{0\}\bigr).  \label{eq:tpc44-cutoff-index}$$ The finite cube has two natural filtrations. Although the identities in this section are elementary, they distinguish averaging a coordinate from physically restricting it to $-1$.

###### ref-e50157be76f72ef4

###### ref-b3ef58d7be1e4eed

For $1\le r\le R$, the forward filtration is $\mathscr F_r^+=\sigma(\varepsilon_{p_1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{p_r})$, put $$M_r:=\mathbb E(F\mid\mathscr F_r^+).  \label{eq:tpc44-forward-martingale}$$ For $0\le r<R$, the reverse filtration retains the signs strictly larger than $p_r$: $$R_r:=\mathbb E\!\left(  F\mid\varepsilon_{p_{r+1}},\ldots,\varepsilon_{p_R}  \right).  \label{eq:tpc44-reverse-martingale}$$ Set $M_0=R_R=0$; then $M_R=R_0=F$.

> **Proposition: Exact largest– and least–prime layers** \[prop:tpc44-prime-layers\] For $0\le r\le R$, the formulas for $M_r$ and $R_r$ below hold; for $1\le r\le R$, so do the difference formulas: $$\begin{aligned}  M_r  &=\sum_{\substack{\kappa>1\\P^{+}(\kappa)\le p_r}}  C(\kappa)\chi_\kappa,  \label{eq:tpc44-forward-spectrum}\\  d_r^+:=M_r-M_{r-1}  &=\sum_{P^{+}(\kappa)=p_r}C(\kappa)\chi_\kappa,  \label{eq:tpc44-forward-difference}\\  R_r  &=\sum_{\substack{\kappa>1\\P^{-}(\kappa)>p_r}}  C(\kappa)\chi_\kappa,  \label{eq:tpc44-reverse-spectrum}\\  d_r^-:=R_{r-1}-R_r  &=\sum_{P^{-}(\kappa)=p_r}C(\kappa)\chi_\kappa.  \label{eq:tpc44-reverse-difference}\end{aligned}$$ Moreover, $$\begin{aligned}  \|d_r^+\|_2^2  &=\sum_{P^{+}(\kappa)=p_r}|C(\kappa)|^2,  \label{eq:tpc44-forward-layer-energy}\\  \|d_r^-\|_2^2  &=\sum_{P^{-}(\kappa)=p_r}|C(\kappa)|^2,  \label{eq:tpc44-reverse-layer-energy}\\  \sum_{r=1}^{R}\|d_r^+\|_2^2  &=\sum_{r=1}^{R}\|d_r^-\|_2^2=V.  \label{eq:tpc44-martingale-parseval}\end{aligned}$$

> **Proof** A Walsh character survives a conditional expectation exactly when it uses only retained coordinates. This gives the four spectral formulas. The layers have disjoint Walsh support, so orthogonality gives the energy identities.

###### ref-b8d061ab502c6627

Evaluating the reverse martingale on the retained all–minus face gives the filtration from TPC–43: $$\mathcal D_0+R_r(-\mathbf 1)  =\mathcal D_0+   \sum_{\substack{\kappa>1\\P^{-}(\kappa)>p_r}}   \mu(\kappa)C(\kappa).  \label{eq:tpc44-rough-filtration}$$ Its exact $L^2$ dissipation is $$\|R_{r-1}\|_2^2-\|R_r\|_2^2=\|d_r^-\|_2^2.  \label{eq:tpc44-reverse-dissipation}$$

There is a deterministic but generally costly layer estimate. Write $$e_r^-:=\sum_{P^{-}(\kappa)=p_r}|C(\kappa)|^2,  \qquad  N_r^-:=\#\{\kappa:C(\kappa)\ne0,\ P^{-}(\kappa)=p_r\}.  \label{eq:tpc44-layer-count-energy}$$ Then $$|F(-\mathbf 1)|  \le\sum_{r=1}^{R}\sqrt{N_r^-e_r^-}.  \label{eq:tpc44-layer-Cauchy}$$ More generally, for $w_r>0$, $$|F(-\mathbf 1)|^2  \le  \left(\sum_r\frac{N_r^-}{w_r}\right)  \left(\sum_rw_re_r^-\right).  \label{eq:tpc44-weighted-layer-Cauchy}$$ These inequalities are exact diagnostics, not endpoint estimates: the support factors $N_r^-$ can be too large. # Uniform control of a whole small–prime face

###### ref-f68ae3248add5c56

###### ref-7147f1594f19010c

Fix $z\ge2$. Every squarefree equality label has the unique split $$u=a b,  \qquad  P^{+}(a)\le z<P^{-}(b),  \label{eq:tpc44-label-low-high}$$ where $a=1$ or $b=1$ is allowed. Let $$\mathcal A_z:=\{a:a\text{ occurs as the low part of an actual label}\},  \qquad \nu_z:=|\mathcal A_z|.  \label{eq:tpc44-low-projection-set}$$ For a high–prime environment $\eta=(\varepsilon_p)_{p>z}$, group the coordinate coefficients by their low parts: $$B_{i,a}(\eta)  :=\sum_{\substack{u\in\mathcal U_i\\u_{\le z}=a}}  b_{i,u}\chi_{u_{>z}}(\eta),  \qquad  W_z(\eta):=\sum_i\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_z}|B_{i,a}(\eta)|^2.  \label{eq:tpc44-B-W}$$

###### ref-f6b7ed1f79bb0c43

> **Lemma: Projected diagonal identity** \[lem:tpc44-projected-diagonal\] One has $$\mathbb E_{\eta}W_z(\eta)=\mathcal D_0.  \label{eq:tpc44-projected-mean}$$

###### ref-4b0544c9225efbd7

> **Proof** Fix $i$ and $a$. Two different full labels $u,u'\in\mathcal U_i$ with the same low part $a$ have different high parts. Indeed $u=ab=u'=ab'$ would imply $u=u'$. Hence $$\mathbb E_\eta|B_{i,a}(\eta)|^2  =\sum_{\substack{u\in\mathcal U_i\\u_{\le z}=a}}|b_{i,u}|^2.  \label{eq:tpc44-B-diagonal}$$ Summing over $i$ and $a$ gives [\[eq:tpc44-projected-mean\]](TPC-044.md#ref-f6b7ed1f79bb0c43).

###### ref-51ee8b8eecbae509

> **Theorem: Robust face compression** \[thm:tpc44-robust-face\] For every $L\ge1$, $$\mathbb P_\eta\!\left(   \sup_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{\{p\le z\}}}   Z_0(\sigma,\eta)>L\nu_z\mathcal D_0  \right)  \le L^{-1}.  \label{eq:tpc44-robust-face-tail}$$ Consequently there exists a high–prime environment $\eta$ for which $$\sup_\sigma Z_0(\sigma,\eta)\le\nu_z\mathcal D_0.  \label{eq:tpc44-robust-face-exists}$$ For each fixed small–prime assignment $\sigma$, one also has $$\mathbb E_\eta Z_0(\sigma,\eta)\le\nu_z\mathcal D_0.  \label{eq:tpc44-fixed-face-mean}$$

###### ref-f8e1965134f3647f

###### ref-675df8fa8e1f844c

###### ref-d4cb11c9def27696

> **Proof** The grouping [\[eq:tpc44-B-W\]](TPC-044.md#ref-277c5a1d88497dc6) gives $$S_i(\sigma,\eta)  =\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_z}\chi_a(\sigma)B_{i,a}(\eta).  \label{eq:tpc44-grouped-coordinate}$$ Cauchy–Schwarz, uniformly in $\sigma$, gives $$|S_i(\sigma,\eta)|^2  \le \nu_z\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_z}|B_{i,a}(\eta)|^2.  \label{eq:tpc44-coordinate-Cauchy}$$ After summing over $i$, $$\sup_\sigma Z_0(\sigma,\eta)\le\nu_zW_z(\eta).  \label{eq:tpc44-uniform-W-bound}$$ Markov’s inequality and [\[lem:tpc44-projected-diagonal\]](TPC-044.md#ref-709cd2e537c00ea4) prove [\[eq:tpc44-robust-face-tail\]](TPC-044.md#ref-42c4a54e291935d0). The finite average of $W_z$ equals $\mathcal D_0$, so some environment satisfies $W_z\le\mathcal D_0$; this gives [\[eq:tpc44-robust-face-exists\]](TPC-044.md#ref-c1261201b592247e). Taking expectations in [\[eq:tpc44-uniform-W-bound\]](TPC-044.md#ref-d4cb11c9def27696) proves [\[eq:tpc44-fixed-face-mean\]](TPC-044.md#ref-51ee8b8eecbae509).

No limiting or compactness argument is involved in the existential statement.

The theorem includes a deterministic statement about the purely smooth Walsh sector. Averaging all high signs after fixing the small signs to $-1$ leaves exactly the kernels supported on primes at most $z$.

###### ref-682a17552acd5f9b

###### ref-5fb7fab1d74840ac

> **Corollary: The smooth physical sector** \[cor:tpc44-smooth-sector\] One has $$0\le  \mathcal D_0+  \sum_{\substack{\kappa>1\\P^{+}(\kappa)\le z}}  \mu(\kappa)C(\kappa)  \le\nu_z\mathcal D_0.  \label{eq:tpc44-smooth-sector}$$

> **Proof** The middle expression is $\mathbb E_\eta Z_0(-\mathbf 1_{\le z},\eta_{>z})$. It is nonnegative, and its upper bound is [\[eq:tpc44-fixed-face-mean\]](TPC-044.md#ref-51ee8b8eecbae509).

###### ref-980fc956f5f447d7

> **Remark: Why there is no union bound** \[rem:tpc44-no-union-bound\] The random variable $W_z$ dominates all small–prime assignments at once. We therefore pay the actual projection count $\nu_z$, not a probability union over $2^{\pi(z)}$ vertices. Conditional moment bounds for each fixed vertex would in any case require controlling collisions after the low coordinates are removed; the grouping above handles those collisions exactly.

# The quantitative cutoff at the TPC endpoint

We now convert the abstract complexity $\nu_z$ into an explicit power of $X$. Only the label size [\[eq:tpc44-label-size\]](TPC-044.md#ref-b8c3c03728514624) is used; no distribution theorem for the physical labels is assumed.

###### ref-0ed064deee979d4f

###### ref-2bf5ced40342904d

> **Lemma: Rankin bound for projected labels** \[lem:tpc44-Rankin-projection\] Let $Y=X^{\lambda+o(1)}$, let $z=(\log X)^A$ with fixed $A>1$, and let $\mathcal U$ be any set of squarefree integers $u\le Y$. The number $\nu_z(\mathcal U)$ of distinct $z$-smooth parts $u_{\le z}$ satisfies $$\nu_z(\mathcal U)  \le X^{\lambda(1-1/A)+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc44-general-Rankin-bound}$$

###### ref-b29127199a599e3f

###### ref-eb46142ed8b82682

###### ref-de37847fa6544faf

###### ref-6f544dec17138be7

> **Proof** Every projected part is a squarefree $z$-smooth integer $a\le Y$. For $0<s<1$, Rankin’s device gives $$\begin{aligned}  \nu_z(\mathcal U)  &\le\sum_{\substack{a\le Y\\a\ \text{squarefree}\\P^{+}(a)\le z}}1  \notag\\  &\le Y^s\prod_{p\le z}(1+p^{-s}).  \label{eq:tpc44-Rankin-product}\end{aligned}$$ Choose $$s=1-\frac1A+\epsilon_X,  \qquad  \epsilon_X=(\log\log X)^{-1/2}.  \label{eq:tpc44-Rankin-s}$$ For sufficiently large $X$, one has $0<s<1$. Moreover, $$\begin{aligned}  \log\prod_{p\le z}(1+p^{-s})  &\le\sum_{n\le z}n^{-s}  \ll_A z^{1-s}  \notag\\  &=\log X\,    \exp\!\left(-A\sqrt{\log\log X}\right)  =o(\log X).  \label{eq:tpc44-Euler-product-small}\end{aligned}$$ The factor $Y^s$ contributes $$X^{\lambda(1-1/A)+o(1)},  \label{eq:tpc44-Rankin-main-factor}$$ which proves the claim.

###### ref-5dd5715d034c9d89

###### ref-97bbe91d3ff03bab

The full admissible range obtained from this argument is worth recording. Put $$A_*:=  \left(1-\frac{1}{400\lambda_0}\right)^{-1}  =\frac{250196}{249671}  =1.00210277\ldots.  \label{eq:tpc44-A-star}$$ For every fixed $1<A<A_*$, $$\lambda_0(1-1/A)<\frac1{400},  \label{eq:tpc44-A-range-gap}$$ so $z=(\log X)^A$ can be absorbed by the endpoint budget.

###### ref-322654103248d016

###### ref-0a11a1cb667f61df

###### ref-26d89941c276d794

###### ref-031ea5571b9569c4

###### ref-0a264c3ae8f73656

> **Theorem: Quantitative robust freezing** \[thm:tpc44-quantitative-freezing\] Let $$A=\frac{501}{500},  \qquad z_X=(\log X)^A,  \qquad  L_X=\exp\!\left(\frac{\log X}{\sqrt{\log\log X}}\right).  \label{eq:tpc44-explicit-parameters}$$ Then $$\nu_{z_X}  \le X^{62549/26302500+o(1)},  \label{eq:tpc44-explicit-nu}$$ and $$\frac1{400}-\frac{62549}{26302500}  =\frac{12829}{105210000}>0.  \label{eq:tpc44-explicit-gap}$$ Consequently $L_X\nu_{z_X}\le K_B$ for all sufficiently large $X$, and $$\mathbb P_{>z_X}\!\left(   \sup_{\sigma_{p\le z_X}}   Z_0(\sigma,\varepsilon_{>z_X})   >K_B\mathcal D_0  \right)  \le L_X^{-1}.  \label{eq:tpc44-explicit-freezing-tail}$$

###### ref-b716d7d796d126ed

> **Proof** Apply [\[lem:tpc44-Rankin-projection\]](TPC-044.md#ref-0ed064deee979d4f) with $\lambda=\lambda_0=62549/52500$. Since $1-1/A=1/501$, the exponent is $$\frac{\lambda_0}{501}  =\frac{62549}{26302500}.  \label{eq:tpc44-explicit-exponent-computation}$$ The exact subtraction in [\[eq:tpc44-explicit-gap\]](TPC-044.md#ref-031ea5571b9569c4) leaves a fixed positive margin. Since $L_X=X^{o(1)}$, that margin implies $L_X\nu_{z_X}\le K_B$. Now apply [\[thm:tpc44-robust-face\]](TPC-044.md#ref-1848a81dcf2a27d1) with $L=L_X$.

The failure probability is summable on dyadic scales. This gives a global-field formulation analogous to the one in TPC–43, but robust under low-prime overwriting.

###### ref-e7b26b796eb942a6

> **Corollary: Almost–sure dyadic overwriting** \[cor:tpc44-dyadic-overwriting\] Let $X_r=2^r$, and fix one deterministic inherited packet at each scale. Couple the packets using one global independent prime–sign field. For almost every field, all sufficiently large $r$ have the following property: after keeping its signs at $p>z_{X_r}$, the signs at every $p\le z_{X_r}$ may be replaced arbitrarily and simultaneously, and every resulting equality energy is at most $K_B(X_r)\mathcal D_0(X_r)$.

###### ref-6ffd6647aaad4d4b

> **Proof** By [\[eq:tpc44-explicit-freezing-tail\]](TPC-044.md#ref-0a264c3ae8f73656), the $r$-th failure probability is at most $$\exp\!\left(  -\frac{r\log2}{\sqrt{\log(r\log2)}}  \right)  \label{eq:tpc44-dyadic-summand}$$ for all sufficiently large $r$, and these probabilities are summable. The first Borel–Cantelli lemma, which needs no independence between scales, proves the result.

The packet sequence must be prescribed. No uniformity over every possible packet sequence is asserted. Also, overwriting the low signs does not overwrite the high signs: the latter remain random in this corollary.

# Exact restriction descent toward the Möbius corner

Conditional expectation deletes Walsh coefficients. Physical de–randomization does something different: setting a sign to $-1$ subtracts paired coefficients. This section records that operation exactly.

###### ref-86d276550fac3d7f

###### ref-1b0f35b8f80af0e2

Let $p_1<\cdots<p_R$ be as in [\[eq:tpc44-active-primes\]](TPC-044.md#ref-eb31d52c0dfdbbda) and put $$P_r:=\prod_{j=1}^{r}p_j,  \qquad P_0:=1.  \label{eq:tpc44-Pr}$$ For squarefree $m$ with $(m,P_r)=1$, define $$C_r(m):=\sum_{a\mid P_r}\mu(a)C(am).  \label{eq:tpc44-Cr-definition}$$ Recall that $C$ is extended by zero and $C(1)=0$. Throughout this section, every sum over $m$ is restricted to squarefree positive integers. We also use the convention $p_0=1$, so restriction to coordinates $p\le p_0$ means no restriction.

###### ref-af53fe20a0c31ecd

###### ref-2030b6f17de8aa76

###### ref-4e64ac6343959b08

###### ref-71d7307d87acd78a

###### ref-915e46229a23c658

> **Proposition: Exact physical restriction** \[prop:tpc44-physical-restriction\] For every $0\le r\le R$, $$F(-\mathbf 1_{\le p_r},\varepsilon_{>p_r})  =\sum_{\substack{m\ge1\\(m,P_r)=1}}C_r(m)\chi_m(\varepsilon).  \label{eq:tpc44-restriction-expansion}$$ The coefficients satisfy $$C_r(m)=C_{r-1}(m)-C_{r-1}(p_rm)  \qquad (m,P_r)=1.  \label{eq:tpc44-coefficient-recurrence}$$ The physical signed sum is invariant under the descent: $$F(-\mathbf 1)  =\sum_{(m,P_r)=1}\mu(m)C_r(m),  \label{eq:tpc44-physical-invariance}$$ and at the terminal stage $$F(-\mathbf 1)=C_R(1).  \label{eq:tpc44-terminal-coefficient}$$

> **Proof** Write every squarefree kernel uniquely as $\kappa=am$, where $a\mid P_r$ and $(m,P_r)=1$. At the restricted face, $\chi_a(-\mathbf 1)=\mu(a)$, so grouping by $m$ proves [\[eq:tpc44-restriction-expansion\]](TPC-044.md#ref-2030b6f17de8aa76). Splitting the divisors of $P_r=P_{r-1}p_r$ according as they contain $p_r$ gives [\[eq:tpc44-coefficient-recurrence\]](TPC-044.md#ref-4e64ac6343959b08). Evaluating the remaining signs at $-1$ gives [\[eq:tpc44-physical-invariance\]](TPC-044.md#ref-71d7307d87acd78a). After every active prime has been processed, the only possible remaining index is $m=1$, proving [\[eq:tpc44-terminal-coefficient\]](TPC-044.md#ref-915e46229a23c658).

###### ref-9c0e89aabe6d779e

Define the restriction energy $$E_r:=\sum_{(m,P_r)=1}|C_r(m)|^2.  \label{eq:tpc44-Er}$$ Thus $E_0=V$ and $E_R=|F(-\mathbf 1)|^2$.

###### ref-a8b3bde2b96bb6bf

###### ref-df92d9efabd5b38c

###### ref-5b7412b8e1c424e2

###### ref-8ebed9f38cc2a3a7

> **Theorem: Exact coefficient–correlation product** \[thm:tpc44-correlation-product\] Whenever $E_{r-1}>0$, define $$\theta_r:=  \frac{   2\operatorname{Re}   \displaystyle\sum_{(m,P_r)=1}   C_{r-1}(m)\overline{C_{r-1}(p_rm)}  }{E_{r-1}}.  \label{eq:tpc44-theta}$$ Then $$-1\le\theta_r\le1,  \qquad  E_r=(1-\theta_r)E_{r-1}.  \label{eq:tpc44-Er-recurrence}$$ If an energy first becomes zero, set every later $\theta_r=0$; every later energy is then zero. With this convention, $$\boxed{  |F(-\mathbf 1)|^2  =V\prod_{r=1}^{R}(1-\theta_r).}  \label{eq:tpc44-exact-correlation-product}$$

###### ref-74d6a045a5f76293

###### ref-6095ab9f96b0ab0b

> **Proof** Partition the indices in $E_{r-1}$ into pairs $m,p_rm$, with $(m,P_r)=1$. Then $$\begin{aligned}  E_{r-1}  &=\sum_{(m,P_r)=1}  \left(   |C_{r-1}(m)|^2+|C_{r-1}(p_rm)|^2  \right),  \label{eq:tpc44-paired-old-energy}\\  E_r  &=\sum_{(m,P_r)=1}  |C_{r-1}(m)-C_{r-1}(p_rm)|^2.  \label{eq:tpc44-paired-new-energy}\end{aligned}$$ Expanding the second line gives [\[eq:tpc44-Er-recurrence\]](TPC-044.md#ref-5b7412b8e1c424e2). The inequality $2|ab|\le|a|^2+|b|^2$ gives $|\theta_r|\le1$. Iteration from $E_0=V$ to $E_R=|F(-\mathbf 1)|^2$ proves the product formula.

The order of the prime restrictions can be changed. The intermediate $\theta_r$ then change, but their product is forced by the endpoints. Thus [\[eq:tpc44-exact-correlation-product\]](TPC-044.md#ref-8ebed9f38cc2a3a7) is an identity along a chosen coordinate path, not an assertion that the local correlations are independent.

###### ref-f7b8f0d0ee86145b

We next relate the small–prime projection count to the restriction energy. Let $\mathcal K_z$ be the set of low parts $\kappa_{\le z}$ of nonzero pair kernels. Since every pair kernel is the symmetric difference of two row labels, $$|\mathcal K_z|\le\nu_z^2.  \label{eq:tpc44-kernel-projection-count}$$

###### ref-646d56cd2c7441ed

###### ref-c21ec18191d8d1e3

> **Proposition: Energy cost of freezing the low primes** \[prop:tpc44-low-restriction-energy\] After every active prime $p\le z$ has been restricted to $-1$, the remaining coefficient energy $E_z=E_{r(z)}$ satisfies $$E_z\le\nu_z^2V.  \label{eq:tpc44-low-energy-bound}$$

###### ref-3dbeb3106aa86356

###### ref-83cde9a1b381ba1e

> **Proof** For a fixed high kernel $m$, the restricted coefficient is a signed sum $$C_{\le z}(m)  =\sum_{a\in\mathcal K_z}\mu(a)C(am),  \label{eq:tpc44-low-kernel-sum}$$ with absent coefficients interpreted as zero. Cauchy–Schwarz gives $$|C_{\le z}(m)|^2  \le|\mathcal K_z|\sum_{a\in\mathcal K_z}|C(am)|^2.  \label{eq:tpc44-low-kernel-Cauchy}$$ Summing over $m$, using unique low–high factorization, and applying [\[eq:tpc44-kernel-projection-count\]](TPC-044.md#ref-f7b8f0d0ee86145b) proves the claim.

Although the exponent $\nu_z^2$ is natural for the squared coefficient spectrum, [\[thm:tpc44-robust-face\]](TPC-044.md#ref-1848a81dcf2a27d1) is stronger for the nonnegative energy itself: it returns to the unsquared row labels and pays only one factor $\nu_z$.

# Conditional face transport and likelihood drift

###### ref-5c584ca1e97e464c

###### ref-c5d3250bcdd2ba08

###### ref-fe6ca08b6058b8e2

###### ref-396504ebb216ae01

The coefficient descent tracks the centered spectrum. There is a second exact descent that preserves the nonnegativity of $Z_0$. For $0\le r\le R$, define $$A_r:=  \mathbb E_{\varepsilon_{p_1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{p_r}}  Z_0(\varepsilon_{\le p_r},-\mathbf 1_{>p_r}).  \label{eq:tpc44-Ar}$$ Then $$A_0=Z_0(-\mathbf 1),  \qquad A_R=\mathcal D_0,  \label{eq:tpc44-Ar-endpoints}$$ and the reverse Walsh formula gives $$A_r=\mathcal D_0+  \sum_{\substack{\kappa>1\\P^{-}(\kappa)>p_r}}  \mu(\kappa)C(\kappa).  \label{eq:tpc44-Ar-spectrum}$$ For an arbitrary cutoff $z$, use [\[eq:tpc44-cutoff-index\]](TPC-044.md#ref-0cc2d0643019240f) and write $$A_z:=A_{r(z)},  \qquad E_z:=E_{r(z)}.  \label{eq:tpc44-Az-definition}$$

###### ref-5975728071cb2d02

###### ref-57047fbddf22620f

###### ref-d68f292d7ffc31f6

###### ref-51b76c5e417f4907

###### ref-e8ebabc864ae5d1c

###### ref-6bf917f14f680274

> **Proposition: Exact least–prime increment** \[prop:tpc44-least-prime-increment\] Put $$L_r:=A_{r-1}-A_r.  \label{eq:tpc44-Lr}$$ Then $$L_r=\sum_{P^{-}(\kappa)=p_r}\mu(\kappa)C(\kappa).  \label{eq:tpc44-Lr-spectrum}$$ Moreover, $$0\le A_{r-1}\le2A_r.  \label{eq:tpc44-face-doubling}$$ If $Z_0(-\mathbf 1)>0$, all $A_r>0$, and $$\delta_r:=\frac{L_r}{A_r}\in[-1,1]  \label{eq:tpc44-delta}$$ satisfies $$\boxed{  \frac{Z_0(-\mathbf 1)}{\mathcal D_0}  =\prod_{r=1}^{R}(1+\delta_r).}  \label{eq:tpc44-face-product}$$

###### ref-4fe779fe558c7ae1

> **Proof** Subtract [\[eq:tpc44-Ar-spectrum\]](TPC-044.md#ref-fe6ca08b6058b8e2) at adjacent indices to obtain the increment formula. Before averaging $\varepsilon_{p_r}$, let $B_-$ and $B_+$ be the two nonnegative conditional averages obtained by setting that sign to $-1$ and $+1$, respectively. Then $$A_{r-1}=B_-,  \qquad A_r=\frac{B_-+B_+}{2},  \label{eq:tpc44-two-face-average}$$ which proves [\[eq:tpc44-face-doubling\]](TPC-044.md#ref-51b76c5e417f4907) and $-1\le\delta_r\le1$. If some $A_r=0$, then $A_{r-1}=0$, so positive $A_0$ forces every $A_r>0$. Finally $A_{r-1}=A_r(1+\delta_r)$; telescope from $r=R$ to $r=1$.

###### ref-7b6700ac9baeb0a6

###### ref-3b13ca6b1330e839

###### ref-2556fd67a11f7147

The increment also has an exact Gram interpretation. After averaging the coordinates $p_1,\ldots,p_{r-1}$, group row labels by their incidence pattern $B$ on those coordinates. With the later signs fixed at $-1$, let $V_{B,0}$ and $V_{B,1}$ be the resulting coefficient vectors in the direct sum of the output spaces, according as $p_r$ does not or does divide the label. Orthogonality in the averaged coordinates gives $$\begin{aligned}  A_{r-1}  &=\sum_B\|V_{B,0}-V_{B,1}\|^2,  \label{eq:tpc44-Gram-face-minus}\\  A_r  &=\sum_B\bigl(\|V_{B,0}\|^2+\|V_{B,1}\|^2\bigr),  \label{eq:tpc44-Gram-face-average}\\  L_r  &=-2\operatorname{Re}\sum_B    \langle V_{B,0},V_{B,1}\rangle.  \label{eq:tpc44-Gram-face-increment}\end{aligned}$$ Consequently, whenever $A_r>0$, the drift $\delta_r=L_r/A_r$ is a normalized real cross inner product between the $p_r$-free and $p_r$-divisible blocks.

###### ref-9f48cb52ea98c7a4

###### ref-d49d49d4d20e0f06

The product has a probability interpretation. Let $U$ be uniform measure on the active prime cube and define the energy–biased measure $$d\mathbb Q_Z:=\frac{Z_0}{\mathcal D_0}\,dU.  \label{eq:tpc44-energy-biased-measure}$$ For $0\le r\le R$, let $$\mathcal E_r:=  \{\varepsilon_{p_{r+1}}=\cdots=\varepsilon_{p_R}=-1\}.  \label{eq:tpc44-tail-face-event}$$

###### ref-54633dcad492a118

###### ref-416b8e6a5b012e2a

###### ref-9b6e08f108dac52a

> **Theorem: Boundary likelihood chain** \[thm:tpc44-likelihood-chain\] If $Z_0(-\mathbf 1)>0$, then $$1+\delta_r  =2\mathbb Q_Z\!\left(  \varepsilon_{p_r}=-1\mid\mathcal E_r  \right),  \label{eq:tpc44-conditional-likelihood}$$ and therefore $$\frac{Z_0(-\mathbf 1)}{\mathcal D_0}  =\prod_{r=1}^{R}  2\mathbb Q_Z\!\left(  \varepsilon_{p_r}=-1  \mid  \varepsilon_{p_{r+1}}=\cdots=\varepsilon_{p_R}=-1  \right).  \label{eq:tpc44-likelihood-product}$$

###### ref-7ebfc9936f57b9a3

###### ref-4e8f983007b951a1

> **Proof** The uniform probability of $\mathcal E_r$ is $2^{-(R-r)}$, and conditioning the energy on that face gives $A_r$. Hence $$\mathbb Q_Z(\mathcal E_r)  =2^{-(R-r)}\frac{A_r}{\mathcal D_0}.  \label{eq:tpc44-face-event-mass}$$ The event $\mathcal E_r\cap\{\varepsilon_{p_r}=-1\}$ is $\mathcal E_{r-1}$. Dividing the corresponding masses gives $$\mathbb Q_Z(\varepsilon_{p_r}=-1\mid\mathcal E_r)  =\frac{A_{r-1}}{2A_r}=\frac{1+\delta_r}{2}.  \label{eq:tpc44-face-conditional-ratio}$$ The product formula follows.

###### ref-37ec4d404b0f0ad9

If $Z_0(-\mathbf 1)=0$, every upper endpoint is automatic. Otherwise, for a target factor $K\ge1$, exact endpoint selection is equivalent to $$\sum_{r=1}^{R}\log(1+\delta_r)\le\log K.  \label{eq:tpc44-log-drift-criterion}$$ This is why pointwise $o(1)$ bounds for $\delta_r$, or even a small quadratic variation, need not suffice: a long sequence of tiny positive drifts may still accumulate.

# A vanishing–variance rough obstruction

We now show that the high–prime condition left by the preceding sections cannot be supplied by a black–box variance, fixed–moment, or low–influence theorem. The example belongs to the same abstract class as [\[eq:tpc44-coordinate-packet\]](TPC-044.md#ref-5e89147c2c99d665): it is the squared norm of a Walsh sum with distinct squarefree labels.

###### ref-9e5e2a45ada3bf07

Fix $z$, choose distinct primes $q_1,\ldots,q_R>z$, and choose a squarefree $u_0$ coprime to all of them. For $\lambda>0$, put $$S(\varepsilon)  :=\chi_{u_0}(\varepsilon)  \left(   1-\frac{\lambda}{R}\sum_{i=1}^{R}\chi_{q_i}(\varepsilon)  \right),  \qquad Z(\varepsilon):=|S(\varepsilon)|^2.  \label{eq:tpc44-obstruction-SZ}$$ The distinct row labels are $u_0,u_0q_1,\ldots,u_0q_R$.

###### ref-83d3d065a3eb1ead

###### ref-75db890ea23c2669

###### ref-2700781ca4e40402

###### ref-6b9a62b6afd681ee

###### ref-892d6e572ca73bd7

###### ref-3ab5324cf0d6e896

###### ref-74a192967e4a337c

###### ref-7ba45f45616549ad

> **Theorem: Rough degree–two late collapse** \[thm:tpc44-rough-obstruction\] The energy [\[eq:tpc44-obstruction-SZ\]](TPC-044.md#ref-9e5e2a45ada3bf07) has the exact expansion $$Z  =1+\frac{\lambda^2}{R}  -\frac{2\lambda}{R}\sum_{i=1}^{R}\chi_{q_i}  +\frac{2\lambda^2}{R^2}   \sum_{1\le i<j\le R}\chi_{q_iq_j}.  \label{eq:tpc44-obstruction-expansion}$$ Thus every nonconstant kernel is $z$-rough and the Walsh degree is at most $2$. Moreover, $$\begin{aligned}  D:=\mathbb EZ&=1+\frac{\lambda^2}{R},  \label{eq:tpc44-obstruction-D}\\  Z(-\mathbf 1)&=(1+\lambda)^2,  \label{eq:tpc44-obstruction-corner}\\  \operatorname{Var}Z  &=\frac{4\lambda^2}{R}   +\frac{2\lambda^4(R-1)}{R^3}.  \label{eq:tpc44-obstruction-variance}\end{aligned}$$ Take $\lambda=R^{1/4}$. Then $$D\longrightarrow1,  \qquad  \frac{\operatorname{Var}Z}{D^2}=O(R^{-1/2})\longrightarrow0,  \qquad  \frac{Z(-\mathbf 1)}D\sim R^{1/2}\longrightarrow\infty.  \label{eq:tpc44-obstruction-asymptotics}$$ For every fixed $1\le q<\infty$, $$\left\|\frac ZD-1\right\|_{L^q(U)}\longrightarrow0.  \label{eq:tpc44-obstruction-fixed-moments}$$ If $d\mathbb Q_Z=(Z/D)dU$, then $$\chi^2(\mathbb Q_Z\|U)=\frac{\operatorname{Var}Z}{D^2}\longrightarrow0,  \qquad  \|\mathbb Q_Z-U\|_{\rm TV}\longrightarrow0,  \label{eq:tpc44-obstruction-TV}$$ while the density at the distinguished point diverges as in [\[eq:tpc44-obstruction-asymptotics\]](TPC-044.md#ref-3ab5324cf0d6e896).

###### ref-a63c5c96583b518a

> **Proof** The factor $\chi_{u_0}$ has modulus one and disappears on squaring. Expanding the remaining real square, using $(\sum_i\chi_{q_i})^2=R+2\sum_{i<j}\chi_{q_iq_j}$, gives [\[eq:tpc44-obstruction-expansion\]](TPC-044.md#ref-75db890ea23c2669). Orthogonality gives the mean and variance, while evaluating every $\chi_{q_i}$ at $-1$ gives [\[eq:tpc44-obstruction-corner\]](TPC-044.md#ref-6b9a62b6afd681ee).
>
> For $\lambda=R^{1/4}$, write $$\frac{\lambda}{R}\sum_{i=1}^{R}\chi_{q_i}  =R^{-1/4}   \left(R^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^{R}\chi_{q_i}\right).  \label{eq:tpc44-obstruction-Khintchine-scaling}$$ Khintchine’s inequality bounds every fixed $L^q$ norm of the parenthesized variable. Hence the left side tends to zero in every fixed $L^q$, and squaring proves [\[eq:tpc44-obstruction-fixed-moments\]](TPC-044.md#ref-74a192967e4a337c). The chi–square identity is the definition of $\mathbb Q_Z$, and total variation is at most one half of its square root.

###### ref-b3bd7aead3cf7365

###### ref-e7c14aa62687a4b1

Even the primewise face increments are individually negligible. Order the $q_i$ arbitrarily and define $A_t$ as in [\[eq:tpc44-Ar\]](TPC-044.md#ref-5c584ca1e97e464c). Direct averaging gives $$A_t=  \left(1+\lambda\frac{R-t}{R}\right)^2  +\frac{\lambda^2t}{R^2},  \label{eq:tpc44-obstruction-At}$$ and $$A_{t-1}-A_t  =\frac{2\lambda}{R}   \left(1+\lambda\frac{R-t}{R}\right).  \label{eq:tpc44-obstruction-Lt}$$ Consequently $$0\le\delta_t\le\frac{2\lambda}{R},  \qquad  \sum_{t=1}^{R}\delta_t^2\le\frac{4\lambda^2}{R}.  \label{eq:tpc44-obstruction-small-deltas}$$ For $\lambda=R^{1/4}$, the maximum is $O(R^{-3/4})$ and the sum of squares is $O(R^{-1/2})$, yet $$\prod_{t=1}^{R}(1+\delta_t)  =\frac{(1+\lambda)^2}{1+\lambda^2/R}  \sim R^{1/2}.  \label{eq:tpc44-obstruction-delta-product}$$

For completeness, the normalized Walsh influence of a prime $q_i$ is also $O(R^{-1})$. Indeed $$\operatorname{Inf}_{q_i}(F)  :=\sum_{q_i\mid\kappa}|C(\kappa)|^2  =\frac{4\lambda^2}{R^2}   +\frac{4\lambda^4(R-1)}{R^4},  \label{eq:tpc44-obstruction-influence}$$ and division by [\[eq:tpc44-obstruction-variance\]](TPC-044.md#ref-892d6e572ca73bd7) gives the claim at $\lambda=R^{1/4}$.

> **Remark: Scope of the counterexample** \[rem:tpc44-obstruction-scope\] The example proves that the abstract nonnegative Walsh–Gram interface does not force good behavior at the all–minus corner. It does not show that the actual packet coefficient $C_X(\kappa)$ realizes this example, nor that the physical endpoint fails. Its role is to identify which additional arithmetic statement is indispensable.

# Literature boundary

The prime–by–prime conditioning in \[[sec:tpc44-prime-martingales](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-044.md#src-965071f6b0f471ac),[sec:tpc44-face-transport](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-044.md#src-b285ae93f5326699)\] is the Walsh analogue of a finite reverse martingale. Its differences group the coefficient spectrum by the least prime factor. Hypercontractive moment bounds for Rademacher multiplicative functions, such as Probability Result 2.3 of [`\citet{Harper2019HighMoments}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-044.md#cite-34ab01111188e823), control such polynomials in the annealed model. They do not, by themselves, control a prescribed vertex of the cube. The robust face theorem uses the additional Gram representation and injective equality labels, rather than a stronger generic moment inequality.

General low–influence principles also have a different quantifier. The invariance theorem of [`\citet[Theorem~3.18]{MosselODonnellOleszkiewicz2010}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-044.md#cite-aa90b7e394fb7449) is distributional under bounded–degree and low–influence hypotheses. Even when those hypotheses hold, the theorem describes typical product–space inputs, not the all–minus input. The obstruction in [\[thm:tpc44-rough-obstruction\]](TPC-044.md#ref-83d3d065a3eb1ead) makes this distinction explicit: its degree is two and its normalized influences vanish, yet its specified corner diverges.

###### ref-df542d6e583f715f

Small–bias spaces provide a finite derandomization of Walsh averages. [`\citet[Theorem~3.2]{NaorNaor1993}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-044.md#cite-8530f0e1376540d1) construct such spaces with a logarithmic randomness seed. Applied to $F(\varepsilon)=\sum C(\kappa)\chi_\kappa(\varepsilon)$, the direct conclusion has the form $$|\mathbb E_{\varepsilon\in\mathcal S}F(\varepsilon)|  \le\epsilon\sum_\kappa|C(\kappa)|.  \label{eq:tpc44-small-bias-l1}$$ It therefore needs an $\ell^1$ coefficient estimate and locates some good sign vector; it does not identify the physical vector. Classical Riesz–product results, for example [`\citet[Theorem~1.2]{Peyriere1975}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-044.md#cite-723523dd31585911), concern singularity of limiting product measures and likewise do not bound a specified finite corner.

###### ref-bf72593f511751a2

Strong theorems for unweighted rough numbers do not supply the missing signed estimate. [`\citet[Theorem~1.1]{GorodetskyRoughVariance}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-044.md#cite-13dc85de209a256b) proves a variance asymptotic for the indicator of integers without small prime factors in averaged short intervals. This controls spatially averaged roughness, not $$\sum_{\kappa}\mu(\kappa)C_X(\kappa)  \label{eq:tpc44-weighted-mobius-sum}$$ for a signed, packet–dependent coefficient. Large–small prime duality [`\citep{Alladi1977}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-044.md#cite-089b132e75be75ec) and recent effective least–prime identities such as [`\citet[Theorem~1.1]{Tenenbaum2026}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-044.md#cite-6423106c40aaa7c8) exploit special divisor or harmonic weights. No corresponding convolution identity has been proved for $C_X$.

Möbius orthogonality to fixed–complexity structured sequences is a second possible route. For instance, [`\citet[Theorem~1.1]{GreenTao2012}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-044.md#cite-6b97b10bc87e7f21) give arbitrary logarithmic saving against polynomial nilsequences of fixed complexity. The present $C_X(\kappa)$ is scale dependent and is not known to be a fixed–complexity nilsequence, a bounded sum of such sequences, or a member of an analogous class. We therefore cite that theorem as a route boundary, not as a black box that applies here.

The closest classical diagnosis is the sieve parity problem. The asymptotic sieve of [`\citet[Theorem~1]{FriedlanderIwaniec1998}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-044.md#cite-a670a8e9d85764df) obtains a prime conclusion only after an explicit bilinear axiom is added to local–density and remainder information. In a broad modern sieve framework, [`\citet[Theorem~2.1]{FordMaynard2024}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-044.md#cite-5b5e23d53c608306) show that insufficient Type–II information allows sequences satisfying nontrivial Type–I and Type–II estimates while still failing to force primes. Their theorems do not apply literally to the generally signed $C_X$, but they support the route map: rough–kernel counting alone cannot control [\[eq:tpc44-weighted-mobius-sum\]](TPC-044.md#ref-bf72593f511751a2); a coefficient–specific bilinear estimate or an equally strong arithmetic substitute is needed.

To avoid an accidental application of an unrelated theorem, we record the current negative information. The coefficient $C_X$ is real but generally signed, packet and mask dependent, and changes with $X$. It is not known to be multiplicative, nonnegative, low degree, uniformly low influence, $\ell^1$-small, smooth, divisor–convolutional, a fixed nilsequence, or controlled in a Type–II norm. The results cited in this section diagnose possible inputs; none proves the remaining physical corner estimate.

# The residual high–prime gate

###### ref-f9b53d489cdbc234

The small–prime theorem and coefficient descent combine into a precise sufficient condition. Process every active prime $p\le z_X$ first, where $z_X=(\log X)^{501/500}$, and call the resulting coefficient energy $E_{z_X}$. If it is nonzero, continue through the high primes and define $$\mathfrak A_{>z_X}  :=\frac{E_R}{E_{z_X}}  =\prod_{p_r>z_X}(1-\theta_r).  \label{eq:tpc44-high-amplification}$$ This is the exact high–face point–evaluation amplification. It may depend on the chosen order of the high primes through the individual factors, but the ratio itself does not.

###### ref-c09ab60792471bdb

###### ref-29113ce974d32b05

###### ref-7809df7c216ca5ef

> **Theorem: A sufficient high–prime correlation gate** \[thm:tpc44-high-gate\] If the actual TPC equality coefficients satisfy $$\mathfrak A_{>z_X}\le X^{o(1)},  \label{eq:tpc44-high-product-hypothesis}$$ then $$Z_0(-\mathbf 1)\le K_B\mathcal D_0  \label{eq:tpc44-conditional-endpoint}$$ for all sufficiently large $X$. A sufficient local condition for [\[eq:tpc44-high-product-hypothesis\]](TPC-044.md#ref-c09ab60792471bdb) is $$\sum_{p_r>z_X}(-\theta_r)_+=o(\log X).  \label{eq:tpc44-negative-theta-sum}$$

###### ref-c3c30e30802c71cf

###### ref-6b0d6204a607c297

###### ref-fd72af0a1720f12c

> **Proof** If $E_{z_X}=0$, then $F(-\mathbf 1)=0$ and the result is immediate. Otherwise, [\[prop:tpc44-low-restriction-energy\]](TPC-044.md#ref-646d56cd2c7441ed) and [\[eq:tpc44-high-amplification\]](TPC-044.md#ref-f9b53d489cdbc234) give $$|F(-\mathbf 1)|  \le\nu_{z_X}V^{1/2}\mathfrak A_{>z_X}^{1/2}.  \label{eq:tpc44-final-F-bound}$$ By [\[eq:tpc44-soft-variance\]](TPC-044.md#ref-bcc1c6f559568e0f), $V^{1/2}\le X^{o(1)}\mathcal D_0$, while [\[thm:tpc44-quantitative-freezing\]](TPC-044.md#ref-322654103248d016) gives $$\nu_{z_X}\le  X^{1/400-12829/105210000+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc44-nu-with-margin}$$ The fixed power margin absorbs the factors $X^{o(1)}$, as well as the diagonal term $\mathcal D_0$, and proves the endpoint.
>
> For $-1\le\theta<1$, $$\log(1-\theta)\le(-\theta)_+.  \label{eq:tpc44-log-theta-bound}$$ If a factor has $\theta=1$, the terminal energy vanishes. Otherwise summing [\[eq:tpc44-log-theta-bound\]](TPC-044.md#ref-fd72af0a1720f12c) proves that [\[eq:tpc44-negative-theta-sum\]](TPC-044.md#ref-7809df7c216ca5ef) implies [\[eq:tpc44-high-product-hypothesis\]](TPC-044.md#ref-c09ab60792471bdb).

###### ref-a9062b2f32f7c8eb

The theorem is conditional, but the condition is now both exact and coefficient specific. It asks that high–prime restrictions not create a polynomial cumulative anti–correlation. A plausible route is to prove a bilinear estimate for the paired coefficient sum $$\sum_m C_{r-1}(m)\overline{C_{r-1}(p_rm)}  \label{eq:tpc44-paired-coefficient-target}$$ uniformly after the preceding restrictions. Merely proving that few labels contain $p_r$ gives, by Cauchy–Schwarz, a square–root density loss; over many primes that is generally not summable. The needed input must also exploit an angle, sign, oscillation, or genuine source–target bilinear structure.

###### ref-c91f6aacc162c493

The conditional face product gives an equivalent one–sided route. If $A_z=0$, then repeated use of [\[eq:tpc44-face-doubling\]](TPC-044.md#ref-51b76c5e417f4907) gives $A_0=Z_0(-\mathbf 1)=0$, so the endpoint is automatic. If $A_z>0$ and one can prove for some cutoff $z$ $$\log\frac{A_z}{\mathcal D_0}  +\sum_{p_r\le z}(\delta_r)_+  \le\left(\frac1{400}+o(1)\right)\log X,  \label{eq:tpc44-face-sufficient-condition}$$ then [\[eq:tpc44-log-drift-criterion\]](TPC-044.md#ref-37ec4d404b0f0ad9) closes the endpoint. By [\[eq:tpc44-Gram-face-increment\]](TPC-044.md#ref-2556fd67a11f7147), each $\delta_r$ is a normalized real cross inner product between the $p_r$-free and $p_r$-divisible blocks. A bound of size $O(1/p_r)$ for its positive part would be summable with only a polylogarithmic loss. No such estimate is asserted here.

## What has and has not moved

TPC–43 proved that almost every full prime–sign environment closes the equality packet. The present theorem proves more: after drawing only the high signs, a density–one set of such environments permits arbitrary adversarial overwriting of every prime up to $(\log X)^{501/500}$. This includes the physical values on a growing block and is therefore a strict partial de–randomization.

The unresolved step is still substantial. One must either prove [\[eq:tpc44-high-product-hypothesis\]](TPC-044.md#ref-c09ab60792471bdb), establish the face drift bound [\[eq:tpc44-face-sufficient-condition\]](TPC-044.md#ref-c91f6aacc162c493), derive a Type–II estimate for [\[eq:tpc44-paired-coefficient-target\]](TPC-044.md#ref-a9062b2f32f7c8eb), or identify a fixed structured class for $C_X$ covered by a deterministic Möbius orthogonality theorem. Failure of one route would be informative, but none may be replaced by a suggestion that the all–minus high corner is random.

## Claim ledger

The unconditional results of this paper are:

1.  exact forward and reverse prime martingale layers;

2.  uniform compression of every small–prime face at cost $\nu_z$;

3.  the Rankin bound and robust freezing through $(\log X)^{501/500}$;

4.  exact coefficient restriction and correlation products;

5.  exact conditional likelihood transport; and

6.  the vanishing–variance degree–two rough obstruction.

The high–prime amplification bound is a clearly labeled sufficient hypothesis, not a proved estimate for the physical coefficients. No result here proves the twin–prime conjecture, a prime–pair asymptotic, fixed–shift four–Möbius cancellation, or a general violation of sieve parity.
