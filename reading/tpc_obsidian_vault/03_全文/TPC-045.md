## Abstract

The “four–Möbius gate” is the nonnegative equality energy left after expanding two source and two target Möbius factors in a fixed-shift prime-pair decomposition. Earlier papers in the twin-prime-correlation (TPC) sequence represented this energy as a Walsh polynomial but left its deterministic all–minus corner unresolved. We study that corner for the actual labels $u=d(\ell d j+h)$. If a prime in the global band $\sqrt{N_+}<p\le N_-/R$ divides a target, then $\ell d j+h=pr$ with $r\ge R$, and setting its sign to $-1$ replaces $u=p(dr)$ by $dr$ exactly. Equal descended labels have maximal multiplicity $\rho_R\le X^{o(1)}(1+L_0/R)$; the actual multiplicity, rather than this ambient upper bound, is the exact squared norm of the grouping map. Combining the descent with robust Walsh-face compression yields an unconditional band/cutoff tradeoff. In particular, the cutoff $(\log X)^{501/500}$ survives while the physical band reaches exponent $55021/105000=0.5240095\ldots$, and the touched component alone admits $(\log X)^{1003/1000}$. At $y=X^{267/400+\eta}$, the complementary very-high-prime sector has an exact affine Gram form; low-sign averaging gives a second, pure Gram synthesis over equal residual labels. The remaining collisions lie on the fixed-determinant ladders $rp-dj\ell=h$ and have coherent physical Möbius sign. The closest dispersion and Kloosterman-fraction results reviewed here do not supply the resulting fixed-$h$, three-weight, vector-valued interface. No Möbius cancellation estimate, parity breach, prime-pair asymptotic, or twin-prime theorem is claimed.

## The first positive result: a physical prime band

###### ref-2593e7c6384600de

###### ref-4870794f5206b5e3

###### ref-f9a8aef2ba5b3739

Let $N_-$ and $N_+$ be the minimum and maximum positive equality targets $n_{\alpha,j}$, and let $\ell_+$ be the largest active source prime. Define the packet-dependent band $$\mathcal P_{\mathrm{bal}}  :=\left\{p\text{ prime}:    \sqrt{N_+}<p\le \frac{N_-}{\ell_+}\right\}.  \label{eq:tpc45-intro-balanced-band}$$ At the endpoint it occupies, up to fixed support margins, the exponent window $$\frac12<\frac{\log p}{\log X}  <1-\frac{99979}{210000}  =\frac{110021}{210000};  \label{eq:tpc45-intro-balanced-window}$$ its width is $$\frac{5021}{210000}=0.0239095\ldots.  \label{eq:tpc45-intro-band-width}$$ This is a genuinely growing prime interval, not a finite set of coordinates.

###### ref-b0a32912302f323e

###### ref-da6a8805589ca618

###### ref-6b2117020b8182f5

###### ref-487de3b835a4a493

If $p\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{bal}}$ divides an active target, write $$n_{\alpha,j}=pr,  \qquad s=d_\alpha r.  \label{eq:tpc45-intro-cofactor}$$ The prime $p$ is unique, $r\ge\ell_+$, and $$\mu(u_{\alpha,j})=\mu(ps)=-\mu(s).  \label{eq:tpc45-intro-sign-collapse}$$ Thus setting every sign in $\mathcal P_{\mathrm{bal}}$ to its physical value deletes that prime exactly from the character. Equal residual labels have at most $$O_h(\tau(s))=X^{o(1)}  \label{eq:tpc45-intro-fiber-bound}$$ preimages in a fixed Hilbert coordinate. The proof is elementary but uses the actual equation $$rp-d_\alpha j\ell_\alpha=h.  \label{eq:tpc45-intro-determinant}$$ After grouping collisions, the new diagonal is at most $X^{o(1)}\mathcal D_0$.

###### ref-21005e9d4ff965ae

###### ref-fdf1b64b640158c3

Combining this descent with the robust-face theorem of TPC–44 yields our principal de-randomization statement. For a density $1-o(1)$ set of the remaining prime-sign environments, all signs in the entire band $\mathcal P_{\mathrm{bal}}$ may be fixed simultaneously to $-1$, while all signs in [\[eq:tpc45-intro-old-cutoff\]](TPC-045.md#ref-4ad350c2c2e425e1) may still be overwritten arbitrarily, and the resulting equality energy remains within the inherited $K_B=X^{1/400+o(1)}$ budget. This is the first step in this chain that physically freezes a growing large-prime band. More generally, if $R=X^{\theta+o(1)}$ and $z=(\log X)^A$, the sufficient frontier is $$(\lambda_L-\theta)_+  +\lambda_0(1-1/A)<\frac1{400}.  \label{eq:tpc45-intro-tradeoff}$$ For example, retaining $A=501/500$ and taking $R=\ell_+X^{-1/10000}$ extends the upper band exponent to $$1-\lambda_L+\frac1{10000}  =\frac{55021}{105000}=0.5240095\ldots,  \label{eq:tpc45-intro-budgeted-endpoint}$$ with exact unused margin $577/26302500$. These statements do not freeze the intervening or still larger primes.

## Two complementary large-prime normal forms

The band theorem is most effective when the cofactor is at least the source-prime length. Two further exact statements locate what happens on either side of that balance.

###### ref-8bc652e881b9e0ca

###### ref-7537435a6a58c653

###### ref-24cfc1d9207c99bc

First, the touched residual labels satisfy $$s\le D_0\sqrt{N_+}  =X^{\lambda_1+o(1)},  \qquad  \lambda_1  =\frac12+\frac{10049}{52500}  =\frac{36299}{52500}.  \label{eq:tpc45-intro-short-label}$$ For the touched component alone, TPC–44’s Rankin projection argument therefore admits every $$1<A<  A_1^*:=\left(1-\frac1{400\lambda_1}\right)^{-1}  =\frac{145196}{144671}.  \label{eq:tpc45-intro-A-star}$$ We choose $A=1003/1000$. The exact endpoint margin is $$\frac1{400}-\lambda_1\left(1-\frac{1000}{1003}\right)  =\frac{30329}{70210000}>0.  \label{eq:tpc45-intro-new-gap}$$ This improves the robust low-prime cutoff of the shortened component to $(\log X)^{1003/1000}$. Untouched labels retain their original size, so this larger cutoff is not asserted for the full packet.

###### ref-d249cc9165dea6b9

###### ref-000c9c0152f4e69b

Second, fix $\eta>0$ and move to $$y=X^{267/400+\eta}.  \label{eq:tpc45-intro-affine-threshold}$$ Every equality label then contains either no prime above $y$ or one such prime, and fixed $(j,p)$ meets at most one source row in each source system. With all lower signs placed at their physical values, the complete equality packet becomes $$Z_0(-\mathbf 1_{\le y},\varepsilon_{>y})  =\left\|v_0+\sum_{p>y}\varepsilon_pv_p\right\|^2.  \label{eq:tpc45-intro-affine-form}$$ Thus the large-prime Walsh hypergraph is exactly a Gram graph: its degree is at most two, and the general coefficient descent of TPC–44 reduces to correlations among the vectors $v_p$.

###### ref-8ef5d9d4e46769d2

Averaging, rather than fixing, the signs below $y$ gives an even sharper rough projection. For explicitly lifted residual vectors $x_p$, $$\mathbb E_{\le y}Z_0(\varepsilon_{\le y},-\mathbf 1_{>y})  =\mathcal D_0-\mathcal D_H+   \left\|\sum_{p>y}x_p\right\|^2,  \qquad  \mathcal D_H=\sum_{p>y}\|x_p\|^2\le\mathcal D_0.  \label{eq:tpc45-intro-pure-Gram}$$ The remaining off-diagonal collisions are exactly equal deleted-label fibers. Writing $n=pr$, each atom on one such fiber lies on [\[eq:tpc45-intro-determinant\]](TPC-045.md#ref-487de3b835a4a493). When $r\ll L_0$, the ambient two-prime ladder has length $O_h(1+L_0/r)$, and all atoms with the same residual label have the same Möbius sign. On every realized fiber, Cauchy–Schwarz is sharp for arbitrary deterministic coefficients; the ambient ladder bound need not be attained.

## What is and is not proved

The unconditional advances are structural and de-randomizing:

1.  the actual coefficients are expanded without an auxiliary sign assumption;

2.  a full mesoscopic band of prime signs is fixed physically at subpolynomial diagonal cost;

3.  the shortened component receives a stronger low-prime cutoff;

4.  the very-large-prime part becomes an exact affine Gram graph;

5.  the remaining small-cofactor obstruction is identified as a fixed-determinant, coefficient-specific synthesis estimate.

We do *not* prove the synthesis estimate in [\[eq:tpc45-intro-pure-Gram\]](TPC-045.md#ref-8ef5d9d4e46769d2); we do not deduce the complete physical equality bound; and we do not obtain a prime-pair asymptotic, a parity breakthrough, or a bounded-gap statement. # The actual equality coefficient interface

This section removes an ambiguity that is harmless in an abstract Walsh argument but not at the present coefficient-specific gate. The Walsh amplitudes are exactly the TPC terminal atoms after the two Möbius values in each row have been stripped; they are not new free coefficients.

Hence both sides vanish. Since $h\ne0$, first $d_\rho=d_\sigma$, then $\ell_\rho=\ell_\sigma$.

> **Proposition: Complete original-variable formula for $C_X$** \[prop:tpc45-C-original-variables\] For $\kappa>1$, the scale-dependent Walsh coefficient is $$C_X(\kappa)=C_X^L(\kappa)+C_X^R(\kappa),  \label{eq:tpc45-C-sides}$$ where $$\begin{aligned}  C_X^L(\kappa)  &:={}  \sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal A_2}\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}  \sum_{\substack{\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal A_1,\ \alpha\ne\beta\\                    \kappa_{\alpha,\beta,j}=\kappa}}  B^L_{\alpha;\gamma,j}  \overline{B^L_{\beta;\gamma,j}},  \label{eq:tpc45-C-left}\\  C_X^R(\kappa)  &:={}  \sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal A_1}\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}  \sum_{\substack{\gamma,\delta\in\mathcal A_2,\ \gamma\ne\delta\\                    \kappa_{\gamma,\delta,j}=\kappa}}  B^R_{\gamma;\alpha,j}  \overline{B^R_{\delta;\alpha,j}}.  \label{eq:tpc45-C-right}\end{aligned}$$ Both inner sums are ordered. > **Proof** Expand every squared coordinate in [\[eq:tpc45-Z-and-diagonal\]](TPC-045.md#ref-ef8832f20acee309). The diagonal row pairs give $\mathcal D_0$. ## Restriction matching in original variables

###### ref-cf9852c9ba53650e

###### ref-aaedb068509b8d7b

Let $p_1<\cdots<p_R$ be the active primes of the centered Walsh spectrum, $P_r=\prod_{t\le r}p_t$, and extend $C_X$ by $C_X(1)=0$. As in TPC–44, put $$C_r(m):=\sum_{a\mid P_r}\mu(a)C_X(am),  \qquad (m,P_r)=1.  \label{eq:tpc45-Cr}$$ To expand its correlation, let $\mathscr E_X$ be the disjoint union of all ordered unequal row pairs in \[[eq:tpc45-C-left](TPC-045.md#ref-41a120042055e501),[eq:tpc45-C-right](TPC-045.md#ref-aa7e19d79b3ec39b)\]. For $e\in\mathscr E_X$, write $$w(e):=b_{i,u_{e,1}}\overline{b_{i,u_{e,2}}},  \qquad  \kappa(e):=\operatorname{sf}(u_{e,1}u_{e,2}).  \label{eq:tpc45-cross-atom}$$ The two labels inside one $e$ share its side, output coordinate, and orbit time. Two different atoms $e,f$, however, need not share any of those data.

> **Proposition: Four-label matching formula** \[prop:tpc45-four-label-matching\] For $p=p_r$, the TPC–44 paired coefficient sum has the exact form $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal T_r  &:={}  \sum_{(m,P_r)=1}C_{r-1}(m)\overline{C_{r-1}(pm)}  \notag\\  &=  \sum_{c\mid P_{r-1}}\mu(c)  \sum_{\substack{e,f\in\mathscr E_X\\                   p\nmid\kappa(e),\ p\mid\kappa(f)\\                   \operatorname{sf}(u_{e,1}u_{e,2}u_{f,1}u_{f,2})=pc}}  w(e)\overline{w(f)}.  \label{eq:tpc45-four-label-matching}\end{aligned}$$ Equivalently, every summand lies on an exact almost-square equation $$u_{e,1}u_{e,2}u_{f,1}u_{f,2}=z^2pc,  \qquad c\mid P_{r-1},  \label{eq:tpc45-four-label-almost-square}$$ for an integer $z$. In row variables, the left side is a product of four factors of the form $$d_\rho(\ell_\rho d_\rho j+h),  \label{eq:tpc45-four-label-row-factor}$$ with one common orbit time inside each of the two ordered pairs, but not necessarily between the pairs.

###### ref-7b4ef7be7b7416be

###### ref-156f035515cc40ce

###### ref-1fda3c81d5d2239b

> **Proof** Insert [\[eq:tpc45-Cr\]](TPC-045.md#ref-cf9852c9ba53650e) twice: $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal T_r  =\sum_m\sum_{a,b\mid P_{r-1}}\mu(a)\mu(b)  \sum_{\kappa(e)=am}\sum_{\kappa(f)=bpm}  w(e)\overline{w(f)}.  \label{eq:tpc45-paired-expanded-ab}\end{aligned}$$ Here $a,b,p,m$ have the coprimalities forced by their displayed factorizations. Put $c=\operatorname{sf}(ab)=ab/(a,b)^2$. Then $c\mid P_{r-1}$, $$\mu(a)\mu(b)=\mu(c),  \qquad  \operatorname{sf}\bigl((am)(bpm)\bigr)=pc.  \label{eq:tpc45-ab-to-c}$$ Conversely, the oriented conditions in [\[eq:tpc45-four-label-matching\]](TPC-045.md#ref-b9c15119bd53eb83) say that the two kernels have the same part outside $P_r$, while precisely the second contains $p$. They therefore recover unique $a,b,m$ in [\[eq:tpc45-paired-expanded-ab\]](TPC-045.md#ref-7b4ef7be7b7416be). Finally, squarefree reduction is addition of prime valuations modulo two, so $$\operatorname{sf}(\kappa(e)\kappa(f))  =\operatorname{sf}(u_{e,1}u_{e,2}u_{f,1}u_{f,2}).  \label{eq:tpc45-sf-associative}$$ This proves both formulas.

The sign $\mu(c)$ in [\[eq:tpc45-four-label-matching\]](TPC-045.md#ref-b9c15119bd53eb83) is supplied only by the previously frozen primes. Neither $w(e)$ nor $w(f)$ has a prescribed sign. Consequently, an estimate for $\mathcal T_r$ requires cancellation or an angle in this actual weighted four-label matching problem; label incidence alone does not determine its sign.

# Prime incidence in the actual fused labels

We next separate source-divisor incidence from target-affine incidence. This distinction is invisible in a generic squarefree label model and is the first arithmetic gain obtained by returning to [\[eq:tpc45-actual-label\]](TPC-045.md#ref-dc00b7356ce7364d).

###### ref-13b62a53f015b803

###### ref-20e6c1beefd9d6c9

Write the fixed dyadic supports as $$D_-\le d_\rho\le D_+,  \quad  M_-\le m_\rho\le M_+,  \quad  J_-\le j\le J_+,  \quad  N_-\le n_{\rho,j}\le N_+,  \label{eq:tpc45-support-endpoints}$$ where $$D_\pm\asymp D_0,  \quad M_\pm\asymp Q,  \quad J_\pm\asymp J,  \quad N_\pm\asymp X.  \label{eq:tpc45-support-scales}$$ Put $\Delta_M=M_+-M_-\ll Q$.

###### ref-ec276afef8d4c5ee

###### ref-58cb4f94e4046e80

> **Lemma: Exact source/target alternative** \[lem:tpc45-source-target-incidence\] Let $p>|h|$ be prime. For a nonzero equality atom, $p\mid u_{\rho,j}$ holds precisely in one of the following disjoint ways:
>
> 1.  $p\mid d_\rho$, in which case $n_{\rho,j}\equiv h\pmod p$ and $p\nmid n_{\rho,j}$;
>
> 2.  $p\nmid d_\rho m_\rho$ and $$j\equiv-hm_\rho^{-1}\pmod p,   \label{eq:tpc45-j-incidence-congruence}$$ equivalently $p\mid n_{\rho,j}$.
>
> In particular, the source prime $\ell_\rho$ does not divide its own label once $\ell_\rho>|h|$.

> **Proof** The label is $d_\rho n_{\rho,j}$, and the two factors are coprime by [\[eq:tpc45-equality-coprimality\]](TPC-045.md#ref-f3816d0fed0428e7). If $p\mid d_\rho$, then $n_{\rho,j}=\ell_\rho d_\rho j+h\equiv h\not\equiv0\pmod p$. Otherwise incidence means $p\mid n_{\rho,j}$. A prime divisor of $m_\rho$ would again give $n_{\rho,j}\equiv h\pmod p$, so $m_\rho$ is invertible and [\[eq:tpc45-j-incidence-congruence\]](TPC-045.md#ref-58cb4f94e4046e80) follows. Taking $p=\ell_\rho$ proves the last assertion.

###### ref-c30d300b71f6aa3b

###### ref-6f6d563fc039369e

###### ref-a0268922b82eb566

###### ref-ac3f439c9635731c

> **Proposition: One-dimensional incidence fibers** \[prop:tpc45-incidence-fibers\] Let $p>\max\{|h|,D_+\}$. Then $$\begin{aligned}  \#\{\rho:p\mid u_{\rho,j}\}  &\le 1+\frac{\Delta_M}{p}  \ll 1+\frac Qp  \qquad\text{for fixed \(j\)},  \label{eq:tpc45-fixed-j-fiber}\\  \#\{j\in\mathcal J:p\mid u_{\rho,j}\}  &\ll 1+\frac Jp  \qquad\text{for fixed \(\rho\)}.  \label{eq:tpc45-fixed-row-fiber}\end{aligned}$$ Moreover, $$\#\{(\rho,j):p\mid u_{\rho,j}\}  \ll X^{o(1)}\left(1+\frac Xp\right).  \label{eq:tpc45-global-p-fiber}$$ All statements hold separately in each source-row system.

###### ref-1751cd2858ef1d47

> **Proof** Only the target alternative of [\[lem:tpc45-source-target-incidence\]](TPC-045.md#ref-ec276afef8d4c5ee) remains. If two rows occur at fixed $j$, then $p\nmid j$, since otherwise $n_{\rho,j}\equiv h\pmod p$. Hence all their distinct integers $m_\rho$ occupy the one class $-hj^{-1}\pmod p$ inside an interval of length $\Delta_M$, proving [\[eq:tpc45-fixed-j-fiber\]](TPC-045.md#ref-6f6d563fc039369e). For a fixed row, [\[eq:tpc45-j-incidence-congruence\]](TPC-045.md#ref-58cb4f94e4046e80) places $j$ in one class in an interval of length $O(J)$, proving [\[eq:tpc45-fixed-row-fiber\]](TPC-045.md#ref-a0268922b82eb566).
>
> For the last bound, write $$n_{\rho,j}=pr,  \qquad  m_\rho j=pr-h.  \label{eq:tpc45-p-r-factorization}$$ There are $O(1+X/p)$ possible positive cofactors $r$. For fixed $(p,r)$, every possible $m_\rho$ divides $pr-h$; the orbit time is then $(pr-h)/m_\rho$, and source separation recovers at most one row from $m_\rho$. The uniform divisor bound $\tau(pr-h)=X^{o(1)}$ proves [\[eq:tpc45-global-p-fiber\]](TPC-045.md#ref-ac3f439c9635731c).

The proposition gives the following genuine three-stage geometry beyond the logarithmic cutoff of TPC–44:

1.  for $p\lesssim D_0$, a source divisor $p\mid d_\rho$ can persist through every orbit time of a row;

2.  for $D_0\ll p\lesssim Q$, incidence is purely affine, with fixed-time multiplicity $O(1+Q/p)$; and

3.  for $p\gg Q$, fixed $(j,p)$ meets at most one row.

Also, once $p\gg J$, a fixed row meets at most one orbit time.

## Balanced and very-high cofactors are complementary

# Exact cofactor descent on a global prime band

We begin with a prime band defined from the whole equality packet, not from one selected row. This distinction is necessary because the sign attached to a prime is global: the same prime cannot be frozen in one occurrence and left random in another. In this section and in the determinant-ladder discussion we denote a band prime by $q$; the generic incidence and Gram sections denote the same prime role by $p$.

Put $$n_{\alpha,j}=\ell_\alpha d_\alpha j+h,  \qquad  u_{\alpha,j}=d_\alpha n_{\alpha,j}.  \label{eq:tpc45-n-u}$$ On the nonzero support, $d_\alpha$ and $n_{\alpha,j}$ are squarefree and $$(d_\alpha,n_{\alpha,j})=(d_\alpha,h)=1.  \label{eq:tpc45-source-target-coprime}$$ Use the global target endpoints $N_\pm$ from the actual incidence interface, chosen as the extrema of the nonzero support, and define $$\ell_+:=\max_\alpha\ell_\alpha.  \label{eq:tpc45-support-extrema}$$ Empty packets are harmless, so we suppose $\mathfrak A_X\ne\varnothing$. > **Lemma: Unique large prime and controlled cofactor** \[lem:tpc45-unique-balanced-cofactor\] Suppose that $q\in\mathcal P_R$ divides a nonzero target $n_{\alpha,j}$, and write $$n_{\alpha,j}=qr.  \label{eq:tpc45-target-q-r}$$ Then $q$ is the only prime from $\mathcal P_R$ dividing that target, and $$R\le r<\sqrt{N_+}.  \label{eq:tpc45-balanced-r-range}$$ Moreover $$s:=d_\alpha r  \label{eq:tpc45-residual-label}$$ is squarefree, $(q,s)=1$, and $$u_{\alpha,j}=qs.  \label{eq:tpc45-u-q-s}$$

> **Proposition: Exact global-band character descent** \[prop:tpc45-character-descent\] Let $\varepsilon$ denote the signs outside $\mathcal P_R$. > **Proof** Untouched atoms give both identities trivially. The use of the full-support quantities $N_-$ and $R$ in [\[eq:tpc45-global-cofactor-band\]](TPC-045.md#ref-67bd6f1b4636ef9d) is essential. The uniform inequality $r\ge R$ for every occurrence is what makes the compression in the next section valid for the whole packet. The clean choice $R=\ell_+$ is the first scale at which the fiber cost below is subpolynomial without additional cancellation.

# Cofactor-band compression and the balanced clean range

We now prove a quantitative fiber theorem for the full family $\mathcal P_R$. The balanced choice $R=\ell_+$ is its subpolynomial clean case.

# Two exact very-high-prime Gram forms

There are two natural operations on the signs below the cutoff $y$: one may set them to their physical value $-1$, or one may average them. The first operation retains every cross-label inner product and gives an affine polynomial in the high signs. The second keeps only equal residual-label fibers and gives a pure quadratic Gram synthesis. These are different identities in different Hilbert spaces.

###### ref-34635511fdb47d4a

The degree-two reduction also simplifies the TPC–44 coefficient correlation. Process the primes in $\mathcal P_y$ increasingly, after all low signs have already been restricted. Just before processing $p$, put $$w_p:=v_0-\sum_{\substack{q\in\mathcal P_y\\q<p}}v_q.  \label{eq:tpc45-wp}$$ The surviving coefficients are $$\begin{aligned}  c_\varnothing^{(p)}  &=\|w_p\|^2+\sum_{q\ge p}\|v_q\|^2-\mathcal D_0,  \label{eq:tpc45-current-c0}\\  c_q^{(p)}&=2\operatorname{Re}\langle w_p,v_q\rangle  \qquad(q\ge p),  \label{eq:tpc45-current-cq}\\  c_{qr}^{(p)}&=2\operatorname{Re}\langle v_q,v_r\rangle  \qquad(p\le q<r).  \label{eq:tpc45-current-cqr}\end{aligned}$$ Let $C_{p^-}(m)$ denote the Walsh coefficient of $\chi_m$ in this current centered polynomial after all lower signs and all $q\in\mathcal P_y$ with $q<p$ have been fixed to $-1$; the index $m=1$ denotes the constant coefficient $C_{p^-}(1)=c_\varnothing^{(p)}$. Hence the general sum over residual kernels is exactly $$\boxed{  \sum_m C_{p^-}(m)\overline{C_{p^-}(pm)}  =c_\varnothing^{(p)}c_p^{(p)}   +\sum_{q>p}c_q^{(p)}c_{pq}.}  \label{eq:tpc45-top-paired-collapse}$$ Indeed, a residual $m$ with at least two prime factors would make $pm$ have degree at least three, whose coefficient vanishes.

## Averaged low signs: the residual-label Gram form

###### ref-3e92cdfe1fe9a0f7

###### ref-5b38c49b9c246896

###### ref-4483cd02a3c41a36

Now use a different Hilbert space, $$\mathcal H_{\rm res}  :=\ell^2\bigl\{(i,s):  i\in\mathcal I_L\sqcup\mathcal I_R,  \ s\text{ squarefree},\ P^{+}(s)\le y\bigr\}.  \label{eq:tpc45-residual-Hilbert-space}$$ For $p\in\mathcal P_y$, define $$(x_p)_{i,s}:=  \begin{cases}   b_{i,sp},&sp\in\mathcal U_i,\\   0,&sp\notin\mathcal U_i.  \end{cases}  \label{eq:tpc45-xp}$$ Let $$\mathcal D_H:=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_y}\sum_i         \sum_{\substack{u\in\mathcal U_i\\u_{>y}=p}}|b_{i,u}|^2.  \label{eq:tpc45-DH}$$

###### ref-3dba395b3f6c42ad

###### ref-089946ef8a9f3235

###### ref-7fa2d3edcc293cb5

###### ref-459515120f616355

> **Theorem: Low-averaged pure Gram identity** \[thm:tpc45-low-averaged-Gram\] Assume both cutoff conditions [\[eq:tpc45-y-geometric-conditions\]](TPC-045.md#ref-895a6afbfa473222) and [\[eq:tpc45-y-label-separation\]](TPC-045.md#ref-93e801267dc79863). Then $$\boxed{  \mathbb E_{\varepsilon_q:q\le y}  Z_0(\varepsilon_{\le y},-\mathbf 1_{>y})  =\mathcal D_0-\mathcal D_H+   \left\|\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_y}x_p\right\|_{\mathcal H_{\rm res}}^2.}  \label{eq:tpc45-low-averaged-Gram}$$ Moreover, $$\sum_p\|x_p\|^2=\mathcal D_H,  \qquad  \|x_p\|^2=\|v_p\|^2,  \label{eq:tpc45-xp-diagonal}$$ and therefore $$\mathbb E_{\le y}Z_0(\varepsilon_{\le y},-\mathbf 1_{>y})  =\mathcal D_0+2\operatorname{Re}   \sum_{p<q}\langle x_p,x_q\rangle.  \label{eq:tpc45-pure-quadratic-Gram}$$ There is no linear term in [\[eq:tpc45-pure-quadratic-Gram\]](TPC-045.md#ref-459515120f616355).

###### ref-eb680af315c80d30

###### ref-05aeeb040108f7ec

> **Proof** Fix an output coordinate $i$. A label with no prime above $y$ is its own low Walsh label $u$, and distinct such full labels are orthogonal under the low-sign average. A label $sp$ with $p>y$ has low Walsh label $s$, while its fixed high character contributes the sign $-1$.
>
> No no-high full label can have the same low label as a high-prime residual. Indeed, by [\[eq:tpc45-support-endpoints\]](TPC-045.md#ref-13b62a53f015b803), a no-high label has size at least $D_-N_-$, whereas $$s=\frac{d_\rho n_{\rho,j}}p  \le\frac{D_+N_+}{y}<D_-N_-  \label{eq:tpc45-residual-size-separation}$$ for a high-prime label. Thus low Walsh orthogonality leaves the atomic energy $\mathcal D_0-\mathcal D_H$ from the no-high labels and, for every low label $s$, the coefficient $$-\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_y}b_{i,sp}  \label{eq:tpc45-matched-high-coefficient}$$ from the high-prime labels. Squaring [\[eq:tpc45-matched-high-coefficient\]](TPC-045.md#ref-05aeeb040108f7ec) and summing $(i,s)$ gives [\[eq:tpc45-low-averaged-Gram\]](TPC-045.md#ref-089946ef8a9f3235). Unique high-prime factorization partitions the high atoms, proving the first identity in [\[eq:tpc45-xp-diagonal\]](TPC-045.md#ref-7fa2d3edcc293cb5). Fixed-$(j,p)$ singleton incidence shows that [\[eq:tpc45-vp\]](TPC-045.md#ref-2fdb8effa2d9ec1e) contains at most one term per output coordinate; multiplication by $\mu(s)$ preserves its modulus, proving the second. Expanding the last norm now gives [\[eq:tpc45-pure-quadratic-Gram\]](TPC-045.md#ref-459515120f616355).

###### ref-22fda37f90988a7b

###### ref-8867ddafe834b612

The Möbius factors suppressed in the stripped definition of $x_p$ may be restored without changing the synthesis norm. Indeed, the diagonal map $$(Uy)_{i,s}:=\mu(s)y_{i,s}  \label{eq:tpc45-residual-unitary-reweighting}$$ is unitary on $\mathcal H_{\rm res}$, and on every occurring factorization $s=dr$ one has $\mu(s)=\mu(d)\mu(r)$. Consequently $$\left\|\sum_p x_p\right\|  =\left\|\sum_p Ux_p\right\|.  \label{eq:tpc45-residual-physical-reweighting}$$ This is the precise bridge from the pure Gram form to the source- and cofactor-Möbius coefficient slots discussed below; it does not impose independence or cancellation on either factor.

###### ref-48626209ab2405ec

###### ref-797baf6567ee2ec0

###### ref-0d55db23ba74a338

###### ref-5a1a4fb37772c33e

The matching condition in this Gram space is completely explicit: $$\langle x_p,x_q\rangle  =\sum_i\sum_{\substack{s\\sp,sq\in\mathcal U_i}}  b_{i,sp}\overline{b_{i,sq}}.  \label{eq:tpc45-xp-xq-matching}$$ For a left coordinate, a term in [\[eq:tpc45-xp-xq-matching\]](TPC-045.md#ref-48626209ab2405ec) consists of rows $\alpha,\beta$ and cofactors $r_\alpha,r_\beta$ satisfying $$\begin{aligned}  p r_\alpha&=\ell_\alpha d_\alpha j+h,  &q r_\beta&=\ell_\beta d_\beta j+h,  \label{eq:tpc45-matching-ladders}\\  d_\alpha r_\alpha&=d_\beta r_\beta=s,  \label{eq:tpc45-residual-match}\end{aligned}$$ and its weight is $$B^L_{\alpha;\gamma,j}  \overline{B^L_{\beta;\gamma,j}}.  \label{eq:tpc45-matching-weight-left}$$ The right formula exchanges the two row systems exactly as in [\[eq:tpc45-C-right\]](TPC-045.md#ref-aa7e19d79b3ec39b).

###### ref-ebb04c54b39e3dbb

Because every active $d$ and $r$ in [\[eq:tpc45-residual-match\]](TPC-045.md#ref-0d55db23ba74a338) is squarefree and coprime, one has $$d_\alpha\mid s,  \qquad r_\alpha=s/d_\alpha,  \qquad  \ell_\alpha=\frac{ps-hd_\alpha}{d_\alpha^2j},  \label{eq:tpc45-matching-row-recovery}$$ and the same formula with $(q,d_\beta)$. Thus a fixed $(i,s,p)$-fiber has at most $\tau(s)$ candidate source divisors; integrality, primality, dyadic support, and the actual mask only delete candidates. This is the exact matching mechanism behind the residual Gram space.

###### ref-5d7c2da4aa11595d

> **Remark: The two Gram matrices must not be identified** \[rem:tpc45-two-Grams-distinct\] The physical-low coefficient $\langle v_p,v_q\rangle_{\mathcal H_{\rm out}}$ sums every pair of $p$- and $q$-rows sharing an output coordinate and weights it by $\mu(s_p)\mu(s_q)$. The low-averaged coefficient $\langle x_p,x_q\rangle_{\mathcal H_{\rm res}}$ retains only the exact matches $s_p=s_q$; on such a match the two low Möbius signs multiply to one. Although $\|v_p\|=\|x_p\|$, their cross inner products are different. The affine form [\[eq:tpc45-affine-energy\]](TPC-045.md#ref-07271ea0c023a470) and the pure Gram form [\[eq:tpc45-low-averaged-Gram\]](TPC-045.md#ref-089946ef8a9f3235) therefore serve different descent routes.

###### ref-bbe801a65a49dbd9

The remaining estimate in either route is coefficient-specific. In the physical-low form it is an angle bound for the actual vectors $v_p$, hence for the signed determinant sums [\[eq:tpc45-cpq-actual-left\]](TPC-045.md#ref-3b7a0384398d0f51). In the low-averaged form it is a Bessel bound for the matching synthesis $$\left\|\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_y}x_p\right\|^2  \label{eq:tpc45-residual-synthesis-gate}$$ on the ladders [\[eq:tpc45-matching-ladders\]](TPC-045.md#ref-797baf6567ee2ec0)– [\[eq:tpc45-residual-match\]](TPC-045.md#ref-0d55db23ba74a338). Singleton prime incidence and the diagonal identity [\[eq:tpc45-xp-diagonal\]](TPC-045.md#ref-7fa2d3edcc293cb5) do not by themselves control either coherent sum.

# The unbalanced fixed-determinant ladders

The upper endpoint $N_-/\ell_+$ in [\[eq:tpc45-balanced-band\]](TPC-045.md#ref-c1d29803c829e27e) is the natural boundary of the fiber argument. We now retain only the uniqueness condition $q>\sqrt{N_+}$ and allow the cofactor $r=n/q$ to be smaller than the source-prime scale.

###### ref-e45e07eee7a2066a

###### ref-425ad5bacfba34bf

###### ref-02a6391a35caaffc

Fix an orbit time $j$, a residual label $s$, and a divisor $d\mid s$. Put $r=s/d$. Every row that deletes to $s$ solves $$rq-dj\ell=h.  \label{eq:tpc45-unbalanced-determinant}$$ Let $g=(r,dj)$. If $g\nmid h$, there are no such rows. Otherwise, for any one integral solution $(q_0,\ell_0)$, the complete ladder is $$\boxed{  q=q_0+\frac{dj}{g}t,  \qquad  \ell=\ell_0+\frac r g t,  \qquad t\in\mathbb Z.}  \label{eq:tpc45-full-determinant-ladder}$$ The two affine forms have fixed nonzero cross determinant $$\frac{dj}{g}\ell_0-\frac r g q_0=-\frac hg.  \label{eq:tpc45-ladder-cross-determinant}$$ In the physical packet both $q$ and $\ell$ must be prime, in their respective support windows. This primality condition deletes points from [\[eq:tpc45-full-determinant-ladder\]](TPC-045.md#ref-425ad5bacfba34bf); it does not shorten the ambient ladder available to a coefficient-blind argument.

###### ref-90cfd8ee3dca05bf

###### ref-0c6c95e55be59881

> **Proposition: Dyadic residual-fiber capacity** \[prop:tpc45-unbalanced-fiber-capacity\] Let $\mathcal R\subset[R,2R]$ be a dyadic cofactor block. For fixed $(j,s)$, the number of rows with $r=s/d\in\mathcal R$, $q>\sqrt{N_+}$, and descended label $s$ is at most $$C_h  \sum_{\substack{d\mid s\\s/d\in\mathcal R}}  \left(1+\frac{L_0d}{s}\right)  \le C_h\tau(s)\left(1+\frac{L_0}{R}\right).  \label{eq:tpc45-unbalanced-fiber-bound}$$

> **Proof** For fixed $d$, the step of the $\ell$-form in [\[eq:tpc45-full-determinant-ladder\]](TPC-045.md#ref-425ad5bacfba34bf) is $r/g$. Since $g\mid h$ and the source-prime window has length $O(L_0)$, the number of integral parameters is $$O\!\left(1+\frac{gL_0}{r}\right)  =O_h\!\left(1+\frac{L_0d}{s}\right).$$ Sum over the eligible divisors $d\mid s$, and use $r\ge R$.

###### ref-f13aa45bd19b9afd

###### ref-5e19566a320ef2a1

###### ref-5c20d3dd88c9c071

At the original equality endpoint, $$L_0=X^{99979/210000+o(1)},  \qquad  J=X^{133/400+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc45-L-J-scales}$$ At the old threshold $q\asymp Q$, where the cofactor scale is $R\asymp J$, the unsieved ladder capacity is already $$\frac{L_0}{J}  =X^{15077/105000+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc45-best-unbalanced-capacity}$$ It exceeds the endpoint allowance $K_B=X^{1/400+o(1)}$ by the fixed power $$\frac{15077}{105000}-\frac1{400}  =\frac{29629}{210000}>0.  \label{eq:tpc45-best-capacity-deficit}$$ For $r=O(1)$, the ambient ladder has length $X^{99979/210000+o(1)}$.

The parametrization also explains why a standard upper-bound sieve is not the missing theorem. It may remove logarithmic powers from the number of parameters for which both affine forms are prime, but the power-sized factor $L_0/R$ remains. More importantly, the physical coefficients on the surviving parameters are neither identical to an unweighted prime-pair count nor controlled by their cardinality. The needed input is a weighted Bessel or dispersion estimate for the actual source and mask coefficients along [\[eq:tpc45-full-determinant-ladder\]](TPC-045.md#ref-425ad5bacfba34bf).

Equation [\[eq:tpc45-unbalanced-determinant\]](TPC-045.md#ref-e45e07eee7a2066a) is a literal fixed-determinant equation, not a heuristic analogy. Nevertheless, a theorem for two arbitrary coefficient sequences and otherwise smooth weights cannot be invoked after silently inserting, in addition, the prime restrictions on $q,\ell$, the Möbius cofactor weight, and the moving source/mask coefficient. Those quantifiers form the next arithmetic interface.

# Sharp operator boundaries and the actual Walsh spectrum

The fiber loss in the preceding sections is an exact operator norm, not an artifact of Cauchy–Schwarz. We first record the finite-dimensional statement.

###### ref-d4b660ca0d8db6a2

Let $E$ be a finite atom set, let $\pi:E\to W$ be a label map, and define $$(G_\pi x)(w):=\sum_{e:\pi(e)=w}x(e).  \label{eq:tpc45-grouping-operator}$$

###### ref-8dc1c5b8c5b7bcce

###### ref-ae34636cc752c5ef

###### ref-ecfa412ee5478c41

> **Proposition: Sharp grouping norm** \[prop:tpc45-sharp-grouping-norm\] If $$\rho(\pi):=\max_w|\pi^{-1}(w)|,  \label{eq:tpc45-grouping-multiplicity}$$ then $$\boxed{  \|G_\pi\|_{\ell^2(E)\to\ell^2(W)}^2=\rho(\pi).}  \label{eq:tpc45-exact-grouping-norm}$$

> **Proof** Cauchy–Schwarz on each fiber gives the upper bound. On a fiber of maximal cardinality, take $x$ constant and nonzero and set it to zero off that fiber. The quotient of the squared norms is exactly the fiber cardinality.

###### ref-3b11610740b4d7a9

For large-prime cofactor descent, all atoms in one descended fiber have the same physical Möbius phase. Indeed, if $s=dr$, then $$\mu(d)\mu(qr)=-\mu(dr)=-\mu(s).  \label{eq:tpc45-fiber-physical-coherence}$$ Thus Möbius does not cancel different points of a fixed-determinant ladder after their common large prime has been removed. Any power improvement over the support exponent in [\[eq:tpc45-unbalanced-fiber-bound\]](TPC-045.md#ref-0c6c95e55be59881), beyond possible logarithmic thinning from primality, must use the actual complex source/mask coefficients or cancellation between different residual labels $s$; it cannot arise from independent Möbius signs inside one fiber.

## What is proved directly about $C_X(\kappa)$

###### ref-a1745f4c348714c3

###### ref-cecda7a4d02de453

###### ref-150b5f4e2c4316ac

Let $C_X(\kappa)$ be the actual Walsh coefficient defined by $$Z_0(\varepsilon)  =\mathcal D_0+\sum_{\kappa>1}C_X(\kappa)\chi_\kappa(\varepsilon).  \label{eq:tpc45-CX-definition}$$ Every equality label contains at most one prime from $\mathcal P_{\rm bal}$. Therefore $$C_X(\kappa)=0  \quad\text{if \(\kappa\) contains at least three primes from  \(\mathcal P_{\rm bal}\)}.  \label{eq:tpc45-no-three-band-primes}$$ There is also no pure singleton coefficient: $$C_X(q)=0  \qquad(q\in\mathcal P_{\rm bal}).  \label{eq:tpc45-no-pure-singleton}$$ Indeed, a pair kernel equal to $q$ would require an untouched label $u\ge d_-N_-$ to equal a touched label with $q$ deleted, which is at most $d_+\sqrt{N_+}$. This contradicts [\[eq:tpc45-touched-untouched-separation\]](TPC-045.md#ref-3d07a553df022450).

###### ref-c7be011013d90cac

###### ref-21b20b8fe1fc6b7f

For distinct $p,q\in\mathcal P_{\rm bal}$, a pure coefficient $C_X(pq)$ can occur only when the two labels delete to the same residual label $s$. Introduce the Hilbert space with coordinates $(i,s)$, and let $x_q(i,s)$ be the coefficient of the unique row carrying $q$ and deleting to $s$, with value zero when that row is absent. Uniqueness follows because two such rows would have the same original label $qs$ in one coordinate, contrary to [\[lem:tpc45-label-injectivity\]](TPC-045.md#ref-7fb28c0d53508ea6). Then $$\begin{aligned}  C_X(pq)&=2\operatorname{Re}\langle x_p,x_q\rangle,  \label{eq:tpc45-Cpq-Gram}\\  \mathcal D_{\rm bal}  :=\sum_{q\in\mathcal P_{\rm bal}}\|x_q\|^2  &\le\mathcal D_0.  \label{eq:tpc45-band-atom-energy}\end{aligned}$$ The second line is the atomic diagonal of the band-touched atoms: equality-label injectivity makes each fixed $(i,s,q)$ entry a singleton, and the same atom cannot carry two band primes.

###### ref-ef197e80fed9c979

###### ref-4f82b7fbe76c2343

###### ref-500dc9a7a7751ca0

Averaging every prime sign outside the band leaves the exact quadratic projection $$\boxed{  \mathbb E_{\varepsilon_a:a\notin\mathcal P_{\rm bal}}Z_0(\varepsilon)  =\mathcal D_0+   \sum_{\substack{p<q\\p,q\in\mathcal P_{\rm bal}}}   C_X(pq)\varepsilon_p\varepsilon_q.}  \label{eq:tpc45-exact-band-quadratic}$$ At the physical band point this is equivalently $$\mathbb E_{a\notin\mathcal P_{\rm bal}}  Z_0(-\mathbf 1_{\mathcal P_{\rm bal}},\varepsilon)  =\mathcal D_0-\mathcal D_{\rm bal}   +\left\|\sum_{q\in\mathcal P_{\rm bal}}x_q\right\|^2.  \label{eq:tpc45-exact-band-Gram-collapse}$$ Combining this identity with [\[eq:tpc45-band-frozen-mean\]](TPC-045.md#ref-b64a781d0a50f30c) proves the actual-coefficient estimate $$\mathcal D_0+  \sum_{\substack{p<q\\p,q\in\mathcal P_{\rm bal}}}C_X(pq)  \le X^{o(1)}\mathcal D_0.  \label{eq:tpc45-pure-band-spectrum-bound}$$ This controls a genuine polynomial band of the physical rough spectrum; it is not an estimate for every kernel containing a band prime.

## The sharp continuation boundary

If the upper endpoint of $\mathcal P_{\rm bal}$ is crossed, cofactors $r<L_0$ appear and the exact norm in [\[prop:tpc45-sharp-grouping-norm\]](TPC-045.md#ref-8dc1c5b8c5b7bcce) may cost $L_0/r$. If a physical fiber contains $H$ atoms, coefficients of equal phase supported on that fiber give grouped energy $H$ times their atomic energy. This is a sharp statement conditional on the declared support; it neither asserts that the actual masks choose equal phases nor proves that a fiber of polynomial cardinality occurs.

Accordingly, the results above do not show that the residual TPC–44 correlation product is small for all high primes, and they do not show that it is large. They prove a physical freezing theorem on the balanced band and identify the first adjacent range where support and diagonal information cease to suffice. No fixed-shift Chowla estimate, prime-pair asymptotic, parity breach, or twin-prime conclusion follows.

# The present analytic boundary

The congruence and determinant reformulations above place the remaining TPC term near several established dispersion and Kloosterman–fraction estimates. The resemblance is exact at the level of the equation, but not at the level of the coefficient class or of the available exponent range. We record the distinction explicitly. In this section $Q_{\mathrm{mod}}$ denotes a modulus scale; it is not the row-length parameter used elsewhere in the paper.

## Unbalanced-convolution dispersion

###### ref-f2384b12826a7e19

###### ref-e10b5a533e150cf0

Fouvry and Radziwiłł prove distribution estimates for a convolution $\sum_{mn\equiv a\, (\bmod q)}\alpha_m\beta_n$ averaged over $q\asymp Q_{\mathrm{mod}}$, with divisor-bounded coefficients and a Siegel–Walfisz hypothesis on the short sequence [`\cite[Corollary~1.1]{FR2022}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-045.md#cite-fb39ce9a20fbe3c6). With $MN\asymp X$, their first range is $$\exp\!\bigl((\log X)^\epsilon\bigr)  \le N  \le Q_{\mathrm{mod}}^{-11/12}X^{17/36-\epsilon}.  \label{eq:tpc45-fr-first-range}$$ Their two other stated ranges have, respectively, $$\begin{aligned}   N&\le X^{7/90-\epsilon},   &Q_{\mathrm{mod}}&\le X^{53/105-\epsilon},\\   N&\le X^{101/630-\epsilon},   &Q_{\mathrm{mod}}&\le X^{53/105-\epsilon},  \end{aligned}  \label{eq:tpc45-fr-other-ranges}$$ with the further hypotheses specified there. Their prime-modulus variant removes the Siegel–Walfisz input but retains the power range in [\[eq:tpc45-fr-first-range\]](TPC-045.md#ref-f2384b12826a7e19) [`\cite[Theorem~1.2]{FR2022}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-045.md#cite-fb39ce9a20fbe3c6).

###### ref-b7e44d8b971e1b89

The algebraic match with our incidence relation is literal: $$p\mid(mj+h)  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad  mj\equiv-h\pmod p.$$ The parameter match fails, however. At the first large-prime block $p\asymp Q_{\mathrm{mod}}=X^{1/2}$, [\[eq:tpc45-fr-first-range\]](TPC-045.md#ref-f2384b12826a7e19) permits only $$N\le X^{17/36-11/24-\epsilon}    =X^{1/72-\epsilon},$$ whereas the TPC orbit variable has length $$J=X^{133/400+o(1)}.$$ The exponent deficit is $$\frac{133}{400}-\frac1{72}  =\frac{1147}{3600}.  \label{eq:tpc45-fr-exponent-deficit}$$ The alternatives in [\[eq:tpc45-fr-other-ranges\]](TPC-045.md#ref-e10b5a533e150cf0) are also shorter than $J$. Moreover, successive physical prime restrictions produce coefficient fields depending on the preceding restrictions, rather than a single fixed product $\alpha_m\beta_j$. Thus the theorem has the right congruence but neither the required length nor the required restriction-stable coefficient interface.

###### ref-be9d92ec2261658d

###### ref-63f012da306fad5c

Wright’s partially fixed-modulus Kloosterman estimate improves the first unbalanced range to $$N\le Q_{\mathrm{mod}}^{-33/28}X^{17/28-\epsilon}  \label{eq:tpc45-wright-first-range}$$ and improves the accompanying modulus cap from $53/105$ to $45/89$ in the other two cases [`\cite[Corollary~2.2]{Wright2026}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-045.md#cite-f8666ba79358b969). At $Q_{\mathrm{mod}}=X^{1/2}$, [\[eq:tpc45-wright-first-range\]](TPC-045.md#ref-be9d92ec2261658d) becomes $$N\le X^{17/28-33/56-\epsilon}    =X^{1/56-\epsilon}.$$ Consequently the gap to the TPC length is still $$\frac{133}{400}-\frac1{56}  =\frac{881}{2800}.  \label{eq:tpc45-wright-exponent-deficit}$$ The advance is genuine, but it does not enter the balanced TPC regime.

## The fixed-determinant coefficient-slot barrier

###### ref-5198d1ad733ac947

Bettin and Chandee estimate trilinear forms with Kloosterman fractions for three arbitrary coefficient sequences [`\cite[Theorem~1]{BC2018}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-045.md#cite-1d469e770d259123). Their fixed-determinant consequence concerns $$m_1n_2-m_2n_1=\Delta  \label{eq:tpc45-bc-fixed-determinant}$$ and places arbitrary arithmetic sequences on $n_1,n_2$, while the weights on $m_1,m_2$ are smooth dyadic functions with controlled derivatives [`\cite[Corollary~1]{BC2018}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-045.md#cite-1d469e770d259123). Hence this corollary has two, not three, arbitrary arithmetic coefficient slots.

###### ref-1c169d9d23046a5e

Our cofactor equation $$pt-mj=h  \label{eq:tpc45-literature-determinant}$$ has the same determinant shape. Its actual coefficient system contains the prime restriction in $p$, the Möbius weight $\mu(t)$, and the source weight $\mu(d_\rho)$ attached to $m_\rho=\ell_\rho d_\rho$, together with the inherited masks in $m_\rho$. Only the orbit variable $j$ is naturally smooth. Assigning two of $p,t,m$ to the arbitrary slots in [\[eq:tpc45-bc-fixed-determinant\]](TPC-045.md#ref-5198d1ad733ac947) therefore leaves the third nonsmooth arithmetic sequence in a slot for which the corollary requires a smooth weight. The three-sequence trilinear theorem does not by itself restore this lost slot in the fixed-$h$ determinant corollary. This is the precise *third-coefficient barrier*; it is not merely a difference of notation.

###### ref-e69d54510fe63a1c

> **Remark: A diagnostic exponent ledger, not a theorem** \[rem:tpc45-bc-ledger-not-theorem\] It is informative to make a deliberately counterfactual calculation. Suppose that two TPC variables could be replaced by admissible smooth weights and that the remaining arbitrary variables had lengths $$P=X^{267/400},\qquad T=X^{133/400},\qquad PT=X.$$ Substituting these lengths into the error term of [`\cite[Corollary~1]{BC2018}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-045.md#cite-1d469e770d259123) produces the formal exponent $$\frac12+\frac7{20}  +\frac14\frac{267}{400}  =1+\frac{27}{1600}.$$ A formal comparison with an $X^{1+1/400+o(1)}$ endpoint budget then leaves $23/1600$ in the exponent. This computation is *not an application* of the Bettin–Chandee corollary: the counterfactual smoothing discards an actual arithmetic weight, and a scalar determinant-count error is not automatically the vector Gram/Bessel energy required here. It is neither a lower bound nor an impossibility theorem. It records only that the most naive two-arbitrary-slot substitution would not close the desired exponent ledger.

## A withdrawn bilinear claim

The first version of the 2026 preprint of Dong, Robles and Zeindler announced an improved bilinear Kloosterman–fraction bound and a saving of $1/12$ over the trivial estimate in the balanced case [`\cite[Theorems~1.4--1.6]{DRZ2026}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-045.md#cite-97c9af6214dba857). The authors subsequently withdrew the paper. The withdrawal notice states that a missing factor in one equation changes $L^5$ to $L^7$, so the argument no longer yields the claimed improved bound [`\cite{DRZ2026}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-045.md#cite-97c9af6214dba857). We therefore do not use the announced $1/12$ saving as a theorem or as an input anywhere in this paper.

There is a second, logically independent boundary. Even if the announced bilinear estimate were available, it concerns a form with two arbitrary sequences and a Kloosterman phase. No fixed-$h$ determinant corollary with the three arithmetic weights in [\[eq:tpc45-literature-determinant\]](TPC-045.md#ref-1c169d9d23046a5e) is supplied. Thus that announced estimate would improve a possible analytic subroutine, but would not by itself cross the third-coefficient interface.

## Why the Bourgain–Sarnak–Ziegler criterion is not a black box

The Bourgain–Sarnak–Ziegler criterion gives a powerful route from prime-dilate decorrelation to multiplicative orthogonality. In one form, if $|F|\le1$, $|\nu|\le1$ is multiplicative, and $$\left|\sum_{m\le M}F(p_1m)\overline{F(p_2m)}\right|  \le \tau M$$ uniformly for the relevant distinct primes $p_1,p_2$, then $$\left|\sum_{n\le N}\nu(n)F(n)\right|  \le 2\sqrt{\tau\log(1/\tau)}\,N$$ for sufficiently large parameters [`\cite[Theorem~2]{BSZ2013}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-045.md#cite-b44aca48ad89da75).

This criterion identifies the right qualitative phenomenon, but it is not directly applicable to the TPC coefficient. Here the coefficient is sparse and depends on the scale $X$; its normalization is governed by a projective energy rather than by the ambient interval length. More importantly, after a physical restriction at one prime, the coefficient used at the next prime is a new restricted field, so the needed correlations involve restriction-dependent pairs rather than the dilates of one fixed bounded function $F$. Dividing by an $\ell^\infty$ norm and applying the criterion would return an ambient-length estimate, not the endpoint Gram-energy bound. A usable BSZ-type reduction would therefore first require a new restriction-stable, energy-normalized prime-dilate theorem; the cited criterion does not supply it.

## Exact frontier statement

The preceding comparisons do not show that [\[eq:tpc45-literature-determinant\]](TPC-045.md#ref-1c169d9d23046a5e) is intractable. They show only that the following interface is absent from the cited black boxes:

1.  the shift $h$ is fixed rather than averaged;

2.  the prime, cofactor-Möbius, and source-Möbius/mask weights remain simultaneously present;

3.  the estimate is stable under the projective and physical restrictions inherited from the TPC packet; and

4.  the output controls a vector-valued Bessel/Gram synthesis norm, not only a scalar congruence discrepancy.

Any future positive input must address these four points explicitly. An estimate averaged over $h$, or one obtained after replacing a nonsmooth coefficient by a smooth weight, may be valuable in its own right but does not close the fixed physical TPC gate.

# The next gate

The preceding descent separates three tasks that should not be merged under the phrase “Möbius randomness.” We formulate them at the level of the actual coefficient packet.

## A block synthesis estimate

###### ref-4089ca609fa64be3

Let $\mathcal F_{i,s}(R)$ denote one residual fiber of atom indices $e$ with cofactor $r\asymp R$, and let $b_{i,e}$ be the inherited complex atom. The support argument gives $$\sum_{i,s}  \left|\sum_{e\in\mathcal F_{i,s}(R)}b_{i,e}\right|^2  \ll_h X^{o(1)}\left(1+\frac{L_0}{R}\right)  \sum_{i,e}|b_{i,e}|^2.  \label{eq:tpc45-support-block-synthesis}$$ The squared norm of the exact grouping operator is the actual maximal fiber size. Thus [\[eq:tpc45-support-block-synthesis\]](TPC-045.md#ref-4089ca609fa64be3) is sharp when its ambient upper bound is replaced by that maximal size; the displayed upper bound need not itself be attained. A power improvement below the actual support frontier must therefore use the source phases, opposite-row masks, or cancellation between different residual labels.

###### ref-172f7866ba8765a3

The natural positive target is a coefficient-specific replacement $$\sum_{i,s}  \left|\sum_{e\in\mathcal F_{i,s}(R)}b_{i,e}\right|^2  \ll X^{o(1)}\mathcal B(R)  \sum_{i,e}|b_{i,e}|^2,  \label{eq:tpc45-target-block-Bessel}$$ where $\mathcal B(R)$ is strictly smaller than $1+L_0/R$ on at least one new cofactor range. Even a small power improvement would enlarge the physical band in [6](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-045.md#src-9d3dd0e16f8f9aef). Estimate [\[eq:tpc45-target-block-Bessel\]](TPC-045.md#ref-172f7866ba8765a3) is a target, not an assumption used in any unconditional theorem above.

## The very-large-prime Bessel gate

###### ref-b19c45e3f1a499d6

###### ref-53b51fae8e726c66

For the low-averaged rough projection, the exact missing estimate is $$\boxed{  \left\|\sum_{p>y}x_p\right\|^2  \ll X^{o(1)}K_B        \sum_{p>y}\|x_p\|^2.}  \label{eq:tpc45-target-high-Bessel}$$ Together with the identity in [7](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-045.md#src-f418c705f951f588), this would place that projected sector within the endpoint budget. It would not by itself fix the signs below $y$. For the complete physical affine face, a sufficient pair of estimates is $$\begin{aligned}  \|v_0\|^2  &\ll X^{o(1)}K_B\mathcal D_0,  \label{eq:tpc45-target-base-vector}\\  \left\|\sum_{p>y}v_p\right\|^2  &\ll X^{o(1)}K_B\mathcal D_0.  \label{eq:tpc45-target-physical-synthesis}\end{aligned}$$ Indeed the triangle inequality then bounds $\|v_0-\sum_pv_p\|^2$. The first line is a base-sector problem and the second is the physical high-prime synthesis problem. Neither follows from the pure rough projection, because lower-sign averaging deletes their cross terms.

## The required determinant input

###### ref-b9e3a81b3fddfdd1

After dyadic decomposition in the cofactor and source variables, a candidate analytic theorem would have to estimate the fixed equation $$rp-dj\ell=h  \label{eq:tpc45-next-fixed-determinant}$$ with all of the following features retained:

1.  $p$ and $\ell$ are prime;

2.  the cofactor and source-divisor signs contribute Möbius weights rather than smooth cutoffs;

3.  the actual projective mask remains in the coefficient;

4.  $h$ is fixed;

5.  the estimate is vector-valued and stable under the preceding physical prime restrictions; and

6.  the final normalization is the atomic energy, not the ambient number of integer solutions.

The Bettin–Chandee fixed-determinant corollary reviewed here lacks one of these arithmetic coefficient slots; see [10](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-045.md#src-9f737ad16c2d5175). Proving a theorem with all six features is the direct route to [\[eq:tpc45-target-block-Bessel\]](TPC-045.md#ref-172f7866ba8765a3). Proving it after averaging $h$, smoothing one arithmetic slot, or restricting to a favorable cofactor block would still be a meaningful weaker theorem, but it would have to be labelled accordingly.

## A reversible research program

The exact descent leaves several logically independent doors.

1.  Prove a power saving over [\[eq:tpc45-support-block-synthesis\]](TPC-045.md#ref-4089ca609fa64be3) on one additional cofactor block and enlarge the unconditional physical band.

2.  Establish a three-arithmetic-weight, fixed-$h$ determinant estimate strong enough for [\[eq:tpc45-target-high-Bessel\]](TPC-045.md#ref-0888793bf4b53575).

3.  First prove an averaged-shift or smoothed-slot analogue, thereby identifying which of fixed $h$, the third coefficient, or the vector-energy normalization is the dominant obstruction.

4.  Construct an actual-mask coefficient packet saturating the support norm. Such a result would be a sharp negative boundary for support-only methods, without asserting that the physical TPC coefficients themselves saturate it.

The current paper completes the geometric reduction needed before any of these analytic attacks. Failure at one door does not invalidate the physical-band theorem, and success in an averaged or smoothed model must not be reported as success at the fixed physical endpoint.
