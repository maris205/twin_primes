## Abstract

The large-prime reduction in the twin-prime-correlation (TPC) sequence admits scalar projective layers of the form $pr-mj=h$, with separated arithmetic weights on the prime $p$, a Möbius cofactor $r$, and an actual source variable $m$, while the orbit variable $j$ is smooth up to a fixed residue-class selector. The physical Hilbert dual later recouples these variables. We separate three questions that are lost if this is described only as a “third-coefficient problem.” First, multiplicative synthesis and Cauchy–Schwarz close the scalar fixed-shift form, and a Fourier–Bohr lift also closes the residual label $s=d(m)r$, at the Cartesian tensor-energy scale. That scale is the sum of the atomic diagonals over all shifts, not the physical diagonal at one shift; flat boxes and an explicit fixed-shift construction show that the two normalizations may differ by a polynomial factor. Second, Poisson summation in the smooth orbit slot gives the exact kernel $K_m(\alpha)=J\sum_k\widehat w(J(k-m\alpha))$. Band-limited orbit weights therefore produce a genuine shift-spectral gap between the geometric envelope and the first sampling aliases. At the TPC endpoint this gap is $X^{-1+o(1)}<\|\alpha\|<X^{-267/400+o(1)}$; corresponding band-pass averages vanish, and low-pass averages have an exact main term. Restriction to any fixed orbit progression preserves this exponent gap. Third, weighted shift averaging leaves the exact fiber-grouping norm unchanged when numerator and diagonal use the same weights. Moreover, sparse TPC alias sampling folds the full shift spectrum, and the currently proved collision-free local-label range does not reach the Poisson scale. Thus the paper proves a deterministic spectral advance and identifies atomic normalization, orbit-output coupling, alias folding, and fixed-shift localization as separate remaining gates. No fixed-shift TPC closure, parity breach, or twin-prime conclusion is claimed.

# Introduction

###### ref-5f0b81ac1492ab74

The TPC sequence isolates a deterministic equality-column problem whose large-prime part is governed by $$pr-mj=h_0.  \label{eq:tpc46-intro-determinant}$$ Here $p$ is prime, $r$ is the complementary factor carrying a Möbius weight, $m$ is an actual source row carrying a second arithmetic coefficient, and $j$ is an orbit variable with a smooth profile restricted to a fixed residue class. TPC–45 identifies the absence of a third arbitrary determinant-coefficient slot in the available fixed-determinant estimates as the next analytic obstruction [`\citep{WangTPC45}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-046.md#cite-1802a7e8047e595c). The natural response is to average the determinant shift and use the progression-smooth $j$-slot. The purpose of this paper is to determine exactly what that operation proves, which normalization it controls, and why it does not yet return the physical column at $h_0$.

There are three families that must not be merged. A *frozen completed family* keeps one coefficient tensor and lets the determinant value range through consecutive integers. The inherited TPC alias bank keeps one coefficient tensor but samples that family on the progression

$$h_0+kM_{\rm al},  \qquad  M_{\rm al}=X^{399/400+o(1)}.$$

A family reconstructed at different physical shifts changes the eligible rows, residue classes, coprimality conditions, and generally the coefficient tensor itself. Fourier analysis applies directly to the first family. It applies to the second only after alias folding, and it does not become a theorem about the third without a uniform provenance statement.

## Main results

###### ref-824a35df19ef741b

###### ref-cc4cfa990fc77994

Our first result is an exact positive closure theorem at a deliberately named norm. For scalar separated coefficients, put $$\mathcal T_h=\sum_{pr-mj=h}a_pb_rc_m\eta_j,  \qquad  \mathcal D_\otimes=  \left(\sum_{p,r}|a_pb_r|^2\right)  \left(\sum_{m,j}|c_m\eta_j|^2\right).$$ Multiplicative synthesis turns $\mathcal T_h$ into an additive correlation. The resulting Cauchy–Schwarz estimate is $$|\mathcal T_h|^2\le \rho_{pr}\rho_{mj}\mathcal D_\otimes.  \label{eq:tpc46-intro-tensor-bound}$$ At the large-prime TPC interface, $\rho_{pr}=1$ and $\rho_{mj}=X^{o(1)}$. A finite Fourier–Bohr transform gives the same tensor-scale closure after retaining the residual label $s=d(m)r$. These are genuine fixed-shift theorems, but $$\mathcal D_\otimes=\sum_h\Delta_h,  \label{eq:tpc46-intro-diagonal-sum}$$ where $\Delta_h$ is the atomic diagonal at shift $h$. The physical problem is normalized by $\Delta_{h_0}$, not its sum over all shifts. Flat boxes and the prescribed-shift construction in [8](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-046.md#src-1f0824683b72b720) show that the two scales can differ by a power of $X$. Hence [\[eq:tpc46-intro-tensor-bound\]](TPC-046.md#ref-824a35df19ef741b) is not renamed as an atomic-normalized TPC estimate.

###### ref-78fff6f706f5c4be

###### ref-2f31fbed7b540ecd

Our second result is a deterministic shift-spectrum theorem. For a Hilbert-valued frozen packet with a $j$-independent vector coefficient, the shift Fourier transform factors through $$K_m(\alpha)  =\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}w(j/J)\mathrm e(mj\alpha)  =J\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}\widehat w\bigl(J(k-m\alpha)\bigr).  \label{eq:tpc46-intro-Poisson-kernel}$$ If $\operatorname{supp}\widehat w\subset[-\sigma,\sigma]$, this kernel vanishes in the exact annulus $$\frac{\sigma}{M_-J}  <\|\alpha\|_{\mathbb T}  <\frac{1-\sigma/J}{M_+}.  \label{eq:tpc46-intro-gap}$$ At the endpoint this is $$X^{-1+o(1)}<\|\alpha\|_{\mathbb T}<X^{-267/400+o(1)}.$$ Restriction to a fixed progression in $j$ changes only the constant at the first alias and preserves these endpoint exponents. Compactly supported smooth orbit profiles satisfy rapid decay on every fixed power-shrinking of the same annulus. As consequences, suitably supported band-pass shift averages vanish exactly, while sufficiently coarse low-pass averages admit an exact zero-mode formula. The smooth version has a power-saving Poisson remainder. We also compute the corresponding averaged atomic diagonal and keep it distinct from the full squared synthesis.

###### ref-08201350330e3c35

Our third result is a set of sharp transfer boundaries. Weighted averaging in $h$ leaves the exact fiber-grouping norm equal to the largest realized fiber when numerator and atomic diagonal use the same weights. Sampling only the TPC alias progression folds the entire shift spectrum into $M_{\rm al}$ branches. For actual reconstructed shifts, zero extension always gives a common ambient coordinate space, while the present rigidity argument proves collision-free equality labels throughout $$|h-h_0|\le  X^{29629/210000-\epsilon},  \label{eq:tpc46-intro-local-range}$$ and makes no assertion that this sufficient range is optimal. Resolving the first Poisson alias by the present route needs a dense window of length at least $X^{267/400+o(1)}$. Finally, positivity alone costs the full effective number of shifts when an averaged nonnegative quantity is localized back to one chosen shift. These statements separate four missing inputs: orbit-frequency regularity for the pulled-back dual field, atomic rather than Cartesian normalization, anti-alias control, and fixed-shift localization.

## Why existing trilinear estimates do not fill the gap

Bettin–Chandee’s trilinear Kloosterman estimate permits three arbitrary sequences, but one is a Kloosterman-numerator sequence [`\citep{BC2018}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-046.md#cite-1d469e770d259123). Their fixed-determinant corollary retains two smooth determinant slots and only two arbitrary arithmetic slots. Averaging the determinant in the Bettin–Chandee–Radziwiłłapplication compresses a frequency and a determinant into a structured numerator sequence after smooth Poisson analysis; it does not remove those two smooth-slot hypotheses [`\citep{BCR2017}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-046.md#cite-dce783658977a95f). The modulus-dispersion results of Fouvry–Radziwiłłand Wright concern different averaged objects [`\citep{FR2022,Wright2026}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-046.md#cite-fb39ce9a20fbe3c6); [\[Wright2026\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-046.md#cite-f8666ba79358b969). Moreover, the physical TPC dualization introduces the joint field $$z_{\text{output row},\,j,\,d(m)r},$$ which need not split into their scalar coefficient slots. The precise comparison is given in [10](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-046.md#src-cff88c25adf6fb0d).

# The exact TPC determinant interface

This section records the coefficient packet to which the later scalar and tensor arguments are applied. There are three distinct objects in play: the physical equality column at one fixed shift, the completed alias family inherited from the determinant completion, and a family of TPC packets reconstructed at genuinely different physical shifts. The first two share one source–mask coefficient field. The third generally does not. Keeping this distinction explicit prevents a statement about an auxiliary completion from being read as an averaged prime-pair statement.

The two logarithms cost $O(\log X)=X^{o(1)}$ on the terminal support.

###### ref-2f2107e2b15af54f

###### ref-b3db5c7e42a5dd20

###### ref-23b5a90d986fb852

> **Corollary: Exact product-ready determinant layers** \[cor:tpc46-product-ready-layers\] Each coordinate in \[[eq:tpc46-left-determinant-sector](TPC-046.md#ref-8d34572750658944),[eq:tpc46-right-determinant-sector](TPC-046.md#ref-67edacadd4504058)\] is an $X^{o(1)}$-projective superposition of layers of the form $$q_\omega(\gamma)  \mathbf 1_{j\equiv a\!\!\pmod{H_0}}  \sum_{\substack{p,r,m\\pr-mj=h_0}}  a_\omega(p)b_\omega(r)c_\omega(m)  \phi_\omega(j/J),  \label{eq:tpc46-three-coefficient-layer}$$ where the prime restriction is in $a_\omega$, the factor $\mu(r)$ is in $b_\omega$, and the literal source and mask coefficient is in $c_\omega$. Equivalently, after opening $m=\ell d$, the layers have the form $$\mathbf 1_{j\equiv a\!\!\pmod{H_0}}  \sum_{\substack{p,r,\ell,d\\pr-dj\ell=h_0}}  a_\omega(p)b_\omega(r)  c_\omega(\ell)f_\omega(d)\phi_\omega(j/J).  \label{eq:tpc46-opened-determinant-layer}$$ No arithmetic coefficient has been replaced by a smooth cutoff in either identity.

###### ref-116bfa7bcdaae75b

The residue selector is fixed because $h_0$ is fixed, but it is not a smooth function of $j/J$. It has the exact finite Fourier expansion $$\mathbf 1_{j\equiv a\!\!\pmod{H_0}}  =\frac1{H_0}\sum_{\tau=0}^{H_0-1}  \mathrm e\!\left(\frac{\tau(j-a)}{H_0}\right).  \label{eq:tpc46-residue-selector-Fourier}$$ Thus every product-ready layer is a finite, $O_{h_0}(1)$-mass superposition of fixed modulations of a smooth orbit profile. It is *progression-smooth*, not literally an unmodulated smooth sequence. The progression form of the shift-spectral theorem is proved in [\[cor:tpc46-progression-shift-gap\]](TPC-046.md#ref-7ec307cab212ad38): for a band-limited profile its exponent range is unchanged because $H_0$ is fixed. The selector alone does not make the actual profile band-limited, and it does not regularize the pulled-back dual field below.

###### ref-f98a0ab2c4a4b5a7

This corollary is an interface theorem, not a scalar fixed-shift estimate. In particular, the coherent-complementary-factor theorem in TPC–36 leaves a character-level quantity of the form $$\left|\sum_r w(r)\widehat R_r(\chi)\right|^2  \label{eq:tpc46-inherited-coherent-gate}$$ and proves that Cauchy–Schwarz in the coherent variable can be sharp. Thus product-ready separation does not itself close even the scalar fixed-$h_0$ determinant sum.

## Residual duality and the tensor coefficient

###### ref-0597ab94820b9b3f

###### ref-bc0942175ee40087

At the physical equality alias, the terminal support has $(d_\rho,n_{\rho,j})=1$. If $n_{\rho,j}=pr$ with $p>\sqrt{N_+}$, then $d_\rho,p,r$ are pairwise coprime and the large-prime deletion leaves the residual label $$s=d_\rho r.  \label{eq:tpc46-physical-residual-label}$$ After deleting zero atoms, every occurring $r$ is squarefree; since $(d_\rho,r)=1$ and $d_\rho$ is squarefree, every occurring residual label $s=d_\rho r$ is squarefree. Let $\mathcal H_{\rm res}$ be the residual Hilbert space with coordinates $(i,s)$, where $i\in\mathcal I_L\sqcup\mathcal I_R$ and $s$ ranges over these supported squarefree labels. In the notation of TPC–45, define $x_p\in\mathcal H_{\rm res}$ on the present large-prime sector by the same coordinate rule: its $(i,s)$-entry is the stripped atom with full label $sp$, and is zero when that atom is absent. This definition does not require the more restrictive very-high cutoff used for the singleton-incidence theorem there. Let $$(Uy)_{i,s}=\mu(s)y_{i,s}.  \label{eq:tpc46-residual-unitary}$$ Thus $|\mu(s)|=1$ on the coordinate set, so $U$ is unitary, and $\|\sum_p x_p\|=\|\sum_pUx_p\|$.

###### ref-cc1ac727b2e515d3

###### ref-c5d7f26cfdf5d948

###### ref-3140f31ec0ca2da3

###### ref-5678c029a5fe0137

> **Proposition: Exact pulled-back dual tensor** \[prop:tpc46-dual-tensor\] For $z=(z^L,z^R)\in\mathcal H_{\rm res}$, the left part of the dual pairing is $$\begin{aligned}  \left\langle\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_{R_0}^{\sharp}}Ux_p,z\right\rangle_L  ={}-&\sum_{\substack{\gamma,j,\rho=(\ell,d)\in\mathcal A_1,\\                        p\in\mathcal P_{R_0}^{\sharp},r\\                        pr-dj\ell=h_0}}  \gamma_\rho^{(1)}  \mathfrak A_{\rho,\gamma}^{\rm act}(j)  \Lambda(pr)\mu(r)  \overline{z^L_{\gamma,j,dr}}.  \label{eq:tpc46-left-dual-tensor}\end{aligned}$$ The right part is $$\begin{aligned}  \left\langle\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_{R_0}^{\sharp}}Ux_p,z\right\rangle_R  ={}-&\sum_{\substack{\alpha,j,\rho=(\ell,d)\in\mathcal A_2,\\                        p\in\mathcal P_{R_0}^{\sharp},r\\                        pr-dj\ell=h_0}}  \gamma_\rho^{(2)}  \mathfrak A_{\alpha,\rho}^{\rm act}(j)  \Lambda(pr)\mu(r)  \overline{z^R_{\alpha,j,dr}}.  \label{eq:tpc46-right-dual-tensor}\end{aligned}$$ Consequently, after the projective separation in [\[cor:tpc46-product-ready-layers\]](TPC-046.md#ref-2f2107e2b15af54f), the new multiplier is precisely $$q_\omega(\gamma)\phi_\omega(j/J)  \mathbf 1_{j\equiv a\!\!\pmod{H_0}}  \overline{z^L_{\gamma,j,d(m)r}}  \label{eq:tpc46-pulled-back-z}$$ on the left, with the symmetric right-hand expression. It is a joint coefficient in the output row, $j$, the source divisor $d(m)$, and the cofactor $r$.

###### ref-c5537ce6f0acc028

> **Proof** For a left atom with $n=pr$, TPC–45 gives the stripped coefficient $$B^L_{\rho;\gamma,j}  =-\gamma_\rho^{\circ,(1)}   \mathfrak A_{\rho,\gamma}^{\rm act}(j)\Lambda(pr).  \label{eq:tpc46-stripped-B}$$ The residual coordinate is $s=dr$. Multiplication by $U$ inserts $\mu(s)=\mu(d)\mu(r)$. Combining $\mu(d)$ with $\gamma^{\circ,(1)}$ gives $\gamma^{(1)}$, and pairing with $z$ gives [\[eq:tpc46-left-dual-tensor\]](TPC-046.md#ref-c5d7f26cfdf5d948). The right calculation is identical after exchanging the two row systems. Substitution of [\[eq:tpc46-global-row-output-separation\]](TPC-046.md#ref-43474ed97253e909) yields [\[eq:tpc46-pulled-back-z\]](TPC-046.md#ref-5678c029a5fe0137).

###### ref-30abbd3f5f8a9b7a

###### ref-6443b01e11243061

The distinction from the scalar layer [\[eq:tpc46-three-coefficient-layer\]](TPC-046.md#ref-b3db5c7e42a5dd20) is exact. An arbitrary unit vector $z$ need not factor in $(\gamma,j,dr)$. Therefore [\[eq:tpc46-pulled-back-z\]](TPC-046.md#ref-5678c029a5fe0137) cannot be absorbed into the $r$-slot, the $m$-slot, or the smooth $j$-slot using the projective theorem of TPC–36. Expanding $z$ into coordinate delta functions gives only a crude illustration of the possible cost. For one fixed opposite row there are at most $$X^{o(1)}QJ=X^{1+o(1)}  \label{eq:tpc46-fixed-output-active-count}$$ active $(j,s)$-coordinates, so this expansion may cost $X^{1/2+o(1)}\|z\|_2$. Globally the number of atomic coordinates is at most $$X^{o(1)}Q^2J=X^{667/400+o(1)},  \label{eq:tpc46-global-active-count}$$ whose square-root scale is $X^{667/800+o(1)}$. These are crude upper bounds for one decomposition, not lower bounds or impossibility theorems. They show only that no $X^{o(1)}$ tensor lift follows from the inherited projective norm and $\|z\|_2=1$. The desired allowance is merely $K_B=X^{1/400+o(1)}$.

## Three shift families

We finish by fixing terminology used later.

###### ref-8727ba87372a4bf6

###### ref-c64c59c1e6775c58

> **Definition: Inherited completed-alias family** \[def:tpc46-completed-alias-family\] Let $M_{\rm al}$ be the triple-prime completion modulus of TPC–42 and put $$c_k=h_0+kM_{\rm al},\qquad k\in\mathcal I_B.  \label{eq:tpc46-completed-intercepts}$$ The completed-alias column replaces $n_{\rho,j}$ by $n_{\rho,j,k}=m_\rho j+c_k$, with zero extension off the terminal support, while retaining the same row systems and the same coefficient $\gamma_\rho^{(s)}\mathfrak A^{\rm act}(j)$ for every $k$.

###### ref-d3e872fa8fe13a59

###### ref-a2d2d7d828bc0fdf

###### ref-b8ab59e03e6a5cb7

The coefficient independence in this definition is an inherited fact, not an assumption [`\citep{WangTPC42,WangTPC43}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-046.md#cite-cc53a3263a6b378e); [\[WangTPC43\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-046.md#cite-26882076da806b18). However, for $k\ne0$ one may have $$g_{\rho,j,k}=(d_\rho,n_{\rho,j,k})=(d_\rho,c_k)>1.  \label{eq:tpc46-alias-gcd}$$ If $n_{\rho,j,k}=pr$ is squarefree and $p>d_\rho$, its fused Walsh label is $$p\,\operatorname{sf}(d_\rho r),  \qquad  \operatorname{sf}(d_\rho r)=\frac{d_\rho r}{(d_\rho,r)^2},  \label{eq:tpc46-alias-residual-label}$$ not necessarily $p d_\rho r$. Accordingly, the alias-averaged dual coefficient is $$z^L_{k,\gamma,j,\operatorname{sf}(d r)}  \label{eq:tpc46-alias-dual-coordinate}$$ rather than the equality-alias coefficient $z^L_{\gamma,j,dr}$.

###### ref-740cb2974a7396ad

###### ref-8556ad39dcd30e3b

> **Definition: Frozen completed family** \[def:tpc46-frozen-family\] For an auxiliary integer $c$, the frozen completed column keeps the physical $h_0$ row systems and source–mask coefficient field, but replaces the determinant equation by $$pr-mj=c.  \label{eq:tpc46-frozen-equation}$$ All terminal factors are evaluated at $n=pr=mj+c$ and are zero off support.

The inherited aliases $c=c_k$ are instances of this frozen family. For a general $c$, it is an algebraic coefficient model. It is not the TPC packet that would be obtained by restarting the construction with physical shift $c$.

###### ref-9cbc2f2a3964a7a3

> **Definition: Actual local-shift family** \[def:tpc46-actual-local-family\] For each nonzero shift $\nu$, the actual local-shift packet is the packet reconstructed with physical shift $\nu$. Its eligible rows, fixed residue factor $\Xi^{(\nu)}$, source residue data, terminal support, and joint multiplier are allowed to depend on $\nu$.

TPC–36 proves $X^{o(1)}$ projective separation for each fixed $\nu$, with a constant depending on $\nu$. It does not prove a uniform bound over a growing local-shift window. Thus later spectral identities for the frozen family become statements about actual shifted TPC packets only after a separate uniform-provenance theorem. No such theorem is assumed in this paper.

# A completed determinant model

The analytic statements are most transparent for a completed family in which the determinant value ranges over every integer. This section defines that family before any TPC specialization.

###### ref-1af051d57a7d83cd

###### ref-ad7c04f581fb1ad7

###### ref-d7ac179884e72244

###### ref-63abd0a1dfbc2959

Let $\mathcal P,\mathcal R,\mathcal M,\mathcal J\subset\mathbb N$ be finite sets. The letter $p$ is only an index in the abstract model; primality will be imposed in the TPC application. For coefficient sequences $a,b,c,\eta$, define $$\mathcal T_h(a,b,c,\eta)  :=\sum_{\substack{p\in\mathcal P,r\in\mathcal R,m\in\mathcal M,j\in\mathcal J\\                     pr-mj=h}}  a_p b_r c_m\eta_j,  \qquad h\in\mathbb Z.  \label{eq:tpc46-scalar-packet}$$ The corresponding Cartesian tensor energy and fixed-shift atomic diagonal are $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal D_\otimes  &:=  \left(\sum_{p,r}|a_pb_r|^2\right)  \left(\sum_{m,j}|c_m\eta_j|^2\right),  \label{eq:tpc46-tensor-energy}\\  \Delta_h  &:=  \sum_{pr-mj=h}|a_pb_rc_m\eta_j|^2.  \label{eq:tpc46-atomic-diagonal}\end{aligned}$$ Every Cartesian tuple has one and only one determinant value, and hence $$\boxed{\sum_{h\in\mathbb Z}\Delta_h=\mathcal D_\otimes.}  \label{eq:tpc46-diagonal-disintegration}$$ This elementary identity is the normalization divide on which several later conclusions turn.

###### ref-e65eadde74138cb8

For Hilbert-valued spectral statements it is useful to allow an arbitrary coefficient on the three arithmetic variables. Let $\mathcal H$ be a complex Hilbert space, let $z_{p,r,m}\in\mathbb C$, and let $v_{p,r,m}\in\mathcal H$. For a profile $w$ on $\mathbb R$, put $$F_h  :=\sum_{p,r,m}\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}  z_{p,r,m}w(j/J)v_{p,r,m}  \mathbf 1_{pr-mj=h}.  \label{eq:tpc46-Hilbert-packet}$$ The arithmetic supports in $p,r,m$ remain finite, while the Schwartz or compactly supported profile makes the $j$-sum absolutely convergent. The essential hypothesis in [\[eq:tpc46-Hilbert-packet\]](TPC-046.md#ref-e65eadde74138cb8) is that the Hilbert vector is independent of $j$. An arbitrary vector $v_{p,r,m,j}$ can encode a different orthogonal output at every orbit time and need not have any orbit spectral gap.

###### ref-f992d66fa0d93b41

###### ref-2a35d2c2b5067333

When a multiplicative residual label is retained, fix a map $$d:\mathcal M\longrightarrow\mathbb N  \label{eq:tpc46-d-map}$$ and define $$F(h,s)  :=\sum_{\substack{pr-mj=h\\d(m)r=s}}  a_pb_rc_m\eta_j.  \label{eq:tpc46-residual-packet}$$ At the physical TPC equality alias, $d(m)$ is the source divisor in the unique row $m=\ell d(m)$, and coprimality gives the residual label $s=d(m)r$. For nonzero completed aliases, the squarefree kernel may replace this literal product; that distinction is treated in [9](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-046.md#src-6d230489256a78a9).

###### ref-39de1bbbd63ff2ac

###### ref-85490c1a8b1f6504

###### ref-d46efc99e06b0733

Finally define the product-fiber multiplicities $$\begin{aligned}  \rho_{\mathcal P,\mathcal R}  &:=  \max_n\#\{(p,r)\in\mathcal P\times\mathcal R:pr=n\},  \label{eq:tpc46-rho-pr}\\  \rho_{\mathcal M,\mathcal J}  &:=  \max_n\#\{(m,j)\in\mathcal M\times\mathcal J:mj=n\}.  \label{eq:tpc46-rho-mj}\end{aligned}$$ For polynomially bounded integer supports, $$\rho_{\mathcal M,\mathcal J}  \le\max_{n\le X^{O(1)}}\tau(n)=X^{o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc46-divisor-multiplicity}$$

###### ref-6ace6ba2c79f2424

At the optimized TPC endpoint the four principal lengths are $$P R=X^{1+o(1)},  \qquad  M=X^{267/400+o(1)},  \qquad  J=X^{133/400+o(1)},  \qquad  MJ=X^{1+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc46-endpoint-boxes}$$ The notation $M$ in [\[eq:tpc46-endpoint-boxes\]](TPC-046.md#ref-6ace6ba2c79f2424) is the source-variable length. The triple-prime alias modulus will always be written $M_{\rm al}$ to prevent a collision of notation.

# Fixed-shift closure at tensor normalization

The third arithmetic coefficient is not, by itself, an obstruction to a fixed-shift estimate. The issue is the norm in which that estimate is obtained.

###### ref-5de14b348e4d7dfd

###### ref-5ed84412e4457ff0

Define multiplicative synthesis sequences $$A(n):=\sum_{pr=n}a_pb_r,  \qquad  C(n):=\sum_{mj=n}c_m\eta_j.  \label{eq:tpc46-product-synthesis}$$ Then $$\mathcal T_h(a,b,c,\eta)=\sum_nA(n)C(n-h).  \label{eq:tpc46-additive-correlation}$$

###### ref-15bd37b86d28605b

###### ref-b77dcb0b02231242

> **Theorem: Scalar tensor closure** \[thm:tpc46-scalar-tensor-closure\] For every integer $h$, $$\boxed{  |\mathcal T_h(a,b,c,\eta)|^2  \le  \rho_{\mathcal P,\mathcal R}\rho_{\mathcal M,\mathcal J}\mathcal D_\otimes.}  \label{eq:tpc46-scalar-tensor-bound}$$

> **Proof** Cauchy–Schwarz in [\[eq:tpc46-additive-correlation\]](TPC-046.md#ref-5ed84412e4457ff0) gives $|\mathcal T_h|^2\le\|A\|_2^2\|C\|_2^2$. On each product fiber, Cauchy–Schwarz gives $$\begin{aligned}  \|A\|_2^2  &\le\rho_{\mathcal P,\mathcal R}\sum_{p,r}|a_pb_r|^2,\\  \|C\|_2^2  &\le\rho_{\mathcal M,\mathcal J}\sum_{m,j}|c_m\eta_j|^2.\end{aligned}$$ Multiplication proves the claim.

The same proof is valid with one synthesized sequence Hilbert-valued.

###### ref-4b702cbdedb9016e

###### ref-95ed7513e3650cea

> **Corollary: One-vector-slot closure** \[cor:tpc46-one-vector-slot\] Let $x_{p,r}\in\mathbb C$ and $y_{m,j}\in\mathcal H$, and put $$G_h:=\sum_{pr-mj=h}x_{p,r}y_{m,j}.$$ Then $$\|G_h\|_{\mathcal H}^2  \le  \rho_{\mathcal P,\mathcal R}\rho_{\mathcal M,\mathcal J}  \left(\sum_{p,r}|x_{p,r}|^2\right)  \left(\sum_{m,j}\|y_{m,j}\|_{\mathcal H}^2\right).  \label{eq:tpc46-one-vector-bound}$$

> **Proof** Use Hilbert-space Cauchy–Schwarz after product synthesis; the fiberwise inequality $\|\sum_{\nu=1}^k y_\nu\|^2\le k\sum_\nu\|y_\nu\|^2$ replaces its scalar counterpart.

In the global large-prime range of TPC–45, product synthesis on the left is actually injective.

###### ref-456e92d0065289ac

###### ref-7f248a3524929b16

> **Lemma: Large-prime product injectivity** \[lem:tpc46-large-prime-injectivity\] Suppose every $p\in\mathcal P$ is prime, $p>\sqrt{N_+}$, and every occurring product satisfies $pr\le N_+$. Then $$\rho_{\mathcal P,\mathcal R}=1.  \label{eq:tpc46-rho-pr-one}$$

> **Proof** If $pr=p'r'\le N_+$ with distinct primes $p,p'>\sqrt{N_+}$, then $pp'$ divides the common product, while $pp'>N_+$, a contradiction.

###### ref-2b8138f46e94fa43

Combining \[[thm:tpc46-scalar-tensor-closure](TPC-046.md#ref-15bd37b86d28605b),[lem:tpc46-large-prime-injectivity](TPC-046.md#ref-456e92d0065289ac)\] with [\[eq:tpc46-divisor-multiplicity\]](TPC-046.md#ref-d46efc99e06b0733) gives $$|\mathcal T_h|^2\le X^{o(1)}\mathcal D_\otimes  \label{eq:tpc46-fixed-h-tensor-endpoint}$$ uniformly in $h$. This estimate retains an arbitrary prime coefficient, an arbitrary cofactor coefficient, and an arbitrary source coefficient. It uses neither determinant-shift averaging nor a Kloosterman-fraction estimate.

The conclusion is nevertheless not the TPC–45 Bessel target. By [\[eq:tpc46-diagonal-disintegration\]](TPC-046.md#ref-63abd0a1dfbc2959), the right side of [\[eq:tpc46-fixed-h-tensor-endpoint\]](TPC-046.md#ref-2b8138f46e94fa43) is the total atomic energy over *all* determinant shifts. TPC–45 requires the atomic diagonal at the one physical shift and inside the actual output geometry. The size and sharpness of that mismatch are quantified in [8](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-046.md#src-1f0824683b72b720).

# Fourier–Bohr scalarization of the residual label

The multiplicative label $s=d(m)r$ can be retained without paying a dimension factor. This shows that the bare residual product is not the source of the tensor-normalization loss.

###### ref-f32101a1642526a4

###### ref-eccaed62a736546a

###### ref-3d29800d73fa74f0

Let $\mathcal Q$ be a finite prime set containing all prime factors of every $r$ and $d(m)$ in the packet, and put $$\mathbb T^{\mathcal Q}:=\prod_{q\in\mathcal Q}\{z_q\in\mathbb C:|z_q|=1\}  \label{eq:tpc46-Bohr-torus}$$ with normalized Haar measure $dz$. For $z=(z_q)_{q\in\mathcal Q}$, define the completely multiplicative character $$\chi_z(n):=\prod_{q\in\mathcal Q}z_q^{v_q(n)}.  \label{eq:tpc46-Bohr-character}$$ Orthogonality gives $$\int_{\mathbb T^{\mathcal Q}}\chi_z(s)\overline{\chi_z(t)}\,dz  =\mathbf 1_{s=t}  \label{eq:tpc46-Bohr-orthogonality}$$ for the supported integers $s,t$.

###### ref-e6a0f69ed07bdc98

###### ref-6b7c4b9b832d36ff

> **Theorem: Residual-label fixed-shift closure** \[thm:tpc46-residual-fixed-shift\] For the residual packet in [\[eq:tpc46-residual-packet\]](TPC-046.md#ref-2a35d2c2b5067333), $$\boxed{  \sum_s|F(h,s)|^2  \le  \rho_{\mathcal P,\mathcal R}\rho_{\mathcal M,\mathcal J}\mathcal D_\otimes.}  \label{eq:tpc46-residual-fixed-shift-bound}$$

###### ref-76d428a6ab323c71

> **Proof** The Bohr transform of $F(h,\cdot)$ is $$\begin{aligned}  \widetilde F_h(z)  &:=  \sum_sF(h,s)\chi_z(s)\notag\\  &=\sum_{pr-mj=h}  a_pb_rc_m\eta_j\chi_z(r)\chi_z(d(m)).  \label{eq:tpc46-residual-Bohr-transform}\end{aligned}$$ For each fixed $z$, this is the scalar determinant form with unit-modulus twists absorbed into $b_r$ and $c_m$. Theorem [\[thm:tpc46-scalar-tensor-closure\]](TPC-046.md#ref-15bd37b86d28605b) therefore bounds $|\widetilde F_h(z)|^2$ by the right side of [\[eq:tpc46-residual-fixed-shift-bound\]](TPC-046.md#ref-6b7c4b9b832d36ff), uniformly in $z$. Parseval in [\[eq:tpc46-Bohr-orthogonality\]](TPC-046.md#ref-3d29800d73fa74f0) and integration over $z$ finish the proof.

###### ref-a99cec75c0d4f9fb

###### ref-ac9f06dc819e997d

There is also a two-spectrum identity which records every remaining collision exactly. Put $$\begin{aligned}  A_z(\alpha)  &:=  \sum_{p,r}a_pb_r\chi_z(r)\mathrm e(-pr\alpha),  \label{eq:tpc46-Az}\\  C_z(\alpha)  &:=  \sum_{m,j}c_m\eta_j\chi_z(d(m))\mathrm e(mj\alpha).  \label{eq:tpc46-Cz}\end{aligned}$$

###### ref-46bd2ddfe5b10c5c

###### ref-d591360ef94cf80a

> **Theorem: Additive–multiplicative Parseval normal form** \[thm:tpc46-double-Parseval\] One has the exact identity $$\boxed{  \sum_{h,s}|F(h,s)|^2  =\int_{\mathbb T}\int_{\mathbb T^{\mathcal Q}}  |A_z(\alpha)|^2|C_z(\alpha)|^2\,dz\,d\alpha.}  \label{eq:tpc46-double-Parseval}$$

> **Proof** The joint Fourier–Bohr transform of $F(h,s)$ equals $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_{h,s}F(h,s)\chi_z(s)\mathrm e(-h\alpha)  &=\sum_{p,r,m,j}a_pb_rc_m\eta_j    \chi_z(r)\chi_z(d(m))    \mathrm e(-(pr-mj)\alpha)\\  &=A_z(\alpha)C_z(\alpha).\end{aligned}$$ Parseval on $\mathbb T\times\mathbb T^{\mathcal Q}$ gives [\[eq:tpc46-double-Parseval\]](TPC-046.md#ref-d591360ef94cf80a).

###### ref-bac79507274f834a

Expansion of [\[eq:tpc46-double-Parseval\]](TPC-046.md#ref-d591360ef94cf80a) shows that its off-diagonal terms are exactly the pairs satisfying both $$pr-mj=p'r'-m'j',  \qquad  d(m)r=d(m')r'.  \label{eq:tpc46-mixed-collisions}$$ Thus the remaining Gram problem is a mixed additive–multiplicative energy, not an unspecified appeal to Möbius randomness.

For squarefree-kernel labels, the same argument uses the finite Walsh group $\{\pm1\}^{\mathcal Q}$ and prime valuations modulo two. At the physical equality alias, TPC coprimality makes the simpler product version above literal.

# The shift spectrum of a smooth determinant packet

We study the completed Hilbert packet [\[eq:tpc46-Hilbert-packet\]](TPC-046.md#ref-e65eadde74138cb8). This is a Fourier model for the equation $pr-mj=h$; it is not yet an assertion that the actual TPC packet has been constructed uniformly for every $h$. Let $J\ge2$ and suppose that its source support satisfies

$$0<M_-\le m\le M_+,$$

###### ref-d0e451e60f68edb3

###### ref-4eaa4162f0c82261

The coefficients and vectors have finite arithmetic support and $w\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R)$. The vectors in [\[eq:tpc46-Hilbert-packet\]](TPC-046.md#ref-e65eadde74138cb8) may depend jointly and arbitrarily on $(p,r,m)$, but they do not depend on $j$. In particular, the residual-label choice $$v_{p,r,m}=e_{d(m)r}  \quad\text{in }\ell^2(\mathbb N)  \label{eq:tpc46-residual-basis-example}$$ is permitted. The sequence $(F_h)_h$ belongs to both $\ell^1(\mathbb Z;\mathcal H)$ and $\ell^2(\mathbb Z;\mathcal H)$. We use the shift Fourier transform $$\widehat F(\alpha):=  \sum_{h\in\mathbb Z}F_h\mathrm e(-h\alpha),  \qquad \alpha\in\mathbb T.  \label{eq:tpc46-shift-Fourier-transform}$$

> **Theorem: Hilbert-valued orbit kernel** \[thm:tpc46-Hilbert-orbit-kernel\] For every $\alpha\in\mathbb T$, $$\begin{aligned}  \widehat F(\alpha)  &=\sum_{p,r,m}    z_{p,r,m}v_{p,r,m}\mathrm e(-pr\alpha)K_m(\alpha),  \label{eq:tpc46-shift-kernel-factorization}\\  K_m(\alpha)  &:=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}w(j/J)\mathrm e(mj\alpha)    =J\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}\widehat w\bigl(J(k-m\alpha)\bigr).  \label{eq:tpc46-Poisson-orbit-kernel}\end{aligned}$$ Moreover, $$\sum_{h\in\mathbb Z}\|F_h\|_{\mathcal H}^2  =\int_{\mathbb T}\|\widehat F(\alpha)\|_{\mathcal H}^2\,d\alpha.  \label{eq:tpc46-Hilbert-shift-Parseval}$$

> **Proof** Absolute convergence allows the order of summation to be changed. Substituting $h=pr-mj$ into [\[eq:tpc46-shift-Fourier-transform\]](TPC-046.md#ref-4eaa4162f0c82261) gives the first identity. Poisson summation applied to $x\mapsto w(x/J)$ gives $$\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}w(j/J)\mathrm e(mj\alpha)  =\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}J\widehat w\bigl(J(k-m\alpha)\bigr),$$ which proves the second. The last assertion is the standard Hilbert-valued Parseval identity; it also follows by applying scalar Parseval after pairing with an orthonormal basis of the finite dimensional span of the vectors $v_{p,r,m}$.

The factor $K_m$ contains all shift frequencies contributed by the smooth orbit variable. Neither the primality of $p$ nor any arithmetic cancellation in the three coefficient slots is used in the following theorem.

> **Theorem: Exact mesoscopic shift-spectral gap** \[thm:tpc46-exact-shift-spectral-gap\] Assume that, for some $\sigma>0$, $$\operatorname{supp}\widehat w\subset[-\sigma,\sigma]  \qquad\text{and}\qquad J>\sigma.  \label{eq:tpc46-bandlimited-orbit-profile}$$ Then $$\widehat F(\alpha)=0  \label{eq:tpc46-exact-gap-vanishing}$$ whenever $$\boxed{  \frac{\sigma}{M_-J}  <\|\alpha\|_{\mathbb T}<  \frac{1-\sigma/J}{M_+}.}  \label{eq:tpc46-exact-gap-annulus}$$ The assertion is vacuous if the displayed interval is empty.

###### ref-ac9b07d91c0b0ee2

> **Proof** Formula [\[eq:tpc46-Poisson-orbit-kernel\]](TPC-046.md#ref-e55ce442fce13a84) and [\[eq:tpc46-bandlimited-orbit-profile\]](TPC-046.md#ref-25f99a2e5b035592) show that $$K_m(\alpha)\ne0  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad  \|m\alpha\|_{\mathbb T}\le\frac{\sigma}{J}.  \label{eq:tpc46-kernel-support-condition}$$ Put $t=\|\alpha\|_{\mathbb T}$ and choose a representative with $|\alpha|=t$. Under [\[eq:tpc46-exact-gap-annulus\]](TPC-046.md#ref-7042dad364644194), every $m\in[M_-,M_+]$ satisfies $$\frac{\sigma}{J}<mt<1-\frac{\sigma}{J}.$$ Thus the distance of $m\alpha$ from both $0$ and the nearest nonzero integer is greater than $\sigma/J$. This contradicts [\[eq:tpc46-kernel-support-condition\]](TPC-046.md#ref-ac9b07d91c0b0ee2), so every $K_m(\alpha)$ in [\[eq:tpc46-shift-kernel-factorization\]](TPC-046.md#ref-deb8dbad7ed33d2e) vanishes.

> **Corollary: Fixed-progression shift gap** \[cor:tpc46-progression-shift-gap\] Let $H_0\in\mathbb N$, let $a$ be a residue class modulo $H_0$, and replace [\[eq:tpc46-Hilbert-packet\]](TPC-046.md#ref-e65eadde74138cb8) by $$F_h^{a,H_0}  :=\sum_{p,r,m}\sum_{\substack{j\in\mathbb Z\\j\equiv a\!\!\pmod{H_0}}}  z_{p,r,m}w(j/J)v_{p,r,m}\mathbf 1_{pr-mj=h}.  \label{eq:tpc46-progression-Hilbert-packet}$$ Assume $\operatorname{supp}\widehat w\subset[-\sigma,\sigma]$ and $J>H_0\sigma$. Then $$\widehat F^{a,H_0}(\alpha)=0  \label{eq:tpc46-progression-gap-vanishing}$$ whenever $$\boxed{  \frac{\sigma}{M_-J}  <\|\alpha\|_{\mathbb T}<  \frac{H_0^{-1}-\sigma/J}{M_+}.}  \label{eq:tpc46-progression-gap-annulus}$$ For fixed $H_0$, the endpoint exponent range remains $X^{-1+o(1)}<\|\alpha\|_{\mathbb T}<X^{-267/400+o(1)}$.

###### ref-48370a5a28c7d2e4

> **Proof** The progression-restricted orbit kernel is $$\begin{aligned}  K_{m,a}^{(H_0)}(\alpha)  &:=  \sum_{\substack{j\in\mathbb Z\\j\equiv a\!\!\pmod{H_0}}}  w(j/J)\mathrm e(mj\alpha)\notag\\  &=\frac{J}{H_0}\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}  \mathrm e(ak/H_0)  \widehat w\!\left(  \frac{J}{H_0}(k-mH_0\alpha)  \right).  \label{eq:tpc46-progression-orbit-kernel}\end{aligned}$$ Indeed, write $j=a+H_0t$ and apply Poisson summation in $t$. Thus a nonzero kernel requires $$\|mH_0\alpha\|_{\mathbb T}\le\frac{H_0\sigma}{J}.$$ If $t=\|\alpha\|_{\mathbb T}$ lies in [\[eq:tpc46-progression-gap-annulus\]](TPC-046.md#ref-85c7498d18fcd538), then every supported $m$ satisfies $$\frac{H_0\sigma}{J}<mH_0t<1-\frac{H_0\sigma}{J}.$$ The same support argument as in [\[thm:tpc46-exact-shift-spectral-gap\]](TPC-046.md#ref-6fa581e0007ba7bb) makes every $K_{m,a}^{(H_0)}(\alpha)$ vanish. The endpoint statement follows because $H_0$ is fixed.

> **Corollary: The endpoint spectral no-man’s-land** \[cor:tpc46-endpoint-shift-gap\] Suppose $$M_-=X^{267/400+o(1)},\qquad  M_+=X^{267/400+o(1)},\qquad  J=X^{133/400+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc46-endpoint-MJ-scales}$$ For a fixed band limit $\sigma$, the exact gap in [\[thm:tpc46-exact-shift-spectral-gap\]](TPC-046.md#ref-6fa581e0007ba7bb) has the exponent form $$X^{-1+o(1)}  <\|\alpha\|_{\mathbb T}<  X^{-267/400+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc46-endpoint-frequency-gap}$$ Thus the zero-frequency orbit envelope, whose width is of order $(MJ)^{-1}$, is separated from the first lattice aliases, whose distance from zero is of order $M^{-1}$.

> **Theorem: Rapid decay for a smooth compact orbit profile** \[thm:tpc46-rapid-smooth-shift-gap\] Let $w\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$, and suppose that for fixed $\mu,\nu>0$ with $\mu+\nu=1$, $$M_-\asymp M_+\asymp X^\mu,  \qquad J\asymp X^\nu.  \label{eq:tpc46-general-MJ-scales}$$ Fix $0<\epsilon<\nu$. For every $A>0$, uniformly on $$X^{-1+\epsilon}  \le\|\alpha\|_{\mathbb T}  \le X^{-\mu-\epsilon},  \label{eq:tpc46-shrunken-smooth-gap}$$ one has $$\begin{aligned}  |K_m(\alpha)|  &\ll_{A,w,\epsilon}JX^{-A\epsilon}  \qquad(M_-\le m\le M_+),  \label{eq:tpc46-smooth-kernel-rapid-decay}\\  \|\widehat F(\alpha)\|_{\mathcal H}  &\ll_{A,w,\epsilon}  JX^{-A\epsilon}  \sum_{p,r,m}|z_{p,r,m}|\|v_{p,r,m}\|_{\mathcal H}.  \label{eq:tpc46-smooth-packet-rapid-decay}\end{aligned}$$ Replacing $A$ by a larger constant absorbs any fixed polynomial size of the coefficient support.

> **Proof** Let $t=\|\alpha\|_{\mathbb T}$. Uniformly in the range [\[eq:tpc46-shrunken-smooth-gap\]](TPC-046.md#ref-bd9f767e55e4d07c), $$Jmt\gg X^\epsilon,  \qquad mt\ll X^{-\epsilon}.$$ For all sufficiently large $X$, the nearest integer to $m\alpha$ is therefore zero. Since $\widehat w$ is Schwartz, $$J\bigl|\widehat w(Jm\alpha)\bigr|  \ll_{A,w}J(Jmt)^{-A}  \ll_{A,w,\epsilon}JX^{-A\epsilon}.$$ For $k\ne0$, one has $|k-m\alpha|\ge |k|/2$. Given the requested output exponent $A$, choose a Schwartz decay order $B$ with $$B\ge A,  \qquad B\nu\ge A\epsilon.$$ Then $$J\sum_{k\ne0}  \bigl|\widehat w(J(k-m\alpha))\bigr|  \ll_{B,w}J^{1-B}  \ll_{A,w,\epsilon}JX^{-A\epsilon}.$$ The zero mode is bounded with the same order $B$, and hence also by the right-hand side of [\[eq:tpc46-smooth-kernel-rapid-decay\]](TPC-046.md#ref-2252970bb88daeb6). The triangle inequality in [\[eq:tpc46-shift-kernel-factorization\]](TPC-046.md#ref-deb8dbad7ed33d2e) proves [\[eq:tpc46-smooth-packet-rapid-decay\]](TPC-046.md#ref-68c5ee825d829ad2).

###### ref-b72afd71ab1ce4a4

> **Remark: Why the vectors must be orbit independent** \[rem:tpc46-j-independent-vectors\] If $v_{p,r,m}$ in [\[eq:tpc46-Hilbert-packet\]](TPC-046.md#ref-e65eadde74138cb8) is replaced by an arbitrary $v_{p,r,m,j}$, then $K_m(\alpha)$ is replaced by the vector-valued discrete transform $$\sum_jw(j/J)v_{p,r,m,j}\mathrm e(mj\alpha),$$ which may have full shift spectrum. The preceding theorems continue to hold under a separately proved uniform band-limit or rapid Fourier decay for these vector-valued profiles; they do not hold for arbitrary orbit-output coupling. In particular, no smoothness of an actual TPC projective mask is being assumed implicitly here.

###### ref-1bcf80772eed2a4c

> **Remark: Amplitude gap versus energy** \[rem:tpc46-amplitude-energy-distinction\] The vanishing in [\[eq:tpc46-exact-gap-vanishing\]](TPC-046.md#ref-b7c0b2148886b501) concerns the Fourier transform of the Hilbert-valued amplitude $F_h$. It gives an orthogonal low-frequency/high-alias decomposition of the total Parseval energy [\[eq:tpc46-Hilbert-shift-Parseval\]](TPC-046.md#ref-ea70485967eff5bf). It does not give the same spectral gap for the scalar sequence $h\mapsto\|F_h\|_{\mathcal H}^2$: multiplication in $h$ convolves shift frequencies, and differences of low and alias frequencies may occupy the intermediate band. Consequently, the amplitude gap alone is not an atomic-normalized Bessel estimate at a prescribed shift.

# Poisson formulas for coarse shift averages

###### ref-fdff84b0fa0a3f0e

We now test the completed packet [\[eq:tpc46-Hilbert-packet\]](TPC-046.md#ref-e65eadde74138cb8) by a window of physical shift length $H>0$. For $h_0\in\mathbb R$ and $W\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R)$, put $$\mathcal A_{H,h_0}(W;F)  :=\frac1H\sum_{h\in\mathbb Z}  W\!\left(\frac{h-h_0}{H}\right)F_h.  \label{eq:tpc46-normalized-shift-average}$$ All identities below are Bochner identities in $\mathcal H$. Their proofs are coefficient blind; the vectors $v_{p,r,m}$ remain arbitrary in $(p,r,m)$, but, as in [6](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-046.md#src-905a3db8ab77ad40), they must be independent of $j$.

## An exact band-pass null interval

###### ref-b9d42772dee100c8

###### ref-72448b07f931eac8

###### ref-8d069a9c001a7b45

###### ref-2778ca493d018c03

###### ref-e0fb29b15562f0f9

> **Theorem: Exact band-pass vanishing** \[thm:tpc46-exact-bandpass-vanishing\] Assume $$\operatorname{supp}\widehat w\subset[-\sigma,\sigma],  \qquad J>\sigma,  \label{eq:tpc46-bandpass-orbit-support}$$ and let $\psi\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R)$ satisfy $$\operatorname{supp}\widehat\psi  \subset\{\xi\in\mathbb R:a\le|\xi|\le b\}  \qquad(0<a\le b).  \label{eq:tpc46-bandpass-window-support}$$ If $$\boxed{  \frac{bM_+}{1-\sigma/J}<H<\frac{aM_-J}{\sigma},}  \label{eq:tpc46-exact-bandpass-H-range}$$ then, for every $h_0$, $$\boxed{\mathcal A_{H,h_0}(\psi;F)=0.}  \label{eq:tpc46-exact-bandpass-average-zero}$$

###### ref-22c2b7159b568050

> **Proof** Insert [\[eq:tpc46-Hilbert-packet\]](TPC-046.md#ref-e65eadde74138cb8) into [\[eq:tpc46-normalized-shift-average\]](TPC-046.md#ref-fdff84b0fa0a3f0e). For a fixed triple $(p,r,m)$, the remaining scalar sum is $$\frac1H\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}f_{p,r,m}(j),  \qquad  f_{p,r,m}(x)  :=w(x/J)\psi\!\left(\frac{pr-h_0-mx}{H}\right).  \label{eq:tpc46-bandpass-Poisson-function}$$ The Fourier support of $x\mapsto w(x/J)$ is contained in $[ -\sigma/J,\sigma/J]$. The Fourier support of the second factor in [\[eq:tpc46-bandpass-Poisson-function\]](TPC-046.md#ref-22c2b7159b568050) is contained in $$[-bm/H,-am/H]\cup[am/H,bm/H].$$ Consequently, every point of $\operatorname{supp}\widehat f_{p,r,m}$ has absolute value between $$\frac{am}{H}-\frac{\sigma}{J}  \quad\text{and}\quad  \frac{bm}{H}+\frac{\sigma}{J}.$$ The upper inequality in [\[eq:tpc46-exact-bandpass-H-range\]](TPC-046.md#ref-2778ca493d018c03) makes the lower endpoint positive for all $m\ge M_-$, while the lower inequality makes the upper endpoint smaller than one for all $m\le M_+$. Hence the Fourier support contains no integer. Poisson summation gives $\sum_jf_{p,r,m}(j)=\sum_k\widehat f_{p,r,m}(k)=0$. Summing over the finite arithmetic support proves the theorem.

###### ref-46b57d60e649d525

At the endpoint [\[eq:tpc46-endpoint-MJ-scales\]](TPC-046.md#ref-3c1cf837ab1c6c59), fixed band limits give a nonempty physical window range $$X^{267/400+o(1)}<H<X^{1-o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc46-endpoint-bandpass-window-range}$$ This is a statement about signed band-pass averages of the completed amplitude. It is not a positive average of its squared norm.

## The exact low-pass main term

###### ref-619458a3988b144e

> **Theorem: Exact low-pass Poisson formula** \[thm:tpc46-exact-lowpass-formula\] Assume [\[eq:tpc46-bandpass-orbit-support\]](TPC-046.md#ref-72448b07f931eac8), and let $W\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R)$ satisfy $$\operatorname{supp}\widehat W\subset[-b,b].  \label{eq:tpc46-lowpass-window-support}$$ If $$\frac{bM_+}{H}+\frac{\sigma}{J}<1,  \label{eq:tpc46-lowpass-no-alias-condition}$$ then $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal A_{H,h_0}(W;F)  ={}&\sum_{p,r,m}  \frac{z_{p,r,m}}{m}v_{p,r,m}  \int_{\mathbb R}W(u)  w\!\left(\frac{pr-h_0-Hu}{mJ}\right)\,du.  \label{eq:tpc46-exact-lowpass-main}\end{aligned}$$

> **Proof** For fixed $(p,r,m)$, set $$f(x):=w(x/J)W\!\left(\frac{pr-h_0-mx}{H}\right).$$ The Fourier support of $f$ is contained in $$\left[-\frac{\sigma}{J}-\frac{bm}{H},        \frac{\sigma}{J}+\frac{bm}{H}\right],$$ which lies strictly inside $(-1,1)$ by [\[eq:tpc46-lowpass-no-alias-condition\]](TPC-046.md#ref-e94a721033384f2d). Poisson summation thus has only its zero mode: $$\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}f(j)=\widehat f(0)=\int_{\mathbb R}f(x)\,dx.$$ With $u=(pr-h_0-mx)/H$, $$\frac1H\int_{\mathbb R}f(x)\,dx  =\frac1m\int_{\mathbb R}W(u)  w\!\left(\frac{pr-h_0-Hu}{mJ}\right)\,du.$$ Multiplying by $z_{p,r,m}v_{p,r,m}$ and summing proves [\[eq:tpc46-exact-lowpass-main\]](TPC-046.md#ref-6f57e734e5fd04db).

###### ref-eb2cc78285bd952e

For later comparison, define the frozen-envelope main term $$\mathcal M_{H,h_0}(W;F)  :=\left(\int_{\mathbb R}W(u)\,du\right)  \sum_{p,r,m}  \frac{z_{p,r,m}}{m}v_{p,r,m}  w\!\left(\frac{pr-h_0}{mJ}\right).  \label{eq:tpc46-frozen-envelope-main}$$

###### ref-7c0d85850a25868c

###### ref-18cfb1b5f5564ed4

> **Corollary: Variation of the exact low-pass main** \[cor:tpc46-lowpass-main-variation\] Under the hypotheses of [\[thm:tpc46-exact-lowpass-formula\]](TPC-046.md#ref-619458a3988b144e), $$\begin{aligned}  \bigl\|\mathcal A_{H,h_0}(W;F)-\mathcal M_{H,h_0}(W;F)\bigr\|_{\mathcal H}  \ll_{W,w}  \frac{H}{M_-J}  \sum_{p,r,m}  \frac{|z_{p,r,m}|}{m}\|v_{p,r,m}\|_{\mathcal H}.  \label{eq:tpc46-exact-main-variation-bound}\end{aligned}$$

> **Proof** The mean-value theorem gives, uniformly in $m\ge M_-$, $$\left|  w\!\left(\frac{pr-h_0-Hu}{mJ}\right)  -w\!\left(\frac{pr-h_0}{mJ}\right)  \right|  \le \|w'\|_\infty\frac{H|u|}{M_-J}.$$ Apply this in [\[eq:tpc46-exact-lowpass-main\]](TPC-046.md#ref-6f57e734e5fd04db), integrate $|uW(u)|$, and use the triangle inequality in $\mathcal H$.

The same main term survives for ordinary compactly supported smooth profiles, with a rapidly decreasing sampling error.

> **Theorem: Smooth Poisson approximation** \[thm:tpc46-smooth-Poisson-approximation\] Let $W,w\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$. Suppose $$H\ge2M_+,  \qquad H\le C_0M_-J  \label{eq:tpc46-smooth-Poisson-range}$$ for a fixed $C_0$. For every $A>0$, $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal A_{H,h_0}(W;F)  ={}&\sum_{p,r,m}  \frac{z_{p,r,m}}m v_{p,r,m}\notag\\  &\quad\times  \int_{\mathbb R}W(u)  w\!\left(\frac{pr-h_0-Hu}{mJ}\right)\,du  +\mathcal E_{H,h_0},  \label{eq:tpc46-smooth-Poisson-main}\\  \|\mathcal E_{H,h_0}\|_{\mathcal H}  &\ll_{A,W,w,C_0}  \left(\frac{M_+}{H}\right)^A\notag\\  &\quad\times  \sum_{p,r,m}  \frac{|z_{p,r,m}|}{m}\|v_{p,r,m}\|_{\mathcal H}.  \label{eq:tpc46-smooth-Poisson-error}\end{aligned}$$ Consequently, $$\begin{aligned}  \bigl\|\mathcal A_{H,h_0}(W;F)-\mathcal M_{H,h_0}(W;F)\bigr\|_{\mathcal H}  &\ll  \left[  \left(\frac{M_+}{H}\right)^A  +\frac{H}{M_-J}  \right]\notag\\  &\quad\times  \sum_{p,r,m}  \frac{|z_{p,r,m}|}{m}\|v_{p,r,m}\|_{\mathcal H},  \label{eq:tpc46-combined-smooth-lowpass-error}\end{aligned}$$ where the implicit constant has the same fixed dependencies.

###### ref-6e0bb5c19d93fe7d

> **Proof** For one triple put $B=pr-h_0$ and $$f(x)=w(x/J)W((B-mx)/H).$$ Poisson summation writes $\sum_jf(j)=\sum_k\widehat f(k)$. After the substitution $u=(B-mx)/H$, $$\widehat f(k)  =\frac Hm\mathrm e(-kB/m)  \int_{\mathbb R}W(u)  w\!\left(\frac{B-Hu}{mJ}\right)  \mathrm e(kHu/m)\,du.  \label{eq:tpc46-smooth-Poisson-Fourier-mode}$$ The $k=0$ term gives the integral in [\[eq:tpc46-smooth-Poisson-main\]](TPC-046.md#ref-01aabf467a801fcd). Set $\delta=H/m$. The range [\[eq:tpc46-smooth-Poisson-range\]](TPC-046.md#ref-fa024c954022fd38) gives $\delta\ge2$ and $H/(mJ)\le C_0$. Repeated integration by parts in [\[eq:tpc46-smooth-Poisson-Fourier-mode\]](TPC-046.md#ref-6e0bb5c19d93fe7d) therefore yields, for $k\ne0$, $$|\widehat f(k)|  \ll_{A,W,w,C_0}\delta(|k|\delta)^{-A-1}.$$ After summing $k\ne0$ and dividing by $H=m\delta$, the error for this triple is $$\ll_{A,W,w,C_0}  \frac1m\left(\frac mH\right)^A  \le  \frac1m\left(\frac{M_+}{H}\right)^A.$$ The Hilbert-space triangle inequality proves [\[eq:tpc46-smooth-Poisson-error\]](TPC-046.md#ref-d566a69a29a07b1f). The mean-value argument from [\[cor:tpc46-lowpass-main-variation\]](TPC-046.md#ref-7c0d85850a25868c) then gives [\[eq:tpc46-combined-smooth-lowpass-error\]](TPC-046.md#ref-2d6c378f0c877cf0).

At the TPC scales [\[eq:tpc46-endpoint-MJ-scales\]](TPC-046.md#ref-3c1cf837ab1c6c59), taking $H=X^{267/400+\epsilon}$ with $0<\epsilon<133/400$ makes the two errors in [\[eq:tpc46-combined-smooth-lowpass-error\]](TPC-046.md#ref-2d6c378f0c877cf0) $$X^{-A\epsilon+o(1)}  \quad\text{and}\quad  X^{-133/400+\epsilon+o(1)},  \label{eq:tpc46-endpoint-smooth-average-errors}$$ respectively. This strong approximation concerns a coarse completed shift average, not the value at a prescribed physical shift.

> **Theorem: Exact averaged atomic diagonal** \[thm:tpc46-exact-averaged-atomic-diagonal\] Assume [\[eq:tpc46-bandpass-orbit-support\]](TPC-046.md#ref-72448b07f931eac8) and [\[eq:tpc46-lowpass-window-support\]](TPC-046.md#ref-e22cde9054bae68c). > **Proof** Insert [\[eq:tpc46-atomic-diagonal-by-shift\]](TPC-046.md#ref-e88933b36908ff80) and eliminate $h$. # Sharp atomic-normalization boundaries

This section shows why the valid tensor estimates in \[[sec:tpc46-tensor-closure](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-046.md#src-f65d33a631b97ae9),[sec:tpc46-bohr](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-046.md#src-fb7ff72e52c50076)\] cannot be substituted for the physical Bessel estimate.

## Averaging all shifts can retain a polynomial loss

###### ref-2e3e3c70c3ff19e9

###### ref-dfffaf9d918eb807

###### ref-5e5e5ca00b26224b

###### ref-1e0c424d92a50494

###### ref-313f3467a8961f91

###### ref-ba0375f2f7978482

Take nonnegative unit coefficients on dyadic boxes and let $$N_h:=\#\{(p,r,m,j):pr-mj=h\}.  \label{eq:tpc46-flat-count}$$ Then $\mathcal T_h=N_h=\Delta_h$. Suppose all differences occupy an interval of $O(X)$ integers. If $$K_1:=\#(\mathcal P\times\mathcal R),  \qquad  K_2:=\#(\mathcal M\times\mathcal J),  \label{eq:tpc46-flat-cardinalities}$$ then $\sum_hN_h=K_1K_2$, and Cauchy–Schwarz gives $$\sum_h|\mathcal T_h|^2  =\sum_hN_h^2  \gg\frac{(K_1K_2)^2}{X}.  \label{eq:tpc46-global-flat-energy}$$ Since $\sum_h\Delta_h=K_1K_2$, $$\frac{\sum_h|\mathcal T_h|^2}{\sum_h\Delta_h}  \gg\frac{K_1K_2}{X}.  \label{eq:tpc46-global-flat-ratio}$$ At the endpoint, a prime $p$-box and three integer boxes have $$K_1=X^{1+o(1)}/\log X,  \qquad  K_2=X^{1+o(1)},  \label{eq:tpc46-flat-endpoint-counts}$$ so the ratio in [\[eq:tpc46-global-flat-ratio\]](TPC-046.md#ref-1e0c424d92a50494) is $$\gg X^{1-o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc46-flat-X-loss}$$ Partitioning the difference range into intervals of any prescribed length $1\le H\ll X$ shows that at least one nonempty $H$-window has ratio at least the global ratio. Therefore no theorem for all coefficient boxes and all shift windows can replace tensor energy by the averaged atomic diagonal at subpolynomial cost.

## A prescribed-shift obstruction for arbitrary slots

The following construction keeps the prime condition but is not asserted to satisfy the actual TPC source factorization or Möbius weights.

###### ref-f053f1cc3ee133ed

###### ref-66943ad747df9328

###### ref-3a48487d6a4e199f

> **Proposition: Fixed-shift coherent box** \[prop:tpc46-fixed-shift-box\] Fix $h\ne0$. Let $p\asymp P$ range over primes, choose $j_0\in\mathbb N$ with $j_0\asymp J$ and $2J<P$, and let $R\ge J$ be an integer. Work at a scale for which $pR>|h|$ for every selected prime, and choose the boxes so that $j_0<\min p$ over those primes. For every such $p$, choose the unique $$r_p\in[R,R+j_0)  \quad\text{with}\quad  pr_p\equiv h\pmod{j_0},  \label{eq:tpc46-rp-choice}$$ and put $$m_p:=\frac{pr_p-h}{j_0}.  \label{eq:tpc46-mp-choice}$$ Let $a,b,c$ be the indicators of the resulting $p,r_p,m_p$ sets, and let $0\le\eta_j\le1$ with $\eta_{j_0}=1$. Then $$\frac{|\mathcal T_h|^2}{\Delta_h}  \ge \#\{p\asymp P:p\text{ prime}\}  =P^{1-o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc46-fixed-shift-obstruction}$$

> **Proof** Since $p>j_0$, the prime $p$ is invertible modulo $j_0$, so [\[eq:tpc46-rp-choice\]](TPC-046.md#ref-f053f1cc3ee133ed) has exactly one representative in the declared half-open interval. Every selected prime supplies the solution $pr_p-m_pj_0=h$. The scale hypothesis gives $m_p>0$. All terms are nonnegative. Since every coefficient has modulus at most one, $\Delta_h\le\mathcal T_h$, while $\mathcal T_h$ is at least the number of selected primes. Hence $|\mathcal T_h|^2/\Delta_h\ge\mathcal T_h$, proving the claim.

###### ref-f208be30c9eb35d9

At the TPC lengths $P=M=X^{267/400+o(1)}$ and $R=J=X^{133/400+o(1)}$, this abstract obstruction has exponent $267/400$, whereas the endpoint budget is $1/400$. The gap is $$\frac{267}{400}-\frac1{400}  =\frac{133}{200}.  \label{eq:tpc46-fixed-box-gap}$$ The construction rules out an arbitrary-three-slot black box at atomic normalization. It does not say that the actual TPC mask realizes the coherent coefficients.

## Weighted shift averaging and exact grouping norms

###### ref-e0c7f052dfc34d21

Let $E$ be a finite atom set with maps $h:E\to\mathbb Z$ and $s:E\to S$. For nonnegative weights $\omega_h$, define $$\|Gx\|_\omega^2  :=\sum_{h_0,s_0}\omega_{h_0}  \left|\sum_{\substack{e:h(e)=h_0\\s(e)=s_0}}x_e\right|^2,  \qquad  \|x\|_{\omega,E}^2  :=\sum_e\omega_{h(e)}|x_e|^2.  \label{eq:tpc46-weighted-grouping}$$

###### ref-99af0f6792ce5d3a

###### ref-5e49f068be4d330c

> **Theorem: Averaging-invariant grouping norm** \[thm:tpc46-weighted-grouping-norm\] After discarding zero-weight fibers, $$\boxed{  \sup_{x\ne0}\frac{\|Gx\|_\omega^2}{\|x\|_{\omega,E}^2}  =\max_{\substack{h_0,s_0\\\omega_{h_0}>0}}  \#\{e:h(e)=h_0,\ s(e)=s_0\}.}  \label{eq:tpc46-weighted-grouping-norm}$$

> **Proof** Fiberwise Cauchy–Schwarz gives the upper bound. On a largest positive-weight fiber, take $x$ constant and nonzero and set it to zero elsewhere. The weight cancels from the quotient and equality is attained.

###### ref-0f5b5c3b9c70734a

###### ref-a6f3636ef0bc881c

Thus dividing both numerator and atomic diagonal by the number of shifts does not lower the exact support-only loss. At the first unbalanced TPC block $R=J$, the available ambient fiber bound from TPC–45 is $$1+\frac{L_0}{J}  =X^{15077/105000+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc46-unbalanced-ambient-frontier}$$ It exceeds $X^{1/400}$ by exponent $$\frac{15077}{105000}-\frac1{400}  =\frac{29629}{210000}.  \label{eq:tpc46-unbalanced-budget-deficit}$$ The actual squared norm is the realized maximum fiber size, which can be smaller than this ambient upper bound. The theorem says only that shift weights alone do not reduce that realized norm.

# Alias folding, local geometry, and fixed-shift localization

The frozen shift variable has a useful Fourier theory because the source–mask coefficient does not move with the intercept. The two shift families available from the inherited TPC construction have opposite defects: the completed aliases range over a macroscopic determinant window but are sampled on a very sparse progression, whereas genuinely reconstructed local-shift packets can presently be placed in one ambient geometry only on a much shorter interval. This section quantifies both statements and then records the sharp price of localizing a weighted average back to one prescribed shift.

## The exact completed-alias fold

###### ref-9e863163f2929304

###### ref-2cd593e0c7aa9398

Let $M_{\rm al}$ and $\mathcal I_B$ be the inherited completion parameters, so that $$M_{\rm al}=X^{399/400+o(1)},\qquad  c_k=h_0+kM_{\rm al},\qquad  K_B:=|\mathcal I_B|=X^{1/400+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc46-alias-scales}$$ For a determinant atom $$x=(p,r,m,j)$$ write $$\Delta(x):=pr-mj.  \label{eq:tpc46-determinant-value}$$ The following identity is the exact form of alias folding; it uses no estimate.

###### ref-23c4c9e542a77f52

###### ref-ac4d771efb976669

###### ref-591999cf332f0c85

> **Lemma: Weighted alias selector** \[lem:tpc46-weighted-alias-selector\] For arbitrary weights $(\omega_k)_{k\in\mathcal I_B}$ and any two integer determinant values $\Delta,\Delta'$, $$\begin{aligned}  &\sum_{k\in\mathcal I_B}\omega_k    \mathbf 1_{\Delta=c_k}\mathbf 1_{\Delta'=c_k}  \notag\\  &\qquad=  \mathbf 1_{\Delta=\Delta'}  \mathbf 1_{\Delta\equiv h_0\!\!\pmod {M_{\rm al}}}  \mathbf 1_{(\Delta-h_0)/M_{\rm al}\in\mathcal I_B}  \omega_{(\Delta-h_0)/M_{\rm al}}.  \label{eq:tpc46-weighted-alias-selector}\end{aligned}$$ Consequently, for every finitely supported coefficient field $A(x)$, $$\begin{aligned}  &\sum_{k\in\mathcal I_B}\omega_k  \left|\sum_{\Delta(x)=c_k}A(x)\right|^2  \notag\\  &\quad=  \sum_{\substack{x,x'\\                   \Delta(x)=\Delta(x')\\                   \Delta(x)\equiv h_0\!\!\pmod {M_{\rm al}}\\                   (\Delta(x)-h_0)/M_{\rm al}\in\mathcal I_B}}  \omega_{(\Delta(x)-h_0)/M_{\rm al}}  A(x)\overline{A(x')}.  \label{eq:tpc46-alias-equal-determinant}\end{aligned}$$

> **Proof** At most one $k$ can satisfy $\Delta=h_0+kM_{\rm al}$. It contributes to the left side of [\[eq:tpc46-weighted-alias-selector\]](TPC-046.md#ref-ac4d771efb976669) precisely when the two determinant values coincide, lie in the required residue class, and their common quotient belongs to $\mathcal I_B$. Expanding the square proves [\[eq:tpc46-alias-equal-determinant\]](TPC-046.md#ref-591999cf332f0c85).

Thus shift averaging does not turn the determinant form into its atomic diagonal. It replaces the fixed equation by an equal-determinant quartic correlation, restricted to one sparse residue class.

###### ref-71c0b796cab737df

###### ref-072e2e2c26090527

There is an equivalent frequency statement. For a finitely supported sequence $F:\mathbb Z\to\mathbb C$, define $$\widehat F(\alpha)=\sum_{c\in\mathbb Z}F(c)\mathrm e(-c\alpha),  \qquad \alpha\in\mathbb T,  \label{eq:tpc46-full-shift-transform}$$ and sample $G(k)=F(h_0+kM_{\rm al})$. Then, for every $\beta\in\mathbb T$, $$\boxed{  \widehat G(\beta)  =\frac1{M_{\rm al}}\sum_{a=0}^{M_{\rm al}-1}  \mathrm e\!\left(\frac{h_0(\beta+a)}{M_{\rm al}}\right)  \widehat F\!\left(\frac{\beta+a}{M_{\rm al}}\right).}  \label{eq:tpc46-exact-alias-fold}$$ Indeed, insertion of [\[eq:tpc46-full-shift-transform\]](TPC-046.md#ref-71c0b796cab737df) into the right side gives $$\sum_cF(c)\mathrm e\!\left(\frac{(h_0-c)\beta}{M_{\rm al}}\right)  \frac1{M_{\rm al}}\sum_{a=0}^{M_{\rm al}-1}  \mathrm e\!\left(\frac{(h_0-c)a}{M_{\rm al}}\right),$$ and the inner average selects $c=h_0+kM_{\rm al}$, leaving $\sum_kG(k)\mathrm e(-k\beta)$. Multiplication by a finite alias window $\omega_k$ convolves the right side of [\[eq:tpc46-exact-alias-fold\]](TPC-046.md#ref-072e2e2c26090527) with $\widehat\omega$. It does not unfold the $M_{\rm al}$ frequency branches.

###### ref-4dc7b6f572c7ba96

> **Remark: What the inherited alias family represents** \[rem:tpc46-alias-not-physical-shifts\] The common coefficient field in the alias family is exactly the one proved in TPC–42 and TPC–43 [`\citep{WangTPC42,WangTPC43}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-046.md#cite-cc53a3263a6b378e); [\[WangTPC43\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-046.md#cite-26882076da806b18). Formula [\[eq:tpc46-exact-alias-fold\]](TPC-046.md#ref-072e2e2c26090527) is therefore an unconditional identity for the completed determinant packet. It is not the Fourier transform of a family of prime-pair problems reconstructed at shifts $h_0+kM_{\rm al}$. In particular, the row coprimality and fixed residue masks have not been recomputed at those integers.

## Common embedding and a collision-free local range

###### ref-cdd5de29de5caa25

We now vary the physical shift itself. Let $$\mathfrak H_X\subset\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\},\qquad  H_*:=\max_{\nu\in\mathfrak H_X}|\nu-h_0|,  \qquad  \widetilde H_*:=|h_0|+H_*.  \label{eq:tpc46-local-shift-set}$$ Choose ambient geometric row systems $\mathcal A_1^*,\mathcal A_2^*$ containing every row in the relevant dyadic source windows, without imposing shift-dependent coprimality. The actual packet at $\nu$ is embedded by setting a row to zero unless $(d_\rho,\nu)=1$, and by setting each terminal atom to zero off its own support. The same zero extension is made on the opposite-row output coordinate. This places all finite packets in a common coordinate space; it does not make their coefficient values equal.

###### ref-47fab484597f2511

For $\rho=(\ell_\rho,d_\rho)$, define the local equality label $$u_{\rho,j}^{(\nu)}  :=d_\rho(\ell_\rho d_\rho j+\nu).  \label{eq:tpc46-local-label}$$ Let $D_+$ be the largest ambient source divisor and $J_-$ the smallest orbit time.

###### ref-7284fbb459008f8e

###### ref-d4fc2259e5ea3752

###### ref-e2e83a5e60cb7b4a

###### ref-e40b4f0a3ce7bc84

> **Lemma: Uniform local-shift label rigidity** \[lem:tpc46-local-label-rigidity\] Suppose $$\widetilde H_*D_+<J_-.  \label{eq:tpc46-local-rigidity-condition}$$ For every fixed $(\nu,j)\in\mathfrak H_X\times\mathcal J$, two active rows in the same source system satisfy $$u_{\rho,j}^{(\nu)}=u_{\sigma,j}^{(\nu)}  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \rho=\sigma.  \label{eq:tpc46-local-label-injective}$$ At the TPC endpoint, every fixed $\epsilon>0$ permits $$H_*\le  X^{29629/210000-\epsilon}.  \label{eq:tpc46-local-shift-exponent}$$

###### ref-2d0431547087ca23

> **Proof** Writing $\rho=(\ell,d)$ and $\sigma=(\ell',e)$, equality of the labels gives $$j(\ell d^2-\ell'e^2)=\nu(e-d).  \label{eq:tpc46-local-collision-equation}$$ If the integer in parentheses is nonzero, the left side has magnitude at least $j\ge J_-$, whereas the right side has magnitude at most $\widetilde H_*D_+<J_-$. Hence both sides vanish. Since $\nu\ne0$, first $d=e$, and then $\ell=\ell'$.
>
> For the exponent, use $$\frac{133}{400}=\frac{69825}{210000},  \qquad  \frac{10049}{52500}=\frac{40196}{210000}.$$ Their difference is $29629/210000$. Since $h_0$ is fixed, $\widetilde H_*=H_*+O_{h_0}(1)$; the fixed $\epsilon$ absorbs this constant and the endpoint $o(1)$-terms.

Since [\[eq:tpc46-local-shift-exponent\]](TPC-046.md#ref-e40b4f0a3ce7bc84) is $o(X)$, common target endpoints remain $N_\pm^{\mathfrak H}=X^{1+o(1)}$ after harmless dyadic enlargement. A common large-prime band can therefore be defined from the minimum and maximum over the entire local family. The label rigidity lemma also shows that zero extension itself creates no same-coordinate collision. It does not control the dependence of the coefficient field on $\nu$.

###### ref-e36b7b4b96bf8def

###### ref-59865be0e8a53571

The support-only cofactor bound acquires an explicit dependence on the window. Suppose a touched target has $n=pr$, residual label $s=dr$, and cofactor $r\asymp R_0$. For fixed $(\nu,j,s)$ and $d\mid s$, the source rows solve $$rp-dj\ell=\nu.  \label{eq:tpc46-local-determinant-ladder}$$ Putting $g=(r,dj)$, one has $g\mid\nu$. The source-prime step in the integral ladder is $r/g$, so a source window of length $O(L_0)$ contains $$O\!\left(1+\frac{gL_0}{r}\right)  \le O\!\left(1+\frac{\widetilde H_*L_0}{R_0}\right)$$ parameters. Summation over $d\mid s$ proves the uniform bound $$\rho_{R_0,\mathfrak H}  :=  \max_{\substack{\nu\in\mathfrak H_X,\ j\in\mathcal J,\ s}}  \#\left\{(d,\ell,p,r):  \begin{array}{c}  r\asymp R_0,\ dr=s,\\  rp-dj\ell=\nu,\ \text{all packet supports hold}  \end{array}\right\}$$ $$\boxed{  \rho_{R_0,\mathfrak H}  \ll \tau(s)\left(1+\frac{\widetilde H_*L_0}{R_0}\right).}  \label{eq:tpc46-local-fiber-bound}$$ At the balanced scale $R_0\asymp L_0$, the presently available support-only bound therefore carries a polynomial factor for a polynomial local window. TPC–36 contains a stronger primitivity statement at an earlier ultra-divisor interface, but the terminal equality interface imported through TPC–43–TPC–45 does not state its preservation. We consequently retain [\[eq:tpc46-local-fiber-bound\]](TPC-046.md#ref-59865be0e8a53571); setting $g=1$ would require a new provenance lemma.

###### ref-f3bbc5a4ff7e3391

###### ref-5f0a27419ebc097e

There is a second uniformity issue. For each fixed $\nu$, let $\mathfrak M_B(\nu;X)$ denote the least weighted projective mass in the analogue of [\[eq:tpc46-global-row-output-separation\]](TPC-046.md#ref-43474ed97253e909). The inherited statement is only $$\mathfrak M_B(\nu;X)\ll_{B,\nu}X^{o(1)}  \qquad\text{for every fixed \(\nu\)}.  \label{eq:tpc46-fixed-shift-projective-only}$$ It does not imply $$\sup_{\nu\in\mathfrak H_X}\mathfrak M_B(\nu;X)=X^{o(1)}  \label{eq:tpc46-missing-uniform-projective}$$ when $\mathfrak H_X$ grows with $X$. The period $|\nu|$ of the fixed residue factor, the eligible rows, and the source residue data all move with $\nu$. For a coherent weighted average, the corresponding sufficient condition is $$\sum_{\nu\in\mathfrak H_X}  |\omega_\nu|\mathfrak M_B(\nu;X)=X^{o(1)}.$$ Neither growing-family statement follows from TPC–36. A fixed finite set of physical shifts is harmless, but it supplies no mesoscopic shift average.

## Why the proved collision-free range misses the Poisson scale

###### ref-d2c2ab463f12454f

###### ref-daad9c9d70c3e07c

###### ref-253304f28c320c4e

The frozen determinant family has a clean shift transform. For one scalar product-ready layer, put $$F(c)=  \sum_{p,r,m,j}  a(p)b(r)c_0(m)w(j/J)  \mathbf 1_{pr-mj=c}.  \label{eq:tpc46-frozen-shift-sequence}$$ Its full shift Fourier transform contains the exact orbit kernel $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}w(j/J)\mathrm e(mj\alpha)  &=J\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}\widehat w\bigl(J(k-m\alpha)\bigr)  \notag\\  &=:K_m(\alpha).  \label{eq:tpc46-Poisson-kernel}\end{aligned}$$ Suppose, for clarity, that $\widehat w$ is supported in a fixed interval. Since $m\asymp Q$ and $mJ\asymp X$, the zero Poisson packet has width $X^{-1+o(1)}$, while the first nonzero sampling centers lie at distance $Q^{-1+o(1)}$. Thus the geometric gap is $$X^{-1+o(1)}  <\|\alpha\|_{\mathbb T}<  X^{-267/400+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc46-Poisson-gap-scales}$$

###### ref-e870bdd3a39e1680

###### ref-81fce33684321b24

###### ref-9731229138bbfed2

To see the required shift length concretely, take a standard modulated weight $$W_{\mathsf H,\alpha_0}(c)  =V\!\left(\frac{c-c_0}{\mathsf H}\right)\mathrm e(\alpha_0c).  \label{eq:tpc46-modulated-shift-weight}$$ Its Fourier multiplier has width $\asymp\mathsf H^{-1}$ around $\alpha_0$. Placing a fixed-shape multiplier inside the gap near its coarse $Q^{-1}$-end requires $$\mathsf H\gg Q=X^{267/400+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc46-Poisson-required-window}$$ In contrast, the presently proved collision-free local-label range reaches only $$H_*\le X^{29629/210000-\epsilon}.$$ The exponent deficit to the first Poisson scale is $$\frac{267}{400}-\frac{29629}{210000}  =\frac{110546}{210000}  =\frac{55273}{105000}>0.  \label{eq:tpc46-Poisson-local-deficit}$$ Hence the dense actual local family, under this rigidity argument, does not reach the scale on which the first sampling aliases can be spectrally resolved by this route. The completed-alias family spans a determinant interval of order $X$, but its spacing is $M_{\rm al}=X^{399/400+o(1)}$; the exact fold [\[eq:tpc46-exact-alias-fold\]](TPC-046.md#ref-072e2e2c26090527) shows why macroscopic span does not replace dense sampling. The frozen completed family is the only one of the three current objects that simultaneously permits dense shifts on the Poisson scale and retains one coefficient field, and it is not a family of actual reconstructed TPC packets.

## Weighted grouping and the sharp localization price

Shift averaging also does not improve the support norm merely by renormalizing numerator and diagonal with the same weights. proves that the exact grouping norm remains the largest realized fiber: the common weight cancels on each block. An analytic averaged estimate may of course beat this support norm by using coefficient cancellation. A separate loss appears when that average is localized back to one prescribed shift.

###### ref-ff9e512e07a58931

###### ref-3f95a79525f85682

###### ref-9734523778918946

> **Lemma: Sharp weighted localization cost** \[lem:tpc46-weighted-localization\] Let $E_\nu\ge0$, let $\omega_\nu\ge0$, and suppose $\omega_{\nu_0}>0$. Define $$\operatorname{Av}_\omega E  :=\frac{\sum_\nu\omega_\nu E_\nu}          {\sum_\nu\omega_\nu}.  \label{eq:tpc46-weighted-average}$$ Then $$\boxed{  E_{\nu_0}  \le  \mathcal L_\omega(\nu_0)\operatorname{Av}_\omega E,  \qquad  \mathcal L_\omega(\nu_0)  :=\frac{\sum_\nu\omega_\nu}{\omega_{\nu_0}}.}  \label{eq:tpc46-localization-cost}$$ The constant is sharp for arbitrary nonnegative data.

> **Proof** The term $\omega_{\nu_0}E_{\nu_0}$ is bounded by the full weighted sum, which proves the inequality. Equality holds when every $E_\nu$ except $E_{\nu_0}$ is zero.

###### ref-82086386f32fefa8

For $K$ essentially equal weights, $\mathcal L_\omega(\nu_0)\asymp K$, independently of whether the weights are normalized to have total mass one. In particular, a fixed-shape nonnegative smooth weight spread across $K_B$ inherited aliases, with central value bounded below, has $$\mathcal L_\omega(0)=X^{1/400+o(1)}  \label{eq:tpc46-alias-localization-loss}$$ when its central value is bounded below. This is precisely the size of the TPC endpoint allowance $K_B$. Therefore an alias-averaged mean-square theorem at its averaged atomic scale does not, by positivity alone, yield the physical $k=0$ bound at the same scale.

###### ref-be65132c7922247b

> **Remark: Claim boundary** \[rem:tpc46-alias-localization-boundary\] The identities above prove four different facts: exact folding of the completed aliases, a collision-free label range inside the common zero-extended geometry, a strict mismatch between that rigidity range and the Poisson resolution scale, and the sharp positivity cost of returning from an average to one shift. They do not show that localization is impossible. A maximal theorem, shift regularity, an amplifier, or a coefficient-specific comparison between neighboring shifts could reduce the loss. None is supplied by TPC–36, TPC–42, TPC–43, or TPC–45. Accordingly, no averaged or frozen-family result in this paper is asserted to close the physical fixed-shift TPC estimate, break the parity barrier, or imply the twin-prime conjecture.

# The external analytic boundary

The deterministic reductions in this paper should not be conflated with an available Kloosterman–fraction theorem. The distinction is already visible at the scalar level and becomes sharper after the physical Hilbert output is restored. We record the coefficient slots, the role of shift averaging, and the normalization supplied by the relevant primary sources.

## Three Kloosterman sequences are not three determinant slots

###### ref-2f472a7ae6a3dfac

###### ref-3daa5dd9bdb5148b

Let $\mathcal A=[A/2,A]$, $\mathcal M=[M/2,M]$, and $\mathcal N=[N/2,N]$. Bettin and Chandee’s trilinear form is $$\mathcal B(M,N,A)  :=  \sum_{\substack{a\in\mathcal A,\ m\in\mathcal M,\ n\in\mathcal N\\(m,n)=1}}  \nu_a\alpha_m\beta_n  \mathrm e\!\left(\vartheta\frac{a\overline m}{n}\right),  \qquad \vartheta\ne0,  \label{eq:tpc46-bc-form}$$ where $\overline m$ is the inverse of $m$ modulo $n$. Their Theorem 1 gives, for arbitrary complex sequences $\alpha,\beta,\nu$, $$\begin{aligned}  |\mathcal B(M,N,A)|  \ll {}&  \|\alpha\|_2\|\beta\|_2\|\nu\|_2  \left(1+\frac{|\vartheta|A}{MN}\right)^{1/2}  \notag\\[-2pt]  &\quad\times  \left\{  (AMN)^{7/20+\varepsilon}(M+N)^{1/4}  +(AMN)^{3/8+\varepsilon}(AN+AM)^{1/8}  \right\}.  \label{eq:tpc46-bc-theorem}\end{aligned}$$ Thus [\[eq:tpc46-bc-form\]](TPC-046.md#ref-2f472a7ae6a3dfac) has three arbitrary sequences, but one of them is a sequence in the *Kloosterman numerator* $a$. This is not the same assertion as allowing three arbitrary weights on variables in a determinant equation [`\citep[Theorem~1]{BC2018}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-046.md#cite-1d469e770d259123).

###### ref-4684bf48a00ec97b

###### ref-62539f1aa0788626

###### ref-9c2917ead9b80efd

###### ref-de58de5ad453a8c5

The distinction is explicit in the fixed-determinant consequence of the same paper. For $$\mathcal T_\Delta  :=  \sum_{\substack{m_i\in[M_i/2,M_i],\ n_i\in[N_i/2,N_i]\\                    m_1n_2-m_2n_1=\Delta}}  f(m_1)g(m_2)\alpha_{n_1}\beta_{n_2},  \qquad \Delta\ne0,  \label{eq:tpc46-bc-determinant}$$ Corollary 1 assumes $$f^{(j)}\ll \eta^jM_1^{-j},  \qquad  g^{(j)}\ll \eta^jM_2^{-j}  \qquad (j\ge0),  \label{eq:tpc46-bc-smooth-slots}$$ and places arbitrary sequences only on $n_1,n_2$. With $$R_*=\frac{M_1N_2}{M_2N_1}+\frac{M_2N_1}{M_1N_2},  \label{eq:tpc46-bc-rstar}$$ the conclusion is $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal T_\Delta  ={}&  \sum_{\substack{n_i\in[N_i/2,N_i]\\(n_1,n_2)\mid\Delta}}  \frac{(n_1,n_2)}{n_1n_2}\alpha_{n_1}\beta_{n_2}  \int_{\mathbb R}  f\!\left(\frac{x+\Delta}{n_2}\right)  g\!\left(\frac{x}{n_1}\right)\,dx  \notag\\  &+O\!\left(  (\eta R_*)^{3/2}\|\alpha\|_2\|\beta\|_2  (N_1N_2)^{7/20}(N_1+N_2)^{1/4+\varepsilon}  (M_1M_2)^\varepsilon  \right).  \label{eq:tpc46-bc-corollary}\end{aligned}$$ Both smooth slots in [\[eq:tpc46-bc-smooth-slots\]](TPC-046.md#ref-62539f1aa0788626) are part of the hypothesis; they are not cosmetic remnants of the proof [`\citep[Corollary~1]{BC2018}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-046.md#cite-1d469e770d259123).

###### ref-94fbde1847e96f8b

###### ref-99b64c7431f245ed

For the TPC equation $$pr-mj=h,  \label{eq:tpc46-tpc-determinant-literature}$$ one favorable identification is $$(m_1,n_2,m_2,n_1)=(p,r,j,m).  \label{eq:tpc46-bc-slot-identification}$$ It places the cofactor and source weights in the two arbitrary slots and the orbit weight in one smooth slot, but it places the prime weight in the other smooth slot. Interchanging $p$ and $r$ merely replaces the missing prime slot by a missing Möbius slot. Other identifications put the nonsmooth source coefficient in a smooth slot. Since one factor from $pr$ and one factor from $mj$ must occupy the two smooth positions, and only the envelope in $j$ is intrinsically smooth, every identification loses at least one of the three arithmetic weights $p,r,m$. The actual fixed residue selector in $j$, retained in [\[eq:tpc46-three-coefficient-layer\]](TPC-046.md#ref-b3db5c7e42a5dd20), only makes direct applicability more restrictive. This is the exact coefficient-slot obstruction isolated geometrically in TPC–45 [`\citep{WangTPC45}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-046.md#cite-1802a7e8047e595c).

## What determinant-shift averaging actually buys

###### ref-1b85e7db54bda31f

###### ref-d173627ea3651499

###### ref-15a6315b198edbd9

There is a genuine use of determinant-shift averaging in the work of Bettin, Chandee, and Radziwiłł. After their smooth decomposition and Poisson summation, a relevant off-diagonal block contains, schematically, $$\sum_{n_1,n_2}  \sum_{0<|\Delta|\le D/d}  \sum_{0<k<H_d}  a_{dn_1}\overline{a_{dn_2}}  \mathrm e\!\left(-k\Delta\frac{\overline{n_1}}{n_2}\right)  \mathcal W_{k,\Delta,n_1,n_2},  \label{eq:tpc46-bcr-offdiagonal}$$ where $\mathcal W$ is supplied by the already smooth variables. They set $$b=k\Delta,  \qquad  \nu_{x,y}(b)  :=\sum_{k\Delta=b}  \mathrm e\!\left(-kx+\frac{\Delta y}{2\pi}\right).  \label{eq:tpc46-bcr-product-compression}$$ Consequently $$|\nu_{x,y}(b)|\le \tau(|b|),  \qquad  \|\nu_{x,y}\|_2\ll B^{1/2+\varepsilon}  \quad (|b|\ll B),  \label{eq:tpc46-bcr-nu-norm}$$ and the pair $(k,\Delta)$ becomes the single numerator sequence in [\[eq:tpc46-bc-form\]](TPC-046.md#ref-2f472a7ae6a3dfac). Bettin–Chandee cancellation is then available in that numerator range [`\citep[Section~5, especially the reduction preceding their small-frequency estimate]{BCR2017}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-046.md#cite-dce783658977a95f).

This mechanism says exactly what the average provides: it lengthens and structures the Kloosterman-numerator sequence. It is available only after Poisson summation has used smooth determinant variables. It does not replace either smooth hypothesis in [\[eq:tpc46-bc-smooth-slots\]](TPC-046.md#ref-62539f1aa0788626), and it does not turn [\[eq:tpc46-bc-corollary\]](TPC-046.md#ref-de58de5ad453a8c5) into a determinant theorem with arbitrary weights on $p,r,m$. An arbitrary external weight on $\Delta$ can also increase the multiplicative energy of the compressed sequence in [\[eq:tpc46-bcr-product-compression\]](TPC-046.md#ref-d173627ea3651499); its $\ell^2$ norm must be proved rather than silently identified with the product of two norms.

## Nearby dispersion estimates

###### ref-0142b733948593ae

###### ref-6db5c27b183f9e49

###### ref-0c7ba8f39c291bca

Fouvry and Radziwiłł study a different average. For divisor-bounded sequences $\alpha_m,\beta_n$, they average the congruence discrepancy $$\sum_{q\asymp Q}  \left|  \sum_{mn\equiv a\, (\bmod q)}\alpha_m\beta_n  -\frac1{\varphi(q)}  \sum_{(mn,q)=1}\alpha_m\beta_n  \right|  \label{eq:tpc46-fr-discrepancy}$$ over the modulus $q$, while the residue $a\ne0$ is fixed. Their first range, under the stated Siegel–Walfisz hypothesis on the short sequence, is $$\exp((\log X)^\varepsilon)  \le N  \le Q^{-11/12}X^{17/36-\varepsilon}.  \label{eq:tpc46-fr-range}$$ Their prime-modulus variant removes that Siegel–Walfisz input in the same power range [`\citep[Corollary~1.1 and Theorem~1.2]{FR2022}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-046.md#cite-fb39ce9a20fbe3c6). Wright’s partially fixed-modulus theorem estimates $$\sum_{\substack{a\asymp A,\ m\asymp M,\ n\asymp N\\(m,nR)=1}}  \nu_a\alpha_m\beta_n  \mathrm e\!\left(\vartheta\frac{a\overline m}{nR}\right)  \label{eq:tpc46-wright-form}$$ and improves the resulting unbalanced-convolution ranges, including $N\le Q^{-33/28}X^{17/28-\varepsilon}$ in the first regime [`\citep[Theorem~2.1 and Corollary~2.2]{Wright2026}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-046.md#cite-f8666ba79358b969). These are genuine advances for modulus dispersion and fixed denominator factors. Neither theorem averages the physical determinant shift in [\[eq:tpc46-tpc-determinant-literature\]](TPC-046.md#ref-94fbde1847e96f8b), supplies the missing determinant coefficient slot, or controls the inherited TPC masks after successive physical restrictions.

## Why a scalar black box would still not prove the atomic estimate

###### ref-81d34efd2c85ea0b

The external results above are scalar. The TPC target inherited from the projective and residual-fiber reductions is an atomic-energy estimate for a Hilbert-valued synthesis, not merely a scalar count [`\citep{WangTPC36,WangTPC42,WangTPC43,WangTPC45}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-046.md#cite-7286aa6023fa493f); [\[WangTPC42\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-046.md#cite-cc53a3263a6b378e); [\[WangTPC43\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-046.md#cite-26882076da806b18); [\[WangTPC45\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-046.md#cite-1802a7e8047e595c). Formally one may write $$\left\|\sum_e x_e\right\|_{\mathcal H}  =\sup_{\|v\|_{\mathcal H}=1}  \left|\sum_e\langle x_e,v\rangle_{\mathcal H}\right|.  \label{eq:tpc46-dual-scalarization}$$ But the scalar coefficient $\langle x_{p,r,m,j},v\rangle$ produced by [\[eq:tpc46-dual-scalarization\]](TPC-046.md#ref-81d34efd2c85ea0b) generally couples all four indices. It need not factor into the separate sequences required by [\[eq:tpc46-bc-form\]](TPC-046.md#ref-2f472a7ae6a3dfac) or [\[eq:tpc46-bc-determinant\]](TPC-046.md#ref-4684bf48a00ec97b). Uniformity of [\[eq:tpc46-bc-theorem\]](TPC-046.md#ref-3daa5dd9bdb5148b) over each of its three sequences does not imply uniformity over such an entangled four-index tensor.

There is a second normalization mismatch. Squaring a scalar estimate and summing over ambient coordinates naturally returns a Cartesian tensor energy, or after shift averaging the sum of diagonals over all shifts. The physical TPC denominator retains only the atomic diagonal at the prescribed shift and after the actual projective identifications. TPC–36 and TPC–42 show why these identifications and coherent defects cannot be discarded; TPC–43 and TPC–45 show why physical sign restriction and large-prime descent must be applied to one globally consistent coefficient packet. None of [\[eq:tpc46-bc-theorem\]](TPC-046.md#ref-3daa5dd9bdb5148b), [\[eq:tpc46-bc-corollary\]](TPC-046.md#ref-de58de5ad453a8c5), [\[eq:tpc46-fr-discrepancy\]](TPC-046.md#ref-0142b733948593ae), or [\[eq:tpc46-wright-form\]](TPC-046.md#ref-0c7ba8f39c291bca) contains this atomic normalization or proves stability under those restrictions.

We therefore use the cited literature only for comparison. It does not prove the vector atomic estimate studied here. Conversely, the slot and normalization mismatches are not impossibility theorems: a future positive result could exploit the special prime/Möbius packet, prove a restriction-stable tensor estimate, or establish a separate localization principle. Such an input would be new and would have to state these features explicitly.

# The next gate

The preceding theorems split the route beyond TPC–45 into four logically different tasks.

## Orbit-frequency regularity of the actual coefficient

###### ref-969843d0965a4722

The spectral gap in [6](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-046.md#src-905a3db8ab77ad40) is stable under a Hilbert vector $v_{p,r,m}$ that is independent of $j$, or more generally under a uniformly band-limited $j$-dependence. The actual dual coefficient has the form $$z_{\mathrm{output\ row},\,j,\,d(m)r}.  \label{eq:tpc46-next-dual-field}$$ No theorem above bounds its orbit bandwidth uniformly over the unit ball of the dual output space. A positive continuation would therefore need either

1.  a restriction-stable frequency decomposition of the actual mask and output field, with projective mass $X^{o(1)}$; or

2.  a vector-valued sampling theorem that controls [\[eq:tpc46-next-dual-field\]](TPC-046.md#ref-969843d0965a4722) without first replacing it by a scalar smooth profile.

The fixed-column decomposition of TPC–36 supplies the scalar smooth profile before dualization, not either of these stronger conclusions.

## Atomic, rather than Cartesian, normalization

###### ref-d1459a9b771f3c86

###### ref-3476c23b7421c340

The fixed-shift tensor theorem naturally gives $$X^{o(1)}\mathcal D_\otimes  =X^{o(1)}\sum_h\Delta_h.  \label{eq:tpc46-next-tensor-scale}$$ The physical target instead asks for a bound on the synthesis at one shift by $$X^{1/400+o(1)}\Delta_{h_0}  \label{eq:tpc46-next-atomic-scale}$$ after the actual output identifications. The examples in [8](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-046.md#src-1f0824683b72b720) show that no theorem for arbitrary three coefficient slots can bridge [\[eq:tpc46-next-tensor-scale\]](TPC-046.md#ref-d1459a9b771f3c86) and [\[eq:tpc46-next-atomic-scale\]](TPC-046.md#ref-3476c23b7421c340). The next arithmetic input must use the actual source phases, the projective masks, or cancellation between residual fibers. Merely retaining Möbius symbols is insufficient: TPC–45 proves that their product is constant on each equal-residual fiber.

## Alias unfolding and localization

The TPC alias family samples the determinant at spacing $M_{\rm al}=X^{399/400+o(1)}$. Its spectrum is the folded sum in [\[eq:tpc46-exact-alias-fold\]](TPC-046.md#ref-072e2e2c26090527); a gap in the completed consecutive-shift family does not survive that folding without an additional anti-alias estimate. Even an averaged estimate on a consecutive window does not select $h_0$ without a maximal or regularity theorem, and the localization factor in [\[lem:tpc46-weighted-localization\]](TPC-046.md#ref-ff9e512e07a58931) is sharp.

###### ref-d40bd9c4ab44e2a5

A useful positive target is therefore a coefficient-specific inequality of the following schematic form, for $H_*\ge1$: $$\sup_{|h-h_0|\le H_*}  \frac{\|F_h\|^2}{\Delta_h+\delta_X}  \ll X^{o(1)}  \left[   \frac1{H_*}\sum_{|h-h_0|\le H_*}   \frac{\|F_h\|^2}{\Delta_h+\delta_X}  \right],  \label{eq:tpc46-next-maximal-target}$$ where fixed normalization constants in the window average are inessential. Here $\delta_X>0$ is a declared regularizer; one also needs uniform packet provenance and a range $H_*$ that overlaps the analytic averaging scale. Formula [\[eq:tpc46-next-maximal-target\]](TPC-046.md#ref-d40bd9c4ab44e2a5) is a target, not an assumption or a consequence of the present paper.

## A reversible continuation

The next paper may pursue any of the following doors.

1.  Prove orbit-frequency decay for one actual projected source/mask sector and transfer a nonempty part of the shift-spectral gap to that sector.

2.  Establish an atomic-normalized estimate on one additional cofactor block, even after averaging a fixed finite set of output rows.

3.  Prove an anti-alias or maximal-in-shift theorem connecting the completed family to the sparse TPC alias family.

4.  Construct actual-mask coefficients that saturate the tensor-to-atomic or fiber frontier. This would give a sharp negative boundary for the present route without asserting that the full physical coefficient is large.

Success at any door is a genuine advance. Failure at one does not invalidate the spectral gap, tensor closure, or sharp boundary results proved here. None of these doors may be relabelled as a fixed-shift prime-pair asymptotic or a breach of the parity barrier.
