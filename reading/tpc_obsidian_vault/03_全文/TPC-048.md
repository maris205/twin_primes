## Abstract

The preceding twin-prime-correlation (TPC) analysis transferred a mesoscopic shift-spectral gap to one canonical orbit-frequency band of the actual residual coefficient field, but left the orthogonal complement uncontrolled. We resolve the resulting all-band geometry. On the quotient circle associated with a fixed progression modulo $H_0$, we construct an exact partition into $L$ translated residue-comb cells and the corresponding orthogonal orbit projectors. Every determinant modulation $m\alpha$ then factors through an exact input-output band matrix whose nonzero entries are resonance tubes. At the critical resolution $L\asymp J$, every band has bounded physical-window trace and each tube has $\alpha$-half-width $(JM)^{-1}=X^{-1+o(1)}$. Thus the original fine-frequency scale is restored as a complete square-function decomposition, with no band cover-count loss.

All-band Parseval alone is not a large-sieve estimate. We prove the sharp boundary: for arbitrary independent orbit inputs on $K$ source slopes, the unlabelled determinant synthesis has operator norm squared exactly $K$. The actual projective layers possess additional structure, namely one common smooth orbit profile. Exploiting it, we prove the Hilbert-valued bound $$\|\widehat{\mathcal F}^{\omega}(\alpha)\|^2  \ll_{\phi_\omega,H_0}  \frac{J}{H_0}  \min\!\left\{K,1+\frac1{J\|\alpha\|_{\mathbb T}}\right\}  \sum_m\|B_{\omega,m}(\alpha)\|^2$$ for $\alpha\ne0$, under an explicit no-wrap condition; at $\alpha=0$ the collision factor is $K$. The factor is sharp, up to constants, for common-profile slope boxes containing the corresponding consecutive cluster. Minkowski reassembles the frozen family built from the complete signed actual $h_0$-coefficient field and produces a canonical projective $m$-square envelope; at each fixed $m$, distinct cofactors retain their literal residual labels $d(m)r$ and are orthogonal before residual grouping.

For $M=X^{267/400+o(1)}$ and $J=X^{133/400+o(1)}$, a dense slope box has critical coarse-edge collision exponent $67/200$. In that sharp model class the common profile saves the full orbit length over arbitrary-slope synthesis, but its loss still exceeds the TPC allowance $X^{1/400+o(1)}$ by $X^{133/400+o(1)}$. No matching lower bound is assumed for the literal slope support. We also prove exact first and second moments for the two-dimensional tube load and a logarithmic annular resonance budget relative to any fixed representation with bounded displayed projective square envelope. Consequently the orbit-band reassembly problem is reduced, not the fixed-shift prime-pair problem: atomic normalization, internal prime cancellation, residual grouping, alias unfolding, and localization of the full physical atom remain open. No parity-barrier breach or twin-prime conclusion is claimed.

# Introduction

###### ref-0c4e7c080d3cc08e

The large-prime descent in the TPC sequence produces the determinant equation $$pr-mj=h_0,  \label{eq:tpc48-intro-determinant}$$ with $m\asymp M$, an orbit length $J$, and a literal residual coordinate $s=d(m)r$. TPC–46 completed the consecutive-shift family and found a mesoscopic shift-spectral gap when the output vector is independent of the orbit coordinate $j$ [`\citep{WangTPC46}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-048.md#cite-2f37865d719a8eb5). TPC–47 then projected the actual $j$-dependent residual coefficient onto one complete residue-comb band. Its canonical low core vanishes there on a nonempty shift interval, while the full frozen family has arbitrary-power leakage [`\citep{WangTPC47}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-048.md#cite-0276fa4f583cdc92). That theorem is genuinely sectorial: it neither controls the orthogonal orbit-frequency complement nor gives a lower bound for the mass retained by the chosen band.

The natural continuation is to tile the entire orbit-frequency circle and retain orthogonality through determinant dilation. Two different questions are hidden in that sentence.

1.  Can the full orbit field be decomposed into exact input-output resonance channels without paying the number of bands?

2.  Does that decomposition by itself reduce coherent summation over the source slopes $m$?

The answer to the first question is yes. The answer to the second is no for arbitrary orbit inputs, but becomes quantitatively positive for the common smooth profiles supplied by the actual projective reassembly. This positive/negative distinction is the main result of the paper.

## Critical orbit tiling

###### ref-02903827f5de66ae

###### ref-d5d430ab54cab726

Let $G=H_0^{-1}\mathbb Z/\mathbb Z\subset\mathbb T$, and partition the quotient $\mathbb T/G$ into $L$ half-open cells. Their inverse images $\mathcal C_k$, $k\in\mathbb Z/L\mathbb Z$, form translated full residue combs of measure $1/L$. If $\Pi_k$ is the corresponding orbit Fourier projector, then $$\Pi_k\Pi_\ell=0\quad(k\ne\ell),  \qquad  \sum_{k\bmod L}\Pi_k=I  \label{eq:tpc48-intro-resolution}$$ in the strong operator topology. On a scalar orbit window of $N$ integers, $$\operatorname{tr}(E_{I,v}^*\Pi_kE_{I,v})=\frac NL.  \label{eq:tpc48-intro-band-trace}$$ Here $E_{I,v}$ denotes zero extension on one fixed unit non-orbit fiber; it is defined precisely in [3](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-048.md#src-8aafc3f134d91b0e). The critical choice $L\asymp J$ therefore has bounded trace per band. It sacrifices the growing single-band trace of TPC–47, but no mass hypothesis is then needed because the orthogonal sum of all bands is the entire orbit space.

###### ref-5e79b461fd9de07f

###### ref-9f1ccf3a59d60a42

###### ref-82bee1c53c57297e

For the modulation $$(U_{m,\alpha}y)_j=\mathrm e(mj\alpha)y_j,  \label{eq:tpc48-intro-modulation}$$ the block $\Pi_kU_{m,\alpha}\Pi_\ell$ is supported precisely where the shifted input cell meets the output cell. After setting $q=k-\ell\pmod L$, the support condition is $$d_G(m\alpha,c_q)<\Delta,  \qquad  c_q=\frac{q}{LH_0},  \qquad  \Delta=\frac1{LH_0}.  \label{eq:tpc48-intro-tube}$$ Here $d_G(x,y):=\operatorname{dist}(x-y,G)$. Thus every channel is a union of exact determinant resonance tubes. At $m\asymp M$, their half-width in $\alpha$ is $$\frac{\Delta}{m}\asymp\frac1{LM}.  \label{eq:tpc48-intro-tube-width}$$ The critical choice $L\asymp J$ restores the fine scale $(JM)^{-1}=X^{-1+o(1)}$. This is a square-function resolution, not a spectral gap.

## The arbitrary-input barrier

###### ref-9cad5615b3211622

###### ref-558784b64bff499e

Write $K=|\mathcal M|$ for the number of supported slopes and define the unlabelled synthesis $$T_\alpha((y_m)_{m\in\mathcal M})  :=\sum_{m\in\mathcal M}U_{m,\alpha}y_m.  \label{eq:tpc48-intro-unrestricted-synthesis}$$ As an operator from $\bigoplus_m\widetilde\mathcal H$ to $\widetilde\mathcal H$, it satisfies the exact identity $$\boxed{\|T_\alpha\|^2=K.}  \label{eq:tpc48-intro-unrestricted-norm}$$ Indeed, the upper bound is Cauchy–Schwarz, while $y_m=U_{m,\alpha}^{-1}g$ makes every output equal to the same vector $g$. Decomposing each $y_m$ into all input bands does not change this example: the input band can compensate the determinant translation. Consequently all-band Parseval is not, by itself, a large-sieve gain.

###### ref-46b6b71b1f1a3e1c

We make the obstruction more precise by retaining the difference channel $q$. The squared norm of the resulting channel-lift operator is exactly the essential maximum $R_L(\alpha)$ of the pointwise resonance-tube multiplicity. The physical channel collapse has norm squared $L$, and $$K\le L R_L(\alpha).  \label{eq:tpc48-intro-factor-barrier}$$ Both constants are optimal in their declared arbitrary-input classes. No statement here says that the actual TPC coefficient saturates these classes.

## The common-profile large sieve

###### ref-b86505f4f20ea77e

###### ref-9213ad285f15cea1

###### ref-c6b5c98d64061404

The inherited projective representation has additional layerwise structure. Every projective layer has one common sampled profile $\phi_\omega(j/J)$ for all source slopes. After grouping the prime and cofactor variables at fixed $m$, its full shift transform is $$\widehat{\mathcal F}^{\omega}(\alpha)  =\sum_{\substack{j\in\mathbb Z\\j\equiv a_\omega\ (H_0)}}  \phi_\omega(j/J)  \left(\sum_m B_{\omega,m}(\alpha)\mathrm e(mj\alpha)\right)  \otimes e_j.  \label{eq:tpc48-intro-layer-normal-form}$$ Let $t=\|\alpha\|_\mathbb T$, with $D_J(0;K):=K$. Under the no-wrap condition $$H_0(M_+-M_-)t\le\frac14,  \label{eq:tpc48-intro-no-wrap}$$ we prove $$\boxed{  \|\widehat{\mathcal F}^{\omega}(\alpha)\|^2  \ll_{\phi_\omega,H_0}  \frac{J}{H_0}D_J(t;K)  \sum_m\|B_{\omega,m}(\alpha)\|^2,}  \qquad  D_J(t;K):=\min\left\{K,1+\frac1{Jt}\right\}\quad(t>0).  \label{eq:tpc48-intro-large-sieve}$$ The proof is a progression Poisson estimate followed by the Schur test for the vector-valued Gram matrix, in the classical large-sieve tradition [`\citep{IwaniecKowalski2004}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-048.md#cite-49dfea87eaada156). A cluster of consecutive slopes shows that $D_J(t;K)$ is sharp up to constants for common-profile slope boxes containing a sufficiently long consecutive cluster.

The full signed actual coefficient is not one projective layer. Minkowski reassembles all layers and leaves a declared projective $m$-square envelope. This is an exact reduction, not an estimate of that envelope by the physical atomic diagonal. At fixed $m$, different cofactors $r$ occupy the distinct residual coordinates $d(m)r$, so the remaining inner object is an exact $r$-square sum of prime exponential sums.

## Endpoint ledger and claim boundary

###### ref-863456f6a0d1bea1

###### ref-0aff071951574acf

###### ref-9e006632964cae23

At the inherited endpoint, $$M=X^{267/400+o(1)},  \qquad  J=X^{133/400+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc48-intro-endpoint}$$ The critical band count is $L=X^{133/400+o(1)}$. On a coarse-edge shift scale $t\asymp M^{-1}$, a dense slope box has normalized collision factor $$D_J(t;K)=X^{67/200+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc48-intro-critical-load}$$ The TPC allowance is only $X^{1/400+o(1)}$, leaving the exact exponent gap $$\frac{67}{200}-\frac1{400}=\frac{133}{400}.  \label{eq:tpc48-intro-allowance-gap}$$ Thus, in the dense sharpness class, the large sieve gains the full orbit length relative to arbitrary slopewise Cauchy, but it does not close atomic normalization. This ledger is not a lower bound for the literal slope support, whose cardinality may be smaller.

The analysis concerns the frozen shift family built from the literal physical $h_0$-coefficient field. Only $h=h_0$ is the inherited physical residual synthesis, and that object belongs to the low-sign-averaged residual Gram route rather than the native affine physical face. Nothing below proves an atomic Bessel bound, controls the ultra-low or alias complements, reconstructs physical packets at new shifts, breaks the parity barrier, or establishes a prime-pair asymptotic.

# The inherited actual residual interface

We state the exact coefficient field before introducing any band decomposition. This prevents an abstract all-band identity from being substituted for the physical source-mask coefficient.

## Physical and frozen families

###### ref-b7a4aa2a76a7ece3

Fix $h_0\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}$, put $H_0=|h_0|$, and let a source row be $\rho=(\ell_\rho,d_\rho)$, with $$m_\rho=\ell_\rho d_\rho\asymp M,  \qquad  M=X^{267/400+o(1)},  \qquad  J=X^{133/400+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc48-row-scales}$$ On the inherited dyadic row cell, write $$0<M_-\le m_\rho\le M_+,  \qquad M_\pm=X^{267/400+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc48-source-interval}$$ As an inherited support hypothesis from the row parametrization in TPC–45/47, the integer $m_\rho$ determines the row $\rho$ on this cell [`\citep{WangTPC45,WangTPC47}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-048.md#cite-1802a7e8047e595c); [\[WangTPC47\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-048.md#cite-0276fa4f583cdc92). Let $$\mathcal M:=\{m_\rho:\rho\text{ occurs on the inherited support}\},  \qquad K:=|\mathcal M|\le MX^{o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc48-supported-slopes}$$ No matching lower bound for the actual value of $K$ is assumed.

Let $\mathcal O_{\mathrm L}$ be the left-side opposite-row set, and let $\mathcal S$ contain all supported residual labels. Put $$\mathcal V_{\mathrm L}=\ell^2(\mathcal O_{\mathrm L}\times\mathcal S),  \qquad  \mathcal H_{\mathrm L}=\mathcal V_{\mathrm L}\widehat\otimes\ell^2(\mathbb Z).  \label{eq:tpc48-output-space}$$ For a supported cofactor $r$, the literal physical residual is $$s=d_\rho r.  \label{eq:tpc48-residual-label}$$ After removal of zero atoms, $d_\rho,r,s$ are squarefree and $\mu(s)=\mu(d_\rho)\mu(r)$ [`\citep{WangTPC43,WangTPC45,WangTPC46}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-048.md#cite-26882076da806b18); [\[WangTPC45\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-048.md#cite-1802a7e8047e595c); [\[WangTPC46\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-048.md#cite-2f37865d719a8eb5).

Let $\gamma_\rho^{(1)}$ be the source coefficient, $\mathfrak A_{\rho,\gamma}^{\rm act}(j)$ the literal joint source-mask multiplier, and $\Lambda(n)=(\log n)\Phi_0(n/X)$. The left physical large-prime residual synthesis is $$\mathcal F_{h_0}^{\rm act,\mathrm L}  =\sum_{\substack{\gamma\in\mathcal O_{\mathrm L},\ j\in\mathbb Z,\ \rho,\ p,\ r\\                    pr-m_\rho j=h_0}}  \gamma_\rho^{(1)}  \mathfrak A_{\rho,\gamma}^{\rm act}(j)  \mu(r)\Lambda(pr)  e_{\gamma,d_\rho r}\otimes e_j,  \label{eq:tpc48-physical-vector}$$ with the inherited prime, terminal, row, and orbit restrictions understood. Every coefficient is zero off those finite supports. TPC–45/46 identify [\[eq:tpc48-physical-vector\]](TPC-048.md#ref-c841794576b7f9c4) with the negative large-prime residual synthesis $-\sum_pUx_p$. It has the literal actual coefficients, but is not the native affine physical Gram face; their cross inner products need not agree [`\citep{WangTPC45,WangTPC46,WangTPC47}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-048.md#cite-1802a7e8047e595c); [\[WangTPC46\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-048.md#cite-2f37865d719a8eb5); [\[WangTPC47\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-048.md#cite-0276fa4f583cdc92).

For every $h\in\mathbb Z$, define $\mathcal F_h^{\rm fr,\mathrm L}$ by replacing the determinant equation in [\[eq:tpc48-physical-vector\]](TPC-048.md#ref-c841794576b7f9c4) with $$pr-m_\rho j=h  \label{eq:tpc48-frozen-determinant}$$ and freezing every row, support, coefficient, mask, and residual label constructed at $h_0$. ## Coefficientwise signed reassembly

###### ref-0342a8ff854a9470

###### ref-a56e33e44f4b3d51

For a supported source integer $m=\ell d(m)$ and cofactor $r$, define the complete vector profile $$G_{m,r}^{\mathrm L}(j)  :=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal O_{\mathrm L}}  \gamma_m^{(1)}\mathfrak A_{m,\gamma}^{\rm act}(j)  e_{\gamma,d(m)r}\in\mathcal V_{\mathrm L}.  \label{eq:tpc48-vector-profile}$$ Then $$\mathcal F_h^{\rm fr,\mathrm L}  =\sum_{p,r,m,j}  \mu(r)\Lambda(pr)G_{m,r}^{\mathrm L}(j)\otimes e_j  \mathbf 1_{pr-mj=h}.  \label{eq:tpc48-vector-frozen-family}$$

###### ref-0233b86112f007a2

The TPC–36 projective row-mask theorem, together with the inherited large-prime and Mellin separations, gives an exact signed Bochner representation [`\citep{WangTPC25,WangTPC36,WangTPC47}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-048.md#cite-39e8dc14c55a2411); [\[WangTPC36\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-048.md#cite-7286aa6023fa493f); [\[WangTPC47\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-048.md#cite-0276fa4f583cdc92): $$\mathcal F_h^{\rm fr,\mathrm L}=\int\mathcal F_h^\omega\,d\nu(\omega),  \label{eq:tpc48-signed-reassembly}$$ where $$\mathcal F_h^\omega  =\sum_{p,r,m}  \sum_{\substack{j\in\mathbb Z\\j\equiv a_\omega\!\!\pmod{H_0}}}  A_\omega(p,r,m)\phi_\omega(j/J)  v_{\omega,p,r,m}\otimes e_j\,  \mathbf 1_{pr-mj=h}.  \label{eq:tpc48-projective-layer}$$ Here $v_{\omega,p,r,m}\in\mathcal V_{\mathrm L}$ is independent of $j$ and is supported on coordinates $(\gamma,d(m)r)$. Every $\phi_\omega\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$. After one fixed common cutoff, these profiles have common compact support and uniform fixed-order $C^B$-seminorm control; for every fixed $B$, the absolute projective mass weighted by $\|\phi_\omega\|_{C^B}$ is $X^{o(1)}$.

The representation is coefficientwise before terminal summation. In particular, every orthogonal orbit projector below is applied to the already reassembled profile [\[eq:tpc48-vector-profile\]](TPC-048.md#ref-0342a8ff854a9470); the projective layers are used to prove a common-profile inequality, never to select a favorable summand. Sharp orbit projectors may create sinc tails, so all band decompositions are understood in $\ell^2$, not in $\ell^1$.

# Critical tiling of the orbit-frequency quotient

###### ref-3a7babc1f22d89b2

###### ref-1fb1f90b16046514

Let $\mathcal V$ be a Hilbert space carrying every non-orbit label and put $$\widetilde\mathcal H=\mathcal V\widehat\otimes\ell^2(\mathbb Z).  \label{eq:tpc48-abstract-orbit-space}$$ The orbit Fourier transform $$\mathscr F_j:\ell^2(\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow L^2(\mathbb T),  \qquad  (\mathscr F_jy)(\beta)=\sum_jy_j\mathrm e(-j\beta),  \label{eq:tpc48-orbit-Fourier}$$ is unitary.

## Half-open residue-comb cells

###### ref-b1b4b4a75d6bfc8d

###### ref-846fc3048d038f26

Fix integers $H_0,L\ge1$, let $$G:=H_0^{-1}\mathbb Z/\mathbb Z\subset\mathbb T,  \qquad  \Delta:=\frac1{LH_0},  \qquad  c_k:=\frac{k}{LH_0}\quad(k\in\mathbb Z/L\mathbb Z),  \label{eq:tpc48-quotient-scales}$$ and use the quotient coordinate $\pi(\beta)=H_0\beta\pmod1$. Let $$I_k:=\left[\frac{k-1/2}{L},\frac{k+1/2}{L}\right)  \pmod1,  \qquad  \mathcal C_k:=\pi^{-1}(I_k).  \label{eq:tpc48-comb-cells}$$ Thus $\mathcal C_k$ is the union of $H_0$ half-open arcs, each centered at $c_k+\tau/H_0$ and having length $\Delta$.

###### ref-f83f321a0c47d3a3

###### ref-cd36750dbcb831cf

###### ref-67fd8fbeba7402e2

###### ref-be674e6832d57092

> **Proposition: Exact residue-comb partition** \[prop:tpc48-comb-partition\] The cells satisfy $$\mathbb T=\bigsqcup_{k\bmod L}\mathcal C_k\quad\text{a.e.},  \qquad  |\mathcal C_k|=\frac1L.  \label{eq:tpc48-comb-partition}$$ The operators $$\Pi_k:=I_\mathcal V\otimes\mathscr F_j^{-1}  M_{\mathbf 1_{\mathcal C_k}}\mathscr F_j  \label{eq:tpc48-band-projectors}$$ are orthogonal projections and give the strong resolution $$\Pi_k\Pi_\ell=\mathbf 1_{k=\ell}\Pi_k,  \qquad  \sum_{k\bmod L}\Pi_k=I_{\widetilde\mathcal H}.  \label{eq:tpc48-projector-resolution}$$ They commute with every bounded operator acting only on $\mathcal V$.

> **Proof** The intervals $I_k$ partition the quotient circle and all have measure $1/L$. Pullback under $\pi$ preserves normalized Haar measure. Multiplication by their indicators gives mutually orthogonal projections summing to the identity on $L^2(\mathbb T)$. Conjugate by $\mathscr F_j$ and tensor with $I_\mathcal V$. The commutation statement follows because the operators act on different tensor factors.

The half-open convention affects only null boundaries. Centered closed arcs are convenient for support statements, but would overlap at their endpoints and should not be used to state an exact partition.

## Physical-window trace

###### ref-ca21a127caec6a41

Let $I\subset\mathbb Z$ contain $N$ orbit times, fix a unit vector $v\in\mathcal V$, and let $$E_{I,v}:\ell^2(I)\longrightarrow\widetilde\mathcal H,  \qquad  E_{I,v}a:=v\otimes\widetilde a,  \label{eq:tpc48-scalar-fiber-inclusion}$$ where $\widetilde a$ is zero extension to $\ell^2(\mathbb Z)$. This isolates one scalar non-orbit fiber and avoids taking a trace over $\mathcal V$.

###### ref-e330922430d4ae2a

###### ref-c57b308d1c396422

> **Theorem: Exact per-band trace** \[thm:tpc48-band-trace\] On this scalar non-orbit fiber, $$\boxed{  \operatorname{tr}(E_{I,v}^*\Pi_kE_{I,v})  =\|\Pi_kE_{I,v}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2  =\frac NL.}  \label{eq:tpc48-band-trace}$$ If only the progression $j\equiv a\pmod{H_0}$ is retained, the trace is $|I\cap(a+H_0\mathbb Z)|/L$. Summing over $k$ recovers the dimension of the corresponding finite orbit window.

> **Proof** The diagonal Fourier matrix coefficient of $\Pi_k$ is $|\mathcal C_k|=1/L$. Summation over the selected orbit coordinates proves the trace formula. Orthogonality gives the Hilbert–Schmidt identity.

###### ref-212e57b7ef90776d

At the inherited scale $N\asymp J$, the critical choice $$L\asymp J  \label{eq:tpc48-critical-L}$$ has bounded trace per full scalar orbit fiber. The individual compressed operators remain positive contractions rather than finite window projections, but the enlarged-space resolution [\[eq:tpc48-projector-resolution\]](TPC-048.md#ref-be674e6832d57092) is exact.

## Conservation under residual grouping

###### ref-444a2e2cdca204cf

###### ref-437a60d3098c72d9

Let $C$ be any bounded operator on the non-orbit factor; in the physical application it is the residual grouping map of TPC–47. Since $C\Pi_k=\Pi_kC$, Parseval gives $$\sum_{k\bmod L}\|C\Pi_ky\|^2=\|Cy\|^2  \le\|C\|^2\|y\|^2.  \label{eq:tpc48-grouped-band-conservation}$$ On the active residual space of TPC–47, $$\|C\|^2=\rho_{\rm res},  \qquad  \rho_{\rm res}=\max_\gamma|\mathcal S_\gamma|.  \label{eq:tpc48-grouping-norm}$$ Thus all-band tiling introduces no additional grouping loss, but it does not bound $\rho_{\rm res}$ by $X^{o(1)}$.

## The input-output band matrix

###### ref-f89219f6dfd56380

For $m\ge1$, $\alpha\in\mathbb T$, and $q\in\mathbb Z/L\mathbb Z$, define $$R_{m,q}(\alpha)  :=\sum_{\ell\bmod L}  \Pi_{\ell+q}U_{m\alpha}\Pi_\ell.  \label{eq:tpc48-channel-operator}$$ All indices in this section are modulo $L$.

###### ref-5bb7e5ea5ec85916

###### ref-c347467dad2a6de5

###### ref-f99e577b959cfcbb

> **Theorem: Exact resonance-channel resolution** \[thm:tpc48-channel-resolution\] For every $m,\alpha$, $$U_{m\alpha}=\sum_qR_{m,q}(\alpha),  \qquad  \sum_q\|R_{m,q}(\alpha)y\|^2=\|y\|^2.  \label{eq:tpc48-channel-resolution}$$ Moreover, $$R_{m,q}(\alpha)\ne0  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad  d_G(m\alpha,c_q)<\Delta,  \label{eq:tpc48-channel-support}$$ where $d_G(x,y)=\operatorname{dist}(x-y,G)$. For almost every $\alpha$, exactly two values of $q$ satisfy [\[eq:tpc48-channel-support\]](TPC-048.md#ref-f99e577b959cfcbb).

###### ref-be660736d4d5d227

> **Proof** Insert the two resolutions of the identity around $U_{m\alpha}$: $$U_{m\alpha}=\sum_{k,\ell}\Pi_kU_{m\alpha}\Pi_\ell,$$ and group the pair by $q=k-\ell$. In orbit Fourier variables, the $(k,\ell)$-block is multiplication by $$\mathbf 1_{\mathcal C_k}(\beta)  \mathbf 1_{\mathcal C_\ell}(\beta-m\alpha),  \label{eq:tpc48-tile-multiplier}$$ followed by translation. For fixed $m,\alpha,\beta$, there is one output cell $k$ containing $\beta$ and one input cell $\ell$ containing $\beta-m\alpha$. Hence exactly one $q=k-\ell$ contributes pointwise. This proves both identities in [\[eq:tpc48-channel-resolution\]](TPC-048.md#ref-c347467dad2a6de5).
>
> The shifted input interval and the output interval have equal length $1/L$ in the quotient coordinate. Their intersection has positive measure precisely when their centers are separated by less than $1/L$, which is [\[eq:tpc48-channel-support\]](TPC-048.md#ref-f99e577b959cfcbb) after division by $H_0$. A point on a circle partitioned by centers spaced $1/L$ lies within distance $1/L$ of exactly two centers, except at the finitely many center points. This proves the last statement.

###### ref-0e25eec1eeb0b2eb

Define the resonance tube $$\mathfrak T_{m,q}^{(L,H_0)}  :=\{\alpha\in\mathbb T:d_G(m\alpha,c_q)<\Delta\}.  \label{eq:tpc48-resonance-tube}$$

###### ref-e49150b8445c72dd

###### ref-62574a1213da7dab

###### ref-639cb9e632734692

###### ref-c7ca2ec01f9ed689

> **Corollary: Tube measure and branch width** \[cor:tpc48-tube-geometry\] For every $m,q$, $$|\mathfrak T_{m,q}^{(L,H_0)}|=\frac2L.  \label{eq:tpc48-tube-measure}$$ It is the union of $mH_0$ intervals, each with half-width $$\frac{\Delta}{m}=\frac1{LH_0m}.  \label{eq:tpc48-tube-halfwidth}$$ For almost every $\alpha$, $$\sum_{q\bmod L}\mathbf 1_{\mathfrak T_{m,q}^{(L,H_0)}}(\alpha)=2.  \label{eq:tpc48-two-active-tubes}$$

> **Proof** Multiplication by $m$ preserves normalized Haar measure on $\mathbb T$. The target neighborhood is a union of $H_0$ arcs of total measure $2H_0\Delta=2/L$. Pulling those arcs back gives the branch count and half-width. The final identity is the last assertion of [\[thm:tpc48-channel-resolution\]](TPC-048.md#ref-5bb7e5ea5ec85916).

###### ref-540d4841c741d160

At $L\asymp J$ and $m\asymp M$, $$\frac1{LH_0m}=X^{-1+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc48-critical-tube-scale}$$ No band summation is involved in this scale: the two active channels already give the exact resolution of one determinant modulation.

## The optimal channel-lift constant

###### ref-d4e5dc7b88d007dd

###### ref-ff92f149810b0cbc

###### ref-b760f4710055e873

Let $\mathcal M\subset\mathbb N$ be a finite set of distinct positive slopes and put $K=|\mathcal M|$. For $\bm y=(y_m)_{m\in\mathcal M}\in\bigoplus_m\widetilde\mathcal H$, define $$(\mathcal A_{\alpha,L}\bm y)_q  :=\sum_{m\in\mathcal M}R_{m,q}(\alpha)y_m  \quad\in\widetilde\mathcal H.  \label{eq:tpc48-channel-lift}$$ For almost every $\beta$, let $k(\beta)$ be the unique cell index with $\beta\in\mathcal C_{k(\beta)}$, and define $$\begin{aligned}  n_{\alpha,L}(\beta,q)  &:=\#\{m\in\mathcal M:        \beta-m\alpha\in\mathcal C_{k(\beta)-q}\},  \label{eq:tpc48-pointwise-load}\\  R_L(\alpha;\mathcal M)  &:=\max_{q\bmod L}\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{\beta\in\mathbb T}  n_{\alpha,L}(\beta,q).  \label{eq:tpc48-max-load}\end{aligned}$$

###### ref-cfe417e8750bc8cf

###### ref-2bd9029161a0c510

###### ref-0d7480fb42357ab3

> **Theorem: Exact channel-lift norm** \[thm:tpc48-channel-norm\] The optimal Hilbert-space constant is $$\boxed{\|\mathcal A_{\alpha,L}\|^2=R_L(\alpha;\mathcal M).}  \label{eq:tpc48-channel-norm}$$ In particular, $$\sum_q\left\|  \sum_mR_{m,q}(\alpha)y_m  \right\|^2  \le R_L(\alpha;\mathcal M)\sum_m\|y_m\|^2.  \label{eq:tpc48-channel-square-function}$$

> **Proof** After orbit Fourier transform, the $q$-output at $\beta$ is the sum of exactly the input fibers counted by $n_{\alpha,L}(\beta,q)$. Pointwise Cauchy–Schwarz, followed by integration and the one-channel-per-input identity in [\[thm:tpc48-channel-resolution\]](TPC-048.md#ref-5bb7e5ea5ec85916), gives the upper bound.
>
> For the reverse inequality, choose $q$ and a positive-measure set $E$ on which the same set $\mathcal M_0$ of $R_L(\alpha;\mathcal M)$ slopes is counted. Such a set exists because only finitely many subsets occur. Fix a unit vector $v\in\mathcal V$, and for $m\in\mathcal M_0$ set $$\widehat y_m(\gamma)=\mathbf 1_{E-m\alpha}(\gamma)v;$$ set all other inputs to zero. Every selected modulation produces $\mathbf 1_Ev$ in the same $q$-channel. The squared output/input norm ratio is therefore $|\mathcal M_0|=R_L(\alpha;\mathcal M)$.

###### ref-4c322f45bd4dd80e

For every fixed $\beta$, the $K$ slopes are distributed among $L$ values of $q$. Hence $$R_L(\alpha;\mathcal M)\ge\left\lceil\frac KL\right\rceil.  \label{eq:tpc48-load-pigeonhole}$$ This lower bound is geometric and makes no assertion about which input vectors occur in the actual TPC coefficient.

# All-band conservation and the reassembly barrier

The output-side square function is exact and useful bookkeeping. It must not be confused with a new atomic estimate.

## Amplitude and diagonal conservation

###### ref-31ab2a73a209703e

###### ref-c041e4165a9fd8bc

Let $Sx=\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}x_eu_e$ be any finite synthesis in $\widetilde\mathcal H$, and define $$\mathcal D(x):=\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}|x_e|^2\|u_e\|^2.  \label{eq:tpc48-abstract-diagonal}$$ For each orbit band, put $$S_kx:=\Pi_kSx,  \qquad  \mathcal D_k(x):=\sum_e|x_e|^2\|\Pi_ku_e\|^2.  \label{eq:tpc48-band-diagonal}$$

###### ref-f1d6831d0f905d95

###### ref-59c94151b41b9a23

###### ref-ebee781ea00994aa

> **Proposition: Exact all-band conservation** \[prop:tpc48-all-band-conservation\] For every $x$, $$\sum_k\|S_kx\|^2=\|Sx\|^2,  \qquad  \sum_k\mathcal D_k(x)=\mathcal D(x).  \label{eq:tpc48-all-band-conservation}$$ Consequently, if $\|S_kx\|^2\le C\mathcal D_k(x)$ for every $k$ with one common constant $C$, then $\|Sx\|^2\le C\mathcal D(x)$. Conversely, whenever $\mathcal D(x)>0$, at least one band with $\mathcal D_k(x)>0$ satisfies $$\frac{\|S_kx\|^2}{\mathcal D_k(x)}  \ge\frac{\|Sx\|^2}{\mathcal D(x)}.  \label{eq:tpc48-one-band-inherits-ratio}$$

> **Proof** The first identity is Parseval for the orthogonal resolution [\[eq:tpc48-projector-resolution\]](TPC-048.md#ref-be674e6832d57092). Apply the same identity to each atom $u_e$, multiply by $|x_e|^2$, and sum to obtain the diagonal identity. Summing the bandwise inequalities gives the first consequence. The full ratio is the $\mathcal D_k$-weighted average of the nonzero band ratios, which proves the second.

Thus an output tiling cannot hide a pre-existing coherent-box or tensor-to-atomic obstruction: some band inherits at least the full ratio. The conclusion is abstract and does not say that every actual band is bad.

## Collapse of the difference channels

###### ref-f8cfffa05792c12b

###### ref-1d5aefffcf799435

Let $$\mathcal Q_L:\bigoplus_{q\bmod L}\widetilde\mathcal H  \longrightarrow\widetilde\mathcal H,  \qquad  \mathcal Q_L((z_q)_q):=\sum_qz_q.  \label{eq:tpc48-channel-collapse}$$ Then $\|\mathcal Q_L\|^2=L$. The channel lift factors the unlabelled determinant synthesis: $$\mathcal Q_L\mathcal A_{\alpha,L}\bm y  =\sum_{m\in\mathcal M}U_{m\alpha}y_m  =:T_\alpha\bm y.  \label{eq:tpc48-channel-factorization}$$

###### ref-3a1e1ebf8824455d

###### ref-08a0b37f223b1ebb

###### ref-c302bc21546696e7

> **Theorem: Sharp unrestricted reassembly barrier** \[thm:tpc48-unrestricted-barrier\] For every $\alpha\in\mathbb T$, $$\boxed{\|T_\alpha\|^2=K.}  \label{eq:tpc48-unrestricted-barrier}$$ Hence $$K\le L R_L(\alpha;\mathcal M),  \label{eq:tpc48-factor-product}$$ and no universal inequality for arbitrary independent orbit inputs can replace the factor $K$ by a smaller all-band constant.

> **Proof** Cauchy–Schwarz gives $\|\sum_mU_{m\alpha}y_m\|^2\le K\sum_m\|y_m\|^2$. For a nonzero finitely supported $g$, take $y_m=U_{m\alpha}^{-1}g$. Then the output is $Kg$, while the input norm squared is $K\|g\|^2$, proving equality. The product bound is also immediate from $T_\alpha=\mathcal Q_L\mathcal A_{\alpha,L}$ and [\[thm:tpc48-channel-norm\]](TPC-048.md#ref-cfe417e8750bc8cf).

The witness is allowed in the ambient arbitrary-input class and can be finitely supported in the orbit coordinate. It is not asserted to be an actual source-mask coefficient. Its role is to identify exactly which extra structure a positive continuation must use.

## A no-wrap collision bound

On the shift range relevant below, the slope points do not wrap around the quotient circle.

###### ref-3cac50e2a9ba8268

###### ref-369290df6c11f80e

###### ref-0a1435dcde5c672d

###### ref-cc63d5215ed0d43c

###### ref-5e4f487dd6604d52

###### ref-bfe5d2d377f7e48d

> **Proposition: Elementary tube-load bounds** \[prop:tpc48-load-bounds\] Assume $\mathcal M\subset[M_-,M_+]\cap\mathbb Z$, $0<t<1$, $L\le M_+$, and $$H_0M_+t<1.  \label{eq:tpc48-load-no-wrap}$$ Then $$\left\lceil\frac KL\right\rceil  \le R_L(t\pmod1;\mathcal M)  \le\min\left\{K,1+\frac2{LH_0t}\right\}.  \label{eq:tpc48-load-bounds}$$ If, more explicitly, $$\mathcal M\subset[M,2M]\cap\mathbb Z,  \qquad K\asymp M,  \qquad L\le M,  \label{eq:tpc48-dense-slope-box}$$ and $$\frac aM\le t\le\frac bM,  \qquad 0<a<b<\frac1{2H_0},  \label{eq:tpc48-coarse-edge-slab}$$ then $$R_L(t\pmod1;\mathcal M)\asymp_{a,b,H_0}\frac ML.  \label{eq:tpc48-dense-load}$$

> **Proof** The lower bound is [\[eq:tpc48-load-pigeonhole\]](TPC-048.md#ref-4c322f45bd4dd80e). Under [\[eq:tpc48-load-no-wrap\]](TPC-048.md#ref-369290df6c11f80e), the points $H_0mt$ are separated by at least $H_0t$ on an interval of length less than one. A quotient cell has length $1/L$, so it contains at most $1+2(LH_0t)^{-1}$ such points; the factor two harmlessly covers a cell meeting the chosen cut of the quotient circle. This is the upper bound. On [\[eq:tpc48-coarse-edge-slab\]](TPC-048.md#ref-5e4f487dd6604d52), the upper bound is $O(M/L)$, while the dense-slope hypothesis and the pigeonhole lower bound give the matching estimate.

For the critical choice $L\asymp J$, a dense slope box therefore has channel load $M/J$ on the coarse-edge slab. However, multiplying by the $L$-channel collapse returns $M$, exactly as [\[eq:tpc48-factor-product\]](TPC-048.md#ref-c302bc21546696e7) predicts.

# A sharp large sieve for a common orbit profile

The barrier in [\[thm:tpc48-unrestricted-barrier\]](TPC-048.md#ref-3a1e1ebf8824455d) permits each slope to choose an independent compensating orbit vector. The projective layers in [\[eq:tpc48-projective-layer\]](TPC-048.md#ref-0233b86112f007a2) do not have that freedom: all slopes share the same sampled scalar profile. This section quantifies the resulting gain.

## The progression Gram kernel

###### ref-0b36fd94b6480f02

###### ref-0b8000222a6fcae7

Fix $a\pmod{H_0}$, $J\ge1$, $\phi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$, and a finite slope set $\mathcal M\subset[M_-,M_+]\cap\mathbb Z$. For Hilbert vectors $b_m\in\mathcal V$, define $$\mathcal L_{\phi,a,\alpha}(\bm b)  :=\sum_{\substack{j\in\mathbb Z\\j\equiv a\!\!\pmod{H_0}}}  \phi(j/J)  \left(\sum_{m\in\mathcal M}b_m\mathrm e(mj\alpha)\right)\otimes e_j.  \label{eq:tpc48-common-profile-operator}$$ Put $K=|\mathcal M|$, $t=\|\alpha\|_\mathbb T$, and $$D_J(t;K):=  \begin{cases}   \min\left\{K,1+\dfrac1{Jt}\right\},&t>0,\\[4pt]   K,&t=0.  \end{cases}  \label{eq:tpc48-collision-factor}$$

###### ref-0f31c322d7dbf192

Let $\psi=|\phi|^2$. The Gram kernel is $$G_d(\alpha)  :=\sum_{j\equiv a\!\!\pmod{H_0}}  \psi(j/J)\mathrm e(dj\alpha),  \qquad d\in\mathbb Z.  \label{eq:tpc48-Gram-kernel}$$

###### ref-2ce11b59f5d2005c

###### ref-bb3d6a9049ae67e3

###### ref-e21a64b53d13515b

> **Lemma: No-wrap progression decay** \[lem:tpc48-Gram-decay\] Assume $t\ge0$ and $$H_0(M_+-M_-)t\le\frac14.  \label{eq:tpc48-large-sieve-no-wrap}$$ For every integer $B\ge2$, $$|G_d(\alpha)|  \le C_{B,H_0,\phi}\frac{J}{H_0}  (1+J|d|t)^{-B}  \label{eq:tpc48-Gram-decay}$$ uniformly for $|d|\le M_+-M_-$.

###### ref-54482766b5cbc1c4

> **Proof** Progression Poisson summation gives $$G_d(\alpha)  =\frac{J}{H_0}\sum_{q\in\mathbb Z}  \mathrm e(aq/H_0)  \widehat\psi\!\left(  \frac{J}{H_0}(q-H_0d\alpha)  \right).  \label{eq:tpc48-Gram-Poisson}$$ Choose the representative of $\alpha$ with absolute value $t$. Under [\[eq:tpc48-large-sieve-no-wrap\]](TPC-048.md#ref-bb3d6a9049ae67e3), the $q=0$ term is bounded by the right side of [\[eq:tpc48-Gram-decay\]](TPC-048.md#ref-e21a64b53d13515b). Every $q\ne0$ satisfies $|q-H_0d\alpha|\ge3/4$. Apply Schwartz decay at order $B+2$ to those terms. If $J\ge H_0$, their sum after the prefactor $J/H_0$ is $O_{B,H_0,\phi}(J^{-B-1})$, which is bounded by the right side of [\[eq:tpc48-Gram-decay\]](TPC-048.md#ref-e21a64b53d13515b) because $1+J|d|t\le1+J/(4H_0)$. If $J<H_0$, the conclusion is absorbed into the constant. Thus only a fixed finite Schwartz seminorm of $\psi$ is used.

## The Hilbert-valued inequality

###### ref-79bcbe813ada6202

###### ref-14711421b0a4dff7

> **Theorem: Common-profile determinant large sieve** \[thm:tpc48-common-profile-large-sieve\] Under [\[eq:tpc48-large-sieve-no-wrap\]](TPC-048.md#ref-bb3d6a9049ae67e3), $$\boxed{  \|\mathcal L_{\phi,a,\alpha}(\bm b)\|^2  \le C_{\phi,H_0}\frac{J}{H_0}  D_J(t;K)\sum_{m\in\mathcal M}\|b_m\|^2.}  \label{eq:tpc48-common-profile-large-sieve}$$ The constant is uniform in the Hilbert space, the vectors $b_m$, the slope locations inside $[M_-,M_+]$, and $J$.

###### ref-27febba4f14bae40

###### ref-9eda73c065f9580f

> **Proof** If $t=0$, Cauchy–Schwarz in the $m$-sum and $\sum_{j\equiv a\ (H_0)}|\phi(j/J)|^2\ll_{\phi,H_0}J/H_0$ give [\[eq:tpc48-common-profile-large-sieve\]](TPC-048.md#ref-14711421b0a4dff7) with $D_J(0;K)=K$. Assume henceforth that $t>0$. Expanding the square in [\[eq:tpc48-common-profile-operator\]](TPC-048.md#ref-0b36fd94b6480f02) gives the Hilbert-valued Gram form $$\sum_{m,n\in\mathcal M}  G_{m-n}(\alpha)\langle b_m,b_n\rangle.  \label{eq:tpc48-Gram-form}$$ By [\[lem:tpc48-Gram-decay\]](TPC-048.md#ref-2ce11b59f5d2005c) with $B=2$, $$\sup_m\sum_{n\in\mathcal M}|G_{m-n}(\alpha)|  \ll_{\phi,H_0}\frac{J}{H_0}  \left(1+\frac1{Jt}\right).  \label{eq:tpc48-Gram-row-sum}$$ The Schur test, valid after scalar pairing in $\mathcal V$, gives this constant. The trivial estimate $|G_d|\ll_{\phi,H_0}J/H_0$ gives instead $(J/H_0)K$. Taking the smaller bound proves [\[eq:tpc48-common-profile-large-sieve\]](TPC-048.md#ref-14711421b0a4dff7).

###### ref-504bf7975bc61d82

By orbit Plancherel, the left side is also the complete all-band square function $$\|\mathcal L_{\phi,a,\alpha}(\bm b)\|^2  =\sum_{k\bmod L}\|\Pi_k  \mathcal L_{\phi,a,\alpha}(\bm b)\|^2  \label{eq:tpc48-large-sieve-all-band}$$ for every choice of $L$. In particular, the estimate already controls the reassembled orbit field and carries no factor equal to the number of bands.

## Sharpness in the common-profile class

###### ref-71b4779b5bb32270

###### ref-74e55ba1d49404e9

###### ref-0f88522e108588ab

> **Proposition: Clustered-slope lower bound** \[prop:tpc48-large-sieve-sharpness\] Assume $t>0$, $\phi\not\equiv0$, and $J\ge J_0(\phi,H_0)$. There are constants $c_{\phi,H_0},C_{\phi,H_0}>0$ with the following property. Suppose $\mathcal M$ contains $D$ consecutive integers and either $D=1$, or $$2\le D\le c_{\phi,H_0}\min\{K,(Jt)^{-1}\}.  \label{eq:tpc48-coherent-cluster-size}$$ Then one can choose collinear Hilbert vectors, supported on those slopes, such that $$\|\mathcal L_{\phi,a,\alpha}(\bm b)\|^2  \ge C_{\phi,H_0}^{-1}\frac{J}{H_0}D  \sum_m\|b_m\|^2.  \label{eq:tpc48-cluster-lower-bound}$$ Consequently $D_J(t;K)$ is optimal up to constants for dense common-profile slope boxes.

> **Proof** Choose an interval on which $|\phi|$ is bounded below. The lower bound on $J$ ensures that its $J$-dilate contains $\gg_{\phi,H_0}J/H_0$ points in the progression, uniformly in the finitely many residue classes $a$. For $D=1$, any one supported slope gives the claim directly. For $D\ge2$, let $j_0$ be the center of the sampled interval and choose the signed lift $\widetilde\alpha\in[-1/2,1/2]$ with $|\widetilde\alpha|=t$. Fix a unit vector $v$, and on a consecutive block put $b_m=\mathrm e(-mj_0\widetilde\alpha)v$. For $j$ in a sufficiently short fixed subinterval around $j_0$, condition [\[eq:tpc48-coherent-cluster-size\]](TPC-048.md#ref-74e55ba1d49404e9) makes all phases $\mathrm e(m(j-j_0)\widetilde\alpha)$ lie in one fixed acute arc. Their sum has modulus $\gg D$. Summing over those $j$ gives $\gg_{\phi,H_0}(J/H_0)D^2$, whereas $\sum_m\|b_m\|^2=D$.

Taking $D=1$ when $Jt\gg1$, and $D\asymp_{\phi,H_0}\min\{K,(Jt)^{-1}\}$ when $Jt\ll1$, proves the stated optimality for slope boxes containing the required consecutive cluster. At $t=0$, choosing all $K$ vectors equal gives the exact factor $K$. The sharpness witness uses arbitrary collinear coefficients inside one admissible common-profile class. It does not assert that the literal actual coefficients realize this coherence.

# Reduction of the complete frozen actual-coefficient family

We now apply [\[thm:tpc48-common-profile-large-sieve\]](TPC-048.md#ref-79bcbe813ada6202) to the full signed representation [\[eq:tpc48-signed-reassembly\]](TPC-048.md#ref-436f722e397fdf72). No orbit band and no projective layer is selected.

## The row-square normal form

###### ref-5dfbf62713cc273e

###### ref-3dfd6ab927a3a04b

For one projective layer, define $$B_{\omega,m}(\alpha)  :=\sum_{p,r}A_\omega(p,r,m)\mathrm e(-pr\alpha)  v_{\omega,p,r,m}\in\mathcal V_{\mathrm L}.  \label{eq:tpc48-row-vector}$$ The arithmetic sum is finite. Fourier transformation in the frozen determinant intercept gives the exact identity $$\widehat{\mathcal F^\omega}(\alpha)  =\sum_{\substack{j\in\mathbb Z\\j\equiv a_\omega\!\!\pmod{H_0}}}  \phi_\omega(j/J)  \left(\sum_mB_{\omega,m}(\alpha)\mathrm e(mj\alpha)\right)  \otimes e_j.  \label{eq:tpc48-layer-shift-transform}$$

###### ref-e4a0d4831e4f50a3

###### ref-284cae75becbce52

###### ref-c56654f5b00c92bd

> **Proposition: Exact residual square at fixed source** \[prop:tpc48-residual-square\] For fixed $\omega,m,\alpha$, put $$B_{\omega,m,r}(\alpha)  :=\sum_pA_\omega(p,r,m)\mathrm e(-pr\alpha)  v_{\omega,p,r,m}.  \label{eq:tpc48-inner-prime-sum}$$ Then $$\boxed{  \|B_{\omega,m}(\alpha)\|^2  =\sum_r\|B_{\omega,m,r}(\alpha)\|^2.}  \label{eq:tpc48-residual-square}$$

> **Proof** For fixed $m$, the vector $B_{\omega,m,r}(\alpha)$ is supported on the residual coordinate $s=d(m)r$. Distinct supported cofactors $r$ give distinct labels and hence orthogonal coordinate subspaces. The prime sum at one fixed $r$ remains coherent, which is why no $p$-square decomposition is claimed.

This identity localizes the remaining arithmetic task: one must control the prime exponential sum inside each residual coordinate and then the outer square sum over $m,r$.

## A representation-independent projective envelope

###### ref-de734bf6448dbfac

###### ref-4261a795292dc8f6

Let $C_{\rm LS}(\phi,H_0)$ denote the least admissible constant $C_{\phi,H_0}$ in [\[eq:tpc48-common-profile-large-sieve\]](TPC-048.md#ref-14711421b0a4dff7). For an admissible signed representation $\mathscr R$ of the actual coefficient, set $$\mathfrak S_{\mathscr R}(\alpha)  :=\int C_{\rm LS}(\phi_\omega,H_0)^{1/2}  \left(\sum_m\|B_{\omega,m}(\alpha)\|^2\right)^{1/2}  d|\nu|(\omega),  \label{eq:tpc48-representation-envelope}$$ and define $$\mathfrak S_{\rm pr}(\alpha)  :=\inf_{\mathscr R}\mathfrak S_{\mathscr R}(\alpha),  \label{eq:tpc48-projective-envelope}$$ where the infimum ranges over all inherited admissible coefficientwise representations described in [2](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-048.md#src-4f6d8f7ed5c03a8e). For integration we keep one fixed global representation, thereby avoiding any measurability assertion for an infimum over an uncountable representation class.

The proof of [\[thm:tpc48-common-profile-large-sieve\]](TPC-048.md#ref-79bcbe813ada6202) shows that $C_{\rm LS}(\phi,H_0)^{1/2}$ is controlled by one fixed finite-order $C^B$-seminorm once the common compact support is fixed. For every fixed admissible representation, the layer data are jointly measurable and Bochner integrable; consequently $\mathfrak S_{\mathscr R}$ is a measurable extended-valued function. Finiteness almost everywhere is part of admissibility. If one works with a broader representation class, all pointwise bounds remain valid with value $+\infty$, while the annular application below explicitly requires a finite envelope.

## A logarithmic relative annulus theorem

###### ref-eb646422f90601b8

###### ref-809077a8c4473b6a

For $$0<\epsilon<\frac\nu2=\frac{133}{800},  \qquad  \mathcal A_\epsilon  :=\left\{\alpha\in\mathbb T:  X^{-1+\epsilon}\le\|\alpha\|_\mathbb T  \le X^{-\mu-\epsilon}\right\},  \label{eq:tpc48-annulus}$$ the annulus is nonempty for large $X$. Moreover, $$H_0(M_+-M_-)\|\alpha\|_\mathbb T=o(1)  \label{eq:tpc48-annulus-no-wrap}$$ uniformly on $\mathcal A_\epsilon$, after the inherited fixed dyadic localization. Hence [\[thm:tpc48-actual-reduction\]](TPC-048.md#ref-caec0473bd56cc16) applies there.

Fix one global admissible signed representation $\mathscr R$. It is important here not to integrate the pointwise infimum $\mathfrak S_{\rm pr}$, for which no measurability assertion was made.

> **Theorem: Relative annular resonance budget** \[thm:tpc48-annular-budget\] For every fixed global admissible representation $\mathscr R$, with the measurable envelope specified in [\[eq:tpc48-representation-envelope\]](TPC-048.md#ref-de734bf6448dbfac), $$\int_{\mathcal A_\epsilon}  \|\widehat{\mathcal F}^{\rm fr,\mathrm L}(\alpha)\|^2\,d\alpha  \le  \frac J{H_0}  \int_{\mathcal A_\epsilon}  D_J(\|\alpha\|_\mathbb T;K)  \mathfrak S_{\mathscr R}(\alpha)^2\,d\alpha.  \label{eq:tpc48-weighted-annular-budget}$$ In particular, if $$\sup_{\alpha\in\mathcal A_\epsilon}  \mathfrak S_{\mathscr R}(\alpha)\le S_*,  \label{eq:tpc48-annular-envelope-bound}$$ then $$\int_{\mathcal A_\epsilon}  \|\widehat{\mathcal F}^{\rm fr,\mathrm L}(\alpha)\|^2\,d\alpha  \ll_{\epsilon,H_0} (\log X)S_*^2.  \label{eq:tpc48-log-annular-budget}$$ The contribution of this annulus to Fourier inversion at $h_0$ satisfies $$\left\|  \int_{\mathcal A_\epsilon}  \widehat{\mathcal F}^{\rm fr,\mathrm L}(\alpha)  \mathrm e(h_0\alpha)\,d\alpha  \right\|  \ll  X^{-(\mu+\epsilon)/2+o(1)}  (\log X)^{1/2}S_*.  \label{eq:tpc48-annular-fixed-shift-contribution}$$

###### ref-736089b1d42f7956

###### ref-e72e255c51ef3948

> **Proof** The first estimate is the squared version of [\[eq:tpc48-actual-reduction\]](TPC-048.md#ref-1bef74722e5380b2), retained for the fixed representation $\mathscr R$. The elementary inequality $$D_J(t;K)\le 1+\frac1{Jt}  \label{eq:tpc48-annulus-D-bound}$$ holds throughout the annulus. By symmetry around zero, $$\begin{aligned}  \int_{\mathcal A_\epsilon}D_J(\|\alpha\|_\mathbb T;K)\,d\alpha  &\ll X^{-\mu-\epsilon}    +\frac1J\log\frac{X^{-\mu-\epsilon}}                          {X^{-1+\epsilon}} \\  &\ll X^{-\nu+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc48-integrated-D}\end{aligned}$$ Multiplication by $J/H_0$ proves [\[eq:tpc48-log-annular-budget\]](TPC-048.md#ref-8c4cb3a552c1d5cf). Finally $|\mathcal A_\epsilon|\ll X^{-\mu-\epsilon}$, so Cauchy–Schwarz in $\alpha$ gives [\[eq:tpc48-annular-fixed-shift-contribution\]](TPC-048.md#ref-2c186a183f79e432).

The theorem is deliberately relative to $S_*$. Neither [\[eq:tpc48-log-annular-budget\]](TPC-048.md#ref-8c4cb3a552c1d5cf) nor [\[eq:tpc48-annular-fixed-shift-contribution\]](TPC-048.md#ref-2c186a183f79e432) estimates the projective envelope by the physical atomic diagonal. It also leaves the ultra-low interval, the complementary shift frequencies, and any sparse alias reconstruction untreated.

# Tube statistics and sharpness ledgers

The pointwise channel load of [\[thm:tpc48-channel-norm\]](TPC-048.md#ref-cfe417e8750bc8cf) is a worst-case quantity. A separate two-dimensional calculation records the exact average incidence geometry. It is useful precisely because it also shows what averaging does *not* prove.

###### ref-7329b8e63a4d73c9

Let $E\subset\mathbb T$ be measurable and let $\mathcal M\subset\mathbb Z$ contain $K$ distinct integers. Define $$\mathcal L_E(\alpha,\beta)  :=\sum_{m\in\mathcal M}\mathbf 1_E(\beta-m\alpha).  \label{eq:tpc48-two-dimensional-load}$$

###### ref-4ec1182ecb24a888

###### ref-2c5b74483b5dd3c5

###### ref-07ce6ad5856cef4a

> **Theorem: Exact first and second tube moments** \[thm:tpc48-tube-moments\] With normalized Haar measure on $\mathbb T^2$, $$\begin{aligned}  \int_{\mathbb T^2}\mathcal L_E(\alpha,\beta)\,d\alpha\,d\beta  &=K|E|,  \label{eq:tpc48-tube-first-moment}\\  \int_{\mathbb T^2}\mathcal L_E(\alpha,\beta)^2\,d\alpha\,d\beta  &=K|E|+K(K-1)|E|^2.  \label{eq:tpc48-tube-second-moment}\end{aligned}$$

###### ref-cc65ced0c8c1fa2b

> **Proof** Translation invariance gives $\int_\mathbb T\mathbf 1_E(\beta-m\alpha)\,d\beta=|E|$, proving the first identity after summing over $m$. For the second, expand the square. The $K$ diagonal terms contribute $K|E|$. If $m\ne n$, the map $$(\alpha,\beta)\longmapsto  (\beta-m\alpha,\beta-n\alpha)  \label{eq:tpc48-pair-torus-map}$$ is a surjective continuous homomorphism of compact tori and therefore pushes normalized Haar measure to normalized Haar measure. Each ordered off-diagonal pair consequently contributes $|E|^2$.

###### ref-aec516b0aa39741b

###### ref-3ede91baa27b6895

For one critical quotient cell $E=\mathcal C_k$, $|E|=1/L$, so $$\mathbb E_{\alpha,\beta}\mathcal L_E=\frac KL,  \qquad  \mathbb E_{\alpha,\beta}\mathcal L_E^2  =\frac KL+\frac{K(K-1)}{L^2}.  \label{eq:tpc48-critical-cell-moments}$$ For dense $K\asymp M$ and critical $L\asymp J$, the mean load is $$\frac KL\asymp\frac MJ=X^{67/200+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc48-average-critical-load}$$ This records the same $K/L$ geometric density scale as the coarse-edge model in [\[eq:tpc48-dense-load\]](TPC-048.md#ref-bfe5d2d377f7e48d). It is not a fixed-$\alpha$ estimate.

> **Remark: What the moment identity does not average** The variable $\beta$ in [\[eq:tpc48-two-dimensional-load\]](TPC-048.md#ref-7329b8e63a4d73c9) is free. The theorem does not give a second moment for the one-variable channel count at a fixed orbit frequency, for $R_L(\alpha;\mathcal M)$, or for the literal source-mask coefficient. Nor may one insert an additional independent restriction $\beta\in\mathcal C_q$ and retain the same formula without a new calculation. The exact two-dimensional moments are an incidence ledger, not an arithmetic cancellation theorem.

## The conserved phase-space product

###### ref-5a483f26d73b0d1d

###### ref-d25648709771d9e8

###### ref-ce36997ba7733b8a

TPC–47 used $L_{47}=X^{\nu-\kappa+o(1)}$ bands at its mesoscopic resolution. The corresponding dense coarse-edge load was $$\frac M{L_{47}}  =X^{\mu-\nu+\kappa+o(1)}  =X^{27/80+o(1)},  \label{eq:tpc48-tpc47-load}$$ and the product satisfied $$L_{47}\frac M{L_{47}}=M.  \label{eq:tpc48-tpc47-phase-product}$$ At the present critical resolution, $$L\frac ML=M  \label{eq:tpc48-critical-phase-product}$$ as well. The additional factor $X^\kappa$ in $L$ removes the mesoscopic $\kappa$-loss from the resolved channel load and restores the exact $X^{-1}$ tube scale, but it cannot remove the intrinsic $M/J=X^{\mu-\nu}$ density of source slopes per critical cell.

There are therefore two complementary sharpness statements:

1.  arbitrary independent orbit inputs recover the full factor $K$ after channel collapse, by [\[thm:tpc48-unrestricted-barrier\]](TPC-048.md#ref-3a1e1ebf8824455d);

2.  common-profile inputs have the smaller factor $D_J(t;K)$, and consecutive slope clusters attain it up to constants, by [\[prop:tpc48-large-sieve-sharpness\]](TPC-048.md#ref-71b4779b5bb32270).

Each inherited projective layer lies inside the second class. The fully reassembled coefficient is instead a signed projective integral controlled through $\mathfrak S_{\mathscr R}$; no theorem in this paper says that it either saturates or improves the common-profile frontier.

# What has changed, what remains, and the next gate

The present step closes the geometric all-band question raised in TPC–47. It does not close the arithmetic fixed-shift question. We record the distinction as a theorem ledger before describing a possible continuation.

## Advances under the inherited exact interface

The following statements are proved here without a new arithmetic hypothesis beyond the exact TPC–36/45/47 representation and support interface.

1.  The orbit-frequency quotient admits an exact orthogonal tiling by $L$ full residue combs, with exact compressed trace $N/L$ on every scalar physical window.

2.  Each determinant modulation has an exact input-output channel resolution. The support consists of explicit resonance tubes, and the critical branch half-width is $X^{-1+o(1)}$.

3.  The optimal norm of the channel lift is exactly the maximal pointwise tube multiplicity. Erasing the channel label restores the unrestricted synthesis norm squared $K$.

4.  The common orbit profile in every inherited projective layer yields the Hilbert-valued factor $D_J(t;K)=\min\{K,1+(Jt)^{-1}\}$, sharp up to constants in its declared class.

5.  Complete signed reassembly, coefficient by coefficient, reduces the literal frozen family to a representation independent pointwise projective $m$-square envelope. At fixed $m$, residual labels give an exact outer $r$-square sum, while the prime sum inside each residual coordinate remains coherent.

6.  Relative to any fixed global projective representation with a bounded envelope, the intermediate annulus has only logarithmic integrated resonance cost.

These are forward results rather than a reformulation of the single-band theorem: all orbit bands are now controlled at once, the fine determinant scale is restored, and the exact obstruction left by their reassembly is identified.

## The remaining arithmetic square-function gate

###### ref-d3ba9294a9cb0afb

By [\[eq:tpc48-residual-square\]](TPC-048.md#ref-c56654f5b00c92bd), the unresolved layerwise object is $$\sum_m\sum_r  \left\|  \sum_p A_\omega(p,r,m)\mathrm e(-pr\alpha)  v_{\omega,p,r,m}  \right\|^2.  \label{eq:tpc48-next-arithmetic-object}$$ Three features prevent the present large sieve from estimating this quantity at the atomic scale:

1.  the $p$-sum is coherent inside a fixed residual coordinate;

2.  the projective integral must be reassembled with its literal signs and total variation, not by selecting a favorable layer;

3.  subsequent residual grouping can cost $\rho_{\rm res}^{1/2}$, for which no $X^{o(1)}$ bound has been inherited at this interface.

For the dense consecutive-slope sharpness model at the coarse edge, the common-profile collision factor exceeds the TPC allowance by the exponent $\nu=133/400$. Any continuation that aims at atomic closure must therefore use arithmetic information inside [\[eq:tpc48-next-arithmetic-object\]](TPC-048.md#ref-d3ba9294a9cb0afb); orbit geometry alone is now known to be insufficient.

## Four precise doors

The next paper may attack any one of the following independently.

Door A: coefficient-specific prime square.

:   Prove cancellation in the coherent prime sum of [\[eq:tpc48-next-arithmetic-object\]](TPC-048.md#ref-d3ba9294a9cb0afb), averaged over $m,r$, beyond the universal common-profile large sieve.

Door B: projective-to-atomic comparison.

:   Bound one fixed exact representation envelope $\mathfrak S_{\mathscr R}$ by the inherited physical atomic diagonal with a quantified loss, or construct an actual-mask witness showing that such a bound is impossible at the desired exponent.

Door C: residual grouping with cofactor structure.

:   Replace the abstract $\rho_{\rm res}$ loss by a coefficient-specific square-function estimate using the literal map $s=d(m)r$. The balanced cofactor fibers from earlier descent cannot be imported here without proving that the two interfaces coincide.

Door D: localization and alias completion.

:   Combine the annular theorem with an ultra-low analysis, an anti-alias or maximal-in-shift theorem, and the literal $h_0$ Fourier inversion. This door is downstream of, rather than a substitute for, the atomic comparison.

Each door has a meaningful negative outcome: an explicit actual-mask counterexample or a sharp lower bound for the necessary loss would locate the next obstruction just as the unrestricted synthesis theorem does here.

## Prohibited conclusions

For clarity, none of the following is asserted.

1.  No estimate of the complete physical atom by its atomic diagonal is proved.

2.  No bound $\rho_{\rm res}=X^{o(1)}$, no new cofactor-range closure, and no cancellation of the internal prime sum is proved.

3.  No sparse-alias unfolding, new-shift physical provenance, or uniform local-shift theorem is proved. Only $h=h_0$ is the inherited physical residual synthesis; all other shifts are frozen algebraic extensions.

4.  The residual synthesis remains the low-sign-averaged Gram route and is not identified with the native affine physical face.

5.  No parity-barrier breach, Hardy–Littlewood asymptotic, bounded prime-gap theorem, or twin-prime conclusion follows.

The exact conclusion is narrower and reusable: after full orbit-frequency resolution, the next non-geometric obstruction is the actual projective $m,r$-square of coherent prime sums. That is the next gate, not a disguised solution of it.
