## Abstract

The preceding twin-prime-correlation (TPC) reduction left a precise finite-dimensional question: does the literal static row mask retain enough of the squared mass of the residue/Mellin cells to compare a bounded-projective envelope with an atomic diagonal? We first remove a representation ambiguity. Weighted survival is canonically attached to a *pre-mask* cell—the fixed-period residue and real-axis Mellin factors are separated before the Fourier–Ferrers–gcd representation of the static mask is introduced. It is not an invariant of an arbitrary final projective layer.

For a binary mask $M$ and a nonzero right weight $b$, let

$$\eta_M(b)=\max_\alpha  \frac{\sum_{\gamma:M(\alpha,\gamma)=0}|b_\gamma|^2}       {\|b\|_2^2}.$$

We prove the exact minimax identity

$$\inf_{a\ne0}\delta_w(M;a,b)=1-\eta_M(b).$$

For the TPC mask, the necessary-and-sufficient quantity $\eta_M$ is bounded by three explicit anti-concentration numbers measuring mass on a single prime-source fibre, in a determinant window of width $\Delta=QX^{-\kappa_0}$, and on a large-gcd fibre. These are the geometric sufficient quantities used below; they are individually sharp, whereas their sum need not be necessary because the deleted sets may overlap.

We then unpack the actual fixed-complexity provenance. An identified nondegenerate direct opposite-row cell has a fixed smooth dyadic shape, a fixed admissible residue set, and unit-modulus residue and real-axis Mellin phases. Fixed-modulus prime and squarefree counts give uniformly

$$\operatorname{Flat}(b)=O(1).$$

Together with the complete-row degree theorem, this yields

$$\delta_w(M;a,b)  =1-O\!\left(L^{-1+o(1)}+D^{-1+o(1)}                  +X^{-\kappa_0+o(1)}\right)  =1-o(1)$$

for every nonzero weight $a$ on the other row, however sparse. Hence, on this identified separated row-output face, the TPC–36 representation has only $X^{o(1)}$ projective-to-atomic energy distortion: the active-cell survival gate consumes no polynomial part of the $1/400$ endpoint allowance. An abstract finite-family selection lemma gives the same survival conclusion for energetic cells under an explicit aggregate lower-frame hypothesis, while controlling their aggregate discarded cell energy. It is not a frame theorem for the signed continuous Mellin reassembly.

The distinction from the upper-only inherited interface is sharp. Concentration on a same-source fibre, a near-product block, or a large-gcd block produces polynomially large packets with zero survival; adding one admitted neighbor gives positive survival as small as the inverse packet size. Such selectors require growing or shrinking complexity and are not generated by the literal fixed-period, fixed-shape, real-axis Mellin provenance. The paper does not compare the full signed TPC–48 profile envelope with the physical atomic diagonal, prove a Mellin frame lower bound, control the coherent prime square, group residual labels, or localize the frozen family back to the physical shift. No parity-barrier breach or twin-prime conclusion is claimed.

For a weighted mask matrix $$T=D_aKD_b,  \qquad  \delta_w(K;a,b)  =\frac{\sum_{\alpha,\gamma}|a_\alpha|^2        |K_{\alpha\gamma}|^2|b_\gamma|^2}        {\|a\|_2^2\|b\|_2^2},  \label{eq:tpc50-intro-survival}$$ that comparison gives $$\frac{\mathcal E_{\mathscr R}(T)^2}{\|T\|_{S_2}^2}  \le \frac{P_{\mathscr R}(K)^2}           {\delta_w(K;a,b)}.  \label{eq:tpc50-intro-transfer}$$ Here $P_{\mathscr R}$ is the bounded-projective cost of one specified mask representation. TPC–36 supplies a representation $\mathscr R_{36}$ with $P_{\mathscr R_{36}}=X^{o(1)}$ [`\citep{WangTPC36}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-050.md#cite-7286aa6023fa493f). The unresolved quantity was therefore the weighted survival of the actual active cells.

The phrase “actual active cell” hides an order-of-operations issue. The final parameter $\omega$ in the TPC–47/48 representation already combines fixed-residue and Mellin separation with the Fourier–Ferrers–gcd decomposition of the static mask [`\citep{WangTPC47,WangTPC48}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-050.md#cite-0276fa4f583cdc92); [\[WangTPC48\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-050.md#cite-b2c88d6d47db0c74). It is not a canonical physical cell. Null pairs can be appended to an exact signed representation, so no nontrivial lower-mass statement can hold uniformly for every final projective layer. The static mask must instead be left intact while the fixed-period and smooth factors are separated. This produces a specified *pre-mask cell*; only then is [\[eq:tpc50-intro-survival\]](TPC-050.md#ref-7e600e19f229c7bb) the right quantity.

## The exact concentration criterion

###### ref-7014fe8379fdf384

###### ref-d6513a61c5389e13

###### ref-6ffa7011733d1df7

For a binary mask $M$, define the largest forbidden proportion of a right weight $b\ne0$ by $$\eta_M(b)  :=\max_\alpha  \frac{\sum_{\gamma:M(\alpha,\gamma)=0}|b_\gamma|^2}       {\|b\|_2^2}.  \label{eq:tpc50-intro-eta}$$ Our first main theorem is the exact identity $$\boxed{  \inf_{a\ne0}\delta_w(M;a,b)=1-\eta_M(b).}  \label{eq:tpc50-intro-exact}$$ Thus a uniform statement over arbitrary pruning of the left row is equivalent to one-sided anti-concentration of the right weight. For the literal TPC static mask $$M_X(\alpha,\gamma)  =\mathbf 1_{\ell_\alpha\ne\ell_\gamma}   \mathbf 1_{|m_\alpha-m_\gamma|>QX^{-\kappa_0}}   \mathbf 1_{(d_\alpha,d_\gamma)\le X^{\kappa_0}},  \label{eq:tpc50-intro-mask}$$ the single quantity $\eta_{M_X}$ is bounded by the sum of three transparent concentration coefficients: same-source, near-product, and large-gcd mass. This both refines the degree–flatness criterion of TPC–49 and identifies the minimal one-sided repair of the inherited upper-only interface.

## Restoring fixed-complexity provenance

The physical opened row before static-mask decomposition has the form $$\mu(d)(\log\ell)\omega_D(d)\psi_L(\ell/L)\zeta_{\ell,d},  \label{eq:tpc50-intro-row}$$ where the smooth shapes and residue periods are fixed [`\citep{WangTPC18,WangTPC25}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-050.md#cite-4abf939102bd1649); [\[WangTPC25\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-050.md#cite-39e8dc14c55a2411). Finite residue Fourier characters and real-axis Mellin row factors have modulus one. The identified direct opposite-row factor uses $e=0$; the restricted source-factor variant with its physical prime logarithm uses $e=1$. Weighted prime and squarefree counts give, for either displayed weight class, $$\|b\|_2^2\asymp LD(\log L)^{2e-1},  \qquad  \|b\|_\infty^2\ll(\log L)^{2e},  \qquad e\in\{0,1\}.  \label{eq:tpc50-intro-row-energy}$$ Since the complete row universe has $N\asymp LD/\log L$, this proves $$\operatorname{Flat}(b)  :=\frac{N\|b\|_\infty^2}{\|b\|_2^2}=O(1)  \label{eq:tpc50-intro-flatness}$$ uniformly in every real Mellin parameter. Combining [\[eq:tpc50-intro-flatness\]](TPC-050.md#ref-bb866c27ac2e37e3) with the complete-row degree theorem of TPC–49 proves $$\boxed{  \delta_w(M_X;a,b)  =1-O\!\left(L^{-1+o(1)}+D^{-1+o(1)}                   +X^{-\kappa_0+o(1)}\right)}  \label{eq:tpc50-intro-positive}$$ for every $a\ne0$. In particular, arbitrary terminal sparsity on one side is harmless when the opposite side retains a canonical fixed-complexity cell.

###### ref-e2fbb0d8ea0f2ddd

At the inherited endpoint, $$L=X^{99979/210000+o(1)},  \qquad  D=X^{10049/52500+o(1)},  \label{eq:tpc50-intro-endpoint-LD}$$ so [\[eq:tpc50-intro-positive\]](TPC-050.md#ref-593d22569b47eb9e) is $1-o(1)$. In the isolated Hilbertized row-output face, its density exponent is therefore zero, rather than the maximal allowed value $1/400$. This closes the static survival subgate for the identified provenance-restored cells; it does not close the full TPC–48 envelope comparison.

## Sharpness of the provenance requirement

###### ref-2e274bf67a05b4fd

The preceding conclusion cannot be recovered from row-count and norm upper bounds alone. The three deletion channels in [\[eq:tpc50-intro-mask\]](TPC-050.md#ref-6ffa7011733d1df7) support correspondingly large concentrated packets with $$\delta_w=0.  \label{eq:tpc50-intro-zero}$$ A near-product block and a large-gcd block can have $X^{267/400-\kappa_0+o(1)}$ rows. Adding one admitted neighbor makes the masked matrix nonzero but leaves survival equal to the inverse of the packet size. At $\kappa_0=1/400$, this may be $X^{-133/200+o(1)}$, far below the isolated-face target.

These are exact logical counterexamples to the upper-only interface, not models of the literal coefficient. A same-source selector tracks one prime as $X$ varies, a near-product selector shrinks at relative scale $X^{-\kappa_0}$, and a gcd selector uses a growing modulus. None is produced by a fixed-period residue expansion or by a unit-modulus real Mellin phase. The positive and negative theorems therefore meet at a clean boundary: fixed complexity gives survival, while growing concentration complexity must be estimated explicitly.

# The canonical pre-mask cell interface

The weighted-survival parameter of TPC–49 is attached to a weighted two-row mask. It is therefore essential to define it before the static mask is expanded into signed rank-one layers. This section fixes that order of operations, records the nondegeneracy inherited from the physical packet, and states the exact transfer theorem available for one specified cell. In particular, a terminal projective parameter $\omega$ from TPC–47/48 is not declared to be a physical cell.

## The physical coefficient before projective expansion

Fix the physical shift $h_0\ne0$ and one source-compatible dyadic packet. Write $\mathcal A_1$ and $\mathcal A_2$ for the two row systems. A row is $$\rho=(\ell_\rho,d_\rho),  \qquad m_\rho=\ell_\rho d_\rho\asymp Q,  \label{eq:tpc50-physical-row}$$ where $\ell_\rho$ is prime, $d_\rho$ is squarefree, and the inherited separation makes $m_\rho$ determine $\rho$. The literal static mask on $\mathcal A_1\times\mathcal A_2$ is $$K_{\alpha\gamma}  :=\mathfrak m_{\rm act}(\alpha,\gamma)  =\mathbf 1_{\ell_\alpha\ne\ell_\gamma}   \mathbf 1_{|m_\alpha-m_\gamma|>\Delta}   \mathbf 1_{(d_\alpha,d_\gamma)\le G},  \label{eq:tpc50-literal-static-mask}$$ with $\Delta=QX^{-\kappa_0}$ and $G=X^{\kappa_0}$. The joint multiplier is $$\mathfrak A_{\alpha,\gamma}^{\rm act}(j)  =K_{\alpha\gamma}\,   \Xi_{\alpha,\gamma}(j)   \mathcal W_{\alpha,\gamma}(j/J).  \label{eq:tpc50-joint-multiplier}$$ Here $\Xi$ is fixed-period in the orbit variable and $\mathcal W$ contains the inherited smooth factors. The physical source coefficient on the left has the form $$\gamma_\alpha^{(1)}  =\mu(d_\alpha)(\log\ell_\alpha)   \omega_D^{(1)}(d_\alpha)   \psi_L^{(1)}(\ell_\alpha/L)\zeta_\alpha^{(1)},  \label{eq:tpc50-left-source-weight}$$ and the right coefficient $\gamma_\gamma^{(2)}$ has the symmetric form. The inherited estimates are upper bounds only: $$\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal A_s}|\gamma_\rho^{(s)}|  \ll QX^{o(1)},  \qquad  \sum_{\rho\in\mathcal A_s}|\gamma_\rho^{(s)}|^2  \ll QX^{o(1)},  \qquad s=1,2.  \label{eq:tpc50-inherited-row-upper-bounds}$$ No lower row count, lower $\ell^2$-mass, or pointwise lower bound is part of this interface [`\citep{WangTPC36,WangTPC45,WangTPC47,WangTPC48}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-050.md#cite-7286aa6023fa493f); [\[WangTPC45\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-050.md#cite-1802a7e8047e595c); [\[WangTPC47\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-050.md#cite-0276fa4f583cdc92); [\[WangTPC48\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-050.md#cite-b2c88d6d47db0c74).

###### ref-2504ab4ce3ef22f0

After large-prime descent, the literal left residual vector at $h_0$ is $$\mathcal F_{h_0}^{\rm act,L}  =\sum_{\substack{\gamma,j,m,p,r\\pr-mj=h_0}}  \gamma_m^{(1)}\mathfrak A_{m,\gamma}^{\rm act}(j)  \mu(r)\Lambda(pr)e_{\gamma,d(m)r}\otimes e_j.  \label{eq:tpc50-physical-residual-vector}$$ Thus the two axes relevant to static-mask survival are the source row $m$ and the opposite row $\gamma$. The terminal factor, the Möbius phase, and the residual basis label are downstream data; they are not additional entries of the row mask.

## Provenance-canonical residue/Mellin cells

We now fix one representation rather than taking an infimum over all algebraically equivalent ones. First split the physical orbit into the actual residue classes $a\pmod {H_0}$. On each class, apply the specific finite Fourier and Mellin inversions supplied by the inherited provenance construction, with their original normalizations and cutoffs. No zero pair, algebraic refactorization, or static-mask Fourier expansion is inserted at this stage. We call the resulting specified decomposition the *provenance-canonical pre-mask cellization* and denote it by $\mathscr C$.

###### ref-1b25c6399892bf2a

Concretely, $\mathscr C$ is the exact identity $$\begin{aligned}  &\gamma_\alpha^{(1)}   \Xi_{\alpha,\gamma}(j)   \mathcal W_{\alpha,\gamma}(j/J)  \notag\\  &\quad={}  \sum_{a\bmod H_0}  \mathbf 1_{j\equiv a\!\!\pmod {H_0}}  \int_{\Theta_a}  u_{a,\tau}^{L}(\alpha)v_{a,\tau}(\gamma)  \phi_{a,\tau}(j/J)\,d\lambda_a(\tau).  \label{eq:tpc50-canonical-pre-mask-cellization}\end{aligned}$$ The source coefficient in [\[eq:tpc50-left-source-weight\]](TPC-050.md#ref-4ec44caed08e8b6f) is absorbed into $u_{a,\tau}^{L}$. Equivalently, one may first separate $\Xi\mathcal W$ and then put $u_{a,\tau}^{L}(\alpha)= \gamma_\alpha^{(1)}u_{a,\tau}(\alpha)$. The right-side cellization is obtained by absorbing $\gamma_\gamma^{(2)}$ into the summation-row factor on the right.

###### ref-0e8b64319faba5eb

###### ref-bb6ea4b05781b1e5

There is one fixed compact set $I_0\subset\mathbb R$, independent of $X,a,\tau$, such that $$\phi_{a,\tau}\in C_c^\infty(I_0).  \label{eq:tpc50-common-profile-support}$$ The common cutoff used to obtain this support equals one throughout the physical normalized orbit range. Hence it does not alter a physical coefficient. For every fixed integer $B\ge0$, $$\sum_{a\bmod H_0}\int_{\Theta_a}  \|u_{a,\tau}^{L}\|_\infty  \|v_{a,\tau}\|_\infty  \|\phi_{a,\tau}\|_{C^B}  \,d|\lambda_a|(\tau)  \ll_{B,h_0}X^{o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc50-canonical-seminorm-mass}$$ This is an upper projective-seminorm estimate. It contains no lower bound for either unary factor. Identity [\[eq:tpc50-canonical-pre-mask-cellization\]](TPC-050.md#ref-1b25c6399892bf2a) is precisely the point at which “cell” is defined in this paper: a cell is $c=(a,\tau)$ before $K$ is decomposed.

###### ref-526eda5678ba5bc3

###### ref-08eb183ec83c904e

###### ref-424db83e0d881f5c

> **Definition: Literal pre-mask cell tensor** \[def:tpc50-literal-cell\] For $c=(a,\tau)$, put $$T_c^L(\alpha,\gamma)  :=u_c^L(\alpha)K_{\alpha\gamma}v_c(\gamma).  \label{eq:tpc50-literal-cell-tensor}$$ The cell is called active when $T_c^L\ne0$. Define its nonnegative unary weights by $$a_c^L(\alpha):=|u_c^L(\alpha)|,  \qquad b_c(\gamma):=|v_c(\gamma)|.  \label{eq:tpc50-cell-unary-weights}$$ The right tensor and weights are defined symmetrically.

Unary complex phases are harmless: diagonal unitary multiplication does not change Frobenius, nuclear, or projective-envelope norms. A genuine common nonzero scalar multiplies both numerator and denominator below and therefore cancels. A source-dependent terminal or Mellin modulus is instead absorbed into the weight $a_c^L$, while its unit phase still leaves survival unchanged.

## Why a terminal $\omega$-layer has no physical survival parameter

###### ref-b86db78d1410b08b

###### ref-0ced8659ae95f424

The order in [\[eq:tpc50-canonical-pre-mask-cellization\]](TPC-050.md#ref-1b25c6399892bf2a) is forced by the meaning of $\delta_c$. After the pre-mask cell is fixed, TPC–36 expands $$K_{\alpha\gamma}  =\int_{\Omega_{36}}p_\eta(\alpha)q_\eta(\gamma)  \,d\nu_{36}(\eta).  \label{eq:tpc50-specified-mask-representation}$$ The final parameter used in TPC–47/48 combines $c$, $\eta$, and the terminal Mellin variables into one symbol $\omega$. An individual such layer contains the rank-one factor $$u_c^L(\alpha)p_\eta(\alpha),  v_c(\gamma)q_\eta(\gamma),  \label{eq:tpc50-terminal-rank-one-layer}$$ not the weighted masked tensor $D_{a_c}KD_{b_c}$. The literal mask is recovered only after the complete signed $\eta$-integral is taken.

Consequently, assigning $\delta_w(K;|u_cp_\eta|,|v_cq_\eta|)$ to a final $\omega$-layer would multiply by $K$ a second time and measure a tensor different from the layer being reassembled. There are three further obstructions:

1.  the signed projective representation is not unique;

2.  a cancelling null pair may be appended without changing the physical coefficient while changing every layerwise statistic;

3.  the TPC–48 layer also contains a profile seminorm, a coherent prime sum, and a residual Hilbert vector, so it is not a canonical two-axis matrix of the form $D_aKD_b$.

Thus no representation-independent “physical $\delta_\omega$” is defined. The pointwise infimum in TPC–48 may remove inefficient representations at the level of the full envelope, but it does not turn their individual layers into physical atoms. Every survival assertion below is therefore made for the fixed pre-mask cellization $\mathscr C$ and the specified mask representation $\mathscr R_{36}$, never for all exact signed representations.

## Specified $\mathscr R_{36}$ transfer

###### ref-aca8a9a8a5444612

###### ref-405e4d6e13e9d03f

## Boundary of the cell theorem

Theorem [\[thm:tpc50-canonical-cell-transfer\]](TPC-050.md#ref-d256445306c4437a) is exactly a separated row-mask statement. It does not make any of the following identifications:

1.  It does not compare the full TPC–48 Banach profile envelope $\mathfrak S_{\mathscr R}(\alpha)$ with the fixed-shift physical atomic diagonal $\mathcal D_0$. Signed $L^1(d|\lambda|)$ reassembly still needs a frame or no-cancellation theorem.

2.  It does not estimate the coherent prime square inside a fixed residual coordinate. The $p$-sum remains inside the norm.

3.  It does not control collisions or grouping under $s=d(m)r$. The mask face is formed before that map.

4.  It does not unfold sparse determinant aliases, localize the completed frozen family back to $h_0$, or identify the residual Gram route with the native affine physical face.

Accordingly, even a subpower lower bound for every identified direct canonical cell would close only the corresponding active-cell survival subgate. It would not prove a fixed-shift Chowla estimate, cross the parity barrier, or imply any prime-pair or twin-prime conclusion.

# Exact forbidden-mass concentration

The weighted-density condition of TPC–49 has an exact one-sided formulation. This formulation is useful here because the determinant and terminal restrictions may concentrate the source weight, whereas the opposite-row cell is the object whose provenance can still be tested directly.

###### ref-8e1fbae5c3ef7a1d

Let $I,J$ be finite nonempty sets. For an arbitrary kernel $K:I\times J\to\mathbb C$ and nonzero weights $a\in\mathbb C^I$, $b\in\mathbb C^J$, put $$\delta_w(K;a,b)  :=  \frac{\displaystyle        \sum_{i\in I}\sum_{j\in J}        |a_i|^2|K(i,j)|^2|b_j|^2}       {\|a\|_2^2\|b\|_2^2}.  \label{eq:tpc50-general-weighted-survival}$$ For the exact concentration theorem below, specialize to a binary mask $M:I\times J\to\{0,1\}$. We allow the numerator in [\[eq:tpc50-general-weighted-survival\]](TPC-050.md#ref-8e1fbae5c3ef7a1d) to be zero. Thus $\delta_w=0$ when the product weight is supported entirely on forbidden entries. This is the continuous extension of the TPC–49 definition to a zero weighted matrix.

###### ref-65a123c0f4871574

For a nonzero right weight $b$, define its forbidden-row concentration by $$\eta_M(b)  :=  \max_{i\in I}  \frac{\displaystyle        \sum_{j\in J:\,M(i,j)=0}|b_j|^2}       {\|b\|_2^2}.  \label{eq:tpc50-exact-forbidden-concentration}$$

###### ref-030c10f566fd1c01

###### ref-2202ef4da4e3af54

###### ref-bd1a4384336d7141

> **Theorem: Exact one-sided survival criterion** \[thm:tpc50-exact-concentration\] For every nonzero $b\in\mathbb C^J$, $$\boxed{  \inf_{a\in\mathbb C^I\setminus\{0\}}  \delta_w(M;a,b)  =1-\eta_M(b).}  \label{eq:tpc50-exact-infimum}$$ Consequently, $$\bigl[\delta_w(M;a,b)\ge\delta_0        \text{ for every }a\ne0\bigr]  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad  \eta_M(b)\le1-\delta_0  \label{eq:tpc50-exact-uniform-equivalence}$$ for every $0\le\delta_0\le1$.

###### ref-7313ecb8f8bc0a71

###### ref-4ccc676b388ea2e5

> **Proof** For $i\in I$, set $$s_b(i)  :=  \frac{\sum_{j\in J}M(i,j)|b_j|^2}{\|b\|_2^2}  =1-  \frac{\sum_{j:\,M(i,j)=0}|b_j|^2}{\|b\|_2^2}.  \label{eq:tpc50-row-survival-profile}$$ Then $$\delta_w(M;a,b)  =\sum_{i\in I}\frac{|a_i|^2}{\|a\|_2^2}s_b(i).  \label{eq:tpc50-survival-convex-combination}$$ The right side is a convex combination of the finitely many values $s_b(i)$. Its infimum is their minimum, attained by a point mass at any minimizing row. By [\[eq:tpc50-row-survival-profile\]](TPC-050.md#ref-7313ecb8f8bc0a71), that minimum is $1-\eta_M(b)$. This proves both assertions.

###### ref-df02e61a4e7755e7

###### ref-886d0a58b68b193f

###### ref-462daf1ae03b0c53

We now specialize the criterion to the three deletions in the TPC static mask. Let rows be $\alpha=(\ell_\alpha,d_\alpha)$, put $m_\alpha=\ell_\alpha d_\alpha$, and define $$M_X(\alpha,\gamma)  :=  \mathbf 1_{\ell_\alpha\ne\ell_\gamma}  \mathbf 1_{|m_\alpha-m_\gamma|>\Delta}  \mathbf 1_{(d_\alpha,d_\gamma)\le G}.  \label{eq:tpc50-three-channel-mask}$$ For a nonzero opposite-row weight $b$, introduce $$\begin{aligned}  \eta_{\rm src}(b)  &:={}  \max_{\ell}  \frac{\displaystyle        \sum_{\gamma:\,\ell_\gamma=\ell}|b_\gamma|^2}       {\|b\|_2^2},  \label{eq:tpc50-source-concentration}\\  \eta_{\rm near}(b)  &:={}  \sup_{x\in\mathbb R}  \frac{\displaystyle        \sum_{\gamma:\,|m_\gamma-x|\le\Delta}|b_\gamma|^2}       {\|b\|_2^2},  \label{eq:tpc50-near-concentration}\\  \eta_{\rm gcd}(b)  &:={}  \max_{d}  \frac{\displaystyle        \sum_{\gamma:\,(d,d_\gamma)>G}|b_\gamma|^2}       {\|b\|_2^2}.  \label{eq:tpc50-gcd-concentration}\end{aligned}$$ The maxima range only over source and divisor labels that occur on the left row set. Enlarging those ranges can make the numerical upper bound larger, but it does not invalidate any displayed inequality.

###### ref-8eca440a187998e0

> **Proposition: Three-channel anti-concentration** \[prop:tpc50-three-channel-concentration\] For the mask [\[eq:tpc50-three-channel-mask\]](TPC-050.md#ref-435f557b88076df2), $$\eta_{M_X}(b)  \le  \eta_{\rm src}(b)  +\eta_{\rm near}(b)  +\eta_{\rm gcd}(b).  \label{eq:tpc50-channel-union-bound}$$ Hence every nonzero source weight $a$ satisfies $$\boxed{  \delta_w(M_X;a,b)  \ge  1-\eta_{\rm src}(b)    -\eta_{\rm near}(b)    -\eta_{\rm gcd}(b).}  \label{eq:tpc50-channel-survival-bound}$$ In particular, the endpoint target $\delta_w\ge X^{-1/400+o(1)}$, uniformly in $a$, follows from $$\eta_{\rm src}(b)+\eta_{\rm near}(b)+\eta_{\rm gcd}(b)  \le1-X^{-1/400+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc50-minimal-checkable-endpoint}$$ If the sum of the three concentrations is $o(1)$, then the stronger conclusion $\delta_w=1-o(1)$ holds uniformly in $a$.

###### ref-44391db69472fbbd

> **Proof** For a fixed left row $\alpha$, the forbidden opposite rows lie in the union $$\{\gamma:\ell_\gamma=\ell_\alpha\}  \cup  \{\gamma:|m_\gamma-m_\alpha|\le\Delta\}  \cup  \{\gamma:(d_\alpha,d_\gamma)>G\}.  \label{eq:tpc50-forbidden-union}$$ The $b^2$-mass of the three sets, divided by $\|b\|_2^2$, is at most the respective quantities in [\[eq:tpc50-source-concentration\]](TPC-050.md#ref-df02e61a4e7755e7)– [\[eq:tpc50-gcd-concentration\]](TPC-050.md#ref-462daf1ae03b0c53). Take the maximum over $\alpha$ and apply [\[thm:tpc50-exact-concentration\]](TPC-050.md#ref-030c10f566fd1c01).

> **Remark: Minimal versus decomposable conditions** \[rem:tpc50-minimal-versus-channel\] The single quantity $\eta_{M_X}(b)$ is necessary and sufficient for uniform one-sided survival. The three channel quantities give a slightly stronger but geometrically interpretable sufficient condition. The examples in [6](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-050.md#src-1db042453f453923) show that each channel condition is individually sharp: none can be discarded under the inherited upper-only interface.

# Energy-selected survival for a family of cells

The fixed-shape theorem below controls every nonzero standard cell. It is useful to have a second statement that remains valid when a partition contains boundary atoms with arbitrarily small energy. The result is elementary, but it separates low cell energy from poor mask survival without selecting a favorable signed summand.

###### ref-3dc3b70fb230a2bb

Let $M:I\times J\to\{0,1\}$, where $|J|=N$, and suppose every row has at most $\epsilon N$ forbidden entries. Let $b_\lambda\in\mathbb C^J$, $\lambda\in\Lambda$, be a finite cell family. Fix $B>0$ and $A>0$, and assume $$|\Lambda|\le S,  \qquad  \|b_\lambda\|_\infty^2\le B^2,  \qquad  \sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\|b_\lambda\|_2^2  \ge ANB^2.  \label{eq:tpc50-frame-hypotheses}$$ The last inequality is only an aggregate lower frame bound. It does not assert orthogonality of the cells or a lower bound for their signed sum.

###### ref-d8077cc96d241976

For $0<\tau<1$, call a cell energetic if $$\|b_\lambda\|_2^2\ge\tau NB^2.  \label{eq:tpc50-energetic-cell}$$

###### ref-2d5e0554bf7ef3c7

###### ref-d390dfc5a46816f4

###### ref-ca971518f3286c66

> **Theorem: Energy selection without a cellwise lower bound** \[thm:tpc50-energy-selection\] Every energetic cell and every nonzero $a\in\mathbb C^I$ satisfy $$\delta_w(M;a,b_\lambda)\ge1-\frac\epsilon\tau.  \label{eq:tpc50-energetic-survival}$$ Moreover, the total energy of the nonenergetic cells is bounded by $$\frac{\sum_{\lambda\text{ nonenergetic}}              \|b_\lambda\|_2^2}       {\sum_{\lambda}\|b_\lambda\|_2^2}  \le\frac{S\tau}{A}.  \label{eq:tpc50-low-cell-energy}$$ Consequently, if $\epsilon=X^{-c+o(1)}$, $S/A=X^{o(1)}$, and $\tau=\epsilon^{1/2}$, then every energetic cell has survival at least $1-X^{-c/2+o(1)}$, while the nonenergetic cells carry only $X^{-c/2+o(1)}$ of the aggregate cell energy.

###### ref-c857aa4636e39988

> **Proof** For an energetic cell, $$\operatorname{Flat}(b_\lambda)  =\frac{N\|b_\lambda\|_\infty^2}        {\|b_\lambda\|_2^2}  \le\frac1\tau.  \label{eq:tpc50-energy-flatness}$$ The degree–flatness inequality gives [\[eq:tpc50-energetic-survival\]](TPC-050.md#ref-d390dfc5a46816f4). Each nonenergetic cell has energy less than $\tau NB^2$; there are at most $S$ of them. Divide their sum by the aggregate lower bound in [\[eq:tpc50-frame-hypotheses\]](TPC-050.md#ref-3dc3b70fb230a2bb) to obtain [\[eq:tpc50-low-cell-energy\]](TPC-050.md#ref-ca971518f3286c66). The last assertion is immediate.

###### ref-1c960252dbb285b2

###### ref-632463738ebc8450

###### ref-7329493a5aa673a7

> **Corollary: Bounded-overlap partitions** \[cor:tpc50-partition-frame\] Suppose functions $w_\lambda:J\to\mathbb C$ have overlap at most $R$, satisfy $$\sum_\lambda w_\lambda(j)=1  \quad(j\in J),  \qquad  |w_\lambda(j)|\le C,  \label{eq:tpc50-partition-unity}$$ and let $0\ne g\in\mathbb C^J$ obey $$\|g\|_2^2\ge cN\|g\|_\infty^2.  \label{eq:tpc50-base-flatness}$$ Then $b_\lambda=gw_\lambda$ satisfies [\[eq:tpc50-frame-hypotheses\]](TPC-050.md#ref-3dc3b70fb230a2bb) with $S=|\Lambda|$, $B=C\|g\|_\infty$, and $A=c/(RC^2)$ (or any smaller positive value).

###### ref-610baf76d4d25c11

> **Proof** At every $j$, Cauchy–Schwarz and the overlap bound give $$1=\left|\sum_\lambda w_\lambda(j)\right|^2  \le R\sum_\lambda|w_\lambda(j)|^2.  \label{eq:tpc50-square-frame-pointwise}$$ Multiply by $|g_j|^2$ and sum over $j$. Thus
>
> $$\sum_\lambda\|gw_\lambda\|_2^2  \ge R^{-1}\|g\|_2^2  \ge (c/R)N\|g\|_\infty^2  =\frac{c}{RC^2}NB^2.$$
>
> The pointwise upper bound is immediate.

TPC–18 uses a fixed bounded-overlap dyadic partition in the opened $d$-variable [`\citep{WangTPC18}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-050.md#cite-4abf939102bd1649); fixed residue cells have overlap one. Provided the base-flatness and aggregate lower-energy hypotheses in [\[cor:tpc50-partition-frame\]](TPC-050.md#ref-1c960252dbb285b2) are verified for the family at hand, [\[thm:tpc50-energy-selection\]](TPC-050.md#ref-2d5e0554bf7ef3c7) then protects its energy-significant boundary atoms even if a uniform lower bound is not imposed on every individual cutoff. It is deliberately an aggregate cell-energy statement. Turning that aggregate into the squared norm of a signed Mellin reassembly is a separate frame/no-cancellation problem.

# Identified fixed-complexity physical cells survive the static mask

This section works *before* the projective decomposition of the static mask. This order is essential. The fixed residue selectors and the smooth row–orbit factors are first put into canonical cells, while the complete static mask remains one kernel. The factors occurring in a particular projective representation of that kernel are not absorbed into the row weights.

## Canonical pre-mask row cells

###### ref-602deb004ab175ae

###### ref-00124a553020ddc0

Set $H=\operatorname{rad}|h_0|$, and fix an integer $q=O_{h_0}(1)$. We take $q$ to be a common multiple of all fixed row and orbit residue moduli in the packet, including the period $H_0$ used in [\[eq:tpc50-canonical-pre-mask-cellization\]](TPC-050.md#ref-1b25c6399892bf2a), and let $$\mathcal R_X  :=\left\{(\ell,d):  L<\ell\leq2L,\ \ell\ {\rm prime},\  D<d\leq2D,\ \mu^2(d)=1,\ (d,H)=1  \right\}.  \label{eq:tpc50-complete-row-universe}$$ We assume $L>4D$, $L,D\to\infty$, and $L,D\leq X^C$ for one fixed $C$. Put $N_X=|\mathcal R_X|$. Then $$N_X\asymp_H\frac{LD}{\log L}.  \label{eq:tpc50-complete-row-count}$$ This follows from the prime number theorem and the elementary squarefree count with fixed coprimality conditions [`\citep{IwaniecKowalski2004}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-050.md#cite-49dfea87eaada156).

###### ref-0ad66dc0f979b4c6

Let $\mathcal A\subset(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^2$ be a fixed row-residue cell. We call it *admissible* when it contains a residue pair for which the prime progression and the squarefree progression in [\[eq:tpc50-complete-row-universe\]](TPC-050.md#ref-602deb004ab175ae) both have positive local density. This is a finite, explicitly checkable condition. Let $\psi,\omega\in C^\infty([1,2])$ be fixed nonzero shapes with positive squared integrals. This includes the constant shape one; compactly supported interior shapes are also allowed. Assume that $\mathcal A$ contains at least one locally admissible residue pair on which the two shapes contribute positive density. For $e\in\{0,1\}$, real $t_1,t_2$, a fixed unit-modulus periodic phase $\chi$, and a scalar $c\neq0$, define $$b_{\ell,d}  :=c(\log\ell)^e\psi(\ell/L)\omega(d/D)  \mathbf 1_{(\ell,d)\bmod q\in\mathcal A}  \chi(\ell,d)\ell^{it_1}d^{it_2}.  \label{eq:tpc50-canonical-cell-weight}$$ The case $e=1$ includes a physical prime-source logarithm; the case $e=0$ includes an unweighted opposite-row cell. Additional real-axis Mellin phases or fixed Fourier characters may be included in $\chi(\ell,d)\ell^{it_1}d^{it_2}$.

In the inherited opened-row notation, let $V=R_{\rm src}^{1/2+o(1)}$ be the hard upper cutoff on $d$ [`\citep{WangTPC25}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-050.md#cite-39e8dc14c55a2411). Thus the phrase is a quantitative support condition, not merely a name for a nonzero summand.

> **Proposition: Identification of the direct opposite-row factors** \[prop:tpc50-direct-provenance-identification\] Let $\mathscr C_{\rm prov}:=\mathscr C$ be the pre-mask cellization obtained by applying exactly the fixed-period Fourier expansion and the real-axis Mellin inversion in the TPC–25 provenance formula, before applying any TPC–36 Fourier–Ferrers–gcd representation of the static mask. Every nonzero coefficient-free opposite-row factor on a nondegenerate interior pre-terminal block of TPC–25/36, extended by zero to $\mathcal R_X$, is of the form [\[eq:tpc50-canonical-cell-weight\]](TPC-050.md#ref-0ad66dc0f979b4c6) with $e=0$, up to replacing $\mathcal A$ by a fixed finite union of admissible residue pairs and making one of finitely many fixed dyadic subdivisions of the inherited row interval. If a source factor contains the physical logarithm and its remaining fixed residue coefficient is split into nonzero fixed values on admissible cells, the same statement holds with $e=1$. A general bounded coefficient satisfying only $|\zeta_{\ell,d}|\ll1$ is not covered by the $e=1$ assertion and is instead retained in the arbitrary weight on the other side.

> **Proof** Before separation, the inherited unary row coefficient is a product of $\mu(d)$, one possible factor $\log\ell$, fixed smooth dyadic cutoffs in $\ell/L$ and $d/D$, and a fixed-period residue factor [`\citep{WangTPC25,WangTPC36}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-050.md#cite-39e8dc14c55a2411); [\[WangTPC36\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-050.md#cite-7286aa6023fa493f). In the direct-cell implementation, residue splitting gives a fixed set $\mathcal A$ and one may take $\chi=1$. Equivalently, in the finite Fourier implementation, each summand carries one fixed character $\chi$, whose modulus is one on its admissible support. For the smooth row–orbit factors, real-axis Mellin inversion replaces each occurrence of $W(\ell dj/X)$ by a scalar transform coefficient, an orbit phase, and row powers $$(\ell d)^{it}d^{is}  =\ell^{it}d^{i(t+s)}.$$ These row powers have modulus one. Products of the finitely many fixed smooth shapes remain fixed smooth shapes, and all transform coefficients may be placed in the nonzero scalar $c$. This is exactly [\[eq:tpc50-canonical-cell-weight\]](TPC-050.md#ref-0ad66dc0f979b4c6); the Möbius sign is another unit phase on the squarefree support.
>
> On a nondegenerate interior block, the zero extension is coefficient-preserving because $\mathcal R_X$ is the complete prime–squarefree dyadic row universe for this pre-terminal packet: condition [\[eq:tpc50-interior-cutoff-condition\]](TPC-050.md#ref-4dbed90e798fcea6) removes the inherited hard cutoff $d\leq V$, while the fixed subdivision and rescaling align the two dyadic intervals. The omitted rows are therefore precisely zeros of its fixed unary supports. No static-mask representation factor $p_\eta$ or $q_\eta$ has yet been introduced. An independently pruned row subset created downstream is not covered by this identification. Any block failing [\[eq:tpc50-interior-cutoff-condition\]](TPC-050.md#ref-4dbed90e798fcea6) is outside the proposition, whether its truncated support has positive or vanishing relative density. Only after a separate recellization and verification of the fixed-shape or aggregate lower-energy hypotheses may [\[thm:tpc50-energy-selection\]](TPC-050.md#ref-2d5e0554bf7ef3c7) select its energetic cells; that theorem does not by itself control their signed Mellin reassembly.

> **Corollary: Location of downstream pruning** \[cor:tpc50-downstream-factor-location\] In the left physical residual route, fix a direct pre-mask residue and Mellin layer and fixed downstream data $(j,p,r)$. The modulus of the unary factor indexed by the opposite row $\gamma$, namely $|v_{a,\tau}(\gamma)|$, is the $e=0$ weight identified in [\[prop:tpc50-direct-provenance-identification\]](TPC-050.md#ref-c81bb4d2dd593239). ###### ref-e16f1cd92439a696

###### ref-bb3705f2be6f9af1

> **Theorem: Fixed-complexity row energy** \[thm:tpc50-fixed-cell-energy\] For every fixed admissible cell as above, there are constants $\mathfrak c_{\rm cell},F_{\rm cell}\in(0,\infty)$ such that $$\sum_{(\ell,d)\in\mathcal R_X}|b_{\ell,d}|^2  =\bigl(\mathfrak c_{\rm cell}+o(1)\bigr)  |c|^2LD(\log L)^{2e-1}.  \label{eq:tpc50-fixed-cell-l2-asymptotic}$$ Moreover, $$\|b\|_\infty^2\ll_{\rm cell}|c|^2(\log L)^{2e}.  \label{eq:tpc50-fixed-cell-linfty}$$ Both statements are uniform in all real Mellin parameters and all unit-modulus phases in [\[eq:tpc50-canonical-cell-weight\]](TPC-050.md#ref-0ad66dc0f979b4c6). Consequently $$\boxed{  \operatorname{Flat}_{\mathcal R_X}(b)  :=\frac{N_X\|b\|_\infty^2}{\|b\|_2^2}  \leq F_{\rm cell}.}  \label{eq:tpc50-fixed-cell-flatness}$$

###### ref-c5c32c77e000b526

###### ref-d9c4107a6eb69a64

> **Proof** Split the fixed set $\mathcal A$ into its finitely many residue pairs. For every prime-admissible class, the fixed-modulus weighted prime number theorem gives [`\citep{IwaniecKowalski2004}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-050.md#cite-49dfea87eaada156) $$\sum_{\substack{L<\ell\leq2L\\                   \ell\ {\rm prime}\\                   \ell\equiv a\pmod q}}  (\log\ell)^{2e}|\psi(\ell/L)|^2  =\bigl(c_{a,\psi}+o(1)\bigr)  L(\log L)^{2e-1},  \label{eq:tpc50-weighted-prime-energy}$$ where $c_{a,\psi}\geq0$. Likewise, Möbius inversion followed by counting in a fixed progression gives $$\sum_{\substack{D<d\leq2D\\                   d\equiv b\pmod q\\                   \mu^2(d)=1,\ (d,H)=1}}  |\omega(d/D)|^2  =\bigl(c_{b,\omega,H}+o(1)\bigr)D,  \label{eq:tpc50-weighted-squarefree-energy}$$ with a nonnegative, explicitly determined local-density constant. Admissibility and nontriviality of the fixed shapes make the sum of the products $c_{a,\psi}c_{b,\omega,H}$ over $(a,b)\in\mathcal A$ strictly positive. This proves [\[eq:tpc50-fixed-cell-l2-asymptotic\]](TPC-050.md#ref-86394a9454251022). Boundedness of the fixed shapes gives [\[eq:tpc50-fixed-cell-linfty\]](TPC-050.md#ref-e16f1cd92439a696). The Mellin and Fourier factors have modulus one, so none of these estimates depends on their real parameters. Finally combine [\[eq:tpc50-complete-row-count\]](TPC-050.md#ref-00124a553020ddc0), [\[eq:tpc50-fixed-cell-l2-asymptotic\]](TPC-050.md#ref-86394a9454251022), and [\[eq:tpc50-fixed-cell-linfty\]](TPC-050.md#ref-e16f1cd92439a696).

The hypothesis is not an unproved distribution conjecture. For every named fixed smooth and residue cell it is decided by a finite local check, and the two asymptotics in the proof are standard fixed-modulus theorems. A zero or locally forbidden Fourier term is simply omitted.

## Weighted survival with arbitrary pruning on the other side

###### ref-a0975df092256ca6

###### ref-02862446d796277e

###### ref-ed87d3d0f2f82025

On $\mathcal R_X\times\mathcal R_X$, put $$\begin{aligned}  M_X(\alpha,\gamma)  &:=  \mathbf 1_{\ell_\alpha\ne\ell_\gamma}  \mathbf 1_{|m_\alpha-m_\gamma|>\Delta}  \mathbf 1_{(d_\alpha,d_\gamma)\leq G},  \label{eq:tpc50-static-mask}\\  m_\alpha&:=\ell_\alpha d_\alpha,  \qquad  \Delta:=LDX^{-\kappa_0},  \qquad  G:=X^{\kappa_0},  \label{eq:tpc50-static-mask-scales}\end{aligned}$$ where $\kappa_0>0$ is fixed. > **Theorem: Canonical-cell survival** \[thm:tpc50-canonical-cell-survival\] Let $b$ be the modulus of a nonzero direct provenance factor from [\[prop:tpc50-direct-provenance-identification\]](TPC-050.md#ref-c81bb4d2dd593239); or, more generally, let it be the modulus of a canonical cell weight [\[eq:tpc50-canonical-cell-weight\]](TPC-050.md#ref-0ad66dc0f979b4c6). For every nonzero weight $a$ on the other row system, with completely arbitrary support and concentration, $$\boxed{  \delta_w(M_X;a,b)  \geq1-F_{\rm cell}\epsilon_X  =1-o(1).}  \label{eq:tpc50-canonical-survival}$$ The conclusion is uniform in the real Mellin parameters and fixed unit-modulus residue phases. It also holds after exchanging the two row systems.

###### ref-a0c24722567ba305

> **Proof** For every fixed $\alpha$, [\[eq:tpc50-mask-degree-defect\]](TPC-050.md#ref-ed87d3d0f2f82025) and the definition of the supremum norm give $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_\gamma M_X(\alpha,\gamma)b_\gamma^2  &\geq\|b\|_2^2-\epsilon_XN_X\|b\|_\infty^2  \notag\\  &\geq  \bigl(1-F_{\rm cell}\epsilon_X\bigr)\|b\|_2^2,  \label{eq:tpc50-rowwise-weighted-survival}\end{aligned}$$ where the second line is [\[eq:tpc50-fixed-cell-flatness\]](TPC-050.md#ref-bb3705f2be6f9af1). Multiply by $a_\alpha^2$, sum over $\alpha$, and divide by the denominator in [\[eq:tpc50-complete-cell-weighted-survival\]](TPC-050.md#ref-5b3c86c9583329cc). No lower-density information about $a$ is used.

This asymmetric formulation is useful for the physical residual packet. The arbitrary side may carry the squarefree target selector, large-prime/cofactor restrictions, terminal weights, or any other coefficient-specific pruning. Only the opposite row cell needs the fixed-complexity flatness proved above. If a later reduction also independently prunes that opposite row, the theorem is not automatic: one must recheck the concentration quantities in [\[eq:tpc50-source-concentration\]](TPC-050.md#ref-df02e61a4e7755e7)–[\[eq:tpc50-gcd-concentration\]](TPC-050.md#ref-462daf1ae03b0c53).

> **Proposition: Large-gcd zero-survival block** \[prop:tpc50-gcd-block\] Assume $G=o(D)$. For all sufficiently large $X$, there is a prime $g$ satisfying $$G<g\le2G,  \qquad (g,H)=1,  \label{eq:tpc50-gcd-prime}$$ and a set $$S_{\rm gcd}  :=\{(\ell,ge)\in\mathcal R_X:  D/g<e\le2D/g,\ \mu^2(e)=1,\ (e,gH)=1\}  \label{eq:tpc50-gcd-block}$$ such that $$|S_{\rm gcd}|\asymp_H\frac Ng  =NX^{-\kappa_0+o(1)},  \qquad  M_X(\alpha,\gamma)=0  \quad(\alpha,\gamma\in S_{\rm gcd}).  \label{eq:tpc50-gcd-block-data}$$ Thus $$\delta_w(M_X;\mathbf 1_{S_{\rm gcd}},\mathbf 1_{S_{\rm gcd}})=0.  \label{eq:tpc50-gcd-zero-survival}$$ At the inherited endpoint the block has size $$|S_{\rm gcd}|  =X^{267/400-\kappa_0+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc50-gcd-block-endpoint}$$

## Why fixed provenance excludes these selectors

###### ref-6e736e7fcf43b014

###### ref-66370d8144571d95

> **Proposition: Unit-phase immunity** \[prop:tpc50-unit-phase-immunity\] Let $u:I\to\mathbb C$, $v:J\to\mathbb C$ have unit modulus and let $U:I\times J\to\mathbb C$ have unit modulus on the support of $M$. > **Proof** Every term in [\[eq:tpc50-general-weighted-survival\]](TPC-050.md#ref-8e1fbae5c3ef7a1d) depends only on the absolute values of the two unary weights and of the kernel entry.

###### ref-7adaeb76fc0c0674

> **Remark: Upper-only compatibility and physical incompatibility** \[rem:tpc50-obstruction-provenance-boundary\] The weights in \[[prop:tpc50-same-source-block](TPC-050.md#ref-226370699f85c879),[prop:tpc50-near-block](TPC-050.md#ref-caad4cb9c42b1d0a),[prop:tpc50-gcd-block](TPC-050.md#ref-a7834da02f7f6fc4),[lem:tpc50-admitted-neighbor](TPC-050.md#ref-430844f1a134a8ca)\] are bounded by one, have support at most $N$, and obey all row-count, pointwise, and $\ell^2$ upper bounds inherited by TPC–45/48. They are therefore genuine countermodels to any deduction based only on that upper interface. They are not asserted to be the literal physical coefficient.
>
> More precisely, the same-source block selects one prime that moves with $X$; the near block requires a normalized cutoff of width $X^{-\kappa_0}$; and the gcd block requires divisibility by a modulus $g\asymp X^{\kappa_0}$. None is generated by a nonzero fixed smooth dyadic family, a fixed-period residue cell, or a unit-modulus Mellin phase. A power-narrow smooth selector would also violate the inherited uniform normalized $C^B$-bounds. >
> Finally, cancellation between different signed residue/Mellin layers does not alter this conclusion. Such cancellation changes the representation condition number and belongs to the frame-efficiency gate, not to the nonnegative survival ratio [\[eq:tpc50-general-weighted-survival\]](TPC-050.md#ref-8e1fbae5c3ef7a1d).

> **Proof** Equation [\[eq:tpc50-R36-projective-mass\]](TPC-050.md#ref-3ee8395fa88f24b3) gives $\varpi=0$, and [\[eq:tpc50-endpoint-survival\]](TPC-050.md#ref-266feeff53f18544) gives $\theta=0$. Substitute in [\[eq:tpc50-isolated-budget-condition\]](TPC-050.md#ref-63724a9e5ccec92d).

###### ref-dc0ea3f75e738bd5

The phrase *consumes no polynomial allowance* concerns exactly the Hilbertized static row-output face in [\[eq:tpc50-canonical-cell-atomic-transfer\]](TPC-050.md#ref-7fb59184f1b4206e). It is not a statement that the remaining $1/400$ allowance closes the full determinant packet. If all remaining operations have total loss exponent $\lambda_{\rm other}$, the full multiplicative ledger would still require $$\lambda_{\rm other}\leq\frac1{400}.  \label{eq:tpc50-remaining-budget}$$ No estimate for $\lambda_{\rm other}$ is inferred here.

## What has and has not been proved

The positive theorem removes one precise obstruction. Even when the source side is arithmetically sparse, the complete static mask deletes only $o(1)$ of the product $\ell^2$-mass of every identified fixed-complexity opposite-row residue/Mellin cell covered by [\[prop:tpc50-direct-provenance-identification\]](TPC-050.md#ref-c81bb4d2dd593239). Therefore active-row sparsity by itself does not create a polynomial projective-to-atomic loss in this canonical order of decomposition.

The following interfaces remain open and are not consequences of \[[thm:tpc50-canonical-cell-survival](TPC-050.md#ref-1d82f1d89fab0768),[cor:tpc50-canonical-cell-transfer](TPC-050.md#ref-97ebbf42ba19253d)\].

###### ref-b289025caa3cbb55

1.  **Continuous Mellin frame efficiency.** The real Mellin phases preserve row flatness, and Schwartz variation gives finite weighted $L^1$ mass, but two distinct Mellin layers are not automatically orthogonal in the physical coefficient Hilbert space. Minkowski reassembly supplies an upper envelope; it does not supply a lower frame bound comparing that envelope with the squared norm of the signed, reassembled physical coefficient. Selecting one favorable Mellin parameter by pigeonhole is invalid.

2.  **The full TPC–48 Banach profile envelope.** The quantity controlled in [\[eq:tpc50-canonical-cell-atomic-transfer\]](TPC-050.md#ref-7fb59184f1b4206e) is one genuinely Hilbertized row-output face. The TPC–48 envelope also carries the large-sieve profile constant and an outer signed Bochner integral. This paper does not identify that Banach profile envelope with a nuclear norm or with the physical diagonal.

3.  **The coherent prime square.** At fixed source $m$ and cofactor $r$, the prime sum is formed before taking the norm. The exact orthogonality between distinct residual labels separates $r$, but it does not separate the primes inside $$\sum_p A_\omega(p,r,m)\mathrm e(-pr\alpha)        v_{\omega,p,r,m}.   \label{eq:tpc50-open-coherent-prime-sum}$$ No prime exponential-sum cancellation, dispersion estimate, or pointwise large-sieve replacement is proved here.

4.  **Residual grouping.** The residual lift distinguishes the labels $s=d(m)r$. Returning to the grouped physical output applies a map whose squared norm is the maximal residual-fiber multiplicity. The present row-mask theorem neither bounds that multiplicity by $X^{o(1)}$ nor finds cancellation between labels in one fiber.

5.  **Frozen completion versus the physical shift.** The frozen family extends the coefficient field constructed at $h_0$ to determinant equations with arbitrary intercept $h$. Only $h=h_0$ has physical provenance. A square estimate for the completed family need not have the same normalization as the one-slice atomic diagonal. No completed-to-$h_0$ localization theorem is supplied.

6.  **Residual and native affine Gram faces.** The large-prime residual synthesis is obtained after low-sign averaging. Its cross-inner-product structure is not automatically the native affine physical Gram structure whose atomic diagonal is $\mathcal D_0$. The static survival theorem does not identify those two faces.

7.  **Slope collision and the remaining endpoint ledger.** The common-profile large sieve may still incur determinant-slope collision, and none of that cost is cancelled by [\[eq:tpc50-endpoint-survival\]](TPC-050.md#ref-266feeff53f18544). Such losses belong to $\lambda_{\rm other}$ in [\[eq:tpc50-remaining-budget\]](TPC-050.md#ref-dc0ea3f75e738bd5).

## Exact scope of the canonical hypothesis

The proof applies unconditionally to the canonical terms produced by direct finite Fourier decomposition of the stated fixed residue masks and by real-axis Mellin inversion of fixed smooth shapes. It does not cover any of the following broadenings without a new argument:

-   a residue modulus growing with $X$;

-   an $X$-dependent smooth family whose nonzero support degenerates to a vanishing fraction of a dyadic row cell;

-   a hard-cutoff boundary block failing [\[eq:tpc50-interior-cutoff-condition\]](TPC-050.md#ref-4dbed90e798fcea6), unless it is recellized and its fixed-shape or aggregate energy hypotheses are verified separately;

-   a Mellin contour shift that introduces non-unit power weights; or

-   an arbitrary bounded row coefficient substituted for the fixed character/phase provenance.

These exclusions are quantitative, not cosmetic: the construction in [\[prop:tpc50-abstract-interface-counterexample\]](TPC-050.md#ref-3b993917c66e0e81) gives zero survival under the last broadening.

Accordingly, the theorem neither breaches the parity barrier nor proves any fixed-shift prime-pair or twin-prime assertion. Its contribution is the sharper localization of the remaining gap: after the actual fixed-complexity provenance is unpacked before the static mask, the identified direct active-cell weighted-survival subdoor is open, while the Mellin-frame, coherent-prime, residual-grouping, and physical-localization doors remain.
