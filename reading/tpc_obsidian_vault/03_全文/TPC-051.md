## Abstract

The preceding twin-prime-correlation reduction left a signed Mellin reassembly between fixed-complexity cell survival and the full projective profile. We determine exactly which frame statement is available. If a continuous Mellin dictionary is allowed arbitrary signed coefficient densities, no lower frame can hold: every synthesis into a finite-dimensional physical Hilbert space has a nonzero smooth kernel. For the phase dictionary at logarithmic samples $y_1,\ldots,y_N$, the kernel is explicit, $$g(t)=\prod_{n=1}^{N}\left(\frac d{dt}+iy_n\right)\rho(t),  \qquad \rho\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R),$$ and neighboring continuous atoms have arbitrarily large total-variation-to-output condition number. Thus a raw continuous Riesz bound is not merely unproved; it is false.

One provenance-fixed scalar Mellin direction is different at the exact algebraic level. For a fixed smooth shape $W$, positive samples $x_n$, and coefficients $c_n$, Mellin inversion gives the exact identities $$\mathcal E_B(W;c)=A_B(W)\|c\|_2,  \qquad  \|\mathcal S_Wc\|_2^2=\sum_n|c_nW(x_n)|^2,$$ where $A_B(W)$ is a polynomially weighted Wiener–Mellin mass. Consequently, on a declared sample set on which $\inf_n|W(x_n)|\geq\eta>0$, the canonical condition number is $O_{B,W,\eta}(1)$. Coordinate samples approaching a zero of the physical cutoff give quantified polynomial, or even super-polynomial, obstructions. For a fixed finite scalar family we bound the quotient condition number in terms of its normalized sampled Gram matrix. Weak convergence of the sample measures and linear independence of the limiting shapes then give an $O(1)$ quotient frame for that abstract fixed-family problem.

We apply this criterion to the prime–squarefree dyadic row universe. Its normalized logarithmic sample measure converges to the measure with density $e^{x+y}$ on $[0,\log2]^2$. Uniformly for a log-Fourier bank of degree at most a fixed power of $\log X$, the sampled Gram is bounded above and below. The literal TPC static mask changes its lower frame bound by at most $$O\!\left(\epsilon_X(2K+1)^2\right),  \qquad  \epsilon_X\ll  L^{-1+o(1)}+D^{-1+o(1)}+X^{-\kappa_0+o(1)}.$$ Hence every declared fixed finite scalar family, and every retained finite log-Fourier bank in the stated range, has zero polynomial sampling-and-mask cost at the inherited numerical endpoint. Passing from this theorem to the actual TPC provenance requires an additional bridge: the genuine orbit-translate family is $X$-dependent and Hilbert-valued, and its weighted continuous Mellin envelope must be compared with the sampled Hilbert energy. Those uniform Gram or relative-tail and norm-comparison hypotheses are formulated here but are not verified. This bridge therefore remains open before coherent prime summation, residual grouping, physical-shift localization, and native-Gram identification. No fixed-shift prime-pair estimate, parity breach, or twin-prime conclusion is claimed.

# Introduction

The TPC reduction has reached a narrow but consequential question. A fixed-complexity residue/Mellin cell survives the literal static row mask with zero polynomial loss [`\citep{WangTPC50}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-051.md#cite-0d8078e2652e9912), whereas the completed TPC–48 estimate is still expressed as a signed outer integral of layerwise Hilbert norms [`\citep{WangTPC48}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-051.md#cite-b2c88d6d47db0c74). Can the latter integral be replaced by the energy of the physically reassembled coefficient? Calling this a “frame problem” is useful only after the domain of the putative frame has been specified. The continuous Mellin coefficient space, the finite family of physical cutoff shapes, and the one provenance-fixed coefficient density are three different domains.

This paper separates them. The strongest naive statement is false for an elementary dimensional reason: at every finite scale the physical coefficient space is finite dimensional, while arbitrary smooth signed Mellin densities form an infinite-dimensional space. We give both an abstract smooth kernel and an explicit differential kernel for the phase dictionary. Thus no amount of numerical conditioning evidence can establish a lower Riesz bound on the full continuous parameter space.

The negative theorem does not apply to one fixed Mellin density. Mellin inversion recombines the unique transform density of a fixed cutoff into the values of that cutoff at declared samples. This gives an exact formula, not a heuristic orthogonality assertion. Separately, for a fixed finite scalar family the remaining question is finite dimensional: after exact redundancies are removed, its condition number is controlled by the least eigenvalue of a normalized sampled Gram matrix. We prove that this eigenvalue is bounded away from zero for every fixed limiting-independent scalar family on the prime–squarefree dyadic row universe. We also prove a uniform version for a retained log-Fourier bank of polylogarithmic degree and show that the literal static mask perturbs its lower bound by only $O(\varepsilon_X(2K+1)^2)$. These statements do not by themselves identify the genuine $X$-dependent, Hilbert-valued orbit-translate family with a fixed scalar family.

## Main results

The results are organized by the logical strength of their domains.

1.  **Continuous-domain no-go.** Every integral synthesis from smooth densities on an interval into a finite-dimensional Hilbert space has a nonzero smooth kernel ([\[thm:tpc51-smooth-kernel\]](TPC-051.md#ref-fd06aabefd01ffaa)). For logarithmic phase samples the kernel is explicit ([\[prop:tpc51-explicit-phase-kernel\]](TPC-051.md#ref-699cca28a51dc7f7)), and neighboring atoms give arbitrarily large signed-measure condition number ([\[prop:tpc51-neighbouring-atoms\]](TPC-051.md#ref-3bcaccc218e99477)).

2.  **Exact canonical direction.** For a fixed smooth shape, its weighted Mellin–Wiener envelope and its physical synthesis norm are given exactly by [\[thm:tpc51-canonical-mellin-direction\]](TPC-051.md#ref-a05382eb64ded690). A declared sample set satisfying $\inf_n|W(x_n)|\geq\eta>0$ has $O_{B,W,\eta}(1)$ condition number. Coordinate samples approaching a finite-order zero incur the corresponding power loss, and a flat cutoff boundary can incur losses beyond every fixed power ([\[cor:tpc51-boundary-zero\]](TPC-051.md#ref-53462fb1c5efeafb)).

3.  **Finite quotient criterion.** For a fixed finite scalar shape family, the squared quotient condition number lies between the inverse least positive eigenvalue and the number of retained shapes times that inverse eigenvalue for its normalized Gram matrix ([\[thm:tpc51-finite-gram-criterion\]](TPC-051.md#ref-6e7bee73421bafb1)). Weak convergence of sample measures and limiting linear independence therefore give an $O(1)$ quotient frame ([\[thm:tpc51-limit-gram\]](TPC-051.md#ref-ad370c5946c0b34e)).

4.  **Arithmetic sampling.** The logarithmic empirical measures of admissible prime–squarefree rows converge unconditionally to $$d\nu(x,y)=e^{x+y}\,dx\,dy,    \qquad 0\leq x,y\leq\log2,$$ and their Fourier moments admit a quantitative fixed-modulus estimate ([\[thm:tpc51-row-equidistribution\]](TPC-051.md#ref-a835ca15e3caaa93)). Consequently every fixed limiting-independent scalar family has a stable sampled Gram, and every log-Fourier bank of degree $K\leq(\log X)^A$ obeys uniform Riesz bounds ([\[thm:tpc51-polylog-fourier-frame\]](TPC-051.md#ref-d440e04317a184b0)).

5.  **Literal-mask robustness for declared finite families.** If a zero–one mask deletes at most $\varepsilon_X$ of the complete row universe in every source row, the lower bound of a two-dimensional degree-$K$ bank changes by at most $C\varepsilon_X(2K+1)^2$, uniformly in an arbitrary nonnegative weight on the other side ([\[thm:tpc51-masked-frame\]](TPC-051.md#ref-3947eeb8f92a92b3)). At the inherited endpoint this is $o(1)$ for every fixed polylogarithmic degree. The fixed finite scalar-family version has constants depending on that family.

6.  **Exact boundary and conditional bridge.** The positive result proves the abstract fixed finite scalar-family and retained finite-bank sampled frames. Transfer to the actual coefficientwise pre-terminal provenance requires a uniform normalized Gram or uniform relative Fourier-tail theorem for the $X$-dependent Hilbert-valued orbit-translate family, together with a norm comparison from the actual weighted continuous envelope to that Hilbert energy. records this criterion and the fact that it is not verified here. The result also does not commute an outer $L^1$ integral through the nonlinear TPC–48 layer norm and does not control the coherent prime square, residual grouping, physical-shift localization, or native affine Gram.

## Three levels of assertion

To prevent a local Hilbert-space lemma from being promoted into a number-theoretic conclusion, we use the following ledger throughout.

Level L0: exact algebra/analysis.

:   Kernels, Mellin inversion identities, Gram formulas, and finite-matrix perturbation bounds. These statements do not use primes.

Level L1: arithmetic interface.

:   Fixed-modulus prime number theory, squarefree progression counts, and the inherited static-mask degree defect. These prove the sampled quotient bounds on the stated row universe.

Level L2: target correlation.

:   A fixed-shift prime-pair or twin-prime estimate. No result at this level is proved or inferred here.

In particular, no actual TPC provenance gate is called closed merely from the abstract fixed-family theorem. Any future use of that word must first verify the bridge criterion in [\[prop:tpc51-actual-bridge-criterion\]](TPC-051.md#ref-b8da9ffd267f1aeb); it can never refer to the parity problem or the full TPC determinant route here.

# The provenance-canonical synthesis interface

This section reconstructs the exact coefficient interface inherited from TPC–25, TPC–36, and TPC–47–50 [`\citep{WangTPC25,WangTPC36,WangTPC47,WangTPC48,WangTPC49,WangTPC50}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-051.md#cite-39e8dc14c55a2411); [\[WangTPC36\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-051.md#cite-7286aa6023fa493f); [\[WangTPC47\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-051.md#cite-0276fa4f583cdc92); [\[WangTPC48\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-051.md#cite-b2c88d6d47db0c74); [\[WangTPC49\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-051.md#cite-089c241d456878e0); [\[WangTPC50\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-051.md#cite-0d8078e2652e9912). There are three different parameter spaces in that interface: a fixed finite residue decomposition, a continuous real-axis Mellin decomposition, and a later projective decomposition of the static mask. Only the first two belong to the provenance-canonical pre-mask cellization. Keeping them separate is essential: a terminal projective parameter from TPC–47/48 is not a physical Mellin atom, and a lower-frame assertion for all of its possible signed representations is false.

## Finite residue variables and continuous Mellin variables

## Why mask parameters are not canonical Mellin cells

###### ref-284e5f0b9d9ed592

Only after [\[eq:tpc51-canonical-cellization\]](TPC-051.md#ref-f91cc520330a8d0c) is fixed does TPC–36 apply the specified mask representation $$K_{\alpha\gamma}  =\int_{\Omega_{36}}p_\eta(\alpha)q_\eta(\gamma)  \,d\nu_{36}(\eta),  \qquad  \int_{\Omega_{36}}  \|p_\eta\|_\infty\|q_\eta\|_\infty\,d|\nu_{36}|(\eta)  =X^{o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc51-specified-mask-representation}$$ The parameter $\eta$ is a finite or finitely representable projective-mask parameter. It records same-source orthogonality, signed Ferrers separation, and gcd compression. It is not part of $\Theta_X^{\rm can}$ and has no representation-independent physical meaning by itself.

There may also be a further continuous Mellin variable when the smooth terminal factor $\Lambda(pr)=(\log pr)\Phi_0(pr/X)$ is separated. Its measure is again fixed by the real-axis Mellin transform of the declared terminal shape and has Schwartz weighted variation. TPC–47 retains this variable, the prime restriction, and $\mu(r)$ in $A_\omega(p,r,m)$ rather than in the opposite-row pre-mask cell. Accordingly it is a canonical *downstream* Mellin parameter, but it is not an element of $\Theta_X^{\rm can}$ in [\[eq:tpc51-canonical-parameter-space\]](TPC-051.md#ref-2945a81534d92c70). If it is retained as an auxiliary coordinate, its measure is tensorized with $\lambda_X$ only after the coefficientwise operator below has been formed.

The terminal symbol $\omega$ in TPC–47/48 combines a canonical pre-mask cell $(a,\tau)$, a mask parameter $\eta$, and any terminal Mellin variables. An individual $\omega$-layer therefore contains $u_{a,\tau}^{L}p_\eta$ and $v_{a,\tau}q_\eta$, while the literal masked tensor is recovered only after the complete signed $\eta$-integration. Appending a cancelling mask-layer pair changes layerwise quantities without changing the physical multiplier. Consequently no canonical cell statistic is assigned to a terminal $\omega$.

## Canonical synthesis, the actual direction, and the reduced Gram

###### ref-43a93e6ebdf7c7ab

###### ref-5fde56a3d38ec928

On its natural Bochner domain define $$\mathcal S_X^{L}f  :=\int_{\Theta_X^{\rm can}}  f(\theta)A_{X,\theta}^{L}\,d\mu_X(\theta)  \in\mathcal H_X^{\rm coeff}.  \label{eq:tpc51-canonical-synthesis}$$ For example it is defined whenever $\int |f(\theta)|\|A_{X,\theta}^{L}\|\,d\mu_X(\theta)<\infty$. After the fixed projective weights are placed in the measure, the literal physical coefficient is the distinguished direction $$\boxed{G_X^{L}=\mathcal S_X^{L}\mathbf 1.}  \label{eq:tpc51-actual-direction}$$ The coefficient function $\mathbf 1$ is not a freely selected Mellin density; it records the fixed provenance measure itself.

###### ref-0c2c4e79b9b1d60c

###### ref-291abceac8135e19

###### ref-f61a18e411c598f3

###### ref-1411762bc5d266bd

For the provenance measure at each fixed $X$, this synthesis is a bounded map from $L^2(\mu_X)$. Indeed, the finite residue part is atomic, the physical output support is finite, the real Mellin phases have unit modulus, and all remaining profile dependence grows at most polynomially in the Mellin variables. Since the density placed in $d\mu_X=d|\lambda_X|$ is Schwartz, one has $$\int_{\Theta_X^{\rm can}}  \|A_{X,\theta}^L\|_{\mathcal H_X^{\rm coeff}}^2\,d\mu_X(\theta)<\infty.  \label{eq:tpc51-L2-synthesis-integrability}$$ Cauchy–Schwarz therefore gives boundedness. Its Gram operator is $$\Gamma_X^L:=(\mathcal S_X^L)^*\mathcal S_X^L,  \label{eq:tpc51-Gram-operator}$$ with integral kernel $$\mathcal G_X^L(\theta,\theta')  :=\langle A_{X,\theta'}^L,A_{X,\theta}^L\rangle_{\mathcal H_X^{\rm coeff}}.  \label{eq:tpc51-Gram-kernel}$$ In particular, $$\|G_X^L\|_{\mathcal H_X^{\rm coeff}}^2  =\iint  \mathcal G_X^L(\theta,\theta')  \,d\mu_X(\theta)d\mu_X(\theta').  \label{eq:tpc51-actual-Gram-quadratic-form}$$

###### ref-ab0d509ab5813fc4

###### ref-f89f33ad15b4cbbc

###### ref-f351990b106048b5

For every fixed $X$, all index sets in [\[eq:tpc51-coefficient-Hilbert-space\]](TPC-051.md#ref-d47b0996b605b602) are finite, so the output space is finite-dimensional, whereas $L^2(\mu_X)$ has a non-atomic continuous Mellin component. Hence $\mathcal S_X^L$ has an infinite-dimensional kernel. A lower frame inequality on all of $L^2(\mu_X)$ is therefore impossible for dimensional reasons. The correct coefficient space is the quotient $$\mathfrak Q_X^L  :=L^2(\mu_X)/\ker\mathcal S_X^L,  \qquad  \|[f]\|_{\mathfrak Q_X^L}  :=\inf_{g\in\ker\mathcal S_X^L}\|f+g\|_{L^2(\mu_X)},  \label{eq:tpc51-quotient-space}$$ or, equivalently, the reduced subspace $$\mathcal K_X^L  :=(\ker\mathcal S_X^L)^\perp  =\overline{\operatorname{ran}(\mathcal S_X^L)^*}.  \label{eq:tpc51-reduced-space}$$ The reduced Gram is $$\Gamma_{X,\rm red}^L  :=\Gamma_X^L|_{\mathcal K_X^L}.  \label{eq:tpc51-reduced-Gram}$$ Its smallest positive spectral value is the exact finite-$X$ lower quotient frame constant. A useful asymptotic theorem must control this value on the specified provenance subspace or must control the single actual direction [\[eq:tpc51-actual-direction\]](TPC-051.md#ref-5fde56a3d38ec928); it must not replace the quotient by the false full-space lower bound.

###### ref-e98c38da7ce095bc

###### ref-f33cd657a5b4db6a

For later comparison with the inherited Minkowski argument, let $w_B(\theta)\ge1$ be the fixed polynomial/seminorm weight produced by the $C^B$ orbit profile. Define the canonical displayed-representation envelope $$\mathcal E_{1,B}^L(f)  :=\int_{\Theta_X^{\rm can}}  w_B(\theta)|f(\theta)|  \|A_{X,\theta}^L\|_{\mathcal H_X^{\rm coeff}}\,d\mu_X(\theta).  \label{eq:tpc51-canonical-L1-envelope}$$ The actual-direction condition number is $$\kappa_{\rm can,B}^L(X)  :=\frac{\mathcal E_{1,B}^L(\mathbf 1)^2}  {\|\mathcal S_X^L\mathbf 1\|_{\mathcal H_X^{\rm coeff}}^2},  \label{eq:tpc51-actual-condition-number}$$ when the denominator is nonzero. Proving $\kappa_{\rm can,B}^L(X)=X^{o(1)}$, or the corresponding uniform reduced-Gram bound on a declared finite provenance subspace, is a meaningful representation-efficiency statement. The existence of $\ker\mathcal S_X^L\ne\{0\}$ by itself is not a physical obstruction; a genuine negative result must place the actual direction, or a required canonical physical family, in a polynomial near-kernel.

## The full TPC–48 envelope is a different nonlinear object

###### ref-1b1270976e7b3153

###### ref-db2d1e77c5a1cb0f

The terminal projective layer in TPC–48 has, after Fourier transformation in the frozen intercept, $$B_{\omega,m}(\alpha)  :=\sum_{p,r}A_\omega(p,r,m)\mathrm e(-pr\alpha)  v_{\omega,p,r,m}.  \label{eq:tpc51-inherited-row-vector}$$ For fixed $m$, distinct residual labels separate the $r$-square, but at fixed $(m,r)$ the $p$-sum remains coherent. A fixed admissible global representation $\mathscr R$ has the envelope $$\mathfrak S_{\mathscr R}(\alpha)  :=\int  C_{\rm LS}(\phi_\omega,H_0)^{1/2}  \left(\sum_m\|B_{\omega,m}(\alpha)\|^2\right)^{1/2}  \,d|\nu|(\omega).  \label{eq:tpc51-full-profile-envelope}$$ Its norm order is exactly:

1.  the outer projective reassembly is $L^1(d|\nu|)$;

2.  the source-row aggregation inside each layer is $\ell^2$ in $m$;

3.  each summand uses the residual Hilbert norm; and

4.  the prime sum at fixed $(m,r)$ is formed before that norm is taken.

Moreover $C_{\rm LS}^{1/2}$ is a profile seminorm weight, not a declared Hilbert norm. The pointwise infimum over admissible representations is not integrated; annular arguments retain one fixed jointly measurable global representation. The resulting field is the completed frozen family, and only its $h=h_0$ slice has literal physical provenance.

###### ref-08c8c4bc0a2d279b

Thus [\[eq:tpc51-full-profile-envelope\]](TPC-051.md#ref-db2d1e77c5a1cb0f) is not the norm of $\mathcal S_X^L\mathbf 1$, is not a finite-matrix nuclear norm, and is not the native physical atomic diagonal $$\mathcal D_0=\sum_i\sum_u|b_{i,u}|^2.  \label{eq:tpc51-native-atomic-diagonal}$$ The operator [\[eq:tpc51-canonical-synthesis\]](TPC-051.md#ref-43a93e6ebdf7c7ab) isolates only the provenance-canonical residue/Mellin no-cancellation question before coherent prime summation. Even a subpower quotient condition number leaves the coherent prime square, the map $s=d(m)r$, frozen-to-$h_0$ localization, and the comparison with the native affine Gram face open.

# Finite-sampling kernels for continuous Mellin synthesis

We first separate a universal continuous-frame question from the provenance-fixed Mellin direction used later. The former has a sharp negative answer for a reason that is independent of arithmetic: at each finite value of $X$, the physical output space is finite dimensional, whereas a freely weighted continuous dictionary has infinitely many coefficient directions.

## A smooth kernel in every finite-dimensional output

Let $\Theta\subset\mathbb R^d$ be open, let $\mathcal H$ be a complex Hilbert space of finite dimension $N$, and let

$$Z:\Theta\longrightarrow\mathcal H$$

###### ref-760ae04558970095

###### ref-d19006bf4826dc6f

be continuous. For $g\in C_c^\infty(\Theta)$, define $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal S_Zg&:=\int_\Theta g(\tau)Z(\tau)\,d\tau,  \label{eq:tpc51-continuous-synthesis}\\  \mathcal E_Z(g)&:=\int_\Theta |g(\tau)|\,\|Z(\tau)\|_\mathcal H\,d\tau.  \label{eq:tpc51-continuous-envelope}\end{aligned}$$ The first integral is a Bochner integral. Both quantities are finite because $g$ has compact support.

###### ref-fd06aabefd01ffaa

###### ref-05bd67490baa803f

###### ref-a7534f935818b6eb

> **Theorem: Universal smooth-kernel obstruction** \[thm:tpc51-smooth-kernel\] Suppose $Z$ is not identically zero. Then there exists a nonzero $g\in C_c^\infty(\Theta)$ such that $$\mathcal S_Zg=0,  \qquad  \mathcal E_Z(g)>0.  \label{eq:tpc51-smooth-kernel}$$ Consequently, there is no finite constant $C$ for which $$\mathcal E_Z(g)\le C\|\mathcal S_Zg\|_\mathcal H  \label{eq:tpc51-false-universal-frame}$$ holds for every $g\in C_c^\infty(\Theta)$.

> **Proof** Choose $\tau_0\in\Theta$ with $Z(\tau_0)\ne0$. By continuity, there is an open ball $U\Subset\Theta$ and a number $m>0$ such that $\|Z(\tau)\|_\mathcal H\ge m$ on $U$. Choose $N+1$ pairwise disjoint balls $U_k\Subset U$ and nonzero functions $\rho_k\in C_c^\infty(U_k)$. Put $$w_k:=\int_\Theta \rho_k(\tau)Z(\tau)\,d\tau\in\mathcal H.$$ The $N+1$ vectors $w_k$ are linearly dependent, so there are coefficients $c_k$, not all zero, such that $\sum_kc_kw_k=0$. The function $g=\sum_kc_k\rho_k$ is nonzero because the supports of the $\rho_k$ are disjoint, and $\mathcal S_Zg=0$. Moreover, $$\mathcal E_Z(g)  \ge m\sum_k |c_k|\int_{U_k}|\rho_k(\tau)|\,d\tau>0.$$ This proves [\[eq:tpc51-smooth-kernel\]](TPC-051.md#ref-05bd67490baa803f); [\[eq:tpc51-false-universal-frame\]](TPC-051.md#ref-a7534f935818b6eb) is then impossible.

The theorem remains true when $N=N(X)$ grows: it is applied separately at each finite $X$. It does not use an artificial refactorization of a fixed physical tensor. It states that the coefficient space in [\[eq:tpc51-continuous-synthesis\]](TPC-051.md#ref-760ae04558970095) cannot be the whole of $C_c^\infty(\Theta)$ if a lower frame is desired.

## An explicit kernel for logarithmic phase samples

###### ref-638a2498d1adff75

###### ref-4539b7695a0bf342

The preceding dimension argument can be made completely explicit for the scalar Mellin phase dictionary. For $h\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$, use the positive-sign Fourier convention $$\mathcal F_+h(y):=\int_\mathbb Rh(t)\mathrm e^{ity}\,dt.  \label{eq:tpc51-positive-Fourier}$$ Integration by parts gives the sign-sensitive identity $$\mathcal F_+\bigl((\partial_t+iy_0)h\bigr)(y)  =i(y_0-y)\mathcal F_+h(y).  \label{eq:tpc51-ibp-sign}$$

###### ref-699cca28a51dc7f7

###### ref-56c9d5fe678c19a0

###### ref-250ae70e6ceea645

###### ref-06358b6f9cd22a65

> **Proposition: Explicit phase-sampling kernel** \[prop:tpc51-explicit-phase-kernel\] Let $y_1,\ldots,y_N\in\mathbb R$, let $a_1,\ldots,a_N\in\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}$, and put $$Z_{\bm y}(t)  :=\bigl(a_ne^{ity_n}\bigr)_{n=1}^N\in\mathbb C^N.  \label{eq:tpc51-phase-dictionary}$$ For every nonzero $\rho\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$, the function $$g_{\bm y,\rho}(t)  :=\prod_{n=1}^N(\partial_t+iy_n)\rho(t)  \label{eq:tpc51-explicit-phase-kernel-function}$$ is nonzero and satisfies $$\int_\mathbb Rg_{\bm y,\rho}(t)Z_{\bm y}(t)\,dt=0.  \label{eq:tpc51-explicit-phase-kernel-output}$$

###### ref-619ca0271804e30b

> **Proof** Iterating [\[eq:tpc51-ibp-sign\]](TPC-051.md#ref-4539b7695a0bf342) gives $$\mathcal F_+g_{\bm y,\rho}(y)  =i^N\prod_{n=1}^N(y_n-y)\mathcal F_+\rho(y).  \label{eq:tpc51-explicit-kernel-multiplier}$$ The $k$-th coordinate of [\[eq:tpc51-explicit-phase-kernel-output\]](TPC-051.md#ref-06358b6f9cd22a65) is $a_k\mathcal F_+g_{\bm y,\rho}(y_k)=0$.
>
> It remains to check that $g_{\bm y,\rho}\ne0$. If it vanished, [\[eq:tpc51-explicit-kernel-multiplier\]](TPC-051.md#ref-619ca0271804e30b) would imply that $\mathcal F_+\rho(y)=0$ for every real $y\notin\{y_1,\ldots,y_N\}$. The Fourier transform of a compactly supported smooth function extends to an entire function. It would therefore vanish identically, forcing $\rho=0$, a contradiction.

###### ref-fed2c8b7ada6257b

There is an equivalent physical-shape formulation. If the logarithmic sample points are $y_n$, choose a nonzero $F_X\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$ of the form $$F_X(s)=\chi(s)\prod_{n=1}^N(s-y_n).  \label{eq:tpc51-sample-zero-shape}$$ Its Fourier transform is Schwartz, while Fourier inversion gives $F_X(y_n)=0$ at every sample. This example depends on the full sample set, and its derivative seminorms need not be uniform in $X$. It therefore rules out a theorem uniform over arbitrary smooth shapes; it does not contradict a theorem for the fixed provenance shapes.

## Near kernels and phase aliases

###### ref-4567c84405f4a3e6

For a finite complex Borel measure $\nu$ on $\Theta$, extend the notation by $$\mathcal S_Z\nu:=\int_\Theta Z(\tau)\,d\nu(\tau),  \qquad  \mathcal E_Z(\nu):=\int_\Theta\|Z(\tau)\|_\mathcal H\,d|\nu|(\tau).  \label{eq:tpc51-measure-synthesis}$$

###### ref-bae679cebb43e52c

###### ref-3bcaccc218e99477

###### ref-ca4bec980a493707

###### ref-6649320a415dbb69

> **Proposition: Neighboring-atom condition number** \[prop:tpc51-neighboring-atoms\] \[prop:tpc51-neighbouring-atoms\] Let $I\subset\mathbb R$ be an interval on which $Z:I\to\mathcal H$ is continuously differentiable, and suppose $$\inf_{t\in I}\|Z(t)\|_\mathcal H\ge m>0,  \qquad  \sup_{t\in I}\|Z'(t)\|_\mathcal H\le L  \quad\text{for some }L>0.  \label{eq:tpc51-atom-regularity}$$ If $t,t+h\in I$ and $h\ne0$, then for $\nu_{t,h}=\delta_t-\delta_{t+h}$, $$\frac{\mathcal E_Z(\nu_{t,h})}       {\|\mathcal S_Z\nu_{t,h}\|_\mathcal H}  \ge\frac{2m}{L|h|},  \label{eq:tpc51-neighbor-condition}$$ with the ratio interpreted as $+\infty$ when the denominator is zero.

> **Proof** The two atoms are distinct, so $\mathcal E_Z(\nu_{t,h})=\|Z(t)\|+\|Z(t+h)\|\ge2m$. The fundamental theorem of calculus gives $$\|\mathcal S_Z\nu_{t,h}\|  =\|Z(t)-Z(t+h)\|  \le L|h|.$$

Thus continuity of a dictionary is an obstruction, rather than a source of a uniform signed lower frame, when its coefficient measure is free. Narrow smooth bumps centered at $t$ and $t+h$ give the same conclusion up to an arbitrarily small relative error.

For completeness, finite phase sampling also has long-range aliases.

###### ref-0ef49042c9999345

###### ref-db65fb8c673c2c3b

> **Proposition: Simultaneous phase alias** \[prop:tpc51-simultaneous-alias\] For the dictionary [\[eq:tpc51-phase-dictionary\]](TPC-051.md#ref-56c9d5fe678c19a0) and every integer $Q\ge2$, there is an integer $q$ with $1\le q\le Q^N$ such that, for every $t\in\mathbb R$, $$\|Z_{\bm y}(t+q)-Z_{\bm y}(t)\|_2  \le\frac{2\pi}{Q}\|\bm a\|_2.  \label{eq:tpc51-phase-alias-bound}$$ Consequently the signed pair $\delta_t-\delta_{t+q}$ has condition number at least $Q/\pi$.

> **Proof** Apply simultaneous Dirichlet approximation to $y_n/(2\pi)$: there is $1\le q\le Q^N$ such that $\|qy_n/(2\pi)\|_{\mathbb R/\mathbb Z}\le Q^{-1}$ for every $n$. Hence
>
> $$|\mathrm e^{iqy_n}-1|\le2\pi/Q.$$
>
> After factoring out the unit phases $\mathrm e^{ity_n}$, summing the squared coordinate bounds proves [\[eq:tpc51-phase-alias-bound\]](TPC-051.md#ref-db65fb8c673c2c3b). The envelope of the signed pair is $2\|\bm a\|_2$.

The alias $q$ may be extremely large, where a fixed Schwartz Mellin density is tiny. Accordingly, \[[prop:tpc51-neighboring-atoms](TPC-051.md#ref-bae679cebb43e52c),[prop:tpc51-simultaneous-alias](TPC-051.md#ref-0ef49042c9999345)\] disprove coefficient-uniform frame statements, but neither proposition estimates the one canonical coefficient measure. That fixed direction is treated next.

# The fixed canonical Mellin direction

The kernel theorems in [3](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-051.md#src-67e91ab7d7e6885b) allow the coefficient density to vary. The provenance-canonical representation does not: a fixed physical shape has one fixed real-axis Mellin transform. On orthogonal atomic coordinates its envelope-to-output ratio has an exact elementary formula.

## Wiener–Mellin mass and exact synthesis

###### ref-1fade5f9bfdc0401

###### ref-cb76d2664bc98d00

###### ref-5afb2452e1589b61

###### ref-741bf9c70c3a5f0a

Let $W\in C_c^\infty((0,\infty))$. We use the normalization $$\begin{aligned}  \widetilde W(t)  &:=\int_0^\infty W(x)x^{-it}\frac{dx}{x},  \label{eq:tpc51-direction-Mellin-transform}\\  W(x)  &=\frac1{2\pi}\int_\mathbb R\widetilde W(t)x^{it}\,dt.  \label{eq:tpc51-direction-Mellin-inversion}\end{aligned}$$ For an integer $B\ge0$, define the polynomially weighted Wiener–Mellin mass $$A_B(W)  :=\frac1{2\pi}\int_\mathbb R(1+|t|)^B        |\widetilde W(t)|\,dt.  \label{eq:tpc51-Wiener-Mellin-mass}$$ Schwartz decay makes $A_B(W)<\infty$. If $W\ne0$, then $$A_B(W)\ge A_0(W)\ge\|W\|_\infty>0  \label{eq:tpc51-Wiener-lower}$$ by [\[eq:tpc51-direction-Mellin-inversion\]](TPC-051.md#ref-cb76d2664bc98d00).

###### ref-a8d04c3fb2109575

###### ref-0bc1fa1a31c627a5

###### ref-119471538bfecbaa

Fix samples $x_1,\ldots,x_N>0$ and a coefficient vector $c=(c_1,\ldots,c_N)\in\mathbb C^N$. Put $$v_t(c):=(c_nx_n^{it})_{n=1}^N.  \label{eq:tpc51-canonical-phase-vector}$$ The canonical synthesis and its order-$B$ envelope are $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal S_Wc  &:=\frac1{2\pi}\int_\mathbb R\widetilde W(t)v_t(c)\,dt,  \label{eq:tpc51-direction-canonical-synthesis}\\  \mathcal E_B(W;c)  &:=\frac1{2\pi}\int_\mathbb R(1+|t|)^B      |\widetilde W(t)|\,\|v_t(c)\|_2\,dt.  \label{eq:tpc51-canonical-envelope}\end{aligned}$$

###### ref-37fc2caed422099f

###### ref-a05382eb64ded690

###### ref-7e36843483536c9c

###### ref-ace48b80fc45e6c6

###### ref-a2ca36d3c78b1a83

> **Theorem: Exact fixed-shape Mellin ledger** \[thm:tpc51-fixed-shape-identity\] \[thm:tpc51-canonical-mellin-direction\] For every $W$, every positive sample set, and every $c\in\mathbb C^N$, $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal S_Wc&=(c_nW(x_n))_{n=1}^N,  \label{eq:tpc51-fixed-shape-output}\\  \mathcal E_B(W;c)&=A_B(W)\|c\|_2.  \label{eq:tpc51-fixed-shape-envelope}\end{aligned}$$ Consequently, whenever $\mathcal S_Wc\ne0$, the canonical condition number is exactly $$\boxed{  \operatorname{cond}_{B,W}(c)  :=\frac{\mathcal E_B(W;c)}{\|\mathcal S_Wc\|_2}  =\frac{A_B(W)\|c\|_2}  {\left(\sum_{n=1}^N|c_n|^2|W(x_n)|^2\right)^{1/2}}.}  \label{eq:tpc51-fixed-shape-condition}$$

> **Proof** Mellin inversion gives [\[eq:tpc51-fixed-shape-output\]](TPC-051.md#ref-7e36843483536c9c) coordinate by coordinate. Since $|x_n^{it}|=1$, $\|v_t(c)\|_2=\|c\|_2$ for every $t$. This proves [\[eq:tpc51-fixed-shape-envelope\]](TPC-051.md#ref-ace48b80fc45e6c6) and then [\[eq:tpc51-fixed-shape-condition\]](TPC-051.md#ref-a2ca36d3c78b1a83).

The same proof works with orthogonal Hilbert atoms in place of the standard coordinate vectors and with positive atomic weights absorbed into $c_n$. It fails after nonorthogonal grouping unless the grouping map is included in the object being estimated.

## Interior stability and the exact worst direction

For a nonempty set $I\subset\{1,\ldots,N\}$, let $\mathbb C^I$ denote the coefficient vectors supported on $I$.

###### ref-a4a9b259690538e9

###### ref-a73e543fc0aa3c75

###### ref-3c38828659d410ff

> **Corollary: Exact interior minimax** \[cor:tpc51-interior-minimax\] If $W(x_n)\ne0$ for every $n\in I$, then $$\sup_{0\ne c\in\mathbb C^I}  \operatorname{cond}_{B,W}(c)  =\frac{A_B(W)}{\min_{n\in I}|W(x_n)|}.  \label{eq:tpc51-interior-minimax}$$ In particular, if $$|W(x_n)|\ge\eta>0  \qquad(n\in I),  \label{eq:tpc51-interior-lower}$$ then every canonical direction supported on $I$ has condition number at most $A_B(W)/\eta$.
>
> If $W(x_{n_0})=0$ for some $n_0$, then the coordinate vector $e_{n_0}$ is an exact kernel direction. Moreover, after adjoining any direction with nonzero output, the unrestricted condition number is unbounded.

> **Proof** Formula [\[eq:tpc51-fixed-shape-condition\]](TPC-051.md#ref-a2ca36d3c78b1a83) is bounded above by the right side of [\[eq:tpc51-interior-minimax\]](TPC-051.md#ref-a73e543fc0aa3c75); equality is attained by a coordinate vector at which $|W(x_n)|$ is minimal. If $W(x_{n_0})=0$, then [\[eq:tpc51-fixed-shape-output\]](TPC-051.md#ref-7e36843483536c9c) gives $\mathcal S_We_{n_0}=0$. Adding an arbitrarily large multiple of this kernel vector to one fixed nonzero-output coefficient leaves a nonzero output of fixed size while the envelope tends to infinity.

Thus a fixed shape has no continuous-frame pathology on a declared interior set where it is bounded away from zero. The relevant condition is lower physical mass, not a lower bound for every Mellin layer.

## Zeros and flat cutoff boundaries

The opposite conclusion holds near a zero of the fixed physical shape. This produces a canonical near kernel without changing the Mellin coefficient measure.

###### ref-4629a55935d5df05

###### ref-487f82004cb2c4ba

###### ref-c0f191520f654f16

> **Proposition: Finite-order zero obstruction** \[prop:tpc51-finite-zero-obstruction\] Let $x_0>0$, let $k\ge1$, and suppose $$W^{(j)}(x_0)=0\quad(0\le j<k),  \qquad  \sup_{x\in U}|W^{(k)}(x)|\le M_k  \label{eq:tpc51-order-k-zero}$$ on an interval $U$ containing $x_0$, where $M_k>0$. If $x_n\in U$, the interval between $x_0$ and $x_n$ lies in $U$, $0<|x_n-x_0|\le\Delta$, and $c=e_n$, then either $W(x_n)=0$, giving an exact kernel direction, or $$\operatorname{cond}_{B,W}(e_n)  \ge \frac{k!\,A_B(W)}{M_k\Delta^k}.  \label{eq:tpc51-order-k-condition}$$

> **Proof** Taylor’s theorem with integral remainder gives $$|W(x_n)|\le\frac{M_k}{k!}|x_n-x_0|^k  \le\frac{M_k}{k!}\Delta^k.$$ If the left side is nonzero, insert this bound in [\[eq:tpc51-fixed-shape-condition\]](TPC-051.md#ref-a2ca36d3c78b1a83) with $c=e_n$. If it vanishes, [\[eq:tpc51-fixed-shape-output\]](TPC-051.md#ref-7e36843483536c9c) gives the stated exact kernel.

###### ref-e02ecb41f5989780

###### ref-53462fb1c5efeafb

###### ref-3c90f994bf799dcc

###### ref-5b26cb96a237f2fb

> **Corollary: Flat-boundary super-polynomial obstruction** \[cor:tpc51-flat-boundary-obstruction\] \[cor:tpc51-boundary-zero\] Suppose $W$ extends smoothly by zero across a boundary point $x_0>0$ of its support. If a sequence of samples satisfies $$0<|x_X-x_0|\le X^{-\sigma}  \label{eq:tpc51-flat-boundary-distance}$$ for some fixed $\sigma>0$, then either $W(x_X)=0$ along a subsequence, producing exact kernel directions there, or, along every subsequence on which $W(x_X)\ne0$, for every $A>0$, $$\operatorname{cond}_{B,W}(e_{x_X})  \gg_{A,B,W,\sigma}X^A.  \label{eq:tpc51-flat-boundary-superpower}$$ Here $e_{x_X}$ denotes the one-coordinate vector at that sample.

> **Proof** Every derivative of $W$ vanishes at $x_0$. For a prescribed $A>0$, choose an integer $k>A/\sigma$. If $\sup_U|W^{(k)}|=0$ on a suitable interval $U$, then $W$ vanishes there and the sample is an exact kernel for all sufficiently large $X$. Otherwise apply [\[prop:tpc51-finite-zero-obstruction\]](TPC-051.md#ref-4629a55935d5df05) with $\Delta=X^{-\sigma}$. Then $\Delta^{-k}=X^{\sigma k}\ge X^A$, and the fixed constant $k!A_B(W)/M_k$ is absorbed into the implied constant.

The corollary is a worst-direction statement. It does not say that a boundary region carries a positive fraction of the physical atomic energy. A bounded-overlap partition and an aggregate lower-energy estimate may discard such directions. Conversely, an upper seminorm bound alone cannot do so.

## Scope of the canonical identity

The exact ledger above concerns a Mellin phase acting diagonally on orthogonal atomic coordinates. It does not commute a signed integral through a layerwise nonlinear norm. In particular, it does not by itself compare a Banach profile of the form $$\int C(\phi_\tau)^{1/2}\|B_\tau\|\,d|\nu|(\tau)$$ with the norm of the signed reassembled physical coefficient. Such a comparison additionally requires either a coefficient-specific vector frame or a reordering that performs canonical Mellin inversion before the nonlinear large-sieve step. No full TPC–48 profile closure is asserted here.

# Finite canonical families and quotient frames

A physical decomposition may contain several fixed shapes. Exact duplicates should be removed algebraically, whereas nearly duplicate shapes must be charged through a condition number. This section gives the finite-dimensional criterion that distinguishes the two cases.

## The normalized sampled Gram

###### ref-445e5e9a1d65f626

Let $K$ be a compact metric space, let $W_1,\ldots,W_R\in C(K)$ be nonzero fixed shapes, and choose positive normalization weights $$A_1,\ldots,A_R>0,  \qquad  D_A:=\operatorname{diag}(A_1,\ldots,A_R).  \label{eq:tpc51-family-normalizations}$$ For the Mellin application, $K$ may be a compact logarithmic box and $A_r$ is the weighted Wiener–Mellin mass of the corresponding fixed physical shape. The abstract argument below uses only positivity of the $A_r$.

###### ref-39f15d95a65d582e

###### ref-6ad74c6f1e03f81a

###### ref-bb725fefd4061c09

Fix samples $x_1,\ldots,x_N\in K$ and positive weights $w_1,\ldots,w_N$. Write $$M:=\sum_{n=1}^Nw_n  \label{eq:tpc51-total-sample-mass}$$ and define the physical sampled synthesis $F:\mathbb C^R\to\mathbb C^N$ by $$(Fc)_n  :=w_n^{1/2}\sum_{r=1}^Rc_rW_r(x_n).  \label{eq:tpc51-family-synthesis}$$ The declared canonical family envelope is $$\mathcal E_{\mathrm{fam}}(c)  :=M^{1/2}\sum_{r=1}^RA_r|c_r|  =M^{1/2}\|D_Ac\|_1.  \label{eq:tpc51-family-envelope}$$ In the Mellin specialization this is exactly the envelope obtained by separating the fixed shapes, applying Mellin inversion to each one, and then using Minkowski’s inequality.

###### ref-8a744f01461b45ce

###### ref-b27e8bb7e7750851

Introduce the normalized matrix and Gram matrix $$\mathsf B:=M^{-1/2}FD_A^{-1},  \qquad  G:=\mathsf B^*\mathsf B.  \label{eq:tpc51-normalized-Gram}$$ Thus, with $d=D_Ac$, $$\frac{\mathcal E_{\mathrm{fam}}(c)}{\|Fc\|_2}  =\frac{\|d\|_1}{\|\mathsf Bd\|_2}.  \label{eq:tpc51-family-condition-reduction}$$

###### ref-8927810aa649bc21

###### ref-5d5332065845d816

If $\ker\mathsf B\ne\{0\}$, the right side depends on the chosen representative of one physical output. The canonical quotient norm is $$\|[d]\|_{1,\mathrm q}  :=\inf_{k\in\ker\mathsf B}\|d+k\|_1,  \label{eq:tpc51-quotient-l1}$$ and the quotient frame condition number is $$K_{\mathrm q}(\mathsf B)  :=\sup_{\mathsf Bd\ne0}  \frac{\|[d]\|_{1,\mathrm q}}{\|\mathsf Bd\|_2}.  \label{eq:tpc51-quotient-condition}$$ This removes exact algebraic redundancy; it does not remove a small positive singular value.

###### ref-a1d58426ddeacaa8

###### ref-6e7bee73421bafb1

###### ref-87c408c2e7657626

> **Theorem: Exact normalized-Gram criterion** \[thm:tpc51-quotient-Gram\] \[thm:tpc51-finite-gram-criterion\] Assume $\mathsf B\ne0$, and let $\sigma_+(\mathsf B)$ be its smallest positive singular value. If $\lambda_+(G)=\sigma_+(\mathsf B)^2$ denotes the smallest positive eigenvalue of $G$, then $$\boxed{  \frac1{\lambda_+(G)}  \le K_{\mathrm q}(\mathsf B)^2  \le\frac{R}{\lambda_+(G)}.}  \label{eq:tpc51-quotient-Gram-bounds}$$ If $G$ is positive definite, the quotient is trivial and [\[eq:tpc51-quotient-Gram-bounds\]](TPC-051.md#ref-87c408c2e7657626) bounds the condition number of the displayed canonical family itself.

> **Proof** Let $V=(\ker\mathsf B)^\perp$. Every output has a unique representative $d_V\in V$, and $$\|[d]\|_{1,\mathrm q}  \le\|d_V\|_1  \le\sqrt R\|d_V\|_2  \le\frac{\sqrt R}{\sigma_+(\mathsf B)}        \|\mathsf Bd\|_2.$$ This proves the upper bound. For the lower bound, take a unit right singular vector $v\in V$ belonging to $\sigma_+(\mathsf B)$. For every $k\in\ker\mathsf B$, orthogonality gives $\|v+k\|_2\ge1$, and hence $\|v+k\|_1\ge1$. Therefore $$\|[v]\|_{1,\mathrm q}\ge1,  \qquad  \|\mathsf Bv\|_2=\sigma_+(\mathsf B),$$ which proves the lower bound.

###### ref-96adb7f663da8dd4

###### ref-8b2720cdafa7d38e

###### ref-634827eca331b474

> **Corollary: Subpower quotient criterion** \[cor:tpc51-subpower-quotient\] For a family depending on $X$, the two hypotheses $$R_X=X^{o(1)},  \qquad  \lambda_+(G_X)^{-1}=X^{o(1)}  \label{eq:tpc51-subpower-Gram-hypotheses}$$ imply $$K_{\mathrm q}(\mathsf B_X)^2=X^{o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc51-subpower-quotient-conclusion}$$ Conversely, if $\lambda_+(G_X)\le X^{-\sigma+o(1)}$ for a fixed $\sigma>0$, then $K_{\mathrm q}(\mathsf B_X)^2\ge X^{\sigma+o(1)}$.

###### ref-35f0011390eafc40

The quotient criterion is stronger than necessary for one specified physical coefficient. For a fixed direction $d_X$ with $\mathsf B_Xd_X\ne0$, it suffices to bound the single ratio $$\frac{\|[d_X]\|_{1,\mathrm q}^2}       {d_X^*G_Xd_X};  \label{eq:tpc51-one-direction-Rayleigh}$$ no estimate of the least eigenvalue on unrelated coefficient directions is then required.

## Duplicate and nearly duplicate shapes

###### ref-11b6eac1523d0449

> **Proposition: Exact duplicate obstruction** \[prop:tpc51-duplicate-shapes\] If two shapes satisfy $W_1(x_n)=W_2(x_n)$ at every sampled point, then the unquotiented physical synthesis $F$ has the nonzero kernel direction $c=e_1-e_2$. Thus no lower bound for the displayed envelope [\[eq:tpc51-family-envelope\]](TPC-051.md#ref-bb725fefd4061c09) can hold on all representatives. After quotienting by this direction, the duplicate contributes no physical dimension.

This is an intrinsic redundancy of the chosen finite family. It is different from appending a null pair to a representation after the canonical family has been fixed.

###### ref-371f700bded81f25

###### ref-81778e850a2520b3

###### ref-612c27b46a60370d

> **Proposition: Near-duplicate condition lower bound** \[prop:tpc51-near-duplicate-shapes\] For two shapes and the signed coefficient $c=(1,-1)$, $$\frac{\mathcal E_{\mathrm{fam}}(c)}{\|Fc\|_2}  =\frac{M^{1/2}(A_1+A_2)}  {\left(\sum_{n=1}^Nw_n        |W_1(x_n)-W_2(x_n)|^2\right)^{1/2}}.  \label{eq:tpc51-near-duplicate-exact}$$ where the ratio is interpreted as $+\infty$ if the denominator vanishes. In that case $c$ is an exact sampled-kernel direction, as in [\[prop:tpc51-duplicate-shapes\]](TPC-051.md#ref-11b6eac1523d0449). In particular, if $$\left(\frac1M\sum_{n=1}^Nw_n  |W_1(x_n)-W_2(x_n)|^2\right)^{1/2}  \le\varepsilon,  \label{eq:tpc51-near-duplicate-distance}$$ then the unquotiented condition number in this direction is at least $(A_1+A_2)/\varepsilon$.

> **Proof** Both assertions follow by evaluating [\[eq:tpc51-family-synthesis\]](TPC-051.md#ref-6ad74c6f1e03f81a) and [\[eq:tpc51-family-envelope\]](TPC-051.md#ref-bb725fefd4061c09) at $c=(1,-1)$.

###### ref-fe7ee2accd1e3ba4

###### ref-d77aad86b4c5f36e

The lower bound can be polynomial even though both individual shapes are perfectly nondegenerate. Here is a Mellin-specific example. Suppose $K$ is contained in a fixed compact interval of $(0,\infty)$, $W_1(x)=W(x)$, and $W_2(x)=W(e^\delta x)$, where $W$ is $C^1$ on a fixed compact interval containing every $x_n$ and $e^\delta x_n$. For $|\delta|\le1$, the mean-value theorem in logarithmic coordinates gives $$|W(e^\delta x_n)-W(x_n)|  \le C_W|\delta|.  \label{eq:tpc51-dilation-near-duplicate}$$ If $A_1=A_2=A_B(W)$, as happens for the weighted Mellin masses of these two dilates, then $$\frac{\mathcal E_{\mathrm{fam}}(1,-1)}       {\|F(1,-1)\|_2}  \gg_W |\delta|^{-1}.  \label{eq:tpc51-dilation-condition}$$ A separation $|\delta|=X^{-\sigma+o(1)}$ therefore creates a genuine polynomial loss. A fixed dyadic separation gives only a fixed constant and is not, by itself, an asymptotic obstruction.

## A positive limiting quotient theorem

###### ref-cd55a7d9de4b3b99

The preceding condition is directly verifiable for a fixed physical family. For each $X$, let $x_{n,X}\in K$ and $w_{n,X}>0$, put $M_X=\sum_{n=1}^{N_X}w_{n,X}$, and define the probability measure $$\mu_X:=\frac1{M_X}\sum_{n=1}^{N_X}w_{n,X}\delta_{x_{n,X}}.  \label{eq:tpc51-family-empirical-measure}$$ Let $F_X$, $\mathsf B_X$, and $G_X$ be the matrices defined above for this sampled set.

###### ref-320a3561ac52307d

###### ref-ad370c5946c0b34e

###### ref-cfddf6cb8ec0df11

###### ref-ed442c3d28acce4d

###### ref-573ca1f7b9ccc97c

> **Theorem: Positive limiting sampled frame** \[thm:tpc51-limiting-frame\] \[thm:tpc51-limit-gram\] Let $K$ be a compact metric space and let $R$ be fixed. Suppose $\mu_X$ converges weakly to a probability measure $\mu$ on $K$, and suppose $W_1,\ldots,W_R\in C(K)$ are linearly independent in $L^2(\mu)$. Define $$(G_\infty)_{rs}  :=\frac1{A_rA_s}    \int_K\overline{W_r(x)}W_s(x)\,d\mu(x).  \label{eq:tpc51-limiting-Gram}$$ Then $G_\infty$ is positive definite and $$G_X\longrightarrow G_\infty  \quad\text{entrywise and in operator norm}.  \label{eq:tpc51-Gram-convergence}$$ Consequently, for all sufficiently large $X$, $$\lambda_{\min}(G_X)  \ge\frac12\lambda_{\min}(G_\infty)>0,  \qquad  K_{\mathrm q}(\mathsf B_X)^2  \le\frac{2R}{\lambda_{\min}(G_\infty)}.  \label{eq:tpc51-limiting-frame-bound}$$

> **Proof** For $u\in\mathbb C^R$, $$u^*G_\infty u  =\int_K\left|    \sum_{r=1}^R\frac{u_r}{A_r}W_r(x)   \right|^2d\mu(x).$$ Linear independence makes this positive for $u\ne0$, so $G_\infty$ is positive definite. Each product $\overline{W_r}W_s$ is continuous on $K$; weak convergence gives entrywise convergence. Since $R$ is fixed, entrywise convergence implies operator-norm convergence. Weyl’s eigenvalue inequality then gives the first bound in [\[eq:tpc51-limiting-frame-bound\]](TPC-051.md#ref-573ca1f7b9ccc97c), and [\[thm:tpc51-quotient-Gram\]](TPC-051.md#ref-a1d58426ddeacaa8) gives the second.

If the original family is linearly dependent in $L^2(\mu)$, one must first quotient by its fixed limiting relations, choose a basis of the resulting image, and apply the theorem to that reduced family. Merely replacing $\lambda_{\min}$ by the smallest positive eigenvalue of the unreduced finite-$X$ Gram can be misleading: an exact limiting relation may appear at finite $X$ as a small positive eigenvalue.

Weak convergence alone is also insufficient when the number of shapes grows with $X$. A polylogarithmic Fourier bank requires a quantitative sampling theorem uniform over that bank. The present theorem provides the fixed-family endpoint and the exact Gram criterion; any growing-bank application must verify the additional uniformity explicitly.

Finally, the quotient frame concerns the canonical smooth-shape reassembly on its declared atomic coordinates. It neither estimates a coherent prime sum inside one residual coordinate nor controls residual grouping, frozen-to-physical localization, or identification with the native affine Gram face. In particular, it is not a closure theorem for the full TPC–48 Banach envelope.

# Prime–squarefree logarithmic sampling

We now verify the finite-Gram hypothesis on the row universe actually used before terminal prime summation. Only standard fixed-modulus prime number theory and Möbius inversion are needed.

###### ref-c2adbf0f56a60d14

###### ref-8d8be301592ccc55

###### ref-160cfd4343ef5c6f

Put $T=\log 2$. Fix positive integers $q,H$, a nonempty set $\mathcal A\subset(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^2$, and $e\in\{0,1\}$. We assume that every prime residue occurring in $\mathcal A$ is reduced modulo $q$, and retain only those squarefree residue classes having positive local density subject to $(d,H)=1$. At least one retained pair is required. Define $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal R_X(\mathcal A)  &:=\bigl\{(\ell,d):L<\ell\leq2L,\ \ell\ {\rm prime},        \ D<d\leq2D,\ \mu^2(d)=1,\ (d,H)=1,\notag\\[-2pt]  &\hspace{47mm}(\ell,d)\bmod q\in\mathcal A\bigr\},  \label{eq:tpc51-row-universe}\\  w_{\ell,d}&:=(\log\ell)^{2e},  \qquad  E_X:=\sum_{(\ell,d)\in\mathcal R_X(\mathcal A)}w_{\ell,d}.  \label{eq:tpc51-row-base-energy}\end{aligned}$$ We suppose $X^c\leq L,D\leq X^C$ for fixed $c,C>0$. The normalized logarithmic empirical measure is $$\nu_X:=\frac1{E_X}  \sum_{(\ell,d)\in\mathcal R_X(\mathcal A)}  w_{\ell,d}\,  \delta_{(\log(\ell/L),\log(d/D))}.  \label{eq:tpc51-empirical-measure}$$

###### ref-c8306cb885a17076

###### ref-5472b3c1c4e9ae48

For the quantitative comparison it is convenient not to discard the harmless finite-$L$ logarithmic density. For all sufficiently large $L$, let $$Z_{L,e}:=\int_0^T e^u(\log L+u)^{2e-1}\,du  \label{eq:tpc51-prime-reference-normalizer}$$ and define the probability measure $$d\nu_{L,e}^{\rm ref}(x,y)  :=\frac{e^x(\log L+x)^{2e-1}}{Z_{L,e}}\,dx\,    e^y\,dy,  \qquad (x,y)\in[0,T]^2.  \label{eq:tpc51-finite-reference-measure}$$ The second factor already has mass one because $\int_0^T e^y\,dy=1$. Notice that the exponent $2e-1$ equals $-1$ when the prime row is unweighted; this is the usual density $dt/\log t$ of primes and is the reason for retaining the finite-$L$ reference measure.

###### ref-a835ca15e3caaa93

###### ref-d82bdddc639f1f2f

###### ref-4ed874913f87b3fe

> **Theorem: Arithmetic logarithmic sampling** \[thm:tpc51-row-equidistribution\] For every continuous $F:[0,T]^2\to\mathbb C$, $$\int F\,d\nu_X  \longrightarrow  \int_{[0,T]^2}F(x,y)e^{x+y}\,dx\,dy.  \label{eq:tpc51-weak-row-limit}$$ More quantitatively, for every fixed $A>0$ and every $0<\delta<1/2$, uniformly when $|\xi|+|\eta|\leq(\log X)^A$, $$\begin{aligned}  &\left|  \int e^{i(\xi x+\eta y)}\,d\nu_X  -\int e^{i(\xi x+\eta y)}\,d\nu_{L,e}^{\rm ref}  \right|  \notag\\  &\hspace{18mm}\leq  C_{A,\delta,q,H,\mathcal A}  (1+|\xi|+|\eta|)^{C_1}  \left(e^{-c_1\sqrt{\log L}}+D^{-1/2+\delta}\right),  \label{eq:tpc51-fourier-moment-error}\end{aligned}$$ where $c_1>0$ and $C_1<\infty$ are independent of $X,\xi,\eta$.

###### ref-959918b8807a86a7

###### ref-ece77edb89bdfc02

> **Proof** Split $\mathcal A$ into its finitely many residue pairs. For a reduced prime class $a\bmod q$, the fixed-modulus prime number theorem and partial summation give, uniformly for $|\xi|\leq(\log X)^A$, $$\begin{aligned}  &\sum_{\substack{L<\ell\leq2L\\                    \ell\ {\rm prime}\\                    \ell\equiv a\pmod q}}  (\log\ell)^{2e}e^{i\xi\log(\ell/L)}  \notag\\  &\quad=  \frac{L}{\varphi(q)}  \int_0^T e^x(\log L+x)^{2e-1}e^{i\xi x}\,dx  +O_{A,q}\!\left(  L(\log L)^{2e-1}(1+|\xi|)^{C_1}  e^{-c_1\sqrt{\log L}}\right).  \label{eq:tpc51-prime-fourier-asymptotic}\end{aligned}$$ The same formula at $\xi=0$ supplies its normalizing mass. The use of the finite-$L$ reference density in [\[eq:tpc51-finite-reference-measure\]](TPC-051.md#ref-5472b3c1c4e9ae48) removes any artificial $1/\log L$ error from this comparison. Any fixed extra power of $\log L$ arising in partial summation is absorbed by decreasing $c_1$, without changing the stated form of the error.
>
> For a locally admissible squarefree class $b\bmod q$, use $\mu^2(d)=\sum_{r^2\mid d}\mu(r)$ and inclusion–exclusion for $(d,H)=1$. For each $r\leq\sqrt{2D}$ and each fixed divisor $s\mid H$, the remaining integer is counted in either no class or one class modulo $\operatorname{lcm}(q,s,r^2)$. Summing the resulting $O(1)$ endpoint errors over $r$, extending the absolutely convergent main-term sum to infinity, and applying partial summation to $e^{i\eta\log(d/D)}$ give $$\begin{aligned}  &\sum_{\substack{D<d\leq2D\\d\equiv b\pmod q\\        \mu^2(d)=1,\ (d,H)=1}}  e^{i\eta\log(d/D)}  \notag\\  &\quad=  \mathfrak c_{b,q,H}D  \int_0^T e^y e^{i\eta y}\,dy  +O_{A,\delta,q,H}\!\left(  D^{1/2+\delta}(1+|\eta|)^{C_1}\right),  \label{eq:tpc51-squarefree-fourier-asymptotic}\end{aligned}$$ uniformly for $|\eta|\leq(\log X)^A$, with $\mathfrak c_{b,q,H}>0$ precisely for the retained classes. This is the usual squarefree count in a fixed progression with a smooth unit-modulus test factor; no distribution theorem for growing moduli is being used.
>
> Multiply [\[eq:tpc51-prime-fourier-asymptotic\]](TPC-051.md#ref-959918b8807a86a7) and [\[eq:tpc51-squarefree-fourier-asymptotic\]](TPC-051.md#ref-ece77edb89bdfc02), sum over the fixed set $\mathcal A$, and divide by the same expression at $(\xi,\eta)=(0,0)$. The residue-dependent main-term constants form one positive finite sum, while the $x$- and $y$-profiles are common to every retained residue pair. The normalized main term is therefore exactly $\nu_{L,e}^{\rm ref}$, and the denominator is bounded away from zero after its natural normalization. This proves [\[eq:tpc51-fourier-moment-error\]](TPC-051.md#ref-4ed874913f87b3fe).
>
> For fixed frequencies the normalized prime reference density converges uniformly to $e^x$. Hence $\nu_{L,e}^{\rm ref}$ converges weakly to the probability measure $e^{x+y}\,dx\,dy$. The quantitative estimate first proves [\[eq:tpc51-weak-row-limit\]](TPC-051.md#ref-d82bdddc639f1f2f) for finite exponential sums with arbitrary real frequencies. This self-adjoint algebra contains the constants and separates points of the closed square, so the Stone–Weierstrass theorem gives the result for every continuous $F$.

## A polylogarithmic log-Fourier bank

###### ref-ad51f657dc1c8028

For an integer $K\geq0$, let $\Lambda_K=\{-K,\ldots,K\}^2$ and $R_K=|\Lambda_K|=(2K+1)^2$. For coefficients $a=(a_k)_{k\in\Lambda_K}$, put $$P_a(x,y):=\sum_{k=(k_1,k_2)\in\Lambda_K}  a_k\exp\!\left(\frac{2\pi i}{T}(k_1x+k_2y)\right).  \label{eq:tpc51-log-fourier-polynomial}$$

###### ref-d440e04317a184b0

###### ref-556ea506e1a9e821

> **Theorem: Polylogarithmic sampled Riesz bounds** \[thm:tpc51-polylog-fourier-frame\] Fix $A>0$. If $K\leq(\log X)^A$, then for all sufficiently large $X$, uniformly in $a$, $$c_A\sum_{k\in\Lambda_K}|a_k|^2  \leq \int|P_a|^2\,d\nu_X  \leq C_A\sum_{k\in\Lambda_K}|a_k|^2,  \label{eq:tpc51-unmasked-fourier-frame}$$ where $c_A>0$ and $C_A<\infty$ are independent of $K,X$. The same statement holds for coefficients in an arbitrary Hilbert space, with $|P_a|^2$ replaced by its squared Hilbert norm.

###### ref-2afc4e35d30661ed

###### ref-62a0cff87c9db29f

###### ref-b712f8a943f666fd

> **Proof** For all sufficiently large $L$, the density in [\[eq:tpc51-finite-reference-measure\]](TPC-051.md#ref-5472b3c1c4e9ae48) is bounded above and below by positive absolute constants on $[0,T]^2$. Parseval on the square therefore gives $$c_0\sum_k|a_k|^2  \leq\int|P_a|^2\,d\nu_{L,e}^{\rm ref}  \leq C_0\sum_k|a_k|^2.  \label{eq:tpc51-reference-frame}$$ Expanding the quadratic form produces frequencies whose coordinates have absolute value at most $4\pi K/T$. By [\[eq:tpc51-fourier-moment-error\]](TPC-051.md#ref-4ed874913f87b3fe), applied with any fixed exponent larger than $A$ so that the harmless constant $8\pi/T$ is absorbed for large $X$, every entry of the empirical Gram differs from the reference Gram by at most $$\rho_X(K)\ll_{A,\delta}  (1+K)^{C_1}  \left(e^{-c_1\sqrt{\log L}}+D^{-1/2+\delta}\right).  \label{eq:tpc51-bank-entry-error}$$ The Schur bound, or simply $(\sum|a_k|)^2\leq R_K\sum|a_k|^2$, shows that the quadratic-form error is at most $$R_K\rho_X(K)\sum_k|a_k|^2.  \label{eq:tpc51-bank-operator-error}$$ Because $R_K\ll(1+K)^2$, $K\leq(\log X)^A$, and $D\geq X^c$, this is $o(1)\sum_k|a_k|^2$. Absorb the error into [\[eq:tpc51-reference-frame\]](TPC-051.md#ref-2afc4e35d30661ed). For Hilbert-valued coefficients the scalar Gram is tensored with the identity, so the same spectral bounds apply.

###### ref-6b4c1e5aea450b5f

> **Corollary: Fixed physical families** \[cor:tpc51-fixed-physical-family\] Let $W_1,\ldots,W_R\in C([0,T]^2)$ be fixed. Quotient their span by the exact relations in $L^2(e^{x+y}\,dx\,dy)$, and choose fixed representatives whose nonzero quotient classes are linearly independent. Then the normalized sampled Gram of this reduced family on $\mathcal R_X(\mathcal A)$ has its least eigenvalue bounded away from zero. In particular, its quotient-frame condition number is $O(1)$.

> **Proof** Apply [\[thm:tpc51-row-equidistribution\]](TPC-051.md#ref-a835ca15e3caaa93) to each of the finitely many continuous products $W_r\overline{W_s}$. The resulting sampled Gram converges in operator norm to the positive-definite limiting Gram, so [\[thm:tpc51-limit-gram\]](TPC-051.md#ref-ad370c5946c0b34e) applies.

The corollary is the direct tool for the finite collection of fixed physical cutoff shapes. The growing Fourier bank is a stronger stress test and a convenient finite recellization device; it is not being identified with an arbitrary continuous Mellin coefficient space.

# Sparse-deletion stability of the sampled quotient frame

We now leave the static mask intact and measure its effect directly on the sampled Gram. This is the order fixed in TPC–50; no Fourier–Ferrers–gcd layer of a projective mask representation is declared to be a physical Mellin cell.

###### ref-c9e49be3f381798f

###### ref-c30acba3bff01fda

Let $\mathcal R_X$ be the complete prime–squarefree row universe and let $M_X(\alpha,\gamma)\in\{0,1\}$ be the literal mask. We use only the inherited degree defect $$\sup_\alpha\#\{\gamma\in\mathcal R_X:M_X(\alpha,\gamma)=0\}  \leq\epsilon_X|\mathcal R_X|,  \label{eq:tpc51-mask-degree-hypothesis}$$ and the same bound after interchanging the two row systems. In the TPC application, $$\epsilon_X\ll_H  L^{-1+o(1)}+D^{-1+o(1)}+X^{-\kappa_0+o(1)}  \label{eq:tpc51-inherited-mask-defect}$$ by the complete-universe degree theorem inherited through TPC–49/50 [`\citep{WangTPC49,WangTPC50}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-051.md#cite-089c241d456878e0); [\[WangTPC50\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-051.md#cite-0d8078e2652e9912). For this TPC application we retain the hypotheses under which that degree theorem was proved: $L>4D$, and the row product $m=\ell d$ determines the prime–squarefree row. The abstract masked frame below applies whenever [\[eq:tpc51-mask-degree-hypothesis\]](TPC-051.md#ref-c9e49be3f381798f) holds; the citation does not furnish that hypothesis outside this complete-universe regime.

###### ref-48422c2198a74588

The elementary Nikolskii bound needed below is $$\sup_{(x,y)\in[0,T]^2}\|P_a(x,y)\|_{\mathcal H}^2  \leq R_K\sum_{k\in\Lambda_K}\|a_k\|_{\mathcal H}^2,  \label{eq:tpc51-nikolskii}$$ which follows from Cauchy–Schwarz and remains valid for coefficients in any Hilbert space $\mathcal H$.

###### ref-3947eeb8f92a92b3

###### ref-dc83de354247231a

###### ref-edae30bfdb6654a1

###### ref-21db60fd3c0b48b6

> **Theorem: One-sided masked log-frame** \[thm:tpc51-masked-frame\] Fix the admissible row cell, base weights, and degree $K\leq(\log X)^A$ of [\[thm:tpc51-polylog-fourier-frame\]](TPC-051.md#ref-d440e04317a184b0). For each source row $\alpha$, let $$P_\alpha(x,y)=\sum_{k\in\Lambda_K}  c_{\alpha,k}  \exp\!\left(\frac{2\pi i}{T}(k_1x+k_2y)\right),  \qquad c_{\alpha,k}\in\mathcal H,  \label{eq:tpc51-row-dependent-polynomial}$$ with only finitely many nonzero source rows. Then $$\begin{aligned}  &\sum_\alpha\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal R_X(\mathcal A)}  M_X(\alpha,\gamma)w_\gamma  \|P_\alpha(x_\gamma,y_\gamma)\|_{\mathcal H}^2  \notag\\  &\qquad\geq E_X  \left(c_A-C_{A,\mathcal A}\epsilon_XR_K\right)  \sum_\alpha\sum_{k\in\Lambda_K}\|c_{\alpha,k}\|_{\mathcal H}^2,  \label{eq:tpc51-masked-lower-frame}\end{aligned}$$ where $(x_\gamma,y_\gamma)$ are the logarithmic coordinates from [\[eq:tpc51-empirical-measure\]](TPC-051.md#ref-160cfd4343ef5c6f). The corresponding upper bound is $$\sum_\alpha\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal R_X(\mathcal A)}  M_X(\alpha,\gamma)w_\gamma  \|P_\alpha(x_\gamma,y_\gamma)\|_{\mathcal H}^2  \leq C_AE_X\sum_{\alpha,k}\|c_{\alpha,k}\|_{\mathcal H}^2.  \label{eq:tpc51-masked-upper-frame}$$ In particular the lower bound remains positive whenever $\epsilon_XR_K=o(1)$. The assertion is uniform in arbitrary source-row concentration because the estimate is applied before the sum over $\alpha$.

> **Corollary: Fixed-shape quotient under the literal mask** \[cor:tpc51-masked-fixed-shapes\] Let $W_1,\ldots,W_R\in C([0,T]^2)$ be a fixed family whose nonzero quotient classes are linearly independent in $L^2(e^{x+y}dxdy)$. ## Canonical Mellin interpretation and the missing bridge

###### ref-d9225cb9ec8ebd80

The real-axis row powers in the provenance formula become, after removing global unit phases, $$\ell^{it_1}d^{it_2}  =L^{it_1}D^{it_2}  e^{i(t_1x_\ell+t_2y_d)}.  \label{eq:tpc51-log-phase-identification}$$ For one declared fixed scalar function of $(x_\ell,y_d)$, integrating these phases against its Mellin transform reconstructs that function exactly; see [\[thm:tpc51-canonical-mellin-direction\]](TPC-051.md#ref-a05382eb64ded690). Such a fixed finite scalar family is governed by [\[cor:tpc51-masked-fixed-shapes\]](TPC-051.md#ref-a6677ed56f275840). This statement is not yet an identification of the actual orbit-dependent TPC family with that scalar model.

###### ref-41167d6bb23b9286

Indeed, the provenance factors include expressions such as $W(m_\gamma j/X)$ and $\Psi(d_\gamma j/K)$. With $z_j=j/J$, their row-log components have the schematic form $$W(c_Xe^{x+y}z_j),  \qquad  \Psi(c'_Xe^yz_j),  \label{eq:tpc51-orbit-translate-shapes}$$ for bounded scale factors on a fixed packet. Retaining the orbit label therefore produces an $X$-dependent Hilbert-valued row function, not automatically a fixed list of scalar functions on $[0,T]^2$.

###### ref-d2ba39dfbce29491

> **Proposition: Distinct log translates are independent** \[prop:tpc51-translate-independence\] Let $0\ne w\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$, and let $a_1,\ldots,a_M\in\mathbb R$ be distinct. Then the functions $w(\,\cdot+a_1),\ldots,w(\,\cdot+a_M)$ are linearly independent on $\mathbb R$.

> **Proof** If $\sum_jc_jw(s+a_j)=0$, Fourier transformation gives $$\widehat w(\xi)\sum_jc_je^{ia_j\xi}=0.$$ The transform $\widehat w$ is not identically zero and hence is nonzero on some open interval. The exponential polynomial on the right vanishes on that interval, so it vanishes identically. Taking its first $M$ derivatives at one point gives a Vandermonde system in the distinct $a_j$, and therefore every $c_j=0$.

Applied to $w(s)=W(e^s)$, this proposition shows that smoothness alone cannot collapse the unrestricted orbit translates in [\[eq:tpc51-orbit-translate-shapes\]](TPC-051.md#ref-41167d6bb23b9286) to a fixed-rank scalar family. Restriction to the physical log box, support losses, and aggregation in the orbit Hilbert space require a separate uniform argument.

###### ref-751a8ca4d111fd95

We record exactly what such an argument must supply. For a family $H=(H_\alpha)_\alpha$ of $\mathcal K_X$-valued functions on the log box, write $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal Q_X^0(H)  &:=\sum_\alpha\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal R_X(\mathcal A)}  w_\gamma\|H_\alpha(x_\gamma,y_\gamma)\|_{\mathcal K_X}^2,  \notag\\  \mathcal Q_X^M(H)  &:=\sum_\alpha\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal R_X(\mathcal A)}  M_X(\alpha,\gamma)w_\gamma  \|H_\alpha(x_\gamma,y_\gamma)\|_{\mathcal K_X}^2.  \label{eq:tpc51-bridge-quadratic-forms}\end{aligned}$$

> **Proposition: Actual-provenance bridge criterion** \[prop:tpc51-actual-bridge-criterion\] Suppose the coefficientwise actual row field, with source and residual labels retained, has an exact representation $$H_{\alpha,X}^{\rm act}(x,y)  =\sum_{r=1}^{R_X}c_{\alpha,r,X}F_{r,X}(x,y),  \qquad F_{r,X}\in C([0,T]^2;\mathcal K_X),$$ after quotienting its exact relations. Let $\mathcal C_X(c)$ be the declared normalized coefficient energy, including the factor $E_X$ and the fixed shape normalizations. Assume one of the following two uniform alternatives.
>
> 1.  The actual vector-valued family has a uniform normalized Gram and sparse-deletion estimate: $$c_0\mathcal C_X(c)\leq\mathcal Q_X^0(H(c))   \leq C_0\mathcal C_X(c),   \qquad   \mathcal Q_X^0(H(c))-\mathcal Q_X^M(H(c))   =o(\mathcal C_X(c))$$ uniformly on the declared quotient subspace, for fixed $0<c_0\leq C_0<\infty$.
>
> 2.  There is a retained log-Fourier bank $P_X(c)$ of degree $K_X\leq(\log X)^A$, with bank coefficient energy comparable to $\mathcal C_X(c)$, such that the tail is uniformly relative: $$\mathcal Q_X^0(H(c)-P_X(c))=o(\mathcal C_X(c)).$$
>
> Finally suppose the actual weighted continuous Mellin envelope satisfies the norm comparison $$\bigl(\mathcal E_{1,B}^L(\mathbf 1)\bigr)^2  \leq X^{o(1)}\mathcal C_X(c^{\rm act}).  \label{eq:tpc51-actual-envelope-bridge}$$ for a nonzero actual quotient direction with $\mathcal C_X(c^{\rm act})>0$. Then, for all sufficiently large $X$, the masked denominator below is positive and the coefficientwise, pre-terminal actual direction has zero positive polynomial representation loss: $$\frac{\bigl(\mathcal E_{1,B}^L(\mathbf 1)\bigr)^2}  {\mathcal Q_X^M(H_X^{\rm act})}  \leq X^{o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc51-conditional-actual-bridge}$$ This conclusion retains the source and residual labels and makes no statement about the later coherent prime or grouping maps.

> **Proof** Under (G1), subtraction gives $\mathcal Q_X^M(H(c))\geq(c_0-o(1))\mathcal C_X(c)$. Under (G2), [\[thm:tpc51-masked-frame\]](TPC-051.md#ref-3947eeb8f92a92b3) gives the same lower bound for $P_X(c)$, while $\mathcal Q_X^M(H-P_X)\leq\mathcal Q_X^0(H-P_X)=o(\mathcal C_X(c))$. The triangle inequality in the masked $L^2$ space transfers the lower bound from $P_X$ to $H$. Combining either lower bound with [\[eq:tpc51-actual-envelope-bridge\]](TPC-051.md#ref-ec167f328621f1c3) proves [\[eq:tpc51-conditional-actual-bridge\]](TPC-051.md#ref-7d4a28694fbfbe2a).

Neither alternative nor [\[eq:tpc51-actual-envelope-bridge\]](TPC-051.md#ref-ec167f328621f1c3) is verified here for the actual orbit-translate family [\[eq:tpc51-orbit-translate-shapes\]](TPC-051.md#ref-41167d6bb23b9286). The fixed scalar corollary and the retained finite-bank theorem prove model cases of the criterion; they do not prove the actual bridge by relabelling.

Most importantly, the source label $\alpha$ remains outside the opposite-row norm in [\[eq:tpc51-masked-lower-frame\]](TPC-051.md#ref-edae30bfdb6654a1). If a later operation coherently sums different source rows before taking a norm, new cross-row Gram terms appear. The theorem neither discards nor estimates those terms.

It says only that the declared sampled scalar family or retained finite bank consumes no positive exponent.

## What the continuous no-go changes

The universal kernel in [\[thm:tpc51-smooth-kernel\]](TPC-051.md#ref-fd06aabefd01ffaa) makes one possible future strategy inadmissible. One may not introduce an arbitrary $L^2$ or signed $L^1$ Mellin coefficient density and then claim a lower frame merely because individual phases have unit modulus. The correct alternatives are:

1.  use the one provenance-fixed density and invert it exactly;

2.  pass to the quotient by the exact synthesis kernel and specify a quotient norm; or

3.  retain a separated finite bank and prove its sampled Gram bound.

The present paper proves each alternative only on its declared model domain. It does not identify the actual orbit-translate provenance with the finite scalar family or retained bank; that step is exactly [\[prop:tpc51-actual-bridge-criterion\]](TPC-051.md#ref-b8da9ffd267f1aeb) and remains unverified.

The boundary obstruction also has two distinct meanings. A uniform coordinatewise diagonal bound fails when an allowed coordinate approaches a zero, and then subdivision or energy selection is needed. An aggregate fixed-family sampled Gram can nevertheless remain stable when a shape has isolated zeros, provided its limiting $L^2(e^{x+y}dxdy)$ quotient classes remain independent. Thus a coordinatewise near kernel is not by itself an aggregate Gram degeneracy, and weighted Mellin mass alone proves neither conclusion.

## Why the full TPC–48 envelope does not follow

###### ref-4c6666e745a7046c

For a fixed representation, the inherited profile has the schematic form $$\mathfrak S_{\mathscr R}(\alpha)  =\int C_{\rm LS}(\phi_\omega,H_0)^{1/2}  \left(\sum_m\|B_{\omega,m}(\alpha)\|^2\right)^{1/2}  d|\nu|(\omega),  \label{eq:tpc51-profile-envelope-reminder}$$ where, inside the Hilbert norm, the prime variable has already been summed coherently. The abstract frames proved here act on declared opposite-row log families while retaining the other row and arithmetic labels. Before they can even be placed into the actual provenance operator, [\[prop:tpc51-actual-bridge-criterion\]](TPC-051.md#ref-b8da9ffd267f1aeb) must be verified. After that bridge, three further noncommutations would still prevent [\[eq:tpc51-profile-envelope-reminder\]](TPC-051.md#ref-4c6666e745a7046c) from being replaced by the physical atomic energy:

1.  the outer total-variation integral is applied after the square root of a layer energy;

2.  $C_{\rm LS}$ is a profile seminorm weight, not the norm of a canonical Mellin coefficient; and

3.  the coherent prime and residual maps need not intertwine with the opposite-row quotient projection.

Minkowski proves an upper envelope in this order; the quotient theorem does not reverse Minkowski.

## The next mathematical doors

The remaining interfaces, in dependency order, are:

1.  **Actual-provenance bridge.** For the genuine $X$-dependent Hilbert-valued orbit-translate family, verify a uniform normalized Gram or a uniform relative Fourier-tail estimate, and prove the weighted continuous-envelope comparison [\[eq:tpc51-actual-envelope-bridge\]](TPC-051.md#ref-ec167f328621f1c3). This is not a consequence of the fixed scalar-family theorem.

2.  **Coherent-prime square.** At fixed source and cofactor, prove a square estimate for $\sum_p A(p,r,m)\mathrm e(-pr\alpha)v_{p,r,m}$ that is stable under the canonical quotient projection, or give a sharp obstruction.

3.  **Residual grouping.** Control the map grouping $s=d(m)r$, including its maximal fiber multiplicity and any usable cancellation within a fiber.

4.  **Physical-shift localization.** Compare the frozen completed family with the single physical intercept $h_0$.

5.  **Native-Gram identification.** Relate the residual low-sign Gram to the native affine physical Gram without discarding cross terms.

6.  **Parity-sensitive input.** Only after the preceding Hilbert interfaces close would a genuinely new number-theoretic input be able to address a fixed-shift prime-pair lower bound.

Failure at any one door would be informative: it would identify an explicit operator or near-kernel rather than an unspecified appeal to the parity barrier. The abstract positive and translate-obstruction results localize the first remaining door more sharply; they do not assert that this actual-provenance door is already open.

## What is certified

The finite checks catch sign errors in the differential phase kernel, failure of the cofactor null-vector identity, accidental singularity of the test Grams, an incorrect sparse-deletion constant, and arithmetic mistakes in the endpoint exponents. Exact rationals avoid tolerance choices and platform-dependent linear algebra.

## What is not certified

The certificate does not prove the analytic uniformity in [\[thm:tpc51-row-equidistribution\]](TPC-051.md#ref-a835ca15e3caaa93); that theorem is proved from the fixed-modulus prime number theorem and the squarefree progression count. It does not enumerate the full physical coefficient system, compute the nonlinear profile in [\[eq:tpc51-profile-envelope-reminder\]](TPC-051.md#ref-4c6666e745a7046c), or test cancellation in a coherent prime sum. Most importantly, its finite Gram examples are consistency tests, not a data-driven substitute for the limiting-independence hypothesis of [\[thm:tpc51-limit-gram\]](TPC-051.md#ref-ad370c5946c0b34e).

Thus the computational and theorem claims have the same boundary as the rest of the paper: exact L0 algebra and the stated L1 row-sampling interface, with no L2 fixed-shift prime-pair conclusion.
