## Abstract

The literal fixed-shift residual map of a prime-squarefree orbit packet first restricts the full pre-terminal array to the inherited terminal equality support, multiplies a surviving input $(m,r,j,\gamma)$ by $\mu(r)\Lambda(mj+h_0)$, and then identifies inputs with the same complete output key $(\gamma,d(m)r,j)$. Positive pre-terminal energy does not imply either terminal activation or survival under this identification. We determine the exact linear and arithmetic structure of the internal grouping gate, keeping these two losses separate.

For a general weighted fiber-summing operator we compute every singular value, its kernel, the Moore–Penrose inverse, and the canonical visible projection. Each active output fiber has exactly one visible direction; all remaining directions are collision modes. For a distinguished input, the grouped energy is an exact energy-weighted average of explicit fiber alignment coefficients. Conditional on separate terminal activation and downstream comparison gates, the remaining internal grouping statement is a lower bound on the angle of the physical array from a known kernel, not a uniform lower bound on the full coefficient space.

The fixed-shift arithmetic adds a more specialized fact. Assume $h_0\ne0$, $D=o(J)$, uniqueness of the declared row labels, and sufficiently large $X$. If the terminal prime $p=(mj+h_0)/r$ is retained, then the refined key $(\gamma,d(m)r,j,p)$ is injective on one declared source system. Hence after terminal restriction, residual grouping factors exactly into a collision-free prime-resolved lift and a final coherent sum over distinct terminal primes. Determinant ladders give the coefficient-blind upper bound $\ll_{h_0}X^{o(1)}(1+L/R)$ for the maximal fiber size on a cofactor block $r\asymp R$. In the balanced range $R\gg L$, whenever the block has nonzero terminal diagonal energy, a rank-by-prime partition contains a collision-free subpacket with at least an $X^{-o(1)}$ fraction of that energy. This selection incurs no fixed positive-power loss inside that block; it is not a lower bound for the unreduced coherent sum.

Finally, on the inherited coprime squarefree equality support only, the source and cofactor signs combine as $\mu(d)\mu(r)=\mu(d r)$, so Möbius contributes one common phase on a residual fiber. Any remaining cancellation belongs to the actual source, mask, profile, and terminal amplitudes. No lower bound for the full fixed-shift coherent-prime square, parity improvement, or prime-pair consequence is claimed.

# Introduction

The preceding two stages of this program placed the pre-terminal packet on an exact physical normalization. TPC–53 identified every literal source, opposite-row, orbit, mask, and Mellin factor and proved a sampled interior profile comparison. TPC–54 then solved the abstract energy-selection problem sharply: structural density, source shape, sampled carrier, cofactor occupancy, and coefficient capacity can be tracked without moving any of them through a nonlinear norm [`\citep{WangTPC53,WangTPC54}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-055.md#cite-d85e91fc7a3dbaf8); [\[WangTPC54\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-055.md#cite-6f1a8ec95b6b34b5). Both results stop before the terminal map. That stopping point is forced, not cosmetic.

Fix one left source system, one direct row/orbit residue slice, and a nonzero physical shift $h_0$. A retained pre-terminal coordinate has the form $$u=(m,r,j,\gamma),  \qquad m=d(m)\ell(m),$$ where $\ell(m)$ is prime and $d(m)$ is squarefree. On the terminal support the equation $$p r-mj=h_0$$ determines at most one prime $p$. The literal output coordinate is $$y=q(u)=(\gamma,s,j),\qquad s=d(m)r.$$ Consequently the physical terminal map is a weighted sum over the full terminal fiber $\mathcal F_y^{\rm term}$. If such a fiber contains two nonzero input directions, the map has a kernel on the unrestricted coefficient space. This elementary obstruction was enough to disprove an automatic passage from pre-terminal coverage to post-terminal energy, but it did not describe the arithmetic fiber or the behavior of the distinguished physical vector.

The present paper asks the next narrow question:

> What is the exact spectral and arithmetic structure of a fixed-$h_0$ residual fiber, and which part of its terminal diagonal energy can be made collision-free without claiming a lower bound for the unreduced coherent sum?

There are four answers.

## Prime resolution gives an exact desingularization

The first answer changes the geometry of the question. Adjoin the unique terminal prime to the output key: $$\widetilde q(u)=(\gamma,s,j,p_u),  \qquad p_u=\frac{mj+h_0}{r}.$$ Under $h_0\ne0$, $D=o(J)$, uniqueness of the declared row labels, and sufficiently large $X$, this refined map is injective on one declared source system. Indeed, equality of $(s,j,p)$ gives equality of the fused squarefree label $$d(m)(mj+h_0)=p s,$$ and the fixed-coordinate label-injectivity argument of the large-prime descent recovers the unique row [`\citep{WangTPC45}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-055.md#cite-1802a7e8047e595c). We also give the short self-contained proof. The fixed-shift terminal map therefore factors as $$\boxed{\begin{aligned}  \text{literal terminal map}  &=\text{forget-}p\text{ synthesis}\\  &\quad\circ\text{terminal-restricted collision-free lift}.  \end{aligned}}$$ Thus distinct points of one residual collision fiber necessarily carry distinct terminal primes. In the literal fixed-$h_0$ representation, “residual grouping” and “coherent terminal-prime synthesis” are not two independent many-to-one operations. The former is precisely the last operation that forgets the prime coordinate. This statement concerns the physical $h_0$-slice; it does not identify the different coherent $p$-sum appearing after Fourier transformation of the completed frozen intercept family in TPC–48 [`\citep{WangTPC48}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-055.md#cite-b2c88d6d47db0c74).

## The grouping spectrum is completely explicit

Let $q:\mathcal U\to\mathcal Y$ be any finite fiber map and $$(Tz)_y=\sum_{q(u)=y}c_uz_u$$ between Hilbert spaces with positive atomic weights $a_u$ and $b_y$. Put $$S_y=\sum_{q(u)=y}\frac{|c_u|^2}{a_u},  \qquad \rho_y^2=b_yS_y.$$ We prove that the nonzero singular values of $T$ are exactly the $\rho_y$. Every active fiber contributes one visible unit vector $$(v_y)_u=\frac{\overline{c_u}}{a_u\sqrt{S_y}}  \quad(q(u)=y)$$ and a kernel of codimension one. This gives the exact pseudoinverse, minimum-norm representative of every output, and quotient norm. On a restricted subspace, a lower bound is equivalent to a positive principal angle from this explicit kernel.

For one distinguished vector $z$, define $$\eta_y(z)=  \frac{\left|\sum_{q(u)=y}c_uz_u\right|^2}  {S_y\sum_{q(u)=y}a_u|z_u|^2}.$$ Then $0\le\eta_y\le1$, and the grouped norm is exactly $$\|Tz\|_b^2  =\sum_y\rho_y^2\eta_y(z)        \sum_{q(u)=y}a_u|z_u|^2.$$ After terminal activation has been proved and any downstream interface gates required by the target correlation have been supplied, the remaining internal grouping question is therefore an arithmetic lower bound for a displayed alignment statistic. There is no hidden spectral gap to infer from multiplicity or positive diagonal energy alone.

## Balanced fibers admit a zero-cost transversal cover

Fix $y=(\gamma,s,j)$, write $s=dr$, and hold $d\mid s$ fixed. The admissible source prime $\ell$ and terminal prime $p$ lie on the exact determinant ladder $$rp-dj\ell=h_0.$$ If $g=(r,dj)\mid h_0$, all integral solutions are $$p=p_0+\frac{dj}{g}t,  \qquad  \ell=\ell_0+\frac r g t.$$ The inherited squarefree support has $(d,r)=1$, so $g=(r,j)\mid h_0$. On a source-prime interval of length $O(L)$, the number of candidates for this $d$ is $O_{h_0}(1+L/r)$. Summing over $d\mid s$ gives the coefficient-blind upper bound $$|\mathcal F_y(R)|  \ll_{h_0}\tau(s)\left(1+\frac LR\right)  \qquad(r\asymp R),$$ already implicit in the determinant-ladder boundary of TPC–45. Since $\tau(s)=X^{o(1)}$ on the polynomial-size support, balanced cofactors $R\gg L$ give subpower multiplicity.

Order the terminal primes in each full fiber and put the $k$-th smallest prime from every fiber into the $k$-th rank class. These classes form a deterministic, coefficient-independent partition into at most the maximal fiber size many collision-free transversals. If the terminal diagonal energy of the block is nonzero, one class captures at least the reciprocal of that number. Hence a balanced block with nonzero terminal diagonal energy contains a collision-free subpacket with an $X^{-o(1)}$ fraction of that energy. The selector is post-terminal and need not be a smooth Mellin cell. More importantly, a large collision-free summand does not lower-bound the sum of all rank classes: the omitted classes can still cancel it.

## The Möbius gauge is common, not random

On the inherited coprime squarefree equality support, $d,r,s=dr$ are squarefree, $(d,r)=1$, and $$\mu(d)\mu(r)=\mu(s).$$ The source coefficient contains $\mu(d)$, while the terminal multiplier contains $\mu(r)$. Their product is therefore constant on a fixed residual fiber. This coherence was first isolated during the large-prime cofactor descent [`\citep{WangTPC45}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-055.md#cite-1802a7e8047e595c); here it is inserted into the complete TPC–53 factor dictionary. The opposite-row phase and opposite profile are also common on a full $(\gamma,s,j)$-fiber. After those factors are removed, only the actual source coefficient, source profile, mask support, and positive-or-signed terminal shape remain in the fiber sum.

This is useful structural progress, but not a parity claim in the classical sieve-theoretic sense [`\citep[Ch.~16]{FriedlanderIwaniec2010}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-055.md#cite-c8acfd6f8b1ff9cf). The inherited theory supplies no uniform sector bound for the remaining complex amplitudes. We prove sharp phase-cone and magnitude-dominance criteria, and we prove that without either type of information the diagonal moduli alone may still form an exact polygonal cancellation.

## Scope and order of the next gate

We use the program’s level labels in the following precise sense. Level L0 consists of abstract finite-dimensional operator identities and inequalities that do not assert their arithmetic hypotheses for the literal packet. Level L1 consists of exact structural statements proved on the declared inherited fixed-$h_0$ support, including the prime-resolved injection and determinant-ladder upper bound. Level L2 would require a quantitative estimate reconnected to the full physical fixed-shift correlation, with activation, normalization, boundary, and all downstream comparison gates controlled. Nothing in this paper is L2.

The balanced transversal theorem is L1 but selects a post-terminal subpacket; it does not prove that the full physical output is large. No step identifies the residual low-sign Gram with the native affine Gram, changes the physical shift, or estimates a twin-prime correlation.

The result does, however, alter the practical order of attack. The next paper need not search for an abstract lower bound for a many-to-one map. After terminal activation and the remaining downstream interface requirements are kept explicit, it should estimate the terminal-prime coherence on one literal fiber or one declared rank interaction, with the source, cofactor, fixed shift, and actual atomic normalization all retained.

## Prime-resolved injectivity

The factorization becomes useful because $\mathcal R_{h_0}$ has no collision after restriction to the terminal-admissible coordinate space.

> **Lemma: Fixed-coordinate fused-label injectivity** \[lem:tpc55-fused-injectivity\] Assume $D=o(J)$, $h_0\ne0$, and the row uniqueness in [\[eq:tpc55-rows\]](TPC-055.md#ref-b3b085986816189a). For all sufficiently large $X$, if two rows in the same source system satisfy $$d_1(d_1\ell_1j+h_0)=d_2(d_2\ell_2j+h_0)  \label{eq:tpc55-fused-label-equality}$$ at the same retained $j\asymp J$, then $(d_1,\ell_1)=(d_2,\ell_2)$.

> **Proof** Rearranging gives $$j(\ell_1d_1^2-\ell_2d_2^2)=h_0(d_2-d_1).  \label{eq:tpc55-fused-injectivity-equation}$$ If the integer in parentheses on the left is nonzero, the left side has absolute value at least a fixed multiple of $J$, while the right side is $O_{h_0}(D)=o(J)$, a contradiction. > **Corollary: Terminal primes parameterize a full collision fiber** \[cor:tpc55-fiber-prime-bijection\] For $y=(\gamma,s,j)$, put $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal F_y^{\rm term}&:=q^{-1}(y)\cap\mathcal U_{h_0}^{\rm term},&  \mathcal F_y^{\rm phys}&:=q^{-1}(y)\cap\mathcal U_{h_0}^{\rm phys},\\  \mathcal P_y^{\rm term}&:=\{p_u:u\in\mathcal F_y^{\rm term}\},&  \mathcal P_y^{\rm phys}&:=\{p_u:u\in\mathcal F_y^{\rm phys}\}.  \end{aligned}  \label{eq:tpc55-fiber-and-primes}$$ For compatibility below, write $\mathcal F_y:=\mathcal F_y^{\rm term}$ and $\mathcal P_y:=\mathcal P_y^{\rm term}$. Then $u\mapsto p_u$ is a bijection from $\mathcal F_y^{\rm term}$ to $\mathcal P_y^{\rm term}$, and its restriction is a bijection from $\mathcal F_y^{\rm phys}$ to $\mathcal P_y^{\rm phys}$. In particular, two distinct terminal-admissible atoms in one full output fiber carry distinct terminal primes.

## The three energies

###### ref-5df9551ac4d0b940

###### ref-62f721c89a4e2f29

###### ref-3cbafaa163fcdd29

###### ref-9c23b8b84f37de1b

The preceding factorization keeps three norms separate. For an arbitrary lifted array $g$, define $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal E_{\rm pre}(g)  &:=\sum_{u\in\mathcal U^{\rm pre}}|g_u|^2,  \label{eq:tpc55-preterminal-energy}\\  \mathcal D_{h_0}(g)  &:=\|D_{h_0}g\|_2^2  =\sum_{u\in\mathcal U_{h_0}^{\rm term}}    |\mu(r)|^2|\Lambda(p_ur)|^2|g_u|^2\notag\\  &=\sum_{u\in\mathcal U_{h_0}^{\rm term}}    |\Lambda(p_ur)|^2|g_u|^2,  \label{eq:tpc55-terminal-diagonal-energy}\\  \mathcal G_{h_0}(g)  &:=\|\mathcal T_{h_0}g\|_2^2  =\sum_y\left|\sum_{u\in\mathcal F_y^{\rm term}}    \mu(r_u)\Lambda(p_ur_u)g_u\right|^2.  \label{eq:tpc55-grouped-energy}\end{aligned}$$ The last equality in $\mathcal D_{h_0}$ uses the inherited squarefree condition $r$ on $\mathcal U_{h_0}^{\rm term}$, so $|\mu(r)|^2=1$; it would be false on an unrestricted cofactor universe. The prime-resolved injection gives the exact identity $$\|\mathcal R_{h_0}g\|_2^2=\mathcal D_{h_0}(g).  \label{eq:tpc55-resolved-isometry}$$ It does not give $\mathcal D_{h_0}\asymp\mathcal E_{\rm pre}$: that comparison also requires terminal activation and two-sided control of $\Lambda$ on the selected support. Nor does it give a lower bound for $\mathcal G_{h_0}/\mathcal D_{h_0}$, because $\Sigma_p$ creates the cross terms.

For the physical vector $G_X^L$, the pre-terminal energy is the raw TPC–53 lifted profile sum. It should not be confused with the normalized $1/(E_XJ)$ model statistic used at an earlier intermediate stage in TPC–52 [`\citep{WangTPC52}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-055.md#cite-9d5877ad7648b068). Equations [\[eq:tpc55-preterminal-energy\]](TPC-055.md#ref-5df9551ac4d0b940)–[\[eq:tpc55-grouped-energy\]](TPC-055.md#ref-3cbafaa163fcdd29) use literal counting-measure atoms throughout.

# Exact singular-value geometry of a fiber sum

The terminal arithmetic will be inserted only after the finite-dimensional operator has been solved exactly.

## Weighted atomic spaces

###### ref-3095c63f636f155e

###### ref-09a77ab8c992d0ce

###### ref-13d021e45bef6fa4

Let $\mathcal U$ and $\mathcal Y$ be finite sets, let $q:\mathcal U\to\mathcal Y$, and assign positive atomic weights $a_u>0$ and $b_y>0$. Write $$\|z\|_a^2=\sum_{u\in\mathcal U}a_u|z_u|^2,  \qquad  \|w\|_b^2=\sum_{y\in\mathcal Y}b_y|w_y|^2.  \label{eq:tpc55-weighted-norms}$$ For coefficients $c_u\in\mathbb C$, define $$(Tz)_y=\sum_{u\in F_y}c_uz_u,  \qquad F_y=q^{-1}(y).  \label{eq:tpc55-general-fiber-operator}$$ Put $$S_y:=\sum_{u\in F_y}\frac{|c_u|^2}{a_u},  \qquad  \rho_y^2:=b_yS_y,  \qquad  \mathcal Y_+:=\{y:S_y>0\}.  \label{eq:tpc55-fiber-row-norm}$$

###### ref-0fe413e975539dfc

###### ref-27c16a61bd3e58cf

###### ref-2b51ada093fa68ac

###### ref-f802524b470b99ba

###### ref-88eedd89109ab898

###### ref-78be5ef03b8e4cd8

> **Theorem: Complete weighted fiber SVD** \[thm:tpc55-complete-svd\] The multiset of nonzero singular values of $T$ is exactly $$\{\!\{\rho_y:y\in\mathcal Y_+\}\!\}, \label{eq:tpc55-all-singular-values}$$ with one occurrence contributed by each active output fiber. Thus distinct fibers with the same value of $\rho_y$ contribute the corresponding singular-value multiplicity. In particular, $$\operatorname{rank}T=|\mathcal Y_+|,  \qquad  \dim\ker T=|\mathcal U|-|\mathcal Y_+|.  \label{eq:tpc55-rank-nullity}$$ For $y\in\mathcal Y_+$, the corresponding unit right and left singular vectors are $$(v_y)_u=  \begin{cases}  \dfrac{\overline{c_u}}{a_u\sqrt{S_y}},&u\in F_y,\\[6pt]  0,&u\notin F_y,  \end{cases}  \qquad  f_y=b_y^{-1/2}e_y,  \label{eq:tpc55-singular-vectors}$$ and $Tv_y=\rho_yf_y$.
>
> If $P_y$ is the $a$-orthogonal projection onto $\mathbb Cv_y$, then $$(P_yz)_u  =\frac{\overline{c_u}}{a_uS_y}    \sum_{v\in F_y}c_vz_v  \quad(u\in F_y),  \label{eq:tpc55-visible-projection}$$ and, with $P=\sum_{y\in\mathcal Y_+}P_y$, $$\mathcal H_a(\mathcal U)  =\ker T\mathbin{\mathop{\oplus}\limits^{\perp_a}}   \operatorname{Ran}P,  \qquad  T^*T=\sum_{y\in\mathcal Y_+}\rho_y^2P_y.  \label{eq:tpc55-svd-decomposition}$$

> **Proof** Conjugate $T$ by the isometries $x_u=\sqrt{a_u}z_u$ and $W_y=\sqrt{b_y}w_y$. The resulting matrix has row $$d_{y,u}=\sqrt{\frac{b_y}{a_u}}c_u\mathbf 1_{q(u)=y}.$$ Distinct rows have disjoint supports, hence are orthogonal, and the squared norm of row $y$ is $b_yS_y=\rho_y^2$. Normalizing the nonzero rows gives [\[eq:tpc55-singular-vectors\]](TPC-055.md#ref-f802524b470b99ba). The projection formula follows by taking the $a$-inner product with $v_y$. The remaining identities are the spectral decomposition and rank–nullity theorem.

Thus a collision fiber has no complicated internal spectrum: it has one visible direction and a completely flat zero spectrum in every transverse direction.

## Kernel basis and coordinate transversals

###### ref-236f22b47d37cacb

###### ref-cac65966fb0b329a

###### ref-92c4a6aa30b45923

###### ref-c7fcc44eb089722b

> **Proposition: Explicit collision kernel** \[prop:tpc55-kernel-basis\] Fix $y\in\mathcal Y_+$ and choose $u_0\in F_y$ with $c_{u_0}\ne0$. Then $$k_{y,u}:=c_ue_{u_0}-c_{u_0}e_u,  \qquad u\in F_y\setminus\{u_0\},  \label{eq:tpc55-kernel-vectors}$$ is a basis of the kernel of the fiber functional $z\mapsto\sum_{u\in F_y}c_uz_u$. Hence a coordinate subspace supported on $S\subseteq\mathcal U$ is a domain of injectivity for $T$ if and only if $$c_u\ne0\quad(u\in S),  \qquad  q|_S\ \text{is injective}.  \label{eq:tpc55-coordinate-injectivity}$$ When these conditions hold, the singular values of the restriction are exactly $$\left\{  |c_u|\sqrt{\frac{b_{q(u)}}{a_u}}:u\in S  \right\}.  \label{eq:tpc55-transversal-singular-values}$$

> **Proof** Every vector in [\[eq:tpc55-kernel-vectors\]](TPC-055.md#ref-cac65966fb0b329a) is killed because its two contributions are $c_{u_0}c_u-c_uc_{u_0}=0$. The vectors are independent and their number is $|F_y|-1$, which is the local nullity. If a coordinate restriction contains a zero coefficient or two directions from one active fiber, it has a kernel. Conversely, under [\[eq:tpc55-coordinate-injectivity\]](TPC-055.md#ref-92c4a6aa30b45923) the restricted matrix has one nonzero entry in each occupied row and column, giving [\[eq:tpc55-transversal-singular-values\]](TPC-055.md#ref-c7fcc44eb089722b).

This necessary-and-sufficient statement is why a collision-free coordinate selection must retain at most one terminal atom from each complete output fiber. Merely bounding the fiber size does not make the unrestricted map injective.

## Canonical quotient and the minimum-norm lift

We use the standard Moore–Penrose inverse convention [`\cite{BenIsraelGreville2003}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-055.md#cite-c226866f1a965f0f).

###### ref-7965b671b05ee5c0

###### ref-690f9ec5cc6cf152

###### ref-111669093da2b68a

###### ref-a43ee82dc85491e4

###### ref-a3b555a331d88250

> **Theorem: Exact pseudoinverse** \[thm:tpc55-pseudoinverse\] For every $w\in\mathcal H_b(\mathcal Y)$, the Moore–Penrose inverse is $$(T^\dagger w)_u  =  \begin{cases}  \dfrac{\overline{c_u}}{a_uS_{q(u)}}w_{q(u)},    &q(u)\in\mathcal Y_+,\\[6pt]  0,&q(u)\notin\mathcal Y_+.  \end{cases}  \label{eq:tpc55-pseudoinverse-formula}$$ Let $Q_+$ denote the $b$-orthogonal projection onto the output coordinates indexed by $\mathcal Y_+$. Then $$\begin{aligned}  TT^\dagger&=Q_+,  &T^\dagger T&=P,  \label{eq:tpc55-pseudoinverse-identities}\end{aligned}$$ and, for every $w$ supported on $\mathcal Y_+$, $T^\dagger w$ is the unique minimum-$a$-norm solution of $Tz=w$, with $$\inf_{Tz=w}\|z\|_a^2  =\sum_{y\in\mathcal Y_+}\frac{|w_y|^2}{S_y}.  \label{eq:tpc55-minimum-lift-energy}$$ If $\mathcal Y_+\ne\varnothing$, then $$\|T^\dagger\|_{b\to a}  =\left(\min_{y\in\mathcal Y_+}\rho_y\right)^{-1}.  \label{eq:tpc55-pseudoinverse-norm}$$ If $\mathcal Y_+=\varnothing$, then $T=T^\dagger=0$ and $\|T^\dagger\|_{b\to a}=0$.

> **Proof** The formula is the inverse on each one-dimensional nonzero singular direction and zero on both the domain kernel and the inactive output coordinates. Direct substitution gives [\[eq:tpc55-pseudoinverse-identities\]](TPC-055.md#ref-111669093da2b68a). Its input energy on one output coordinate is $$\sum_{u\in F_y}a_u  \left|\frac{\overline{c_u}}{a_uS_y}w_y\right|^2  =\frac{|w_y|^2}{S_y}.$$ Orthogonality of the fibers proves the energy and operator-norm formulas. When there is no active fiber, both operators are zero, which gives the last assertion.

Thus the quotient by the collision kernel has a canonical realization. Choosing one representative coordinate in each fiber is useful for support selection, but it is not the minimum-norm quotient unless the fiber already has one active coordinate.

## Restricted subspaces and the exact missing angle

Assume throughout this subsection that $\mathcal Y_+\ne\varnothing$. If $\mathcal Y_+=\varnothing$, then $T=0$, so no nonzero subspace can be a domain of injectivity for $T$.

###### ref-ed780d58b195b095

###### ref-1772109737571f15

Let $$\rho_{\min}=\min_{y\in\mathcal Y_+}\rho_y,  \qquad  \rho_{\max}=\max_{y\in\mathcal Y_+}\rho_y.  \label{eq:tpc55-rho-extremes}$$ For a nonzero subspace $V\subseteq\mathcal H_a(\mathcal U)$, define $$\alpha(V)  :=\inf_{\substack{z\in V\\\|z\|_a=1}}\|Pz\|_a,  \qquad  \gamma_T(V)  :=\inf_{\substack{z\in V\\\|z\|_a=1}}\|Tz\|_b.  \label{eq:tpc55-principal-angle}$$

###### ref-c0a73e7f1a101007

###### ref-b4b506161661a0e1

###### ref-531b710d7fc1ea36

###### ref-6383113d1306a195

> **Theorem: Sharp angle criterion** \[thm:tpc55-angle-criterion\] For every nonzero subspace $V$, in finite dimension, $$\alpha(V)>0  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad  V\cap\ker T=\{0\},  \label{eq:tpc55-angle-injectivity}$$ and $$\rho_{\min}\alpha(V)  \le\gamma_T(V)  \le\rho_{\max}\alpha(V).  \label{eq:tpc55-angle-bounds}$$ After normalizing the output coordinate $y$ by $1/\rho_y$, the resulting operator $\widehat T$ satisfies the exact identity $$\|\widehat Tz\|_b=\|Pz\|_a,  \qquad  \gamma_{\widehat T}(V)=\alpha(V).  \label{eq:tpc55-normalized-angle-identity}$$

> **Proof** By [\[thm:tpc55-complete-svd\]](TPC-055.md#ref-0fe413e975539dfc), $$\rho_{\min}^2\|Pz\|_a^2  \le\|Tz\|_b^2  \le\rho_{\max}^2\|Pz\|_a^2.$$ Taking the infimum on the unit sphere of $V$ proves [\[eq:tpc55-angle-bounds\]](TPC-055.md#ref-531b710d7fc1ea36). Compactness of that sphere gives [\[eq:tpc55-angle-injectivity\]](TPC-055.md#ref-b4b506161661a0e1). Dividing each nonzero singular value by itself gives [\[eq:tpc55-normalized-angle-identity\]](TPC-055.md#ref-6383113d1306a195).

Pointwise injectivity on a sequence of changing subspaces is therefore not enough for a uniform theorem. The subspaces must remain quantitatively separated from the explicit collision kernel.

## Alignment of a distinguished vector

###### ref-d19c95993d648868

For $z\in\mathcal H_a(\mathcal U)$, put $$E_y(z):=\sum_{u\in F_y}a_u|z_u|^2,  \qquad  A_y(z):=\sum_{u\in F_y}c_uz_u,  \label{eq:tpc55-fiber-energy-amplitude}$$ and, when $S_yE_y(z)>0$, define $$\eta_y(z):=\frac{|A_y(z)|^2}{S_yE_y(z)};  \label{eq:tpc55-fiber-alignment}$$ set $\eta_y=0$ in the zero case.

###### ref-df6fd0984eec943b

###### ref-c47bf0d4c221ee4d

###### ref-b2a255ae1b9123d5

> **Theorem: Exact visible-energy identity** \[thm:tpc55-visible-energy\] For every $z$ and every active fiber $y\in\mathcal Y_+$, $$0\le\eta_y(z)\le1,  \qquad  \|P_yz\|_a^2=\eta_y(z)E_y(z),  \label{eq:tpc55-local-visible-energy}$$ and $$\boxed{  \|Tz\|_b^2  =\sum_{y\in\mathcal Y_+}\rho_y^2\eta_y(z)E_y(z).}  \label{eq:tpc55-exact-alignment-identity}$$ On an active fiber, equality $\eta_y=1$ holds precisely when the restriction of $z$ to that fiber is a nonzero scalar multiple of $v_y$. On an inactive fiber $y\notin\mathcal Y_+$, one has $\eta_y=0$ by convention, all coefficients $c_u$ on $F_y$ vanish, and the entire fiber is killed by $T$.

> **Proof** Cauchy–Schwarz in the weighted fiber gives $|A_y|^2\le S_yE_y$. Formula [\[eq:tpc55-visible-projection\]](TPC-055.md#ref-88eedd89109ab898) gives $\|P_yz\|_a^2=|A_y|^2/S_y$. Multiplying by $\rho_y^2=b_yS_y$ and summing gives [\[eq:tpc55-exact-alignment-identity\]](TPC-055.md#ref-b2a255ae1b9123d5). The equality cases are the equality case of Cauchy–Schwarz and the definition of the kernel.

For the physical terminal map, the inherited atomic norms have $a_u=b_y=1$ and $$c_u=\mu(r_u)\Lambda(p_ur_u).  \label{eq:tpc55-physical-svd-coefficient}$$ The theorem must be applied with these literal coefficients. Choosing artificial weights $a_u=|c_u|^2$ would hide the terminal amplitude in a new domain norm and is not a physical normalization theorem.

# Arithmetic anatomy of a full residual fiber

## Divisor–prime parameterization

###### ref-4ffa1948518228be

###### ref-48306122bc4ca390

###### ref-e9fc36f6a62bd3f0

###### ref-525e2862de8f1143

###### ref-65a6bc6dc85b6b52

###### ref-d88675e6342c91b5

###### ref-264837cf162858b7

Fix an output $y=(\gamma,s,j)$ and write a source row as $m=d\ell$. Equations $$s=dr,  \qquad  pr-d\ell j=h_0  \label{eq:tpc55-fiber-equations}$$ give $$r=\frac sd,  \qquad  p=\frac{d^2\ell j+h_0d}{s},  \qquad  \ell=\frac{ps-h_0d}{d^2j}.  \label{eq:tpc55-fiber-parameterization}$$ Since $s$ is squarefree on the nonzero equality support, $d\mid s$ there automatically satisfies $(d,s/d)=1$. To avoid replacing the declared physical carrier by a larger Diophantine model, define the exact admissible triple set $$\mathfrak A_y  :=\left\{(d,\ell,p):  \begin{array}{l}  u=(d\ell,s/d,j,\gamma)\in\mathcal U_{h_0}^{\rm term},\quad q(u)=y,\\  d=d_{d\ell},\quad \ell=\ell_{d\ell},\quad p=p_u  \end{array}\right\},  \label{eq:tpc55-admissible-triple-set}$$ and let $$\mathfrak D_y^{\rm div}  :=\{d:\text{there exist }\ell,p\text{ with }(d,\ell,p)\in\mathfrak A_y\}.  \label{eq:tpc55-admissible-divisor-set}$$ Writing $\mathcal F_y^{\rm term}:=\mathcal F_y$, the map $$u=(m,r,j,\gamma)  \longmapsto(d_m,\ell_m,p_u)  \label{eq:tpc55-exact-fiber-bijection-map}$$ is an exact bijection $$\boxed{\mathcal F_y^{\rm term}\longleftrightarrow\mathfrak A_y.}  \label{eq:tpc55-exact-fiber-set}$$ Indeed, the reverse map sends $(d,\ell,p)$ to $(d\ell,s/d,j,\gamma)$, and membership in $\mathfrak A_y$ retains all source, residue, terminal, and carrier restrictions by definition. The physical fiber $$\mathcal F_y^{\rm phys}:=\mathcal F_y\cap\mathcal U_{h_0}^{\rm phys}  \label{eq:tpc55-physical-fiber-subset}$$ corresponds only to the subset of $\mathfrak A_y$ for which $g_u\ne0$. Thus the terminal fiber must not be identified with its physical subset.

The projection of $\mathfrak A_y$ to $p$ is one-to-one by [\[cor:tpc55-fiber-prime-bijection\]](TPC-055.md#ref-b9cc7484afda5afc). Projection to $d$ need not be: one fixed divisor $d\in\mathfrak D_y^{\rm div}$ can support several pairs of source and terminal primes. Thus $\tau(s)$ alone is not a fiber-size bound.

## A fixed-divisor determinant ladder

###### ref-4687d085f2683280

Fix $d\mid s$, put $r=s/d$, and let $$g=(r,dj).  \label{eq:tpc55-ladder-gcd}$$ Because $(d,r)=1$, one has $g=(r,j)$. The elementary solution theory of the determinant equation gives the exact ladder.

###### ref-fb2650387793dd1c

###### ref-67d72fe3021d0934

###### ref-fed450c17489126b

###### ref-03efaea98b50904b

> **Lemma: Complete integral ladder** \[lem:tpc55-complete-ladder\] The equation $$rp-dj\ell=h_0  \label{eq:tpc55-determinant-ladder-equation}$$ has an integral solution if and only if $g\mid h_0$. When one solution $(p_0,\ell_0)$ exists, all integral solutions are $$\boxed{  p=p_0+\frac{dj}{g}t,  \qquad  \ell=\ell_0+\frac r g t,  \qquad t\in\mathbb Z.}  \label{eq:tpc55-complete-ladder}$$ The two affine forms have nonzero cross determinant $$\frac{dj}{g}\ell_0-\frac r g p_0=-\frac{h_0}{g}.  \label{eq:tpc55-ladder-cross-determinant}$$

> **Proof** The gcd criterion is the standard solvability condition for a linear Diophantine equation. Differences of two solutions satisfy $r\Delta p=dj\Delta\ell$. Dividing by $g$ gives $\Delta p=(dj/g)t$ and $\Delta\ell=(r/g)t$. Substitution of $(p_0,\ell_0)$ in the original equation gives the cross determinant.

Primality of both affine forms, fixed residue classes, the mask, and the smooth terminal support only delete ladder points. They do not make the ambient spacing shorter.

## Exact ambient ladders and a coefficient-blind capacity upper bound

###### ref-24eb1f633755eb6e

Let the source-prime support have diameter at most $C_LL$, with $C_L$ fixed, and define the exact cofactor-block fiber $$\mathcal F_y(R):=\{u\in\mathcal F_y:R\le r_u\le2R\}.  \label{eq:tpc55-cofactor-block}$$

###### ref-7ea88943dfab8f0a

###### ref-597ec54eaad139a0

###### ref-cd18091e22b38fa2

> **Proposition: Ambient-ladder capacity upper bound** \[prop:tpc55-fiber-capacity\] For every fixed $(\gamma,s,j)$, the number of terminal-admissible atoms with $R\le r=s/d\le2R$ satisfies $$\begin{aligned}  |\mathcal F_y(R)|  &\le  \sum_{\substack{d\in\mathfrak D_y^{\rm div}\\                   R\le s/d\le2R\\(s/d,j)\mid h_0}}  \left(1+\left\lfloor  \frac{C_LL(s/d,j)}{s/d}\right\rfloor\right)  \label{eq:tpc55-exact-capacity-sum}\\  &\ll_{h_0,C_L}  \tau(s)\left(1+\frac LR\right).  \label{eq:tpc55-fiber-capacity}\end{aligned}$$ The same bound holds after imposing every literal prime, residue, mask, and profile support restriction.

> **Proof** For a fixed $d$, the $\ell$-coordinate in [\[eq:tpc55-complete-ladder\]](TPC-055.md#ref-fed450c17489126b) has spacing $r/g$. Hence an interval of diameter $C_LL$ contains at most $1+\lfloor C_LL g/r\rfloor$ integral ladder points. Solvability forces $g=(r,j)\mid h_0$, so $g\le|h_0|$. Sum over the eligible divisors and use $r\ge R$. Further arithmetic and support conditions only remove points.

The refined first line is more informative than the final divisor bound: it records which admissible divisors actually meet the cofactor block and the orbit gcd. Both lines are upper bounds. No matching lower bound, or even nonemptiness of a particular fiber, is asserted.

###### ref-3d999bba8bc7d985

###### ref-f9217f8524712c09

###### ref-287285b63d90aade

> **Corollary: Balanced subpower multiplicity** \[cor:tpc55-balanced-multiplicity\] Suppose every residual label has polynomial size in $X$, and $R\ge cL$ for a fixed $c>0$. Then $$M_X(R):=\max_y|\mathcal F_y(R)|=X^{o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc55-balanced-subpower}$$ If $R=X^{\theta_R+o(1)}$ and $L=X^{\theta_L+o(1)}$, the coefficient-blind polynomial capacity cost is at most $$\mu_{\rm cap}=(\theta_L-\theta_R)_+.  \label{eq:tpc55-capacity-exponent}$$

> **Proof** The standard divisor bound [`\citep[Ch.~1]{MontgomeryVaughan2007}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-055.md#cite-3c91c7e57f203d4b) gives $\tau(s)=X^{o(1)}$ uniformly for polynomial-size $s$. Apply [\[prop:tpc55-fiber-capacity\]](TPC-055.md#ref-7ea88943dfab8f0a). At the exponent level, $1+L/R=X^{(\theta_L-\theta_R)_++o(1)}$.

An upper-bound sieve may remove logarithmic powers by requiring the two ladder forms to be prime. Such a count does not by itself remove the positive-power term $L/R$ from this ambient coefficient-blind upper bound, furnish a matching lower bound, or estimate the actual complex amplitudes on the surviving points. Those are different tasks.

## A local separation certificate

The prime parameterization also gives a simple collision-separation identity that does not use $h_0$.

###### ref-b56a6c3178ac279b

###### ref-8dba9875b4096351

> **Proposition: Scaled-prime gap** \[prop:tpc55-scaled-prime-gap\] If two distinct inputs $u_1,u_2\in\mathcal F_{\gamma,s,j}$ have rows $m_i=d_i\ell_i$ and terminal primes $p_i$, then $$\frac{p_1}{d_1}-\frac{p_2}{d_2}  =\frac{j(m_1-m_2)}s,  \qquad  \left|\frac{p_1}{d_1}-\frac{p_2}{d_2}\right|  \ge\frac js.  \label{eq:tpc55-scaled-prime-gap}$$ Consequently, inside this fixed output fiber, any interval in the coordinate $p/d$ of diameter strictly less than $j/s$ contains at most one input atom.

> **Proof** Since $r_i=s/d_i$, the terminal equation gives $$\frac{p_i}{d_i}=\frac{m_i j+h_0}{s}.$$ Subtract. Distinct inputs have distinct rows by [\[thm:tpc55-prime-resolved-injection\]](TPC-055.md#ref-e298957b1568eff1), so $|m_1-m_2|\ge1$.

This is a deterministic local certificate, not an economical global cover: the number of $p/d$-intervals needed over the entire physical range can have a positive polynomial size. The rank construction in the next section uses the actual fiber multiplicity instead.

# Canonical collision-free transversals

The exact multiplicity bound can be converted into a selection theorem without assigning free phases or changing the physical atomic weights.

## Rank coloring of the terminal primes

###### ref-7f1c12d751156d25

###### ref-79d12d789b3533b7

Assume $\mathcal U_{h_0}^{\rm term}\ne\varnothing$. For each nonempty terminal output $y=(\gamma,s,j)$, order its terminal-prime set from [\[cor:tpc55-fiber-prime-bijection\]](TPC-055.md#ref-b9cc7484afda5afc): $$p_1(y)<p_2(y)<\cdots<p_{n_y}(y),  \qquad n_y=|\mathcal F_y|.  \label{eq:tpc55-prime-order}$$ Let $u_k(y)$ be the unique input with terminal prime $p_k(y)$, and put $$M=\max_y n_y,  \qquad  \mathcal S_k:=\{u_k(y):n_y\ge k\},  \quad 1\le k\le M.  \label{eq:tpc55-rank-transversals}$$ The construction depends only on the physical terminal prime and a fixed ordering rule, not on the coefficient values.

###### ref-62689b38bad51819

###### ref-7902ab2445adf23b

> **Theorem: Canonical prime-rank cover** \[thm:tpc55-prime-rank-cover\] The terminal-admissible input set is the disjoint union $$\mathcal U_{h_0}^{\rm term}  =\bigsqcup_{k=1}^{M}\mathcal S_k,  \label{eq:tpc55-rank-partition}$$ and $q|_{\mathcal S_k}$ is injective for every $k$. Conversely, a partition of the input coordinates into collision-free coordinate sets requires at least $M$ sets whenever a fiber of size $M$ is present. Thus the number of rank classes is optimal.

> **Proof** Every input has one rank inside its fiber, proving the disjoint union. A fixed rank contributes at most one input to each fiber, so $q$ is injective there. A fiber with $M$ coordinates forces its members into different collision-free sets, proving optimality.

###### ref-6cfe253124cc231e

For any nonnegative atomic energy $e_u$, write $$E_k=\sum_{u\in\mathcal S_k}e_u,  \qquad E=\sum_ue_u.  \label{eq:tpc55-rank-energies}$$

###### ref-f8be763b5297ea6d

###### ref-d98de453594e8265

> **Corollary: Sharp energy capture** \[cor:tpc55-rank-energy-capture\] At least one rank class satisfies $$E_k\ge\frac EM.  \label{eq:tpc55-rank-pigeonhole}$$ The constant $1/M$ is optimal for arbitrary nonnegative atomic data.

> **Proof** Sum the $M$ rank energies. Equality is possible when all $E_k=E/M$.

## Specialization to terminal diagonal energy

###### ref-9def7e64248e6f88

###### ref-86b2f70b780b0e03

Let $$x=D_{h_0}g,  \qquad  e_u=|x_u|^2  =|\Lambda(p_ur_u)|^2|g_u|^2.  \label{eq:tpc55-terminalized-array}$$ On a collision-free rank class, grouping creates no cross term: $$\|\mathcal T_{h_0}(g\mathbf 1_{\mathcal S_k})\|_2^2  =\sum_{u\in\mathcal S_k}|x_u|^2=E_k.  \label{eq:tpc55-transversal-exact-energy}$$

###### ref-163113c4839d1818

###### ref-f79f44069dcd8b2c

For a cofactor scale $R$, define the exact terminal block $$\mathcal B_R  :=\{u\in\mathcal U_{h_0}^{\rm term}:R\le r_u\le2R\},  \qquad  g_R:=g\mathbf 1_{\mathcal B_R},  \label{eq:tpc55-terminal-block}$$ and, using [\[eq:tpc55-cofactor-block\]](TPC-055.md#ref-24eb1f633755eb6e), its maximal fiber size $$\mathcal F_y(R)=\mathcal F_y\cap\mathcal B_R,  \qquad  M_X(R):=\max_y|\mathcal F_y(R)|.  \label{eq:tpc55-block-fibers}$$ Within each nonempty $\mathcal F_y(R)$, order the terminal primes and let $\mathcal S_k(R)$ contain the atom of block rank $k$, whenever it exists. Thus $\{\mathcal S_k(R):1\le k\le M_X(R)\}$ is the prime-rank cover applied to $\mathcal B_R$, rather than the restriction of a possibly different global rank ordering.

###### ref-6b5200777d549ecc

###### ref-aaddf30a6e48b59f

###### ref-3d65df15ce0a21bd

> **Theorem: Balanced collision-free block subpacket** \[thm:tpc55-balanced-transversal\] Assume that every residual label $s$ occurring in $\mathcal B_R$ has polynomial size in $X$, and let $g$ be a physical array such that $\mathcal D_{h_0}(g_R)>0$. There is a canonical family of $$M_X(R)\ll_{h_0,C_L}X^{o(1)}\left(1+\frac LR\right)  \label{eq:tpc55-number-of-transversals}$$ collision-free prime-rank classes covering every terminal-admissible atom in that block. At least one block rank class satisfies $$\boxed{  \|\mathcal T_{h_0}(g_R\mathbf 1_{\mathcal S_k(R)})\|_2^2  \ge\frac{\mathcal D_{h_0}(g_R)}{M_X(R)}.}  \label{eq:tpc55-balanced-transversal-energy}$$ In particular, if $R\ge cL$, then conditional on nonzero block terminal diagonal energy, the selected collision-free block subpacket incurs no fixed positive-power loss relative to that block energy.

> **Proof** Combine \[[prop:tpc55-fiber-capacity](TPC-055.md#ref-7ea88943dfab8f0a),[thm:tpc55-prime-rank-cover](TPC-055.md#ref-62689b38bad51819)\]. Apply [\[cor:tpc55-rank-energy-capture\]](TPC-055.md#ref-f8be763b5297ea6d) to the physical terminal diagonal weights of $g_R$ in [\[eq:tpc55-terminalized-array\]](TPC-055.md#ref-9def7e64248e6f88), then use [\[eq:tpc55-transversal-exact-energy\]](TPC-055.md#ref-86b2f70b780b0e03).

This is a positive post-terminal selection result on a specified nonzero block. It says that the balanced block terminal diagonal admits a coordinate subpacket with no fixed positive-power selection cost, on which physical grouping is exactly isometric after the literal terminal multiplier has been applied. It does not assert that the block energy is nonzero, compare that energy with pre-terminal or global energy, or say that a preassigned rank captures it: the family is canonical, while the choice of its energy-rich member may depend on $g_R$.

## Why the selected lower bound does not return to the full sum

###### ref-1cad92675b115ab3

Write $$X_k:=\mathcal T_{h_0}(g\mathbf 1_{\mathcal S_k}),  \qquad  \mathcal T_{h_0}g=\sum_{k=1}^{M}X_k.  \label{eq:tpc55-rank-output-sum}$$ The $X_k$ occupy the same output coordinate system and are not orthogonal. Therefore $\|X_k\|^2\le\|\sum_lX_l\|^2$ is false in general.

###### ref-732710783181f31f

###### ref-314cf200121c7b40

For one selected class $S=\mathcal S_k$, put $$D_S=\sum_{u\in S}|x_u|^2,  \qquad  D_R=\sum_{u\notin S}|x_u|^2.  \label{eq:tpc55-selected-complement-energy}$$ Every remaining rank class is also collision-free. The triangle and Cauchy–Schwarz inequalities give the valid one-sided estimate $$\boxed{  \|\mathcal T_{h_0}g\|_2  \ge  \left(\sqrt{D_S}-\sqrt{(M-1)D_R}\right)_+.}  \label{eq:tpc55-transversal-complement-bound}$$ Indeed, the sum of the norms of the other $M-1$ rank outputs is at most $\sqrt{M-1}\sqrt{D_R}$. Unless the selected rank dominates its complement at this scale or the inter-rank phases are controlled, [\[eq:tpc55-transversal-complement-bound\]](TPC-055.md#ref-314cf200121c7b40) can be zero.

The obstruction is real already for a two-atom fiber: two equal terminal amplitudes with opposite remaining phases give positive diagonal energy in both rank classes and zero full output. Hence [\[thm:tpc55-balanced-transversal\]](TPC-055.md#ref-6b5200777d549ecc) cannot be upgraded by monotonicity.

## Selection versus the canonical quotient

The rank class is a sparse coordinate representative of each occupied fiber. The minimum-norm representative from [\[thm:tpc55-pseudoinverse\]](TPC-055.md#ref-7965b671b05ee5c0) instead distributes an output over all fiber coordinates in proportion to $\overline{c_u}/a_u$. These solve different problems:

1.  the pseudoinverse is canonical for reconstructing an already known grouped output;

2.  a rank transversal is canonical for eliminating collisions before a lower-bound argument; and

3.  neither construction supplies the phase relation among all rank outputs of the distinguished physical array.

The rank selector is also a post-terminal arithmetic selector. It need not be representable by a fixed smooth source/Mellin box, and the present paper does not move earlier profile estimates through it. Any later analytic use must verify stability of the relevant estimates on this exact subset or work directly with the full coherence statistic below.

# The physical fiber: common gauges and the exact coherence gate

## Extraction of every common factor

###### ref-7aad37359eef43a8

###### ref-549f79c896957900

###### ref-e224012c293a4da8

Fix a physical output $y=(\gamma,s,j)$ with nonzero terminal diagonal energy. For $u=(m,r,j,\gamma)\in\mathcal F_y^{\rm phys}$, write $m=d\ell$. Combining [\[eq:tpc55-source-coefficient\]](TPC-055.md#ref-046f6118db0ba0cc), [\[eq:tpc55-literal-atom\]](TPC-055.md#ref-00508b94593176d1), and [\[eq:tpc55-squarefree-support\]](TPC-055.md#ref-17eb7ff5af790257) gives $$\mu(r)\mu(d)=\mu(s).  \label{eq:tpc55-common-mobius-gauge}$$ Moreover, $\gamma,s,j,a$ are fixed on the fiber. Define the common output factor $$\begin{aligned}  \Omega_y  &:={}  \mu(s)\eta_\gamma\mathscr R(q_\gamma)\xi_a  W_2(m_\gamma j/X)\Psi_2(d_\gamma j/K),  \label{eq:tpc55-common-output-factor}\end{aligned}$$ and the remaining source–terminal amplitude $$\begin{aligned}  b_u  &:={}  (\log\ell)\omega_D(d)\psi_L(\ell/L)\zeta_m  K_{m\gamma}  W_1(mj/X)\Psi_1(dj/K)  \Lambda(mj+h_0).  \label{eq:tpc55-remaining-fiber-amplitude}\end{aligned}$$ The equality $\Lambda(p_ur)=\Lambda(mj+h_0)$ has been used.

###### ref-db5bfb2cf5ffb7ec

###### ref-baf3dc92044cb5a9

###### ref-24e7a179e18e6389

###### ref-c8d3decd553a8fef

> **Proposition: Literal fiber gauge factorization** \[prop:tpc55-physical-gauge-factorization\] For every $u\in\mathcal F_y^{\rm phys}$, $$(D_{h_0}G_X^L)_u=\Omega_y b_u.  \label{eq:tpc55-terminalized-factorization}$$ Consequently $$\begin{aligned}  (\mathcal T_{h_0}G_X^L)_y  &=\Omega_y\sum_{u\in\mathcal F_y^{\rm phys}}b_u,  \label{eq:tpc55-physical-fiber-sum}\\  \mathcal E_y^{\rm diag}  &:=\sum_{u\in\mathcal F_y^{\rm phys}}|(D_{h_0}G_X^L)_u|^2  =|\Omega_y|^2\sum_{u\in\mathcal F_y^{\rm phys}}|b_u|^2.  \label{eq:tpc55-physical-fiber-diagonal}\end{aligned}$$ In particular, the product of the two Möbius factors is the common sign $\mu(s)$ on one full residual fiber; the individual factors $\mu(d)$ and $\mu(r)$ may vary. The opposite-row unit phase is also common on the fiber.

> **Proof** Substitute the literal factors. The source sign $\mu(d)$ and terminal sign $\mu(r)$ combine by [\[eq:tpc55-common-mobius-gauge\]](TPC-055.md#ref-7aad37359eef43a8). All opposite-row factors depend only on $(\gamma,j)$, and the direct orbit multiplier depends only on the fixed class $a$. The remaining factors are exactly [\[eq:tpc55-remaining-fiber-amplitude\]](TPC-055.md#ref-e224012c293a4da8). Squaring before summation gives [\[eq:tpc55-physical-fiber-diagonal\]](TPC-055.md#ref-c8d3decd553a8fef).

This extends the common-Möbius-phase observation of TPC–45 to the exact four-factor packet. It rules out one tempting but incorrect heuristic: independent Möbius randomness inside a fixed residual fiber cannot be the source of a grouping gain or loss. The amplitude $b_u$ still contains the inherited coefficient $\zeta_m$, source shapes, the literal mask, and terminal amplitude, and these are not proved to have a common phase.

## Terminal coherence and the cross-term identity

###### ref-1aa0fa560a626ab8

For an arbitrary terminalized array $x=D_{h_0}g$, define on every nonzero terminal fiber $$D_y(x):=\sum_{u\in\mathcal F_y^{\rm term}}|x_u|^2,  \qquad  \chi_y(x):=  \frac{\left|\sum_{u\in\mathcal F_y^{\rm term}}x_u\right|^2}{D_y(x)}.  \label{eq:tpc55-fiber-coherent-gain}$$ Put $\chi_y=0$ when $D_y=0$.

###### ref-7d680b8fcac332f9

###### ref-76371a45182fa714

###### ref-2b5201c3d641e3ed

###### ref-c363c9a15dd0ac03

> **Theorem: Exact terminal-coherence identity** \[thm:tpc55-terminal-coherence\] If $n_y=|\{u\in\mathcal F_y^{\rm term}:x_u\ne0\}|$, then $$0\le\chi_y(x)\le n_y,  \label{eq:tpc55-coherence-range}$$ and $$\begin{aligned}  \|\mathcal T_{h_0}g\|_2^2  &=\sum_y\chi_y(x)D_y(x)  \label{eq:tpc55-coherence-energy}\\  &=\mathcal D_{h_0}(g)  +2\operatorname{Re}   \sum_y\sum_{\substack{u<v\\u,v\in\mathcal F_y^{\rm term}}}  x_u\overline{x_v}.  \label{eq:tpc55-exact-cross-term}\end{aligned}$$ The upper endpoint is sharp for every $n_y\ge1$. The lower endpoint is sharp for $n_y\ge2$; a nonzero one-atom fiber has $\chi_y=1$.

> **Proof** The upper bound is Cauchy–Schwarz and the lower bound is positivity. Expanding each squared fiber sum gives [\[eq:tpc55-coherence-energy\]](TPC-055.md#ref-2b5201c3d641e3ed) and [\[eq:tpc55-exact-cross-term\]](TPC-055.md#ref-c363c9a15dd0ac03). Equal nonzero phases and equal moduli give $\chi_y=n_y$. When $n_y\ge2$, a nonzero vector in the fiber kernel gives $\chi_y=0$; when $n_y=1$, the quotient is identically one.

###### ref-568dcb0d3239b01c

For $\mathcal D_{h_0}(g)>0$, define the global physical grouping coherence $$\chi_{\rm grp}(g)  :=\frac{\|\mathcal T_{h_0}g\|_2^2}{\mathcal D_{h_0}(g)}  =\frac{\sum_y\chi_yD_y}{\sum_yD_y}.  \label{eq:tpc55-global-grouping-coherence}$$ When $\mathcal D_{h_0}(g)=0$, put $\chi_{\rm grp}(g):=0$. This convention is compatible with the zero terminal output. This number can be zero, one, or larger than one. It is the exact ratio needed when terminal diagonal energy, rather than pre-terminal energy, is the input normalization.

###### ref-935bd643708e0ff9

###### ref-4d80a0da853006d0

The alignment from [\[thm:tpc55-visible-energy\]](TPC-055.md#ref-df6fd0984eec943b) uses $$E_y(g):=\sum_{u\in\mathcal F_y^{\rm term}}|g_u|^2,  \qquad  S_y:=\sum_{u\in\mathcal F_y^{\rm term}}|\Lambda(p_ur_u)|^2.  \label{eq:tpc55-physical-alignment-data}$$ Whenever $S_yE_y(g)D_y(D_{h_0}g)>0$, the two diagnostics are related by $$\eta_y(g)  =\chi_y(D_{h_0}g)   \frac{D_y(D_{h_0}g)}{S_yE_y(g)}.  \label{eq:tpc55-eta-chi-relation}$$ In the zero cases both diagnostics retain their separately declared conventions; the displayed quotient is not used. They should not be interchanged when terminal weights are nonuniform.

## Two sharp positive criteria

The exact coherence can be bounded below if new phase or magnitude information is supplied.

###### ref-cd82615291b7e28c

###### ref-17cef87305ea4628

###### ref-f144b6b97b53c42a

###### ref-a4fd08ddfa492c87

> **Theorem: Sector criterion** \[thm:tpc55-sector-criterion\] Suppose that for every active fiber $y$ there is an angle $\vartheta_y$ such that $$|\arg(e^{-i\vartheta_y}x_u)|\le\theta<\frac\pi2  \qquad(u\in\mathcal F_y^{\rm term}, x_u\ne0).  \label{eq:tpc55-sector-hypothesis}$$ Then $$\left|\sum_{u\in\mathcal F_y^{\rm term}}x_u\right|^2  \ge\cos^2\theta  \left(\sum_{u\in\mathcal F_y^{\rm term}}|x_u|\right)^2  \ge\cos^2\theta D_y(x),  \label{eq:tpc55-sector-lower-bound}$$ and hence $$\|\mathcal T_{h_0}g\|_2^2  \ge\cos^2\theta\,\mathcal D_{h_0}(g).  \label{eq:tpc55-global-sector-bound}$$ For $\theta=0$, all amplitudes lie on one ray and grouping cannot reduce terminal diagonal energy.

> **Proof** After rotation by $e^{-i\vartheta_y}$, the real part of each amplitude is at least $\cos\theta$ times its modulus. The modulus of the sum is at least its real part. Sum over the fibers.

The common gauge in [\[prop:tpc55-physical-gauge-factorization\]](TPC-055.md#ref-db5bfb2cf5ffb7ec) removes $\mu(s)\eta_\gamma$ before testing [\[eq:tpc55-sector-hypothesis\]](TPC-055.md#ref-17cef87305ea4628). It does not prove that the remaining $b_u$ satisfy the sector condition.

###### ref-6e12a74d54dd5006

###### ref-5bb2530c85011a26

###### ref-b51fa1b27de83658

> **Theorem: Sharp magnitude-only lower envelope** \[thm:tpc55-polygon-envelope\] Fix $n\ge1$ and nonnegative magnitudes $r_1,\ldots,r_n$, put $$L_1=\sum_{i=1}^nr_i,  \qquad R_1=\max_i r_i.  \label{eq:tpc55-polygon-data}$$ Then $$\min_{\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_n\in\mathbb R}  \left|\sum_{i=1}^nr_i e^{i\theta_i}\right|  =(2R_1-L_1)_+,  \qquad  \max_{\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_n\in\mathbb R}  \left|\sum_{i=1}^nr_i e^{i\theta_i}\right|=L_1.  \label{eq:tpc55-polygon-envelope}$$ Thus magnitudes alone force a positive fiber lower bound if and only if one term strictly dominates the sum of all the others.

> **Proof** The triangle inequality gives the maximum and the lower bound $R_1-(L_1-R_1)=2R_1-L_1$. If $R_1\ge L_1-R_1$, place every smaller segment opposite the largest to attain that difference. If $R_1\le L_1-R_1$, the polygon inequality permits the segments to close, attaining zero.

This theorem is a sharp non-implication for magnitude-based selection. Unless a dominance inequality is proved, the moduli selected in TPC–54 do not imply a lower bound uniformly over their possible phases. It does not assert that the fixed physical phases realize the minimizing polygon.

## Exact gate factorization

###### ref-778a818dcfcf82fb

###### ref-49fbde32d54a1ebf

For any selected pre-terminal packet with $\mathcal E_{\rm pre}(g)>0$, define $$\delta_{\rm term}(g)  :=\frac{\mathcal D_{h_0}(g)}{\mathcal E_{\rm pre}(g)}.  \label{eq:tpc55-terminal-activation-ratio}$$ Combining definitions gives the exact ratio $$\boxed{  \frac{\|\mathcal T_{h_0}g\|_2^2}{\mathcal E_{\rm pre}(g)}  =\delta_{\rm term}(g)\,\chi_{\rm grp}(g).}  \label{eq:tpc55-exact-gate-factorization}$$ The two factors ask different questions:

1.  $\delta_{\rm term}$ measures which pre-terminal atoms meet a prime and a nonzero terminal cutoff, together with the literal size of $\Lambda$;

2.  $\chi_{\rm grp}$ measures coherent addition after those terminal atoms are resolved.

Neither is supplied by pre-terminal carrier coverage. The balanced transversal theorem obtains $\chi_{\rm grp}=1$ on one selected rank class relative to that class’s terminal diagonal, at the cost of deleting the other ranks. For the undeleted physical vector, the second factor in [\[eq:tpc55-exact-gate-factorization\]](TPC-055.md#ref-49fbde32d54a1ebf) remains the next arithmetic gate.

# Endpoint ledger, claim boundary, and the next gate

## The capacity cost at the inherited endpoint

###### ref-1b1cf91695d38125

###### ref-45459fd18d4b76fb

###### ref-921a350a948c6f3a

###### ref-ad868169968af35e

The declared row scales inherited from TPC–53 [`\citep{WangTPC53}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-055.md#cite-d85e91fc7a3dbaf8) are $$Q=X^{267/400+o(1)},  \qquad  J=X^{133/400+o(1)},  \qquad  D=X^{10049/52500+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc55-endpoint-scales}$$ Therefore $$L=\frac QD  =X^{99979/210000+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc55-endpoint-L}$$ On a cofactor block $R=X^{\theta_R+o(1)}$, the worst-case transversal-selection exponent certified by [\[cor:tpc55-balanced-multiplicity\]](TPC-055.md#ref-3d999bba8bc7d985) is $$\mu_{\rm cap}  =\left(\frac{99979}{210000}-\theta_R\right)_+.  \label{eq:tpc55-endpoint-capacity-cost}$$ Thus every fixed balanced range $R\gg L$ has $$M_X(R)=X^{o(1)},  \qquad \mu_{\rm cap}=0.  \label{eq:tpc55-balanced-zero-cost}$$ This means that the rank cover has no fixed positive-power loss inside a balanced block. It is not a claim that terminal diagonal energy is concentrated on, or even nonzero in, such a block.

###### ref-acbbe81f843792e2

###### ref-ff04dd18f7a46ade

At the older cofactor scale $R\asymp J$, the coefficient-blind ladder certificate gives the worst-case factor $$\frac LR  =X^{15077/105000+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc55-old-range-cost}$$ The exponent in this support-only certificate exceeds the nominal endpoint allowance $X^{1/400+o(1)}$ by $$\frac{15077}{105000}-\frac1{400}  =\frac{29629}{210000}>0.  \label{eq:tpc55-old-range-deficit}$$ Thus that certificate alone cannot close the endpoint budget at $R\asymp J$. The actual physical multiplicity may be smaller; no matching lower bound is asserted. Primality can give logarithmic thinning but does not improve this support-only power count.

## A complete loss ledger

###### ref-196432980c94cd15

###### ref-f797657bff0509b1

###### ref-4653f2c6ff941949

###### ref-df20b05f223e7dbf

###### ref-af44da74e37ede69

Suppose a parent physical atomic energy is first restricted to a declared compatible packet with fraction $$X^{-\lambda_{\rm sel}+o(1)},  \label{eq:tpc55-selection-loss}$$ and then to the pre-terminal cofactor block $$\mathcal B_R^{\rm pre}  :=\{u\in\mathcal U^{\rm pre}:R\le r_u\le2R\},  \qquad g_R^{\rm pre}:=g\mathbf 1_{\mathcal B_R^{\rm pre}},  \label{eq:tpc55-preterminal-block}$$ with energy fraction $$X^{-\lambda_{\rm blk}+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc55-block-loss}$$ Suppose first that terminal activation on this full pre-terminal block satisfies $$\delta_{\rm term}(g_R^{\rm pre})  \ge X^{-\lambda_{\rm term}+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc55-terminal-loss}$$ The resulting terminal diagonal energy is then partitioned by the block prime-rank classes of [\[thm:tpc55-balanced-transversal\]](TPC-055.md#ref-6b5200777d549ecc). Selecting the energy-rich collision-free rank costs at most the certified exponent $\mu_{\rm cap}$. The selected-rank grouped energy therefore has the formal lower exponent $$\lambda_{\rm sel}+\lambda_{\rm blk}  +\mu_{\rm cap}+\lambda_{\rm term}.  \label{eq:tpc55-selected-rank-ledger}$$ This conclusion is relative to the selected post-terminal rank. The order is essential: terminal activation is established on the pre-terminal block before the rank pigeonhole is applied to its terminal diagonal energy. The selected-rank statement does not return to the full output.

###### ref-cecbf15016ad85a0

###### ref-57b13005ccaaf9ed

###### ref-d5d34db161fa3f03

For the undeleted physical terminal vector of the same block, instead suppose one proves $$\chi_{\rm grp}\ge X^{-\lambda_{\rm coh}+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc55-coherence-loss}$$ Then the exact factorization [\[eq:tpc55-exact-gate-factorization\]](TPC-055.md#ref-49fbde32d54a1ebf) gives the combined exponent $$\lambda_{\rm sel}+\lambda_{\rm blk}  +\lambda_{\rm term}+\lambda_{\rm coh}.  \label{eq:tpc55-full-output-ledger}$$ Any later native-Gram comparison, frozen-family localization, boundary truncation, or ultra-tail reconnection must add its own nonnegative loss. To remain within the nominal TPC endpoint budget, the complete eventual sum must satisfy $$\boxed{  \lambda_{\rm sel}+\lambda_{\rm blk}  +\lambda_{\rm term}+\lambda_{\rm coh}+\lambda_{\rm loc}  +\lambda_{\rm Gram}+\lambda_{\rm bdry}+\lambda_{\rm tail}  \le\frac1{400}.}  \label{eq:tpc55-full-endpoint-budget}$$ At equality, a further error of size $X^{-1/400+o(1)}$ need not be absorbed; strict slack or a constant-level comparison is the safe closure condition.

The present paper proves none of $\lambda_{\rm blk}$, $\lambda_{\rm term}$, $\lambda_{\rm coh}$, or the downstream losses for the full inherited packet. It proves $\mu_{\rm cap}=0$ for the coordinate-selection problem conditional on a declared balanced block with nonzero terminal diagonal energy.

## Six-point physical audit

The interfaces used above satisfy the following audit.

1.  *Literal coefficients.* The source coefficient, binary mask, four smooth profile factors, cofactor sign, and terminal amplitude are displayed in [\[eq:tpc55-source-coefficient\]](TPC-055.md#ref-046f6118db0ba0cc), [\[eq:tpc55-mask\]](TPC-055.md#ref-d903697f5f74f698), [\[eq:tpc55-four-factor-profile\]](TPC-055.md#ref-c1581f890c2ddb43), and [\[eq:tpc55-terminal-diagonal\]](TPC-055.md#ref-8aa1e3551b17715a).

2.  *Fixed physical shift.* Every arithmetic equation uses the one fixed $h_0$. The frozen Fourier family is mentioned only to distinguish it from this map.

3.  *Atomic normalization.* Domain, prime-resolved output, and grouped output all use inherited counting measure. The terminal coefficient is not hidden in the norm.

4.  *Canonical representation.* The map is the literal TPC–53 coordinate map. No projective representation or favorable Mellin layer is selected.

5.  *Actual support.* The full pre-terminal universe, terminal-admissible subset, and physically nonzero subset are separated in [\[eq:tpc55-preterminal-universe\]](TPC-055.md#ref-82f045d4a17984d6), [\[eq:tpc55-terminal-universe\]](TPC-055.md#ref-f17549c53885e9db), and [\[eq:tpc55-physical-active-set\]](TPC-055.md#ref-cceb1a43a9e7ceef).

In particular, the following implications remain forbidden:

1.  subpower maximal multiplicity does not imply a lower bound for the unselected full sum;

2.  common Möbius phase does not imply a sector condition for $\zeta_m$ and the source/mask profile;

3.  a collision-free selected rank need not be a compatible smooth pre-terminal cell;

4.  refined-key, equivalently fixed $(s,j,p,\gamma)$, injectivity does not make fixed $(s,j,\gamma)$ injective, because different terminal primes are exactly the collision modes;

5.  the physical fixed-$h_0$ prime synthesis is not the frozen-intercept Fourier $p$-sum; and

6.  a residual output norm is not automatically the native affine physical Gram face.

## The next gate

###### ref-913a8399bf817f1f

The next paper should keep one literal full fiber or one explicitly declared cofactor block and study $$\sum_{u\in\mathcal F_{\gamma,s,j}^{\rm phys}} b_u,  \label{eq:tpc55-next-fiber-sum}$$ with $b_u$ given by [\[eq:tpc55-remaining-fiber-amplitude\]](TPC-055.md#ref-e224012c293a4da8). There are three honest doors:

1.  prove a deterministic sector, dominance, or restricted-Gram lower bound for the distinguished physical amplitudes on one nonempty literal regime;

2.  prove a two-sided coherent terminal-prime square after an explicitly labeled averaging or smoothing, then quantify exactly why that model does or does not localize back to fixed $h_0$; or

3.  construct a literal admissible packet whose remaining amplitudes approach the collision kernel, giving a coefficient-specific saturation or no-go theorem for this route.

An estimate for an arbitrary smooth proxy, a random-phase model, or an averaged shift would be useful only under its own label. It would not be reported as a fixed-shift parity gain.

#### Conclusion.

The residual-grouping obstruction is now explicitly identified at the operator and support levels. Under the declared hypotheses, the refined terminal key is injective and the prime-resolved operator restricted to terminal-admissible coordinates is a weighted coordinate embedding; forgetting the terminal prime creates rank-one output fibers with an explicit collision kernel. Balanced fibers admit an optimal subpower transversal cover, and the product of the two literal Möbius signs is common inside each fiber. After terminal activation is controlled, the remaining internal grouping question is how the distinguished source–mask–prime amplitudes align with the visible direction. Fixed-shift localization, native-Gram comparison, boundary control, and tail reconnection remain separate downstream gates. Every one of these steps is strictly upstream of a parity-sensitive or twin-prime conclusion.
