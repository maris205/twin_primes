## Abstract

For the literal fixed-shift packet, the terminal map first restricts the full pre-terminal array to the exact prime equality support, multiplies a surviving atom by its cofactor sign and shaped terminal logarithm, and then sums distinct terminal primes inside a complete output fiber $y=(\gamma,d(m)r,j)$. TPC–55 proved that adjoining the terminal prime to the output key removes every collision on one declared source system. The remaining internal gate is therefore the coherent square of one explicit prime-indexed vector in each fiber. That square can vanish even when its terminal diagonal is positive.

We prove a universal lower-selection theorem for this exact gate. Fix a unit gauge on each output fiber and partition the gauge-normalized realized nonzero amplitudes into $M\ge3$ equal angular sectors. One global sector index in these normalized coordinates, used simultaneously on every fiber, selects a coordinate subpacket whose grouped energy is at least $$\frac{\cos^2(\pi/M)}{M}$$ times the full terminal diagonal energy. In each fiber the selected atoms lie, after its own fixed gauge, in the same relative cone of half-angle $\pi/M<\pi/2$. The corresponding absolute cones may rotate with the fiber, and the estimate is independent of the fiber cardinalities. Among equal finite sectorizations, $M=5$ is optimal and gives the exact constant $(3+\sqrt5)/40$. Allowing a continuously rotated angular window gives the slightly larger constant $c_*=\theta_*\cos^2\theta_*/\pi=0.1311275\ldots$, where $2\theta_*\tan\theta_*=1$. We also prove a quantitative phase-error version.

For the literal physical vector, the common gauge is the exact product of the residual Möbius sign, opposite-row phase, orbit factor, and common opposite-row profiles. The terminal-prime bijection makes every selected sector an unambiguous coordinate projection on the actual terminal support. Consequently, conditional on a separately established terminal-activation ratio, the adaptive pentant subpacket has no positive polynomial coherence loss at this selected grouping gate. Relative to the earlier rank-by-prime selection, it improves the retained-energy constant at that gate, at the price of coefficient adaptivity: it retains a constant fraction of the terminal diagonal without a maximal-multiplicity factor or a balanced cofactor hypothesis.

The qualifier *adaptive* is essential for the universal all-coordinate guarantee. We prove that any selector fixed independently of the coefficient phases and retaining two coordinates in one fiber has a nonzero collision-kernel direction on the unrestricted coordinate space. The inherited class with only an upper bound on the complex source factor can realize that cancellation locally on any nondegenerate two-atom literal carrier; this is not promoted to a global literal-class no-go. Thus the theorem is a fixed-$h_0$, post-terminal selected-square result, not a lower bound for the undeleted output. It does not prove terminal activation, a coefficient-independent pre-terminal phase realization, frozen-shift localization, native-Gram comparison, a parity improvement, or a twin-prime statement.

Write the terminalized amplitude indexed by that prime as $$x_{y,p}  =\mu(r_{y,p})\Lambda(p r_{y,p})g_{u(y,p)}.  \label{eq:tpc56-intro-literal-amplitude}$$ The exact diagonal and grouped energies are $$\mathcal D_{h_0}(g)=\sum_y\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_y}|x_{y,p}|^2,  \qquad  \mathcal G_{h_0}(g)=\sum_y\left|\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_y}x_{y,p}\right|^2.  \label{eq:tpc56-intro-two-energies}$$ All sums in [\[eq:tpc56-intro-two-energies\]](TPC-056.md#ref-b74cf9051ac597df) are over the exact finite terminal support; $\mathcal P_y$ is not an ambient interval of primes. The ratio $\mathcal G_{h_0}/\mathcal D_{h_0}$ can be zero. TPC–55 supplied two ways to avoid that zero: impose an acute sector condition, or retain at most one prime in each fiber. The first was only a criterion. The second was an unconditional post-terminal construction, but on a cofactor block it retained only the reciprocal of the maximal fiber multiplicity.

The present paper asks a narrower question:

> Can one always extract a genuinely coherent terminal-prime subpacket while retaining a constant fraction of the full terminal diagonal, independently of the fiber sizes?

###### ref-d6f856fc7579f75a

The answer is yes if the selection is permitted to use the realized phases. The proof is elementary, but its placement in the physical operator is order-sensitive. Split the complex plane into five equal sectors after removing the common output gauge. The five sectors partition the terminal diagonal, so one of them carries at least one fifth of that energy. Inside one sector every amplitude has real projection at least $\cos(\pi/5)$ times its modulus. Therefore the selected grouped square is at least $$c_5\mathcal D_{h_0}(g),  \qquad  c_5:=\frac{\cos^2(\pi/5)}5       =\frac{3+\sqrt5}{40}.  \label{eq:tpc56-intro-pentant-constant}$$ Only one sector index is selected globally in the gauge-normalized phase coordinates; it is not chosen separately for every output fiber. The corresponding absolute angular window is rotated by the canonical gauge of that fiber.

###### ref-b3cbfe1d1577e6a2

###### ref-722dae31e4e26ba2

This gives a real advance at the selected grouping gate. No $X^{\varepsilon}$, divisor, or maximal-multiplicity factor appears. If a pre-terminal packet satisfies the separate activation estimate $$\mathcal D_{h_0}(g)\ge  X^{-\lambda_{\rm term}+o(1)}\mathcal E_{\rm pre}(g),  \label{eq:tpc56-intro-activation}$$ then the selected physical output satisfies $$\mathcal G_{h_0}(P_*g)  \ge c_5X^{-\lambda_{\rm term}+o(1)}\mathcal E_{\rm pre}(g).  \label{eq:tpc56-intro-selected-transfer}$$ Thus the adaptive coherence exponent is zero. In contrast with the rank-by-prime transversal, no balanced condition such as $R\gg L$ is needed.

There is an equally important negative half. The sets selected by the five-sector rule depend on the phases of $x_{y,p}$. Once the realized vector is fixed, each is an ordinary orthogonal coordinate projection; as a rule on a coefficient class, however, the construction is nonlinear. On the unrestricted coordinate space, if a coefficient-independent set contains two coordinates of one fiber, the vector assigning opposite values to those coordinates lies in the grouping kernel. The inherited hypothesis $|\zeta_m|\ll1$ also realizes this cancellation *inside* any nondegenerate two-atom literal fiber by sufficiently small choices of the two relevant source factors. This proves failure of a uniform fiberwise lower bound for such a selector. It does not by itself disprove a global lower bound over the coupled literal coefficient class, because the same row coefficients may activate other fibers. A global literal-class no-go would require control or isolation of those additional outputs.

###### ref-306c2458915ddb81

This dichotomy is useful for the route ahead: $$\boxed{  \begin{array}{c}  \text{adaptive terminal phases}\Rightarrow  \text{constant-energy coherent subpacket},\\[2pt]  \text{uniform selection on unrestricted arrays}\Rightarrow  \text{at most one atom per fiber},  \end{array}}  \label{eq:tpc56-intro-dichotomy}$$ It turns an unstructured prime-collision space into a five-channel phase interface. A future paper can now ask whether the five channels come from fixed residue and sign-stable source cells, or whether every such attempted pre-terminal realization meets a collision obstruction, with global literal-class saturation requiring separate control of row couplings.

# A universal phase-sector lower selection theorem

Let $\mathcal U$ be a finite set partitioned into fibers $$\mathcal U=\bigsqcup_{y\in\mathcal Y}\mathcal F_y,  \label{eq:tpc56-abstract-fibers}$$ and let $x=(x_u)_{u\in\mathcal U}\in\mathbb C^{\mathcal U}$. The gauge family is part of the data and is fixed before the maximizing sector index is selected. These gauges only rotate output coordinates and hence do not change any grouped norm.

## Equal sectors and one global choice

On the phase circle use the half-open sectors $$I_k=  \left[\frac{2\pi k}{M}-\frac\pi M,        \frac{2\pi k}{M}+\frac\pi M\right)  \pmod {2\pi},  \qquad 0\le k<M.  \label{eq:tpc56-equal-sectors}$$ For $x_u\ne0$, put $$\varphi_u=\operatorname{Arg}(\overline{\omega_{q(u)}}x_u)\pmod {2\pi},  \label{eq:tpc56-normalized-phase}$$ where $q(u)=y$ for $u\in\mathcal F_y$, and define $$\mathcal S_k(x;\omega)=\{u:x_u\ne0,\ \varphi_u\in I_k\}.  \label{eq:tpc56-sector-support}$$ Let $P_k=P_k(x;\omega)$ be the orthogonal coordinate projection onto $\mathcal S_k(x;\omega)$. Zero coordinates can be assigned to any sector; they affect none of the identities. The notation records an important fact: after $x$ is fixed, $P_k$ is linear and orthogonal, but the rule $x\mapsto P_k(x;\omega)$ is nonlinear.

###### ref-1245be1e3b8c5d42

Define the selected diagonal and grouped energies $$D_k(x):=\|P_kx\|_2^2,  \qquad  C_k(x):=\|\Sigma P_kx\|_2^2.  \label{eq:tpc56-sector-energies}$$

###### ref-518d39f8f6ab5738

###### ref-f2ddea796f632651

###### ref-86ab857ed4b47cf4

###### ref-e83d2a1bf115fad0

###### ref-c4fbfe20babe6670

> **Theorem: Equal-sector compression and selection** \[thm:tpc56-equal-sector-selection\] For every finite fiber system, every $x$, every fixed family of unit gauges, and every integer $M\ge3$, $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_{k=0}^{M-1}D_k(x)  &=\|x\|_2^2,  \label{eq:tpc56-sector-diagonal-partition}\\  C_k(x)&\ge\cos^2(\pi/M)D_k(x)  \qquad(0\le k<M),  \label{eq:tpc56-each-sector-lower}\\  \sum_{k=0}^{M-1}C_k(x)  &\ge\cos^2(\pi/M)\|x\|_2^2.  \label{eq:tpc56-sector-compression-lower}\end{aligned}$$ Let $k_*$ be the least index maximizing $D_k(x)$. Then this canonical tie rule gives $$\boxed{  \|\Sigma P_{k_*}x\|_2^2  \ge c_M\|x\|_2^2,  \qquad  c_M:=\frac{\cos^2(\pi/M)}M.}  \label{eq:tpc56-universal-sector-lower}$$ The same index $k_*$ is used on every fiber in the normalized coordinates $\operatorname{Arg}(\overline{\omega_y}x_u)$. Since $\omega_y$ may vary with $y$, the corresponding absolute angular window is a fiber-dependent rotation.

###### ref-856ceb5780d3ded7

###### ref-9896bfcdb7c19276

> **Proof** The sector supports partition the nonzero coordinates, proving [\[eq:tpc56-sector-diagonal-partition\]](TPC-056.md#ref-f2ddea796f632651). Fix $k$ and a fiber $y$. For every $u\in\mathcal F_y\cap\mathcal S_k$, one can write $$\overline{\omega_y}e^{-2\pi i k/M}x_u  =|x_u|e^{i\delta_u},  \qquad |\delta_u|\le\frac\pi M,  \label{eq:tpc56-sector-angle-form}$$ with one endpoint understood by the half-open convention. Therefore $$\begin{aligned}  \left|\sum_{u\in\mathcal F_y\cap\mathcal S_k}x_u\right|  &\ge  \operatorname{Re}\left(  \overline{\omega_y}e^{-2\pi i k/M}  \sum_{u\in\mathcal F_y\cap\mathcal S_k}x_u\right)\notag\\  &\ge\cos(\pi/M)  \sum_{u\in\mathcal F_y\cap\mathcal S_k}|x_u|\notag\\  &\ge\cos(\pi/M)  \left(\sum_{u\in\mathcal F_y\cap\mathcal S_k}|x_u|^2\right)^{1/2}.  \label{eq:tpc56-fiber-sector-proof}\end{aligned}$$ Squaring and summing over $y$ proves [\[eq:tpc56-each-sector-lower\]](TPC-056.md#ref-86ab857ed4b47cf4); summing over $k$ gives [\[eq:tpc56-sector-compression-lower\]](TPC-056.md#ref-e83d2a1bf115fad0). Finally, $D_{k_*}\ge M^{-1}\|x\|_2^2$, so [\[eq:tpc56-each-sector-lower\]](TPC-056.md#ref-86ab857ed4b47cf4) gives [\[eq:tpc56-universal-sector-lower\]](TPC-056.md#ref-c4fbfe20babe6670).

###### ref-effe2a6f4ae416f3

The theorem contains two different objects. The phase-split synthesis $$x\longmapsto  \left(\sum_{u\in\mathcal F_y\cap\mathcal S_k}x_u\right)_{(y,k)}  \label{eq:tpc56-phase-split-synthesis}$$ has a lower bound on the distinguished vector used to define its sectors. The original output instead sums the $M$ sector outputs at fixed $y$. Those final $M$ channels may cancel. Thus [\[eq:tpc56-sector-compression-lower\]](TPC-056.md#ref-e83d2a1bf115fad0) is not a lower bound for $\|\Sigma x\|_2$.

## Why five equal sectors are best in this family

###### ref-eca5c3023423131e

###### ref-d4d7b4fae2e41419

> **Proposition: Optimal equal-sector count** \[prop:tpc56-optimal-equal-sector-count\] Among integers $M\ge3$, the certified constant $c_M=M^{-1}\cos^2(\pi/M)$ is uniquely maximized at $M=5$. Its value is $$\boxed{  c_5=\frac{\cos^2(\pi/5)}5      =\frac{3+\sqrt5}{40}      =0.1309016994\ldots .}  \label{eq:tpc56-pentant-constant}$$

###### ref-debe103b3ae04419

###### ref-78957eeed7e38557

> **Proof** For real $t\ge3$, let $f(t)=t^{-1}\cos^2(\pi/t)$. Direct differentiation gives $$\frac{f'(t)}{f(t)}  =\frac{2\pi\tan(\pi/t)-t}{t^2}.  \label{eq:tpc56-equal-sector-derivative}$$ After setting $\theta=\pi/t$, the unique critical point is determined by $2\theta\tan\theta=1$. The left side is strictly increasing on $(0,\pi/2)$, and the corresponding $t$ lies strictly between $4$ and $5$. Hence the integer maximum is either $4$ or $5$. Now $$c_4=\frac18,  \qquad  c_5=\frac{3+\sqrt5}{40}>\frac18.  \label{eq:tpc56-four-five-comparison}$$ The monotonicity on either side of the critical point proves uniqueness.

The word *optimal* in [\[prop:tpc56-optimal-equal-sector-count\]](TPC-056.md#ref-eca5c3023423131e) refers only to the constants certified by equal finite sectorizations. It is not a global optimality theorem over all nonlinear subset rules.

## A continuously rotated window

###### ref-0306a0d6b7345124

The finite pentant library is convenient for a literal audit. If a continuum of rotations is allowed, the constant can be improved slightly. For $0<\theta<\pi/2$ and $\alpha\in\mathbb T=\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Z)$, retain the coordinates satisfying $$\operatorname{dist}_{\mathbb T}(\varphi_u,\alpha)\le\theta  \label{eq:tpc56-rotating-window}$$ and denote the associated coordinate projection by $P_{\alpha,\theta}$.

###### ref-144bd4e1e60f4f30

###### ref-3846e460abf165af

###### ref-b11d42620fb54011

###### ref-1b5ae8a60496ec0d

> **Theorem: Rotating-window selection** \[thm:tpc56-rotating-window\] For every $0<\theta<\pi/2$, there exists $\alpha\in\mathbb T$ such that $$\|\Sigma P_{\alpha,\theta}x\|_2^2  \ge c(\theta)\|x\|_2^2,  \qquad  c(\theta):=\frac\theta\pi\cos^2\theta.  \label{eq:tpc56-rotating-window-bound}$$ The function $c(\theta)$ has a unique maximizer $\theta_*\in(0,\pi/2)$ characterized by $$2\theta_*\tan\theta_*=1.  \label{eq:tpc56-optimal-window-equation}$$ Numerically, $$\theta_*=0.6532711871\ldots,  \qquad  c_*=c(\theta_*)=0.1311275284\ldots .  \label{eq:tpc56-optimal-window-constant}$$

###### ref-b007ce27ebcc1b51

###### ref-1376187329ddb0c9

> **Proof** For each nonzero coordinate, the set of centers $\alpha$ satisfying [\[eq:tpc56-rotating-window\]](TPC-056.md#ref-0306a0d6b7345124) has normalized Haar measure $\theta/\pi$. Fubini therefore gives $$\int_{\mathbb T}\|P_{\alpha,\theta}x\|_2^2  \frac{d\alpha}{2\pi}  =\frac\theta\pi\|x\|_2^2.  \label{eq:tpc56-window-average-energy}$$ Some center retains at least that much diagonal energy. The proof of [\[eq:tpc56-fiber-sector-proof\]](TPC-056.md#ref-9896bfcdb7c19276), with $\theta$ in place of $\pi/M$, proves [\[eq:tpc56-rotating-window-bound\]](TPC-056.md#ref-3846e460abf165af). Finally, $$c'(\theta)=\frac{\cos\theta}{\pi}  \bigl(\cos\theta-2\theta\sin\theta\bigr),  \label{eq:tpc56-window-derivative}$$ so the unique interior critical point satisfies [\[eq:tpc56-optimal-window-equation\]](TPC-056.md#ref-b11d42620fb54011); endpoint values are zero.

## Stability under phase uncertainty

The sector theorem is not destroyed by small errors in the phases used to form the coordinate sets.

###### ref-c3475315fb93445b

###### ref-9be44ce5a198010f

###### ref-55054d2275ebbba6

###### ref-564121ae5dab1798

> **Theorem: Phase-error stability** \[thm:tpc56-phase-error-stability\] Suppose approximate normalized phases $\widehat\varphi_u$ satisfy $$\operatorname{dist}_{\mathbb T}(\widehat\varphi_u,\varphi_u)\le\varepsilon  \qquad(x_u\ne0).  \label{eq:tpc56-phase-error-hypothesis}$$ Partition the coordinates by the $M$ equal sectors of the approximate phases, and choose the least sector with maximal true diagonal energy. If $$0\le\varepsilon<\frac\pi2-\frac\pi M,  \label{eq:tpc56-phase-error-range}$$ then the resulting projection $\widehat P_*$ satisfies $$\boxed{  \|\Sigma\widehat P_*x\|_2^2  \ge  \frac{\cos^2(\pi/M+\varepsilon)}M\|x\|_2^2.}  \label{eq:tpc56-phase-error-lower}$$

> **Proof** The approximate sectors still partition the active coordinates, so the selected sector carries at least $M^{-1}\|x\|_2^2$ diagonal energy. Every true phase in that sector lies within $\pi/M+\varepsilon<\pi/2$ of its center. Apply the real-projection argument in [\[eq:tpc56-fiber-sector-proof\]](TPC-056.md#ref-9896bfcdb7c19276) with this enlarged half-angle.

The error $\varepsilon$ may include both an atomwise phase error and a fiberwise error in the chosen common gauge. The theorem requires exact magnitudes only to identify an energy-maximizing sector; a separate magnitude-approximation error would have to be charged explicitly.

# Specialization to the literal terminal-prime square

We now apply [\[thm:tpc56-equal-sector-selection\]](TPC-056.md#ref-518d39f8f6ab5738) to $x=D_{h_0}G_X^L$. The specialization uses the exact physical support twice: first to identify each coordinate with one terminal prime, and then to remove the common fiber gauge without changing any phase difference.

## Prime-indexed pentants

###### ref-bbaff025a06fde22

###### ref-cfec0cf6ed90108d

###### ref-f48d14f8dc4e87e3

For an active output $y$, let $u(y,p)$ be the unique input associated with $p\in\mathcal P_y^{\rm phys}$ by [\[prop:tpc56-prime-resolved-bijection\]](TPC-056.md#ref-74cf0b98c1fc567b), and put $$x_{y,p}  :=(D_{h_0}G_X^L)_{u(y,p)}  =\mu(r_{y,p})\Lambda(p r_{y,p})    [G_X^L]_{u(y,p)}.  \label{eq:tpc56-prime-indexed-amplitude}$$ Then $$\begin{aligned}  D_y&:=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_y^{\rm phys}}|x_{y,p}|^2,  \label{eq:tpc56-fiber-diagonal}\\  C_y&:=\left|\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_y^{\rm phys}}x_{y,p}\right|^2.  \label{eq:tpc56-fiber-coherent-square}\end{aligned}$$ Empty fibers have $D_y=C_y=0$. This defines an orthogonal coordinate projection $P_k^{\rm sec}$ on the terminal coordinate space. Extend it by zero to the rest of $\mathcal U^{\rm pre}$. For the fixed realized vector, diagonal coordinate projections commute with the terminal diagonal: $$D_{h_0}P_k^{\rm sec}G_X^L  =P_k^{\rm sec}D_{h_0}G_X^L.  \label{eq:tpc56-selector-commutation}$$

> **Theorem: Literal pentant extraction** \[thm:tpc56-literal-pentant-extraction\] Assume the hypotheses of [\[prop:tpc56-prime-resolved-bijection\]](TPC-056.md#ref-74cf0b98c1fc567b). > **Proof** If $\mathcal D_{h_0}(G_X^L)=0$, take the zero projection. Otherwise apply [\[thm:tpc56-equal-sector-selection\]](TPC-056.md#ref-518d39f8f6ab5738) to the finite terminalized vector with the gauges [\[eq:tpc56-unit-output-gauge\]](TPC-056.md#ref-491682cdfc9f0f7b). The first conclusion is the diagonal pigeonhole. ## Cofactor blocks and comparison with rank selection

###### ref-fb33fdf204636542

###### ref-5e91816683ab4dee

For a cofactor interval $\mathcal B_R=[R,2R]$, put $$g_R=g\mathbf 1_{r\in\mathcal B_R}.  \label{eq:tpc56-cofactor-block}$$ The fibers of $D_{h_0}g_R$ are simply intersections of the full fibers with the block. No new collision is created, so the sector theorem gives $$\mathcal G_{h_0}(P_{*,R}g_R)  \ge c_5\mathcal D_{h_0}(g_R)  \label{eq:tpc56-block-sector-lower}$$ for every block, empty or nonempty. Unlike the TPC–55 rank cover, this does not require $R\gg L$ or a bound for the maximal fiber size.

The two selectors solve different problems:

| Feature                                   | Prime-rank transversal                   | Phase-sector selector                         |
|:------------------------------------------|:-----------------------------------------|:----------------------------------------------|
| Atoms retained per fiber                  | At most one                              | Arbitrarily many in one acute sector          |
| Terminal diagonal retained                | At least reciprocal maximal multiplicity | At least $1/5$ globally                       |
| Grouped/diagonal ratio on selected packet | Exactly one                              | At least $\cos^2(\pi/5)$                      |
| Balanced cofactor input                   | Needed for subpower loss                 | Not needed                                    |
| Dependence on coefficient phases          | None                                     | Essential under the current coefficient class |
| Pre-terminal smooth provenance            | Not automatic                            | Not automatic                                 |

Thus [\[eq:tpc56-literal-pentant-lower\]](TPC-056.md#ref-83e365603ebc96ef) improves the retained-energy constant over rank selection at the *adaptive selected* grouping gate, at the price of coefficient adaptivity. It removes the multiplicity cost there, but does not return the deleted four pentants to the full physical output.

# Adaptive necessity and the pre-terminal phase gate

The constant in [\[thm:tpc56-literal-pentant-extraction\]](TPC-056.md#ref-a657bbaabbb33966) does not come from a lower singular value of the full grouping map. It comes from using the distinguished vector to choose a favorable coordinate cone. We now show exactly why that dependence is necessary for the universal unrestricted-array guarantee, record the corresponding local fiberwise obstruction in the bounded literal class, and state a sufficient condition for replacing it by a finite fixed pre-terminal library.

## The sharp coefficient-independent alternative

Let $\Sigma$ be the abstract grouping map [\[eq:tpc56-abstract-grouping\]](TPC-056.md#ref-7d4205b0f4625dcd). For a fixed set $S\subseteq\mathcal U$, write $P_S$ for its coordinate projection. Here $S$ is chosen from the fiber geometry and is independent of the input vector.

The following is the unweighted specialization of the TPC–55 coordinate-transversal criterion, recalled here for comparison [`\citep{WangTPC55}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-056.md#cite-3087863e74e38aea).

###### ref-5eda4a2469de5617

###### ref-735f840cf5ab87ed

###### ref-4ecbca42707c3b73

> **Theorem: Fixed-selector dichotomy** \[thm:tpc56-fixed-selector-dichotomy\] The following are equivalent:
>
> 1.  There exists $c>0$ such that $$\|\Sigma P_Sx\|_2^2\ge c\|P_Sx\|_2^2   \qquad(x\in\mathbb C^{\mathcal U}).   \label{eq:tpc56-fixed-selector-lower}$$
>
> 2.  Every fiber contains at most one selected coordinate: $$|S\cap\mathcal F_y|\le1\qquad(y\in\mathcal Y).   \label{eq:tpc56-fixed-selector-transversal}$$
>
> When these conditions hold, [\[eq:tpc56-fixed-selector-lower\]](TPC-056.md#ref-735f840cf5ab87ed) is an identity with $c=1$. If they fail, the restricted map has a nonzero two-coordinate kernel vector.

> **Proof** If [\[eq:tpc56-fixed-selector-transversal\]](TPC-056.md#ref-4ecbca42707c3b73) holds, every selected output is either zero or one input coordinate, hence $\|\Sigma P_Sx\|_2=\|P_Sx\|_2$. Conversely, suppose $u,v\in S\cap\mathcal F_y$ are distinct. Take $x_u=1$, $x_v=-1$, and all other coordinates zero. Then $P_Sx=x\ne0$ but $\Sigma P_Sx=0$, so no positive $c$ is possible.

Thus a single coefficient-independent selector with a uniform lower bound on the unrestricted coefficient space is exactly a transversal. The pentant theorem escapes this dichotomy because its coordinate set changes with the realized phases. It retains a constant fraction of the energy without claiming a lower bound for all vectors on the same selected set.

## Realization of the local collision in the literal class

###### ref-0344709c9f394cc1

The abstract kernel direction is compatible with the source hypothesis actually inherited by the literal packet. Factor the remaining amplitude [\[eq:tpc56-remaining-amplitude\]](TPC-056.md#ref-65a746f398c744ef) as $$b_u=\kappa_u\zeta_{m_u},  \label{eq:tpc56-baseline-times-zeta}$$ where $\kappa_u$ is the product of every displayed factor other than $\zeta_{m_u}$. Call a terminal atom *baseline nondegenerate* when $\Omega_{q(u)}\kappa_u\ne0$.

###### ref-f2843dd4013a95e3

###### ref-2a02501b79d2ad7b

###### ref-04ec7e9b8fe94c15

> **Proposition: Literal two-atom saturation** \[prop:tpc56-literal-two-atom-saturation\] Suppose $C>0$ and one exact physical-capable fiber $\mathcal F_y^{\rm term}$ contains two distinct baseline-nondegenerate atoms $u_1,u_2$. In the inherited coefficient class $|\zeta_m|\le C$, there are coefficient choices for which the restriction of that fiber to $\{u_1,u_2\}$ has positive terminal diagonal and zero coherent output. More precisely, for a sufficiently small $t\ne0$, one may take $$\zeta_{m_{u_1}}=\frac{t}{\kappa_{u_1}},  \qquad  \zeta_{m_{u_2}}=-\frac{t}{\kappa_{u_2}},  \label{eq:tpc56-cancelling-zeta-choice}$$ and set the source factors belonging to every other row in this fiber to zero. Then $$D_y=2|\Omega_y t|^2>0,  \qquad  C_y=0.  \label{eq:tpc56-literal-local-cancellation}$$ Here $D_y,C_y$ refer to the resulting fiber, with all its other rows zeroed.

> **Proof** Distinct terminal atoms in a fixed fiber have distinct rows. Indeed, the row determines $d$, and fixed $s=dr$ then determines $r$; fixed $(j,\gamma)$ would otherwise give the same input. Thus the two values of $\zeta_m$ in [\[eq:tpc56-cancelling-zeta-choice\]](TPC-056.md#ref-2a02501b79d2ad7b) are independent coordinates. Choose $|t|$ small enough that both values have modulus at most $C$. Equations [\[eq:tpc56-baseline-times-zeta\]](TPC-056.md#ref-0344709c9f394cc1) and [\[eq:tpc56-physical-gauge-factorization\]](TPC-056.md#ref-5407b0f1eec523db) give terminal amplitudes $\Omega_yt$ and $-\Omega_yt$, proving [\[eq:tpc56-literal-local-cancellation\]](TPC-056.md#ref-04ec7e9b8fe94c15).

The proposition is a no-go statement for a uniform *fiberwise* lower bound over the inherited bounded coefficient class. It does not assert that the distinguished inherited coefficients realize these choices, that such a two-atom fiber carries global energy, or that other physical output fibers vanish. Those stronger conclusions would require additional support information.

Prime-resolved injectivity, common Möbius phase, a nonzero terminal weight, and even multiplicity two therefore do not imply positive coherence for arbitrary bounded source phases. This is a coefficient space obstruction, not a proof that the intended arithmetic vector cancels.

## A finite fixed phase-provenance criterion

###### ref-cba9e3efc681bfcf

There is a precise way in which the adaptive selector could become compatible with an earlier source decomposition. For an integer $M\ge2$, let $$\mathcal U^{\rm pre}=\bigsqcup_{k=0}^{M-1}\mathcal A_k  \label{eq:tpc56-preterminal-phase-partition}$$ be a partition chosen independently of terminal primality and independently of the numerical values of the source coefficients. Its coordinate projections are fixed before $D_{h_0}$ is applied.

###### ref-4ff9a332f99ca1b3

###### ref-e78c21a5e7cf0912

###### ref-8fc21265a1d05bc8

> **Theorem: Pre-terminal phase-realization criterion** \[thm:tpc56-preterminal-phase-realization\] Let $g\in\mathbb C^{\mathcal U^{\rm pre}}$ and put $x=D_{h_0}g$. Suppose $0\le\theta<\pi/2$, and there are angles $\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_{M-1}$ and unit output gauges $\omega_y$, all fixed from coefficient-independent pre-terminal and output data before the realized numerical source phases are seen. In particular, neither the angles nor the gauges may be chosen from the realized phases of $x$. Suppose every nonzero terminal amplitude in $\mathcal A_k\cap\mathcal F_y^{\rm term}$ satisfies $$\operatorname{dist}_{\mathbb T}  \left(\operatorname{Arg}(\overline{\omega_y}x_u),\alpha_k\right)  \le\theta.  \label{eq:tpc56-preterminal-sector-hypothesis}$$ Then one of the *fixed* pre-terminal cells, chosen by the least-index energy tie rule after terminal activation, satisfies $$\boxed{  \|\Sigma D_{h_0}P_{\mathcal A_{k_*}}g\|_2^2  \ge\frac{\cos^2\theta}{M}\|D_{h_0}g\|_2^2.}  \label{eq:tpc56-preterminal-sector-lower}$$ The same statement holds after restriction to a declared output-fiber family, relative to its terminal diagonal energy.

> **Proof** The fixed cells partition the full pre-terminal coordinates and hence their terminal diagonal energies sum to $\|D_{h_0}g\|_2^2$. One cell retains at least an $M^{-1}$ fraction. On each output fiber its surviving amplitudes obey the acute-sector hypothesis [\[eq:tpc56-preterminal-sector-hypothesis\]](TPC-056.md#ref-e78c21a5e7cf0912). The real-projection proof of [\[eq:tpc56-fiber-sector-proof\]](TPC-056.md#ref-9896bfcdb7c19276) gives a factor $\cos^2\theta$.

This theorem separates two kinds of adaptivity. Choosing one member of a fixed finite library by energy pigeonhole is harmless at the exponent level. Defining the library itself from the realized terminal phases, as in [\[thm:tpc56-literal-pentant-extraction\]](TPC-056.md#ref-a657bbaabbb33966), is a new post-terminal operation.

The row provenance in TPC–25 permits fixed residue characters and unit-modulus factors inside $\zeta_m$ [`\citep{WangTPC25}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-056.md#cite-39e8dc14c55a2411). The interface inherited by TPC–53–55, however, records only $|\zeta_m|\ll1$. It does not prove that the distinguished $\zeta_m$ phases are determined by a fixed finite residue partition. Even if they are, one must also select sign-stable cells for every source and profile factor in [\[eq:tpc56-remaining-amplitude\]](TPC-056.md#ref-65a746f398c744ef) and prove that those cells carry the required terminal diagonal energy. Geometric nonempty support alone is insufficient by the concentration obstruction of TPC–54 [`\citep{WangTPC54}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-056.md#cite-6f1a8ec95b6b34b5).

Accordingly, [\[thm:tpc56-preterminal-phase-realization\]](TPC-056.md#ref-4ff9a332f99ca1b3) is the exact next positive criterion, not a property silently attributed to the current packet.

# Why averaged phases and projective layers do not close the gate

The sector theorem is pointwise in the one physical terminal vector. Two nearby routes produce legitimate positive averages but do not imply a lower bound for the prescribed undeleted output. We record the elementary non-implications because both have appeared upstream in the TPC decomposition.

## Shift averages do not select the prescribed shift

Write $e(t)=e^{2\pi i t}$, and give $\mathbb T$ normalized Haar measure. Let $\mathscr H\subset\mathbb Z$ be a finite set of intercepts containing $h_0$, and let $F_h$ be vectors in an arbitrary Hilbert space. A completed frozen family may satisfy a lower estimate for $\sum_{h\in\mathscr H}\|F_h\|^2$, or equivalently for a Fourier integral. That is not a pointwise estimate at $h_0$.

###### ref-ecb922fff34e5a34

###### ref-c72731e9bd6b3089

###### ref-85de3ab38ba25a7f

> **Proposition: Sharp fixed-member nonlocalization** \[prop:tpc56-fixed-member-nonlocalization\] Assume $|\mathscr H|\ge2$ and let $w_h\ge0$ be weights with $w_{h_1}>0$ for some $h_1\ne h_0$. There is no positive constant $c$, depending only on the weights, such that $$\|F_{h_0}\|^2  \ge c\frac{\sum_{h\in\mathscr H}w_h\|F_h\|^2}               {\sum_{h\in\mathscr H}w_h}  \label{eq:tpc56-false-average-localization}$$ for all vector families. The same failure holds when the right-hand side is represented by Parseval as the $L^2$-energy of $$\widehat F_w(\alpha)  =\sum_{h\in\mathscr H}\sqrt{w_h}\,F_he(h\alpha).  \label{eq:tpc56-frozen-fourier-family}$$

###### ref-279f24a6e6813568

> **Proof** Take $F_{h_0}=0$, choose $F_{h_1}\ne0$, and set every other member to zero. The right-hand side of [\[eq:tpc56-false-average-localization\]](TPC-056.md#ref-c72731e9bd6b3089) is positive and the left-hand side is zero. Parseval gives $$\int_{\mathbb T}\|\widehat F_w(\alpha)\|^2\,d\alpha  =\sum_{h\in\mathscr H}w_h\|F_h\|^2,  \label{eq:tpc56-parseval-shift-average}$$ so the Fourier formulation has the same counterexample.

###### ref-5b6175ab1dd65c40

Consequently, a completed-family theorem transfers to the physical member only if it is already uniform pointwise in $h$, or if one separately proves a localization estimate such as $$\|F_{h_0}\|^2  \ge X^{-\lambda_{\rm loc}+o(1)}  \frac{\sum_hw_h\|F_h\|^2}{\sum_hw_h}.  \label{eq:tpc56-required-shift-localization}$$ TPC–48 proves that its frozen coefficient equals the active coefficient at the one original intercept $h_0$ [`\citep{WangTPC48}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-056.md#cite-b2c88d6d47db0c74). For $h\ne h_0$, the support and coefficients remain frozen from $h_0$; they are algebraic extensions, not newly reconstructed physical packets. Thus an average over that family is useful only with its frozen-family label until [\[eq:tpc56-required-shift-localization\]](TPC-056.md#ref-5b6175ab1dd65c40) is supplied.

## Layerwise lower squares do not survive signed reassembly

The projective representation also contains a signed or complex reassembly over auxiliary layers. Upper estimates pass through this operation by Minkowski; lower estimates do not.

###### ref-d0d59582874c0f67

###### ref-2313928766f08b67

> **Proposition: Lower-reassembly obstruction** \[prop:tpc56-lower-reassembly-obstruction\] Let $\nu$ be a nonzero finite complex measure on $\Omega$ such that $\dim L^2(|\nu|)\ge2$, and let $Z_\omega$ range in a nonzero Hilbert space. There is no positive constant $c$, depending only on $|\nu|$, such that $$\left\|\int_\Omega Z_\omega\,d\nu(\omega)\right\|^2  \ge c\int_\Omega\|Z_\omega\|^2\,d|\nu|(\omega)  \label{eq:tpc56-false-lower-reassembly}$$ for all layer fields.

> **Proof** Let $\rho=d\nu/d|\nu|$, so $|\rho|=1$ almost everywhere. Since $\dim L^2(|\nu|)\ge2$, choose a nonzero $f\in L^2(|\nu|)$ orthogonal to the constants, and choose a nonzero vector $v$. Put $Z_\omega=\overline{\rho(\omega)}f(\omega)v$. Then $$\int_\Omega Z_\omega\,d\nu(\omega)  =v\int_\Omega f\,d|\nu|=0,  \qquad  \int_\Omega\|Z_\omega\|^2\,d|\nu|(\omega)>0.$$

###### ref-3c8a8de3bbbd2d86

A favorable projective layer, or even a lower square in every layer, is therefore not a lower square for any signed physical reassembly with $\dim L^2(|\nu|)\ge2$. A valid transfer would require a separately proved lower frame estimate of the form $$\left\|\int Z_\omega\,d\nu(\omega)\right\|^2  \ge X^{-\lambda_{\rm reas}+o(1)}  \int\|Z_\omega\|^2\,d|\nu|(\omega)  \label{eq:tpc56-required-lower-reassembly}$$ on the distinguished coefficient field or on a rigorously specified subspace. Such an estimate is not a consequence of bounded total variation.

## The five selected channels still do not lower-bound their sum

The same warning applies internally to [\[eq:tpc56-five-channel-reassembly\]](TPC-056.md#ref-09dde897e4c110f4). The sector theorem proves positive energy before the five channels are reassembled. It does not control the signed cross terms between them. For example, two nonzero channels with values $v$ and $-v$ in the same output coordinate have positive split energy and zero sum. This is compatible with the local literal saturation of [\[prop:tpc56-literal-two-atom-saturation\]](TPC-056.md#ref-f2843dd4013a95e3).

There are three honest ways to use the present result downstream:

1.  retain the adaptive pentant as an explicitly selected physical subpacket and prove that every later operation respects that selection;

2.  verify the fixed pre-terminal phase partition of [\[thm:tpc56-preterminal-phase-realization\]](TPC-056.md#ref-4ff9a332f99ca1b3); or

3.  estimate the five-channel cross terms and prove a lower-reassembly inequality for the undeleted distinguished vector.

An average over auxiliary phases can help with the third route only after a pointwise localization estimate identifies the physical phase.

## Parity boundary

No step above supplies a lower-bound sieve for simultaneous primes. The terminal support is taken exactly as inherited; the sector theorem starts after the primality restriction and after the terminal diagonal is formed. It proves that a constant part of an already activated complex vector can be selected into an acute cone. It does not prove that the vector is activated often, that the undeleted phases align, or that a fixed even gap occurs infinitely often. In particular, the result is compatible with the classical parity obstruction [`\citep{FriedlanderIwaniec2010}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-056.md#cite-c8acfd6f8b1ff9cf) and is not reported as a parity-sensitive advance.

# Endpoint ledger, audit, and the next gate

## The selected coherence exponent

###### ref-a6a9dffb4379a862

###### ref-750be71d281692ff

###### ref-801f07760742e7b8

The inherited endpoint scales are $$Q=X^{267/400+o(1)},  \qquad  J=X^{133/400+o(1)},  \qquad  D=X^{10049/52500+o(1)},  \label{eq:tpc56-endpoint-QJD}$$ and hence $$L=Q/D=X^{99979/210000+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc56-endpoint-L}$$ The constant $c_5=(3+\sqrt5)/40$ in [\[eq:tpc56-literal-pentant-lower\]](TPC-056.md#ref-83e365603ebc96ef) is independent of every scale. Therefore the adaptive selected grouping gate has $$\lambda_{\rm coh}^{\rm sec}=0  \label{eq:tpc56-zero-sector-exponent}$$ relative to terminal diagonal energy. Unlike the coefficient-blind prime-rank certificate in TPC–55, this statement has no factor $1+L/R$ and remains constant even at $R\asymp J$. Its price is structural adaptivity, not a hidden polynomial exponent.

###### ref-b3f1f23fad654b2f

###### ref-9a3342a9c627cb35

###### ref-51cd4641e4062cef

To place the theorem in the full prospective chain, suppose a parent physical atomic energy is first restricted to a compatible packet and a cofactor block at costs $$X^{-\lambda_{\rm sel}+o(1)},  \qquad  X^{-\lambda_{\rm blk}+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc56-upstream-selection-losses}$$ Suppose terminal activation and any restriction to a declared output-fiber family cost $$X^{-\lambda_{\rm term}+o(1)},  \qquad  X^{-\lambda_{\rm fib}+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc56-activation-coverage-losses}$$ Then [\[cor:tpc56-activation-coverage-transfer\]](TPC-056.md#ref-5d925617574c014a) gives the selected fixed-shift residual-output exponent $$\lambda_{\rm sel}+\lambda_{\rm blk}  +\lambda_{\rm term}+\lambda_{\rm fib},  \label{eq:tpc56-selected-ledger}$$ with no added positive-power coherence cost.

Formula [\[eq:tpc56-selected-ledger\]](TPC-056.md#ref-51cd4641e4062cef) is valid only for the explicitly adaptive terminal subpacket. It may enter the eventual physical proof chain only if later localization, Gram comparison, boundary truncation, and tail reconstruction preserve that coordinate selection, or if the selector is realized by the fixed pre-terminal criterion of [\[thm:tpc56-preterminal-phase-realization\]](TPC-056.md#ref-4ff9a332f99ca1b3). This is a logical gate, not an exponent silently set to zero.

###### ref-7c383e807e4bb831

###### ref-06205263f3f1a494

For the undeleted physical output, one must instead prove $$\mathcal G_{h_0}(g)  \ge X^{-\lambda_{\rm coh}+o(1)}\mathcal D_{h_0}(g).  \label{eq:tpc56-full-coherence-hypothesis}$$ The present paper does not prove [\[eq:tpc56-full-coherence-hypothesis\]](TPC-056.md#ref-7c383e807e4bb831). If a route uses a completed frozen shift family or signed projective layers, it must also charge $\lambda_{\rm loc}$ and $\lambda_{\rm reas}$ from estimates of the forms [\[eq:tpc56-required-shift-localization\]](TPC-056.md#ref-5b6175ab1dd65c40) and [\[eq:tpc56-required-lower-reassembly\]](TPC-056.md#ref-3c8a8de3bbbd2d86). The complete prospective undeleted ledger is therefore $$\boxed{  \begin{aligned}  &\lambda_{\rm sel}+\lambda_{\rm blk}  +\lambda_{\rm term}+\lambda_{\rm fib}+\lambda_{\rm coh}  +\lambda_{\rm loc}+\lambda_{\rm reas}\\  &\hspace{23mm}  +\lambda_{\rm Gram}+\lambda_{\rm bdry}+\lambda_{\rm tail}  \le\frac1{400}.  \end{aligned}}  \label{eq:tpc56-full-endpoint-budget}$$ For a direct pointwise theorem at the already reassembled physical $h_0$, no localization or projective-reassembly gate is inserted. At equality in [\[eq:tpc56-full-endpoint-budget\]](TPC-056.md#ref-06205263f3f1a494), a further error of size $X^{-1/400+o(1)}$ need not be absorbable; strict slack or a closed constant-level comparison remains the safe endpoint condition.

## Six-point physical audit

The proof satisfies the following interface audit.

1.  *Literal coefficients.* The source weight, bounded complex factor, binary mask, four profiles, cofactor sign, and shaped terminal logarithm are all displayed in [\[eq:tpc56-source-coefficient\]](TPC-056.md#ref-a2d791a3618992c4)– [\[eq:tpc56-terminal-diagonal\]](TPC-056.md#ref-9bfb5e126b3b3826). Sector membership is formed from their product, not from a proxy coefficient.

2.  *Fixed physical shift.* Every terminal equality uses the one specified $h_0$. Frozen intercepts appear only in the nonlocalization warning of [6](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-056.md#src-e5500a132d60d12f).

The pentants are the five fixed equal arcs, the common output gauge is [\[eq:tpc56-unit-output-gauge\]](TPC-056.md#ref-491682cdfc9f0f7b), and ties use the least index. ## The next gate

Complete within-sector cancellation is now uniformly excluded on a constant-energy subpacket. The next stage should investigate the *undeleted* five-channel problem by routes that are still pointwise in $h_0$. Three concrete quantities are available.

1.  Measure the terminal diagonal carried by singleton fibers. Their coherent square equals their diagonal without any phase hypothesis. Equivalently, bound the energy-weighted collision incidence of multiple fibers.

2.  Use the distinct terminal primes to form an auxiliary prime-modulated polynomial, compute its exact Parseval diagonal, and quantify the microscopic localization needed to return its average energy to the physical phase zero.

3.  Audit whether the actual $\zeta_m$ and sign-stable source profiles admit the fixed finite phase partition in [\[thm:tpc56-preterminal-phase-realization\]](TPC-056.md#ref-4ff9a332f99ca1b3); if not, prove a saturation theorem for every such fixed partition.

Door A is phase-blind but needs arithmetic occupancy. Door B has an exact mean square but a pointwise localization barrier. Door C would convert the present adaptive theorem into a transportable pre-terminal selection. A negative theorem for any door remains useful and must retain its own label.

#### Conclusion.

The literal coherent-prime gate admits a universal constant-energy escape from its collision kernel: five gauge-normalized relative phase sectors suffice, and one global normalized-sector index retains at least $(3+\sqrt5)/40$ of the full terminal diagonal after grouping. The absolute windows may rotate with the fibers. This removes every positive polynomial multiplicity cost from the adaptive selected square. The same theorem also exposes its exact limitation. On the unrestricted coordinate space, uniform coefficient-independent lower selection is possible only on collision-free transversals. The literal two-atom result proves the corresponding local fiberwise obstruction, not a global coefficient-class no-go. The undeleted five-channel interference, terminal activation, fixed-shift localization, native-Gram comparison, boundary, and tail gates remain open, strictly before any parity-sensitive or twin-prime conclusion.
