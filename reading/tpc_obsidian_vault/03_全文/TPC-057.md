## Abstract

The literal fixed-shift residual output is a coherent sum over distinct terminal primes inside each complete fiber $y=(\gamma,s,j)$. TPC–56 extracted a constant-energy phase sector, but that post-terminal adaptive selection did not lower-bound the undeleted output. We develop a phase-blind route that acts on no selected coordinates: the terminal diagonal carried by actual singleton fibers is already an exact positive part of the full grouped square.

For an arbitrary finite fiber system, let $D$ be the diagonal energy, $G$ the undeleted grouped energy, and $D_1$ the energy on fibers with exactly one active coordinate. We prove $$G\ge D_1\ge(D-I_1)_+,$$ where the rooted collision incidence is $$I_1=\sum_u|x_u|^2\bigl(n_{q(u)}-1\bigr).$$ More generally, rooted collision moments $I_r=\sum_u|x_u|^2\binom{n_{q(u)}-1}{r}$ give an exact finite inclusion–exclusion formula for $D_1$ and sharp Bonferroni bounds. Thus the sufficient estimate $I_1\le(1-\delta)D$ yields the undeleted lower square $G\ge\delta D$, without a transversal, a phase sector, or a monotonic return from a selected packet. A core cofactor block can be used directly: every collision partner lies in a fixed enlarged block.

For the literal bounded source class, write each terminal amplitude as $x_u=c_u\zeta_{m(u)}$ and form the baseline weighted row–fiber graph. We compute the sharp coefficient-uniform singleton constant $$\beta_X=\min_{m:W_m>0}\frac{W_{m,1}}{W_m}  =\inf_{\substack{0<D(\zeta)\\|\zeta_m|\le C}}    \frac{D_1^{\rm phys}(\zeta)}{D(\zeta)}.$$ This identifies rowwise singleton occupancy, rather than unweighted global abundance, as the exact uniform gate.

We then parameterize every literal collision. Two colliding factorizations have a unique factor exchange $$d_1=ab,\quad d_2=ac,\quad r_1=ce,\quad r_2=be,$$ with $a,b,c,e$ pairwise coprime and squarefree, and satisfy $$\frac{e}{(e,h_0)}\mid b\ell_1-c\ell_2.$$ Same-divisor collisions disappear for $r>\operatorname{rad}|h_0|L$; high-cofactor cross-divisor collisions require an exchange of two comparable large factors. A fixed row pair and exchange pattern occupies one orbit residue class. Separately, after $(s,j,d)$ is fixed, the source and terminal primes run along two nonproportional affine forms.

These results reduce singleton occupancy to a concrete weighted one-atom versus two-atom incidence estimate with all source and terminal primes retained. The current carrier and coefficient hypotheses do not prove a positive value of $\beta_X$ or a positive singleton proportion. Terminal activation, a parity improvement, and a twin-prime conclusion are not claimed.

# Introduction

The preceding four stages separated the fixed-shift prime square into auditable gates. TPC–53 fixed the literal coefficient dictionary and raw atomic normalization [`\citep{WangTPC53}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-057.md#cite-d85e91fc7a3dbaf8). TPC–54 separated pre-terminal energy selection from actual terminal occupancy [`\citep{WangTPC54}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-057.md#cite-6f1a8ec95b6b34b5). TPC–55 identified the complete residual fiber and proved that adjoining the terminal prime removes every collision before the final coherent sum [`\citep{WangTPC55}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-057.md#cite-3087863e74e38aea). TPC–56 then showed that a phase-adaptive pentant contains a constant fraction of the terminal diagonal and has a uniformly positive grouped square [`\citep{WangTPC56}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-057.md#cite-8eb3ab4efaf1630e).

The last result is exact, but it deliberately deletes four phase channels. It therefore does not answer the undeleted question. If $$x_{y,p}  :=(D_{h_0}G_X^L)_{u(y,p)}  \label{eq:tpc57-intro-amplitude}$$ is the terminal amplitude indexed by the exact terminal prime $p$ in the full output fiber $y=(\gamma,s,j)$, then the two physical energies are $$\mathcal D_{h_0}(G_X^L)  =\sum_y\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_y^{\rm phys}}|x_{y,p}|^2,  \qquad  \mathcal G_{h_0}(G_X^L)  =\sum_y\left|\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_y^{\rm phys}}x_{y,p}\right|^2.  \label{eq:tpc57-intro-two-energies}$$ The first quantity ignores interference; the second is the actual undeleted grouped square. Arbitrary phases can make one nonsingleton fiber vanish. Neither bounded multiplicity nor a large collision-free selected rank prevents the omitted coordinates from cancelling it.

This paper opens a phase-blind door that does not delete any coordinate:

> How much terminal diagonal energy already lies in fibers containing exactly one active atom, and what arithmetic event prevents an atom from being such a singleton?

On a singleton fiber there is no interference to estimate. Its grouped square equals its diagonal exactly, while every other grouped square is nonnegative. Thus singleton energy embeds monotonically in the full output even though a selected block or rank output does not.

The block version avoids another common logical error. Let the root atom lie in a cofactor core $R\le r\le2R$, but count all of its collision partners in the full active support. ## Why unweighted singleton counts are insufficient

###### ref-04629864366e8ed0

The inherited source hypothesis has the form $$x_u=c_u\zeta_{m(u)},  \qquad |\zeta_m|\le C,  \label{eq:tpc57-intro-row-factorization}$$ where $c_u$ contains every literal mask, profile, terminal, and common fiber factor independent of $\zeta_m$. One source row may feed many different fibers. Therefore a large unweighted set of singleton atoms does not give a coefficient-uniform energy proportion: the allowed source energy can concentrate on rows meeting mostly nonsingleton fibers.

###### ref-b7bda2d884d6ce61

###### ref-4c4edd1d97aa92ae

We encode the exact obstruction in a weighted bipartite graph. Its left vertices are source rows, its right vertices are baseline terminal fibers, and its edges are baseline-nondegenerate terminal atoms with weights $|c_u|^2$. If $W_m$ is the total edge weight from row $m$ and $W_{m,1}$ is the part ending in structurally singleton fibers, define $$\beta_X  :=\min_{m:W_m>0}\frac{W_{m,1}}{W_m}.  \label{eq:tpc57-intro-beta}$$ The exact minimax theorem is $$\boxed{  \inf_{\substack{0<D(\zeta)\\|\zeta_m|\le C}}  \frac{D_1^{\rm phys}(\zeta)}{D(\zeta)}  =\beta_X.}  \label{eq:tpc57-intro-minimax}$$ Thus a uniform Door-A theorem is equivalent to a rowwise weighted occupancy statement. If $\beta_X=0$, singleton energy cannot by itself give a coefficient-uniform positive fraction. This does not say that the full grouped energy vanishes, nor does it say that the distinguished source coefficients concentrate on a minimizing row.

The arithmetic side is equally explicit. Suppose two distinct terminal atoms in one fiber have $$m_i=d_i\ell_i,  \qquad  d_1r_1=d_2r_2=s.  \label{eq:tpc57-intro-collision-data}$$ The inherited equality support makes $s$ squarefree and $(d_i,r_i)=1$. Consequently there are unique pairwise coprime squarefree integers $a,b,c,e$ such that $$d_1=ab,  \qquad d_2=ac,  \qquad r_1=ce,  \qquad r_2=be.  \label{eq:tpc57-intro-factor-exchange}$$ The two terminal equations become $$ce\,p_1-ab\ell_1j=h_0,  \qquad  be\,p_2-ac\ell_2j=h_0,  \label{eq:tpc57-intro-exchange-equations}$$ and force $$\boxed{  \frac{e}{(e,h_0)}\mid b\ell_1-c\ell_2.}  \label{eq:tpc57-intro-exchange-divisibility}$$ This is not an ambient divisor model: the reverse statement retains every literal carrier, primality, mask, cutoff, residue, and orbit condition.

The factor exchange gives deterministic sparsity. Same-$d$ collisions are impossible for $r>\operatorname{rad}|h_0|L$. At large cofactor scale, a cross-$d$ collision must exchange two distinct comparable factors of quantified size. After the rows and exchange pattern are fixed, simultaneous integrality occupies one residue class of $j$. In the separate fixed-$(s,j,d)$ description, the source and terminal primes run along two nonproportional affine forms. These are the exact orbit and two-point objects that a future upper-bound sieve would have to sum.

## What advances and what remains open

The advance is a complete reduction of the phase-blind undeleted route: $$\begin{gathered}  \text{terminal activation}  +\text{weighted rooted-collision deficit}  \\  \Longrightarrow  \text{undeleted fixed-\(h_0\) lower square}.  \end{gathered}$$ All collisions in the numerator now have a unique factor-exchange and orbit-ladder description. The remaining estimate compares weighted one-atom terminal energy with a weighted two-atom incidence sum, retaining the two source primes and the two terminal primes in every collision.

The current TPC–53–56 hypotheses do not imply [\[eq:tpc57-intro-needed-incidence\]](TPC-057.md#ref-3e16d5c66e1fb67d). The retained carrier has no declared product-density theorem, terminal activation is itself open, a maximum-degree bound does not force any singleton, and the bounded source class permits rowwise energy concentration. An upper-bound sieve for the two affine prime forms can estimate a numerator, but a positive proportion requires a compatible lower denominator and uniform control of the carrier and weights. For the distinguished vector, put $$x_u:=(D_{h_0}G_X^L)_u.  \label{eq:tpc57-distinguished-terminal-amplitude}$$ If $u=(d\ell,r,j,\gamma)\in\mathcal U_{h_0}^{\rm term}$ and $y=q(u)=(\gamma,s,j)$, define $$\begin{aligned}  \Omega_y  &:={}  \mu(s)\eta_\gamma\mathscr R(q_\gamma)\xi_a  W_2(m_\gamma j/X)\Psi_2(d_\gamma j/K),  \label{eq:tpc57-common-output-factor}\\  \kappa_u  &:={}  (\log\ell)\omega_D(d)\psi_L(\ell/L)K_{m\gamma}  W_1(mj/X)\Psi_1(dj/K)\Lambda(mj+h_0).  \label{eq:tpc57-row-free-terminal-factor}\end{aligned}$$ The Möbius identity in [\[eq:tpc57-terminal-equality-support\]](TPC-057.md#ref-f0a6bdb5b1178f66) gives the exact factorization $$\boxed{  x_u=\Omega_{q(u)}\kappa_u\zeta_m      =c_u\zeta_m,  \qquad c_u:=\Omega_{q(u)}\kappa_u.}  \label{eq:tpc57-exact-row-factorization}$$ Thus $c_u$ contains every displayed literal factor except the bounded distinguished row coefficient $\zeta_m$. ## Hostile interface audit

The following invariants are part of every subsequent statement.

1.  *One literal coefficient.* Collision energies use $|x_u|^2$ from [\[eq:tpc57-distinguished-terminal-amplitude\]](TPC-057.md#ref-4d91ab564c0aeaad), not an unweighted count and not a coefficient envelope.

2.  *One fixed shift.* The equality is always $p_ur_u-m_uj=h_0$ for the same specified $h_0\ne0$. No average over shifts, frozen intercept, or shift-dependent reselection is part of the interface.

3.  *Three different supports.* The containments $$\mathcal U_{h_0}^{\rm phys}         \subseteq\mathcal U_{h_0}^{\rm term}         \subseteq\mathcal U^{\rm pre}$$ are not identities. In particular, ambient solutions of the determinant equation are not automatically terminal or physical atoms.

4.  *Complete fibers.* Singleton multiplicity uses the full key $q(u)=(\gamma,d_mr,j)$ and every active terminal atom. Restricting a cofactor block, phase sector, rank, or prime interval changes the fiber system and cannot be undone by monotonicity of a coherent square.

5.  *Prime resolution.* The refined key $\widetilde q$ is injective, but the physical output forgets $p_u$ and coherently adds all primes in one $q$-fiber. The prime-resolved diagonal therefore does not itself lower-bound the undeleted grouped output.

6.  *Raw ledger.* Repeated cofactor labels remain separate counting-measure atoms. Selection, cofactor-block occupancy, terminal activation, singleton occupancy, native-Gram comparison, boundary control, and tail reconnection must each be charged separately before any endpoint conclusion is stated.

Accordingly, the literal interface supplies exact identities and support geometry only. It does not assert that a chosen block is nonempty, that terminal diagonal energy is comparable to pre-terminal energy, that a positive fraction of physical fibers are singletons, or that the undeleted grouped square has an asymptotic lower bound.

# Singleton energy and collision moments

This section isolates the finite-dimensional statement needed for the undeleted terminal output. No arithmetic, phase hypothesis, or choice of a subpacket is used. Let $\mathcal U$ and $\mathcal Y$ be finite sets, let $q:\mathcal U\to\mathcal Y$ be a fiber map, and write $$\mathcal F_y:=q^{-1}(y).  \label{eq:tpc57-abstract-fiber}$$ For $x=(x_u)_{u\in\mathcal U}\in\mathbb C^{\mathcal U}$, its active support and its active fiber multiplicities are $$\mathcal U(x):=\{u\in\mathcal U:x_u\ne0\},  \qquad  n_y(x):=|\mathcal F_y\cap\mathcal U(x)|.  \label{eq:tpc57-active-multiplicity}$$ The diagonal and undeleted grouped energies are $$D(x):=\sum_{u\in\mathcal U}|x_u|^2,  \qquad  G(x):=\sum_{y\in\mathcal Y}        \left|\sum_{u\in\mathcal F_y}x_u\right|^2.  \label{eq:tpc57-diagonal-grouped-energies}$$ Define the physical singleton energy by $$D_1(x):=  \sum_{\substack{y\in\mathcal Y\\n_y(x)=1}}  \sum_{u\in\mathcal F_y}|x_u|^2.  \label{eq:tpc57-singleton-energy}$$ The word “physical” here refers to the active support of the one vector $x$: a fiber that becomes a singleton because some of its ambient coordinates vanish is counted in [\[eq:tpc57-singleton-energy\]](TPC-057.md#ref-e36752fa38e38e2c).

###### ref-7fef593bc638300d

###### ref-e5945bd328c29f42

> **Theorem: Undeleted singleton floor** \[thm:tpc57-singleton-floor\] For every finite fiber system and every $x\in\mathbb C^{\mathcal U}$, $$\boxed{G(x)\ge D_1(x).}  \label{eq:tpc57-singleton-floor}$$ This is a lower bound for the complete grouped vector; no coordinate is deleted before grouping.

> **Proof** If $n_y(x)=1$, there is a unique $u_y\in\mathcal F_y$ with $x_{u_y}\ne0$, and hence $$\left|\sum_{u\in\mathcal F_y}x_u\right|^2=|x_{u_y}|^2.$$ Every remaining summand in $G(x)$ is nonnegative. Summing the displayed identity over the singleton fibers proves [\[eq:tpc57-singleton-floor\]](TPC-057.md#ref-e5945bd328c29f42).

###### ref-b90c9f2a500e9b54

We next encode singleton occupancy by rooted collision moments. Adopt the convention $\binom ab=0$ when $b>a$, and define $$I_r(x):=  \sum_{u\in\mathcal U(x)}|x_u|^2  \binom{n_{q(u)}(x)-1}{r},  \qquad r\ge0.  \label{eq:tpc57-rooted-collision-moments}$$ Thus $I_0(x)=D(x)$, while $$I_1(x)  =\sum_{u\in\mathcal U(x)}|x_u|^2    \bigl(n_{q(u)}(x)-1\bigr)  =\sum_{\substack{u,v\in\mathcal U(x)\\u\ne v,\ q(u)=q(v)}}|x_u|^2.  \label{eq:tpc57-first-rooted-collision}$$ The last sum is over ordered collision pairs, with the energy placed on the root $u$.

> **Theorem: Exact collision-moment expansion** \[thm:tpc57-collision-moment-expansion\] Suppose $x\ne0$, and put $$M(x):=\max_{y\in\mathcal Y}n_y(x).  \label{eq:tpc57-max-active-multiplicity}$$ Then $$\boxed{  D_1(x)=\sum_{r=0}^{M(x)-1}(-1)^r I_r(x).}  \label{eq:tpc57-exact-singleton-expansion}$$ More generally, every admissible odd truncation is a lower bound and every admissible even truncation is an upper bound: $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_{r=0}^{2k+1}(-1)^rI_r(x)  &\le D_1(x)  &&\text{if }2k+1\le M(x)-1,  \label{eq:tpc57-bonferroni-lower}\\  D_1(x)  &\le\sum_{r=0}^{2k}(-1)^rI_r(x)  &&\text{if }2k\le M(x)-1.  \label{eq:tpc57-bonferroni-upper}\end{aligned}$$ In particular, $$\boxed{  G(x)\ge D_1(x)\ge D(x)-I_1(x),}  \qquad  G(x)\ge\bigl(D(x)-I_1(x)\bigr)_+.  \label{eq:tpc57-first-collision-floor}$$

###### ref-97008865ea520e30

###### ref-c156df1db96e3959

> **Proof** For an active coordinate $u$, set $N=n_{q(u)}(x)-1$. The binomial identity $$\sum_{r=0}^{N}(-1)^r\binom Nr  =(1-1)^N  =\mathbf 1_{\{N=0\}}  \label{eq:tpc57-binomial-singleton-indicator}$$ shows, after multiplication by $|x_u|^2$ and summation over $u$, that the right-hand side of [\[eq:tpc57-exact-singleton-expansion\]](TPC-057.md#ref-0c772d3d8468d2bb) equals $D_1(x)$.
>
> For $N\ge1$ and $0\le s<N$, the partial-sum identity $$\sum_{r=0}^{s}(-1)^r\binom Nr  =(-1)^s\binom{N-1}{s}  \label{eq:tpc57-binomial-partial-sum}$$ has the sign asserted in [\[eq:tpc57-bonferroni-lower\]](TPC-057.md#ref-91d80a6a859429ff)– [\[eq:tpc57-bonferroni-upper\]](TPC-057.md#ref-4286fa6febf525c6); for $s\ge N$, the partial sum is already the exact value in [\[eq:tpc57-binomial-singleton-indicator\]](TPC-057.md#ref-97008865ea520e30). Summing again with the nonnegative weights $|x_u|^2$ proves both Bonferroni inequalities. If $M(x)=1$, then $I_1(x)=0$ and $D_1(x)=D(x)$ directly. Otherwise the case $s=1$, together with [\[thm:tpc57-singleton-floor\]](TPC-057.md#ref-7fef593bc638300d) and $G(x)\ge0$, proves [\[eq:tpc57-first-collision-floor\]](TPC-057.md#ref-78b9fb3cbb8be80f).

###### ref-7710a36493a9cbc8

> **Proposition: Sharpness of the first-moment floor** \[prop:tpc57-first-moment-sharpness\] The nonnegative lower bound in [\[eq:tpc57-first-collision-floor\]](TPC-057.md#ref-78b9fb3cbb8be80f) is sharp using only the two numbers $D$ and $I_1$. More precisely, for every $D_0>0$ and $I_0\ge0$ there is a finite fiber system and a vector $x$ such that $$D(x)=D_0,  \qquad I_1(x)=I_0,  \qquad G(x)=\bigl(D_0-I_0\bigr)_+.  \label{eq:tpc57-first-moment-equality-example}$$

> **Proof** Suppose first that $I_0\le D_0$. Use one singleton fiber carrying a coordinate of squared modulus $D_0-I_0$, omitting it when $I_0=D_0$. If $I_0>0$, use one further fiber with two coordinates $$x_{u_1}=\sqrt{I_0/2},  \qquad  x_{u_2}=-\sqrt{I_0/2}.$$ The two-atom fiber has diagonal energy and first rooted collision moment equal to $I_0$, and its grouped energy is zero. This proves the claim in the stated range.
>
> Now suppose $I_0>D_0$, write $$\frac{I_0}{D_0}=k+\theta,  \qquad k\in\mathbb N,  \qquad 0\le\theta<1,$$ and use two fibers of degrees $k+1$ and $k+2$, with total diagonal energies $(1-\theta)D_0$ and $\theta D_0$, respectively. Omit the second fiber when $\theta=0$. Within each nonzero fiber, take equal coordinate magnitudes and phases proportional to all roots of unity of the corresponding degree. Each fiber sum is zero. A fiber of degree $n$ contributes $(n-1)$ times its diagonal energy to $I_1$, and therefore the two fibers give $$I_1(x)  =\bigl(k(1-\theta)+(k+1)\theta\bigr)D_0  =(k+\theta)D_0  =I_0.$$ They have total diagonal energy $D_0$ and grouped energy zero, completing the proof.

> **Proposition: Core-to-full collision bridge** \[prop:tpc57-core-to-full-bridge\] Let $\mathcal B\subseteq\mathcal U$, and define $$\begin{aligned}  D_{\mathcal B}(x)  &:=\sum_{u\in\mathcal B}|x_u|^2,  \label{eq:tpc57-core-energy}\\  I_{\mathcal B}^{\rm full}(x)  &:=\sum_{u\in\mathcal B\cap\mathcal U(x)}|x_u|^2        \bigl(n_{q(u)}(x)-1\bigr).  \label{eq:tpc57-core-full-incidence}\end{aligned}$$ Here the multiplicity in [\[eq:tpc57-core-full-incidence\]](TPC-057.md#ref-efa00f0d77dadb73) is computed in the full active support $\mathcal U(x)$, not merely in $\mathcal B$. Then $$\boxed{  G(x)\ge  \bigl(D_{\mathcal B}(x)-I_{\mathcal B}^{\rm full}(x)\bigr)_+.}  \label{eq:tpc57-core-full-floor}$$ Consequently, if $D_{\mathcal B}(x)>0$ and, for some $0<\delta\le1$, $$I_{\mathcal B}^{\rm full}(x)  \le(1-\delta)D_{\mathcal B}(x),  \label{eq:tpc57-undeleted-incidence-criterion}$$ then the complete undeleted output satisfies $$G(x)\ge\delta D_{\mathcal B}(x).  \label{eq:tpc57-undeleted-positive-criterion}$$

> **Proof** Let $$E_{\mathcal B}^{\rm mult}(x)  :=\sum_{\substack{u\in\mathcal B\cap\mathcal U(x)\\n_{q(u)}(x)\ge2}}|x_u|^2.$$ Every active coordinate in $\mathcal B$ not counted by $E_{\mathcal B}^{\rm mult}(x)$ belongs to a singleton of the full fiber system. Hence [\[thm:tpc57-singleton-floor\]](TPC-057.md#ref-7fef593bc638300d) gives $$G(x)\ge D_{\mathcal B}(x)-E_{\mathcal B}^{\rm mult}(x).$$ For every term in $E_{\mathcal B}^{\rm mult}(x)$, the integer $n_{q(u)}(x)-1$ is at least one. Therefore $$E_{\mathcal B}^{\rm mult}(x)\le I_{\mathcal B}^{\rm full}(x),$$ which proves [\[eq:tpc57-core-full-floor\]](TPC-057.md#ref-c585da8b7dec70b7). Substitution of [\[eq:tpc57-undeleted-incidence-criterion\]](TPC-057.md#ref-acf812048860462a) proves [\[eq:tpc57-undeleted-positive-criterion\]](TPC-057.md#ref-5bb2363544e2d560).

# A cofactor-core bridge to the complete physical output

The singleton floor becomes useful only after two support issues are kept separate. First, a cofactor block is a set of input coordinates, whereas the target norm is the complete undeleted output. A lower bound for the grouped norm of the restricted block is not monotone under restoring the omitted coordinates. Second, whether a terminal-admissible coordinate is physically active depends on the distinguished source coefficient. This section inserts both distinctions into the literal fixed-shift map and identifies the exact weighted collision estimate that would close the singleton route.

To state the route without hiding an occupancy assertion, let $\mathcal E_{\rm par}$ be the parent physical atomic energy before selection of the declared literal packet. Define the selected pre-terminal energy and the core pre-terminal energy by $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal E_{\rm pre}(G_X^L)  &:=\sum_{u\in\mathcal U^{\rm pre}}|[G_X^L]_u|^2,  \label{eq:tpc57-selected-preterminal-energy}\\  \mathcal E_R^{\rm pre}(G_X^L)  &:=\sum_{u\in\mathcal B_R^{\rm pre}}|[G_X^L]_u|^2.  \label{eq:tpc57-core-preterminal-energy}\end{aligned}$$ Suppose, along a sequence of scales, $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal E_{\rm pre}(G_X^L)  &\ge X^{-\lambda_{\rm sel}+o(1)}\mathcal E_{\rm par},  \label{eq:tpc57-parent-selection-hypothesis}\\  \mathcal E_R^{\rm pre}(G_X^L)  &\ge X^{-\lambda_{\rm blk}+o(1)}        \mathcal E_{\rm pre}(G_X^L),  \label{eq:tpc57-core-block-occupancy-hypothesis}\\  \mathcal D_R^{\rm core}  &\ge X^{-\lambda_{\rm term}+o(1)}        \mathcal E_R^{\rm pre}(G_X^L),  \label{eq:tpc57-core-terminal-activation-hypothesis}\\  \delta_R  &\ge X^{-\lambda_{\rm sing}+o(1)},  \qquad  \mathcal I_R^{\rm full}\le(1-\delta_R)\mathcal D_R^{\rm core}.  \label{eq:tpc57-core-singleton-fraction-hypothesis}\end{aligned}$$ Then the exact bridge gives $$\boxed{  \mathcal G_{h_0}(G_X^L)  \ge  X^{-(\lambda_{\rm sel}+\lambda_{\rm blk}        +\lambda_{\rm term}+\lambda_{\rm sing})+o(1)}  \mathcal E_{\rm par}.}  \label{eq:tpc57-prospective-activation-ledger}$$

The new singleton gate is exactly $\lambda_{\rm sing}$. There is no additional coherence exponent after [\[eq:tpc57-core-singleton-fraction-hypothesis\]](TPC-057.md#ref-e34aa48008bf759f), because the surviving core energy lies in singleton fibers of the complete output. Native-Gram, boundary, and tail comparisons remain separate downstream operations.

# The sharp rowwise singleton constant

The terminal amplitudes inherited from the literal packet contain one bounded coefficient $\zeta_m$ for each source row. This section gives the exact singleton constant that is uniform over that coefficient class. The result is a finite weighted-graph theorem; no distribution of primes is assumed.

###### ref-b850a15943666b1f

###### ref-80d3e18a3b778195

Let $\mathcal M$ be a finite set of source rows, let $\mathcal Y$ be a finite set of output fibers, and let $\mathcal U^\circ$ be a finite set of baseline edges with maps $$m:\mathcal U^\circ\to\mathcal M,  \qquad  q:\mathcal U^\circ\to\mathcal Y.  \label{eq:tpc57-row-fiber-maps}$$ Assume that a fixed pair $(m_0,y)$ supports at most one edge. Attach to each $u\in\mathcal U^\circ$ a nonzero baseline amplitude $c_u\in\mathbb C$ and the positive weight $$w_u:=|c_u|^2.  \label{eq:tpc57-baseline-edge-weight}$$ The baseline fiber degree is $$d_y^\circ:=|\{u\in\mathcal U^\circ:q(u)=y\}|.  \label{eq:tpc57-baseline-fiber-degree}$$

For a row coefficient vector $\zeta=(\zeta_m)_{m\in\mathcal M}$, set $$x_u(\zeta):=c_u\zeta_{m(u)}.  \label{eq:tpc57-row-factorized-amplitude}$$ Its physical degree is $$n_y(\zeta)  :=|\{u\in\mathcal U^\circ:q(u)=y,\ \zeta_{m(u)}\ne0\}|.  \label{eq:tpc57-physical-row-degree}$$ Define $$\begin{aligned}  D(\zeta)  &:=\sum_{u\in\mathcal U^\circ}|x_u(\zeta)|^2,  \label{eq:tpc57-row-diagonal-energy}\\  D_1^{\rm phys}(\zeta)  &:=\sum_{\substack{y\in\mathcal Y\\n_y(\zeta)=1}}       \sum_{\substack{u\in\mathcal U^\circ\\q(u)=y}}       |x_u(\zeta)|^2.  \label{eq:tpc57-row-physical-singleton-energy}\end{aligned}$$ The row weights and their structurally singleton portions are $$\begin{aligned}  W_m  &:=\sum_{\substack{u\in\mathcal U^\circ\\m(u)=m}}w_u,  \label{eq:tpc57-total-row-weight}\\  W_{m,1}  &:=\sum_{\substack{u\in\mathcal U^\circ\\m(u)=m,\ d_{q(u)}^\circ=1}}w_u.  \label{eq:tpc57-singleton-row-weight}\end{aligned}$$ Assume $\mathcal U^\circ\ne\varnothing$, discard the rows with $W_m=0$, and put $$\beta_X:=\min_{m:W_m>0}\frac{W_{m,1}}{W_m}.  \label{eq:tpc57-beta-definition}$$ The subscript records the intended arithmetic application; the theorem itself is finite-dimensional.

> **Theorem: Exact rowwise minimax constant** \[thm:tpc57-row-minimax\] For every $C>0$, $$\boxed{  \inf_{\substack{\zeta\in\mathbb C^{\mathcal M},\ 0<D(\zeta)\\                    |\zeta_m|\le C\ (m\in\mathcal M)}}  \frac{D_1^{\rm phys}(\zeta)}{D(\zeta)}  =\beta_X.}  \label{eq:tpc57-exact-row-minimax}$$ In particular, every admissible $\zeta$ satisfies $$D_1^{\rm phys}(\zeta)\ge\beta_XD(\zeta).  \label{eq:tpc57-uniform-row-singleton-floor}$$ If $G(\zeta)$ denotes the undeleted grouped energy associated with [\[eq:tpc57-row-factorized-amplitude\]](TPC-057.md#ref-ad41e62e782caf5c), then $$G(\zeta)\ge\beta_XD(\zeta).  \label{eq:tpc57-uniform-row-grouped-floor}$$

###### ref-b646dbda8730a38c

###### ref-528ce2ba1ea77efa

> **Proof** A baseline singleton edge remains a physical singleton whenever its row coefficient is nonzero. Consequently, $$D_1^{\rm phys}(\zeta)  \ge\sum_m|\zeta_m|^2W_{m,1}  \ge\beta_X\sum_m|\zeta_m|^2W_m  =\beta_XD(\zeta).  \label{eq:tpc57-row-minimax-lower-proof}$$ This proves the lower bound in [\[eq:tpc57-exact-row-minimax\]](TPC-057.md#ref-34fb072a5e337d4e). The grouped estimate [\[eq:tpc57-uniform-row-grouped-floor\]](TPC-057.md#ref-8c06e7287995e23e) then follows from [\[thm:tpc57-singleton-floor\]](TPC-057.md#ref-7fef593bc638300d).
>
> Choose a row $m_0$ attaining the minimum in [\[eq:tpc57-beta-definition\]](TPC-057.md#ref-7b850aa2e3884eb9). Set $\zeta_{m_0}=C$, and for every other row with positive weight set $\zeta_m=\varepsilon$, where $0<\varepsilon<C$. Rows of zero weight may be assigned coefficient zero. All baseline edges are then active, so $n_y(\zeta)=d_y^\circ$. Hence the physical singleton energy is exactly the structural singleton energy: $$\frac{D_1^{\rm phys}(\zeta)}{D(\zeta)}  =  \frac{C^2W_{m_0,1}        +\varepsilon^2\sum_{m\ne m_0}W_{m,1}}       {C^2W_{m_0}        +\varepsilon^2\sum_{m\ne m_0}W_m}.  \label{eq:tpc57-row-minimax-extremizing-family}$$ Letting $\varepsilon\to0^+$ proves the reverse inequality and therefore the exact identity [\[eq:tpc57-exact-row-minimax\]](TPC-057.md#ref-34fb072a5e337d4e).

There is an exact rowwise collision-moment form of $W_{m,1}$. Put $$M^\circ:=\max_{y\in\mathcal Y}d_y^\circ  \label{eq:tpc57-max-baseline-degree}$$ and, for $r\ge0$, define $$J_{m,r}  :=\sum_{\substack{u\in\mathcal U^\circ\\m(u)=m}}  w_u\binom{d_{q(u)}^\circ-1}{r}.  \label{eq:tpc57-row-collision-moments}$$ Thus $J_{m,0}=W_m$, and $$J_{m,1}  =\sum_{\substack{u\in\mathcal U^\circ\\m(u)=m}}w_u    \bigl(d_{q(u)}^\circ-1\bigr)  \label{eq:tpc57-first-row-collision-moment}$$ is the baseline rooted collision incidence carried by row $m$.

> **Proposition: Row collision hierarchy** \[prop:tpc57-row-collision-hierarchy\] For every row $m$ with $W_m>0$, $$\boxed{  W_{m,1}=\sum_{r=0}^{M^\circ-1}(-1)^rJ_{m,r}.}  \label{eq:tpc57-exact-row-singleton-expansion}$$ The corresponding Bonferroni truncations alternate around $W_{m,1}$. In particular, $$W_{m,1}\ge W_m-J_{m,1}.  \label{eq:tpc57-first-row-bonferroni}$$ If for some $0<\delta\le1$, $$J_{m,1}\le(1-\delta)W_m  \qquad\text{for every }m\text{ with }W_m>0,  \label{eq:tpc57-uniform-row-incidence-target}$$ then $$\beta_X\ge\delta  \qquad\text{and}\qquad  G(\zeta)\ge\delta D(\zeta)  \label{eq:tpc57-uniform-row-incidence-conclusion}$$ for every admissible coefficient vector with positive energy.

> **Proof** Apply the binomial identity $$\mathbf 1_{\{d=1\}}  =\sum_{r=0}^{d-1}(-1)^r\binom{d-1}{r}$$ to $d=d_{q(u)}^\circ$, multiply by $w_u$, and sum over the edges rooted at row $m$. This proves [\[eq:tpc57-exact-row-singleton-expansion\]](TPC-057.md#ref-87bd251062c73fcd). Its first odd Bonferroni truncation is [\[eq:tpc57-first-row-bonferroni\]](TPC-057.md#ref-b44395ddf6eee8d6). Under [\[eq:tpc57-uniform-row-incidence-target\]](TPC-057.md#ref-aa3dd0c3885a1705), this gives $W_{m,1}\ge\delta W_m$ on every positive-weight row, hence $\beta_X\ge\delta$. The last assertion follows from [\[thm:tpc57-row-minimax\]](TPC-057.md#ref-3d2d6c288ca4ae3d).

For one distinguished coefficient vector, uniform row control is stronger than necessary. The following weighted criterion records the weaker target.

> **Corollary: Distinguished weighted collision criterion** \[cor:tpc57-distinguished-row-criterion\] For every coefficient vector $\zeta$, $$G(\zeta)  \ge  \left(  D(\zeta)-\sum_m|\zeta_m|^2J_{m,1}  \right)_+.  \label{eq:tpc57-weighted-row-collision-floor}$$ Consequently, if $D(\zeta)>0$ and $$\sum_m|\zeta_m|^2J_{m,1}  \le(1-\delta)D(\zeta),  \label{eq:tpc57-distinguished-row-incidence-target}$$ then $$G(\zeta)\ge\delta D(\zeta).  \label{eq:tpc57-distinguished-row-incidence-conclusion}$$

> **Proof** Multiply [\[eq:tpc57-first-row-bonferroni\]](TPC-057.md#ref-b44395ddf6eee8d6) by $|\zeta_m|^2$, sum over $m$, and use [\[eq:tpc57-row-minimax-lower-proof\]](TPC-057.md#ref-b646dbda8730a38c) before its second inequality. This gives $$D_1^{\rm phys}(\zeta)  \ge D(\zeta)-\sum_m|\zeta_m|^2J_{m,1}.$$ Now apply [\[thm:tpc57-singleton-floor\]](TPC-057.md#ref-7fef593bc638300d) and nonnegativity of $G(\zeta)$. The hypothesis [\[eq:tpc57-distinguished-row-incidence-target\]](TPC-057.md#ref-d54cb91c7380a8c0) gives the final claim.

> **Remark: What the minimax theorem does and does not prove** \[rem:tpc57-row-minimax-scope\] The number $\beta_X$ is the exact best uniform constant for the *singleton-energy* route over the declared bounded row-coefficient class. If $\beta_X=0$, the singleton ratio can be arbitrarily small in that class. This does not imply that the grouped energy itself can be made zero: nonsingleton fibers may still contribute constructively. Nor does it give a negative statement about one distinguished coefficient vector. Conversely, a positive value of $\beta_X$ is a finite-system statement; an asymptotic lower bound for $\beta_X$ requires separate information about the baseline terminal carrier.

# Exact factor exchange inside a residual fiber

###### ref-77af44227979283b

We work only with the literal fixed-shift carrier inherited from TPC–55–TPC–56. Fix $h_0\ne0$, put $$H:=\operatorname{rad}|h_0|,$$ and take $X$ sufficiently large that $L>|h_0|$. A terminal atom is written $$u=(d\ell,r,j,\gamma),  \qquad  d\in[D,2D],\quad \ell\in[L,2L]\cap\mathbb P,$$ where $d,r,dr$ are squarefree, $(d,r)=1$, and $$rp-d\ell j=h_0  \label{eq:tpc57-terminal-equation}$$ for the terminal prime $p$, with every retained-row, cofactor, residue, profile, and terminal-support condition imposed exactly as in the literal carrier. ## The factor-swap normal form

###### ref-4a885ba3cfe913bf

###### ref-be995ef4cd24fce7

###### ref-c55caadda3175547

> **Proposition: Unique factor exchange** \[prop:tpc57-factor-exchange\] Let $$u_i=(d_i\ell_i,r_i,j,\gamma),\qquad i=1,2,$$ be two terminal atoms in the same coarse output fiber. There are unique positive, pairwise coprime, squarefree integers $a,b,c,e$ such that $$\boxed{  d_1=ab,\qquad d_2=ac,\qquad  r_1=ce,\qquad r_2=be,  \qquad s=abce.}  \label{eq:tpc57-factor-swap}$$ They are given by $$a=(d_1,d_2),\qquad  b=d_1/a,\qquad c=d_2/a,  \qquad e=s/(abc)=(r_1,r_2).  \label{eq:tpc57-factor-swap-parameters}$$ Conversely, any tuple of the form [\[eq:tpc57-factor-swap\]](TPC-057.md#ref-be995ef4cd24fce7) that satisfies both literal terminal equations and all carrier restrictions gives two atoms in one coarse fiber.

> **Proof** Because $s$ is squarefree and $d_i r_i=s$ with $(d_i,r_i)=1$, every prime of $s$ lies in exactly one of four disjoint classes: in both $d_1,d_2$, in $d_1,r_2$, in $d_2,r_1$, or in both $r_1,r_2$. Their products are respectively $a,b,c,e$, which proves existence, pairwise coprimality, and [\[eq:tpc57-factor-swap\]](TPC-057.md#ref-be995ef4cd24fce7). The four prime classes are recovered from $(d_1,d_2,r_1,r_2)$, so the representation is unique. The converse is immediate after the literal support conditions, rather than only the displayed equations, are imposed.

###### ref-259a38c4b34a054a

The normal form may also be viewed relative to one atom. If $d_1=d$ and $r_1=r$, a different divisor choice is obtained by choosing $b\mid d$ and $c\mid r$ and exchanging these two coprime factor packets: $$d_2=\frac d b c,  \qquad  r_2=\frac r c b.  \label{eq:tpc57-relative-factor-swap}$$ The pair $(b,c)$ is unique once the partner divisor $d_2$ is known.

###### ref-9a4d0cf2bf23ea6f

###### ref-3fcb50bfa40dd0d1

###### ref-91562ae88eaa6148

> **Proposition: Exchange identity and exact divisibility** \[prop:tpc57-exchange-identity\] Under the hypotheses of [\[prop:tpc57-factor-exchange\]](TPC-057.md#ref-4a885ba3cfe913bf), the two terminal equations imply $$\boxed{  e(cp_1-bp_2)=aj(b\ell_1-c\ell_2).}  \label{eq:tpc57-exchange-identity}$$ Moreover, for $i=1,2$, $$(r_i,j)=(r_i,h_0),  \label{eq:tpc57-terminal-gcd-in-factor-section}$$ and consequently $$\boxed{  \frac{e}{(e,h_0)}\mid b\ell_1-c\ell_2.}  \label{eq:tpc57-exchange-divisibility}$$

> **Proof** Substitute [\[eq:tpc57-factor-swap\]](TPC-057.md#ref-be995ef4cd24fce7) into the two copies of [\[eq:tpc57-terminal-equation\]](TPC-057.md#ref-77af44227979283b) and subtract: $$cep_1-ab\ell_1j= bep_2-ac\ell_2j.$$ This is [\[eq:tpc57-exchange-identity\]](TPC-057.md#ref-9a4d0cf2bf23ea6f).
>
> For sufficiently large $X$, one has $(\ell_i,r_i)=1$. Indeed, the prime $\ell_i$ dividing $r_i$ would divide both terms on the left of [\[eq:tpc57-terminal-equation\]](TPC-057.md#ref-77af44227979283b), and hence would divide the fixed nonzero integer $h_0$, contrary to $\ell_i\ge L>|h_0|$. Thus $(d_i\ell_i,r_i)=1$, and reduction of the terminal equation modulo $r_i$ gives $$j\equiv-h_0(d_i\ell_i)^{-1}\pmod {r_i}.$$ Multiplication by a unit modulo $r_i$ proves [\[eq:tpc57-terminal-gcd-in-factor-section\]](TPC-057.md#ref-3fcb50bfa40dd0d1).
>
> Finally $(a,e)=1$, so [\[eq:tpc57-exchange-identity\]](TPC-057.md#ref-9a4d0cf2bf23ea6f) gives $e\mid j(b\ell_1-c\ell_2)$. Since $(e,j)=(e,h_0)$, division by this gcd proves [\[eq:tpc57-exchange-divisibility\]](TPC-057.md#ref-91562ae88eaa6148).

## Same-divisor and cross-divisor separation

###### ref-3d123c00569c30fc

###### ref-14f18fa4bcd8af9c

> **Corollary: Same-divisor collisions require a short cofactor** \[cor:tpc57-same-divisor-short\] Suppose $u_1\ne u_2$ and $d_1=d_2=d$. Then $r_1=r_2=r$, $\ell_1\ne\ell_2$, and $$\frac r{(r,h_0)}\mid \ell_1-\ell_2.  \label{eq:tpc57-same-divisor-spacing}$$ In particular, $$\boxed{r\le HL.}  \label{eq:tpc57-same-divisor-bound}$$ Therefore, on a cofactor block $r\in[R,2R]$ with $R>HL$, a fixed divisor $d$ contributes at most one terminal atom to any coarse output fiber.

> **Proof** In the unique factorization of [\[prop:tpc57-factor-exchange\]](TPC-057.md#ref-4a885ba3cfe913bf), equality $d_1=d_2$ gives $b=c=1$, $a=d$, and $e=r$. Distinctness then forces $\ell_1\ne\ell_2$, while [\[eq:tpc57-exchange-divisibility\]](TPC-057.md#ref-91562ae88eaa6148) gives [\[eq:tpc57-same-divisor-spacing\]](TPC-057.md#ref-14f18fa4bcd8af9c). Since $r$ is squarefree, $(r,h_0)\mid H$, and since two points of $[L,2L]$ differ by at most $L$, the asserted bound follows.

> **Proposition: Large exchanged factors in a balanced block** \[prop:tpc57-large-exchange\] Assume in addition that $L>4D$, that $d_1\ne d_2$, and that $$d_i\in[D,2D],\qquad r_i\in[R,2R]\qquad(i=1,2).  \label{eq:tpc57-common-dyadic-block}$$ Then $$b\ell_1-c\ell_2\ne0  \label{eq:tpc57-nonzero-source-exchange}$$ and the exchanged factors in [\[eq:tpc57-factor-swap\]](TPC-057.md#ref-be995ef4cd24fce7) obey $$\boxed{  b^2\ge\frac{R}{6HL},  \qquad  c^2\ge\frac{R}{6HL}.}  \label{eq:tpc57-large-exchange-bound}$$ Equivalently, both exchanged factor packets have size at least $$\left(\frac{R}{6HL}\right)^{1/2}.$$

> **Proof** If $b\ell_1=c\ell_2$, pairwise coprimality of $b,c$ implies $b\mid\ell_2$ and $c\mid\ell_1$. But $b,c\le2D<L/2\le\ell_i/2$, and the $\ell_i$ are prime. Hence $b=c=1$, contrary to $d_1\ne d_2$. This proves [\[eq:tpc57-nonzero-source-exchange\]](TPC-057.md#ref-e154d63b805c91e3).
>
> By [\[eq:tpc57-exchange-divisibility\]](TPC-057.md#ref-91562ae88eaa6148), squarefreeness of $e$, and the definition of $H$, $$e\le H|b\ell_1-c\ell_2|  \le2HL(b+c).$$ The ratio $b/c=d_1/d_2$ lies in $[1/2,2]$. Thus $e\le6HLb$ and, symmetrically, $e\le6HLc$. Since $r_2=be\ge R$ and $r_1=ce\ge R$, one also has $e\ge R/b$ and $e\ge R/c$. Combining the two pairs of inequalities gives [\[eq:tpc57-large-exchange-bound\]](TPC-057.md#ref-30d8376b15b763f7).

## The exact partner candidate set

Fix a terminal atom $u=(d\ell,r,j,\gamma)$. Define $\mathfrak C(u)$ to be the set of triples $(b,c,\ell')$ for which

1.  $b\mid d$, $c\mid r$, and $(b,c)\ne(1,1)$;

2.  $$d'=\frac d b c,         \qquad r'=\frac r c b$$ are integers in the declared divisor and cofactor supports;

3.  $\ell'$ is in the exact declared source-prime support and the atom $$u'=\left(d'\ell',r',j,\gamma\right)$$ belongs to the literal terminal-admissible universe, including all retained-pair, residue, cutoff, and carrier restrictions.

###### ref-c478178fcf2be570

The terminal prime of $u'$ is not a free coordinate: it is exactly $$p'=\frac{d'\ell'j+h_0}{r'}.  \label{eq:tpc57-partner-prime}$$

###### ref-7a2308ae06c6a9c0

###### ref-70c2836f8a4afd51

> **Proposition: Exact partner parameterization** \[prop:tpc57-exact-partner-set\] The map $$u'\longmapsto  \left(\frac d{(d,d')},        \frac{d'}{(d,d')},\ell'\right)  \label{eq:tpc57-partner-map}$$ is a bijection from the distinct-divisor terminal partners of $u$ in its full coarse output fiber onto $\mathfrak C(u)$. Same-divisor partners are exactly the further source primes on the single determinant ladder with $d'=d$ and $r'=r$.

> **Proof** For a partner $u'$, apply [\[prop:tpc57-factor-exchange\]](TPC-057.md#ref-4a885ba3cfe913bf) with $d_1=d,r_1=r$. It gives the unique divisors $b=d/(d,d')$ and $c=d'/(d,d')$, and [\[eq:tpc57-relative-factor-swap\]](TPC-057.md#ref-259a38c4b34a054a); literal membership gives all the conditions defining $\mathfrak C(u)$. Conversely, every element of $\mathfrak C(u)$ defines a literal terminal atom with $d'r'=dr=s$, the same $j,\gamma$, and hence the same output key. Uniqueness of the factor swap and of the source row proves bijectivity. The case $d'=d$ has $b=c=1$ and is precisely the fixed-divisor ladder.

###### ref-36a747596a804d5a

###### ref-b6f55e890544a7af

Assume now that the root cofactor satisfies $r\in[R,2R]$, and constrain its partners to the same block $r'\in[R,2R]$. Define $$\mathfrak E_R(d,r)  :=\left\{(b,c):\exists\ell'\;    \begin{array}{l}    (b,c,\ell')\in\mathfrak C(u),\\[-2pt]    r'=(r/c)b\in[R,2R]    \end{array}\right\}.  \label{eq:tpc57-same-block-exchange-set}$$ The fixed-divisor ladder spacing gives the deterministic degree estimate $$\#\{u'\ne u:q(u')=q(u),\ r'\in[R,2R]\}  \le  \left\lfloor\frac{HL}{R}\right\rfloor  +\left(1+\left\lfloor\frac{HL}{R}\right\rfloor\right)    |\mathfrak E_R(d,r)|.  \label{eq:tpc57-partner-degree-bound}$$ When $d'\ne d$, every pair counted by $\mathfrak E_R(d,r)$ additionally satisfies [\[eq:tpc57-large-exchange-bound\]](TPC-057.md#ref-30d8376b15b763f7). This is sharper structural information than the untruncated divisor bound $|\mathfrak E_R(d,r)|\le\tau(d)\tau(r)$, although it does not by itself force the candidate set to be empty.

###### ref-f04ecddce63f746a

###### ref-a86a1dfecef6fda3

> **Corollary: High-cofactor structural exclusion** \[cor:tpc57-high-cofactor-exclusion\] Under the hypotheses above, if $$R>24HLD^2,  \label{eq:tpc57-high-cofactor-threshold}$$ then every coarse terminal fiber restricted to $d\in[D,2D]$ and $r\in[R,2R]$ contains at most one atom.

> **Proof** The hypothesis implies $R>HL$, so [\[cor:tpc57-same-divisor-short\]](TPC-057.md#ref-3d123c00569c30fc) excludes a same-divisor pair. For a cross-divisor pair, $b\le d_1\le2D$, whereas [\[eq:tpc57-large-exchange-bound\]](TPC-057.md#ref-30d8376b15b763f7) and [\[eq:tpc57-high-cofactor-threshold\]](TPC-057.md#ref-a86a1dfecef6fda3) give $b^2>4D^2$, a contradiction.

There is also a full-fiber version, with only a harmless change of the constant. It is useful when a core cofactor block is used merely to find energy and no block-restricted output is introduced.

###### ref-b3868cada7d8371b

###### ref-6fc691ae69fbc0a8

> **Corollary: A high-cofactor atom is a full-fiber singleton** \[cor:tpc57-high-cofactor-full-singleton\] Assume $L>4D$. Let $u_1$ be a terminal atom with $d_1\in[D,2D]$ and $r_1\in[R,2R]$, while every possible source divisor in its full literal carrier remains in $[D,2D]$. If $$R>48HLD^2,  \label{eq:tpc57-full-singleton-threshold}$$ then $u_1$ has no distinct terminal partner anywhere in its full coarse output fiber.

> **Proof** Every partner has $r_2/r_1=d_1/d_2\in[1/2,2]$, hence $r_2\in[R/2,4R]$. A same-divisor partner is excluded by [\[cor:tpc57-same-divisor-short\]](TPC-057.md#ref-3d123c00569c30fc). For a cross-divisor partner, the proof of [\[prop:tpc57-large-exchange\]](TPC-057.md#ref-806052e07a293c8b) still gives $e\le6HLb$, while $r_2=be\ge R/2$. Therefore $$b^2\ge\frac{R}{12HL}>4D^2,$$ contradicting $b\le d_1\le2D$.

The last corollary is a support theorem only. In fact, the inherited large-prime carrier has a global cofactor ceiling that is disjoint from this high-cofactor regime; see \[[eq:tpc57-large-prime-r-ceiling](TPC-057.md#ref-5bc19ca827664657),[eq:tpc57-high-cofactor-global-gap](TPC-057.md#ref-8012f6f4428639ed)\]. Thus the corollary is a sharp structural boundary, not an activation mechanism for the present physical packet.

###### ref-6685f1928faa0835

Finally, the factor geometry localizes possible partners up to a constant scale enlargement. If one atom has $r_1\in[R,2R]$, then every atom in its full coarse fiber satisfies $$\frac12\le\frac{r_2}{r_1}=\frac{d_1}{d_2}\le2,  \qquad  r_2\in[R/2,4R].  \label{eq:tpc57-cofactor-saturation}$$ Thus a singleton audit for a core block needs to inspect only its constant-factor saturation, not remote cofactor scales. This observation does not license a lower bound for a block-restricted output to be returned to the full sum; it merely identifies the complete set in which a full fiber partner can occur.

# Orbit congruence and the two-affine-prime reduction

We retain the literal hypotheses and notation of [6](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-057.md#src-a023c92331ed109d). In particular $h_0\ne0$ is fixed, $H=\operatorname{rad}|h_0|$, $X$ is sufficiently large that $L>|h_0|$, and no solution is counted unless it belongs to the exact retained carrier.

## Exact orbit congruence

###### ref-53c53347e1f1a7c1

###### ref-d99eb724e8653cd4

> **Lemma: The terminal gcd is fixed by the intercept** \[lem:tpc57-terminal-gcd\] For every terminal atom $u=(d\ell,r,j,\gamma)$, $$(d\ell,r)=1,  \qquad  j\equiv-h_0(d\ell)^{-1}\pmod r,  \qquad  \boxed{(r,j)=(r,h_0).}  \label{eq:tpc57-exact-orbit-congruence}$$ Since $r$ is squarefree, the common gcd in [\[eq:tpc57-exact-orbit-congruence\]](TPC-057.md#ref-d99eb724e8653cd4) divides $H$.

> **Proof** The literal carrier gives $(d,r)=1$. If the source prime $\ell$ divided $r$, then both $rp$ and $d\ell j$ would be divisible by $\ell$, so the terminal equation would imply $\ell\mid h_0$, impossible for $\ell\ge L>|h_0|$. Hence $(d\ell,r)=1$. Reducing $rp-d\ell j=h_0$ modulo $r$ gives the displayed congruence. Multiplication by a unit modulo $r$ preserves the gcd with $r$, so $(r,j)=(r,h_0)$. The final assertion follows from squarefreeness.

###### ref-0e21099d030c81c4

###### ref-1a965811d584abb3

> **Corollary: Orbit-time sparsity for a fixed row and cofactor** \[cor:tpc57-fixed-row-orbit-sparsity\] Fix $d,r,\ell$ satisfying the literal source and cofactor conditions. In an orbit interval of diameter at most $C_JJ$, the terminal equality can hold for at most $$1+\left\lfloor\frac{C_JJ}{r}\right\rfloor  \label{eq:tpc57-fixed-row-j-count}$$ integer values of $j$. Intersecting with the declared orbit residue class, profiles, or terminal-prime condition can only decrease this number.

> **Proof** By [\[lem:tpc57-terminal-gcd\]](TPC-057.md#ref-53c53347e1f1a7c1), every possible $j$ lies in one fixed residue class modulo $r$. The elementary spacing bound in an interval of diameter $C_JJ$ proves the result.

The congruence is stronger than the necessary statement $(r,j)\mid h_0$: on the literal terminal support the gcd is exactly $(r,h_0)$. It does not assert that the unique residue class contains a retained $j$, or that the resulting quotient is prime.

## A simultaneous two-row orbit class

###### ref-639d943538dd6f1c

Use the factor-exchange notation $$d_1=ab,\quad d_2=ac,  \qquad r_1=ce,\quad r_2=be,$$ with $a,b,c,e$ pairwise coprime. For fixed $(a,b,c,e,\ell_1,\ell_2)$, the two terminal equalities require $$\begin{aligned}  j&\equiv-h_0(ab\ell_1)^{-1}\pmod {ce},\\  j&\equiv-h_0(ac\ell_2)^{-1}\pmod {be}.  \end{aligned}  \label{eq:tpc57-two-orbit-congruences}$$ All inverses exist by [\[lem:tpc57-terminal-gcd\]](TPC-057.md#ref-53c53347e1f1a7c1).

###### ref-31c9196c24f2e87d

###### ref-6cbb0fea518efc81

###### ref-eb7761d73d606c5a

###### ref-6c0b2bddbec247db

> **Proposition: Exact CRT compatibility and joint orbit spacing** \[prop:tpc57-joint-orbit-class\] The two congruences in [\[eq:tpc57-two-orbit-congruences\]](TPC-057.md#ref-639d943538dd6f1c) are compatible if and only if $$\boxed{  \frac e{(e,h_0)}\mid b\ell_1-c\ell_2.}  \label{eq:tpc57-crt-compatibility}$$ When compatible, they define one residue class modulo $$\operatorname{lcm}(ce,be)=bce.  \label{eq:tpc57-joint-orbit-modulus}$$ Consequently an orbit interval of diameter $C_JJ$ contains at most $$1+\left\lfloor\frac{C_JJ}{bce}\right\rfloor  \label{eq:tpc57-joint-orbit-count}$$ simultaneously admissible integer times. A fixed inherited orbit class can only thin this set further.

> **Proof** The two residue classes must agree modulo $(ce,be)=e$. Multiplying their equality modulo $e$ by the available units shows that compatibility is equivalent to $$e\mid h_0(b\ell_1-c\ell_2),$$ which is exactly [\[eq:tpc57-crt-compatibility\]](TPC-057.md#ref-6cbb0fea518efc81). The Chinese remainder theorem then gives one class modulo the least common multiple $bce$, and the spacing estimate follows.

This is an orbit-counting statement, not a prime-counting theorem. Once $j$ is chosen, both terminal candidates are determined, but the CRT does not prove either candidate prime.

## The determinant ladder

###### ref-c8b73314799eb57a

###### ref-088a0c23b5cef593

Fix a coarse output $y=(\gamma,s,j)$ and an admissible divisor $d\mid s$, and put $r=s/d$. The source and terminal primes must solve $$rp-dj\ell=h_0.  \label{eq:tpc57-ladder-equation}$$ Let $$g:=(r,dj)=(r,j),  \label{eq:tpc57-ladder-gcd}$$ where the second equality uses $(d,r)=1$.

###### ref-5f6af91b91724f01

###### ref-8e13d8186d03872f

###### ref-1f40aa565f56d8f0

###### ref-fb77741e85a1a2a9

> **Proposition: Complete nonproportional affine reduction** \[prop:tpc57-two-affine-reduction\] Equation [\[eq:tpc57-ladder-equation\]](TPC-057.md#ref-c8b73314799eb57a) has an integral solution if and only if $g\mid h_0$. If $(\ell_0,p_0)$ is one solution, every integral solution is uniquely $$\boxed{  \ell(t)=\ell_0+\frac r g t,  \qquad  p(t)=p_0+\frac{dj}{g}t,  \qquad t\in\mathbb Z.}  \label{eq:tpc57-two-affine-forms}$$ The two affine forms have nonzero cross determinant $$\frac{dj}{g}\ell_0-\frac r g p_0  =-\frac{h_0}{g}\ne0.  \label{eq:tpc57-affine-determinant}$$ On the literal terminal support, $$g=(r,h_0)\mid H.  \label{eq:tpc57-literal-ladder-gcd}$$

> **Proof** The gcd criterion for the linear Diophantine equation is $g\mid h_0$. Differences of two solutions satisfy $r\Delta p=dj\Delta\ell$. After division by $g$, the two remaining coefficients are coprime, so $\Delta\ell=(r/g)t$ and $\Delta p=(dj/g)t$, proving [\[eq:tpc57-two-affine-forms\]](TPC-057.md#ref-8e13d8186d03872f). Substitution of the base solution gives [\[eq:tpc57-affine-determinant\]](TPC-057.md#ref-1f40aa565f56d8f0). Finally, [\[lem:tpc57-terminal-gcd\]](TPC-057.md#ref-53c53347e1f1a7c1) gives $g=(r,j)=(r,h_0)$, and squarefreeness of $r$ gives the divisibility by $H$.

Let $\mathfrak T_{\gamma,s,j,d}\subset\mathbb Z$ be the exact set of parameters $t$ in [\[eq:tpc57-two-affine-forms\]](TPC-057.md#ref-8e13d8186d03872f) for which

1.  $\ell(t)$ belongs to the declared source-prime row support;

2.  the pair $(d\ell(t),r)$, the orbit time $j$, and the opposite row $\gamma$ satisfy every retained literal carrier condition;

3.  $p(t)$ is in the declared terminal-prime support and its shaped terminal amplitude is nonzero.

###### ref-73b7a3b45b02b536

For terminal-admissible occupancy one does not additionally delete a zero source coefficient; for physical occupancy one intersects this set with the exact nonzero coefficient support. With that convention, the fixed divisor occupancy is the identity $$N_{\gamma,s,j,d}  =\#\mathfrak T_{\gamma,s,j,d}  =\sum_{t\in\mathbb Z}    \mathbf 1_{\mathfrak T_{\gamma,s,j,d}}(t).  \label{eq:tpc57-exact-ladder-occupancy}$$ This definition retains the literal carrier and does not replace it by all prime values of the two affine forms.

###### ref-bc1efbec0005a756

###### ref-23d92e40f4436b70

> **Corollary: Deterministic ladder capacity** \[cor:tpc57-ladder-capacity\] For source-prime support inside $[L,2L]$, $$N_{\gamma,s,j,d}  \le1+\left\lfloor\frac{Lg}{r}\right\rfloor  \le1+\left\lfloor\frac{LH}{r}\right\rfloor.  \label{eq:tpc57-exact-ladder-capacity}$$ In particular, $r>LH$ forces $N_{\gamma,s,j,d}\le1$.

> **Proof** The first affine form in [\[eq:tpc57-two-affine-forms\]](TPC-057.md#ref-8e13d8186d03872f) has spacing $r/g$, and $[L,2L]$ has diameter $L$. The second inequality uses [\[eq:tpc57-literal-ladder-gcd\]](TPC-057.md#ref-fb77741e85a1a2a9). Every further literal condition only deletes parameters.

## What prime input remains

###### ref-f4b00309a7a67570

After the deterministic reductions, actual occupancy on one fixed divisor is a simultaneous-primality problem for the two nonproportional affine forms $$\ell_0+\frac r g t,  \qquad  p_0+\frac{dj}{g}t,  \label{eq:tpc57-two-prime-problem}$$ whose determinant is the fixed nonzero integer $-h_0/g$. The determinant rules out proportional forms and exposes the local congruence data. It does not provide a lower bound for prime values. In particular:

1.  a lower bound on an individual growing ladder is a two-linear-form prime problem and is not supplied by the elementary geometry;

2.  an upper-bound sieve may thin the ambient ladder by logarithmic factors, but it neither proves terminal activation nor compares a collision second moment to the physical terminal energy;

3.  averaging over $d,r,j,s$ may be more tractable than one fixed ladder, but uniformity in the moving coefficients, literal masks, residue classes, and physical coefficient weights requires a separate analytic theorem.

Thus [\[prop:tpc57-two-affine-reduction\]](TPC-057.md#ref-5f6af91b91724f01) is an exact arithmetic reduction of the next gate. It is not a singleton-density theorem, a parity-breaking estimate, or a fixed-$h_0$ prime-pair lower bound.

# Occupancy barrier, endpoint ledger, and the next gate

The preceding sections give exact lower bounds once a positive fraction of the terminal diagonal lies in singleton fibers of the complete physical output. They do not supply that fraction. This section records why the existing support bounds do not imply it, states the precise weighted factor-exchange estimate still missing, and places that estimate in the inherited $1/400$ ledger.

## Why a multiplicity upper bound is not singleton occupancy

The TPC–55 determinant-ladder argument gives an upper bound for the number of terminal atoms in one residual fiber. Such a bound is useful for a post-terminal transversal cover, but it points in the wrong direction for the undeleted singleton route.

###### ref-5dcb2a859c81995a

###### ref-884ae93eea58ea7a

> **Proposition: Sharp occupancy barrier** \[prop:tpc57-occupancy-barrier\] For every integer $M\ge2$ and every $E>0$, there is a finite fiber system and a vector $x$ such that $$D(x)=E,  \qquad  \max_y n_y(x)\le M,  \qquad  D_1(x)=G(x)=0.  \label{eq:tpc57-bounded-degree-zero-singleton}$$ Consequently no positive lower bound for $D_1/D$, or for $G/D$, follows from a finite maximal-fiber bound alone. The conclusion remains true when the maximal degree is $2$.

> **Proof** Use one fiber with two coordinates $$x_{u_1}=\sqrt{E/2},  \qquad  x_{u_2}=-\sqrt{E/2}.$$ Its active degree is $2$, its singleton energy is zero, and its grouped sum vanishes. This is also the case $D_0=I_0=E$ of [\[prop:tpc57-first-moment-sharpness\]](TPC-057.md#ref-f110c23f6f572d71).

The obstruction is not cancellation-specific. Even if both coordinates in every doubleton have the same phase, the singleton energy is still zero. Thus replacing the grouped square by a random-phase heuristic would not prove the required occupancy statement. Likewise, terminal activation $\mathcal D_R^{\rm core}>0$ says only that some terminal energy survives; it does not say whether that energy lies in degree-one or multiple fibers.

The rowwise minimax theorem makes the same boundary coefficient-sensitive. For a declared baseline carrier, the best constant uniform over the bounded row-coefficient class is $$\beta_X=\min_{m:W_m>0}\frac{W_{m,1}}{W_m}.$$ If one positive-weight row has no structurally singleton edge, then $\beta_X=0$. An upper bound on every fiber degree does not exclude this case. Conversely, $\beta_X=0$ is only a failure of the uniform singleton route; it is not a proof that the distinguished grouped vector vanishes.

## The current endpoint scales

###### ref-4a4e64087f58de3a

###### ref-63e5921504737149

###### ref-e7a2854f1a346c7b

The literal scale dictionary inherited from TPC–53 and retained in TPC–55–56 is $$Q=X^{267/400+o(1)},  \qquad  J=X^{133/400+o(1)},  \qquad  D=X^{10049/52500+o(1)},  \label{eq:tpc57-endpoint-QJD}$$ and therefore $$L=Q/D=X^{99979/210000+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc57-endpoint-L}$$ For a dyadic cofactor block $r\asymp R=X^{\theta_R+o(1)}$, the inherited coefficient-blind capacity bound is $$M_X(R)  \ll X^{o(1)}\left(1+\frac LR\right),  \qquad  \mu_{\rm cap}  =\left(\frac{99979}{210000}-\theta_R\right)_+.  \label{eq:tpc57-capacity-exponent}$$

###### ref-15ec4fef12b80e48

The enlarged partner range $[R/2,4R]$ is the union of three dyadic blocks with scales comparable to $R$. Hence $$\max_y d_y^+(R)  \ll X^{o(1)}\left(1+\frac LR\right)  \label{eq:tpc57-enlarged-capacity-bound}$$ under the same polynomial-size residual-label hypothesis as TPC–55. The constant-factor enlargement changes no exponent.

###### ref-2369fbf8d1a122a9

###### ref-54ec25c150c683ea

At the older cofactor scale $R\asymp J$, one has $$\frac LR  =X^{15077/105000+o(1)},  \qquad  \mu_{\rm cap}=\frac{15077}{105000}.  \label{eq:tpc57-old-cofactor-capacity}$$ This support-only exponent exceeds the nominal endpoint allowance by $$\frac{15077}{105000}-\frac1{400}  =\frac{29629}{210000}>0.  \label{eq:tpc57-capacity-endpoint-deficit}$$

###### ref-ab153ac45463755f

###### ref-5bc19ca827664657

###### ref-8012f6f4428639ed

The factor-exchange geometry also gives an exact support theorem in a much higher cofactor regime: by [\[cor:tpc57-high-cofactor-full-singleton\]](TPC-057.md#ref-b3868cada7d8371b), every core atom is a singleton in its complete terminal fiber if $$R>48HLD^2,  \qquad H=\operatorname{rad}|h_0|.  \label{eq:tpc57-endpoint-high-cofactor-test}$$ Here $L>4D$ holds for all sufficiently large $X$ at the declared scales, and $H=X^{o(1)}$ because $h_0$ is fixed. Let $N_+$ denote the inherited maximum positive target size. The large-prime descent imposes the stronger global range restriction [`\citep{WangTPC45}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-057.md#cite-1802a7e8047e595c) $$p>\sqrt{N_+},  \qquad  r=\frac{mj+h_0}{p}<\sqrt{N_+}=X^{1/2+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc57-large-prime-r-ceiling}$$ On the other hand, $$LD^2=X^{180371/210000+o(1)},  \qquad  \frac{\sqrt{N_+}}{HLD^2}  =X^{-75371/210000+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc57-high-cofactor-global-gap}$$ Consequently no terminal atom in the inherited physical carrier can lie in a block satisfying [\[eq:tpc57-endpoint-high-cofactor-test\]](TPC-057.md#ref-ab153ac45463755f) once $X$ is large. The high-cofactor theorem is a valid structural boundary, but its physical activation door is closed for the present packet.

###### ref-741378bbe2b3c595

For comparison, at the older scale $R\asymp J$, $$\frac{R}{HLD^2}  =X^{-55273/105000+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc57-high-cofactor-endpoint-gap}$$ Thus even before using the global ceiling, the old block misses the threshold by a fixed positive power.

For $R\gg L$, the capacity is only $X^{o(1)}$; nevertheless a carrier made entirely of doubletons still has zero singleton energy. Thus moving to a balanced block removes the positive-power multiplicity cost of a transversal cover but does not prove the occupancy estimate needed here. Primality may thin the ambient ladder by logarithmic factors; the existing upper-bound sieve does not turn this support count into a weighted degree-one lower bound.

## The exact missing weighted factor-exchange estimate

###### ref-ca083bc3ac376831

###### ref-fb570f7c4d5f5f66

###### ref-4e2340f40065d6a8

The weakest first-moment statement needed for the distinguished vector is not an unweighted count. By [\[eq:tpc57-exact-weighted-physical-incidence\]](TPC-057.md#ref-8e30f2334128f29c), it is precisely $$\boxed{  \sum_m|\zeta_m|^2    \mathfrak J_{m,1}^{R,+}(\zeta)  \le  (1-\delta_R)  \sum_m|\zeta_m|^2W_{m,R},  \qquad  \delta_R>0.}  \label{eq:tpc57-exact-missing-weighted-estimate}$$ Every pair counted on the left satisfies the full literal system $$\begin{gathered}  u=(d\ell,r,j,\gamma),  \qquad  v=(d'\ell',r',j,\gamma),  \qquad  dr=d'r',\qquad u\ne v,\\  pr-d\ell j=h_0,  \qquad  p'r'-d'\ell'j=h_0,\\  R\le r\le2R,  \qquad  R/2\le r'\le4R,  \end{gathered}  \label{eq:tpc57-complete-factor-exchange-system}$$ with both terminal primes, all squarefree/coprime conditions, the literal mask, every profile cutoff, and the actual row coefficients retained. The energy weight is attached to the root $u$: $$|x_u|^2=|c_u|^2|\zeta_{m_u}|^2.  \label{eq:tpc57-root-physical-weight}$$ Neither the partner count nor the root count may replace this weight without a separate comparison theorem.

###### ref-0aed5b7987d52a12

## Six-point physical audit

The proposed singleton bridge passes the following audit.

1.  *Literal coefficients.* The terminal amplitude is the exact $x_u=(D_{h_0}G_X^L)_u$. Its row-factorized form $x_u=c_u\zeta_{m_u}$ retains the common output factor, source shapes, binary mask, profiles, cofactor sign, and shaped terminal logarithm. The collision statistic is weighted by $|x_u|^2$, not by a proxy counting measure.

2.  *Fixed physical shift.* Both equations in [\[eq:tpc57-complete-factor-exchange-system\]](TPC-057.md#ref-fb570f7c4d5f5f66) use the one specified $h_0$. No averaged or frozen intercept is introduced.

3.  *Physical atomic normalization.* The pre-terminal, core terminal, collision, and grouped energies all use the inherited raw counting-measure atoms. Repetitions in the cofactor label remain distinct, and the terminal multiplier is not hidden in a modified input norm.

4.  *Canonical representation.* Collisions are defined by the complete output key $q(u)=(\gamma,d_mr,j)$, with the terminal-prime coordinate resolved exactly as in TPC–55. The core and enlarged block are deterministic cofactor ranges. No favorable rank, phase sector, projective layer, or ambient prime ladder is selected.

5.  *Actual active support.* The baseline terminal carrier and the physically active carrier are kept distinct. The exact statistic $\mathfrak J_{m,1}^{R,+}(\zeta)$ uses active degrees, while $J_{m,1}^{R,+}$ is explicitly labeled as a support-only majorant. The enlarged-block lemma accounts for every full-output partner of a core root.

6.  *Endpoint budget.* Block occupancy, terminal activation, and singleton occupancy have separate exponents. Native-Gram, boundary, and tail losses remain visible in [\[eq:tpc57-full-endpoint-budget\]](TPC-057.md#ref-3a0ca3456942b398). No support-only multiplicity estimate is entered as a positive singleton fraction, and the high-cofactor support theorem is not used outside its literal range.

## The next gate

The factor-exchange localization leaves one closed structural shortcut and four live analytic or negative routes.

1.  *Closed high-cofactor shortcut.* The support theorem would give $\delta_R=1$ above $48HLD^2$, but \[[eq:tpc57-large-prime-r-ceiling](TPC-057.md#ref-5bc19ca827664657),[eq:tpc57-high-cofactor-global-gap](TPC-057.md#ref-8012f6f4428639ed)\] show that the inherited carrier contains no such terminal atom. This door is closed for the current packet. Reopening it would require a different large-prime decomposition or physical carrier, not merely a new dyadic choice of $R$.

2.  *Distinguished weighted incidence.* Estimate the exact left side of [\[eq:tpc57-exact-missing-weighted-estimate\]](TPC-057.md#ref-ca083bc3ac376831), retaining the root energy $|c_u\zeta_{m_u}|^2$. This is the weakest first-moment target and may exploit concentration of the actual coefficients away from collision-rich rows.

3.  *Uniform row factor exchange.* Prove [\[eq:tpc57-baseline-uniform-row-target\]](TPC-057.md#ref-f8d6e6e8f688da73) by analyzing the coupled system [\[eq:tpc57-complete-factor-exchange-system\]](TPC-057.md#ref-fb570f7c4d5f5f66) for each source row. This would give a theorem uniform over the inherited bounded coefficient class, but is strictly stronger than Door B.

4.  *Higher rooted moments.* If the first-moment truncation is too crude, define core-rooted moments with $\binom{n_{q(u)}^{\rm phys}-1}{r}$. The exact Bonferroni hierarchy of [\[thm:tpc57-collision-moment-expansion\]](TPC-057.md#ref-be8268609f93a313) can then certify singleton energy through an odd higher truncation. Each new moment requires its own weighted factor-exchange estimate.

5.  *Saturation or route closure.* If none of the preceding estimates can hold, construct literal carriers for which the weighted singleton constant tends to zero, or prove that every available support-only estimate is saturated by collision-rich rows. Such a theorem would close the singleton door without asserting cancellation of the distinguished full output.

Door A records a closed structural boundary. Door B is the economical live analytic target. The enlarged block shows that Door B is local in cofactor scale, but the occupancy barrier shows that it is not a consequence of the known ladder capacity bound. A positive estimate, together with core terminal activation, would give the first undeleted fixed-$h_0$ coherent lower square in this route; a sharp negative estimate would identify precisely why singleton occupancy cannot carry the endpoint argument.

#### Conclusion.

The undeleted terminal square has an exact phase-blind floor: every unit of core energy whose full physical fiber is a singleton survives unchanged. All partners of a cofactor-core atom lie in one constant-factor enlarged block, and the resulting obstruction is the explicit coefficient-weighted factor-exchange incidence [\[eq:tpc57-exact-missing-weighted-estimate\]](TPC-057.md#ref-ca083bc3ac376831). The high-cofactor regime is disjoint from the inherited carrier; on its admissible cofactor range, the current support theory bounds how many partners may occur but does not show that a positive weighted fraction has none. Terminal activation, weighted singleton occupancy, native-Gram comparison, boundary control, and tail reconnection therefore remain separate gates, all strictly before any parity-sensitive or twin-prime conclusion.
