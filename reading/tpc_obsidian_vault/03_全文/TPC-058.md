## Abstract

The low-sign average and the physical all-minus corner of a large-prime Walsh packet produce two different Gram forms. The former resolves the low residual label $s$; the latter erases $s$ inside each native output coordinate. We identify the missing map exactly. On the canonical low-Walsh coefficient space it is the signed fiber sum $$(\mathsf E_\mu z)_i=\sum_t\mu(t)z_{i,t},$$ or, after the physical Möbius gauge has already been absorbed, the unsigned erasure $(\mathsf E_1A)_i=\sum_sA_{i,s}$. Its quadratic defect is the complete signed off-diagonal exchange form. Each active output fiber has one bright direction and a dark space of codimension one. Thus any support-only lower comparison fails as soon as two residual labels share a native output; this obstruction is sharp and is not removed by terminal-prime singleton incidence.

For the actual coefficient image $E=\operatorname{Ran}T$, we prove that the optimal uniform native comparison is the least eigenvalue of the compression of $\mathsf E_\mu^*\mathsf E_\mu$ to $E$. We give its exact generalized-Gram and Moore–Penrose forms, a dimension kill test, and the necessary fixed-carrier convention. At one fixed nonzero shift $h_0$, the terminal residual vector from the preceding prime-fiber construction is identified coordinate by coordinate with the gauged high-Walsh lift. The complete native affine column on the declared slice then factors through two independent gates: a residual-label alias constant and a base–high affine noncancellation constant. Neither constant is proved positive at the endpoint. The endpoint very-high face also lies in a smaller cofactor band than the inherited $R\asymp J$ packet, so its activation requires a separate relocation/occupancy theorem. The results are exact L0 linear algebra and L1 interface closure; they do not give a parity improvement or a twin-prime conclusion.

# Introduction

###### ref-1e1272bc38e39975

###### ref-00b1110f26a0403b

The fixed-shift residual program has reached a point at which two nonnegative quantities that look similar must be separated sharply. The very-high-prime decomposition of TPC–45 produces the physical all-minus affine corner $$\mathcal N_{\rm nat}  :=\left\|v_0-\sum_{p>y}v_p\right\|^2,  \label{eq:tpc58-intro-native}$$ whereas averaging the low Walsh signs produces $$\mathcal R_{\rm low}  :=\mathcal D_0-\mathcal D_H    +\left\|\sum_{p>y}x_p\right\|^2.  \label{eq:tpc58-intro-residual}$$ For each individual $p$, the diagonal norms agree, $\|v_p\|=\|x_p\|$, but the cross inner products do not [`\citep{WangTPC45}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-058.md#cite-1802a7e8047e595c). The prime-resolved work in TPC–55–57 addresses the coherent sum over $p$ at a fixed residual label. It does not yet return that residual energy to the native output space [`\citep{WangTPC55,WangTPC56,WangTPC57}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-058.md#cite-3087863e74e38aea); [\[WangTPC56\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-058.md#cite-8eb3ab4efaf1630e); [\[WangTPC57\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-058.md#cite-d87f234f729af8b2).

###### ref-535503542174452e

The missing operation is simple to state and consequential to analyze: $$p\longmapsto(i,s)  \quad\hbox{followed by}\quad  (i,s)\longmapsto i.  \label{eq:tpc58-two-groupings}$$ The first arrow groups terminal primes while retaining the residual label $s$. The second erases $s$ inside the native output coordinate $i$. TPC–57 studies the first arrow. This paper studies the second. In particular, one prime in every $(i,s)$-fiber does not imply one residual label in every $i$-fiber.

## The exact erasure identity

###### ref-f18fa2cec5f15a3f

###### ref-bc1a6b0fe4bf4fb9

###### ref-72d6146199e6a3a3

Fix the baseline active residual labels $\mathcal L_i$ at output $i$, and write $z_i=(z_{i,t})_{t\in\mathcal L_i}$. Once the Möbius gauge is included, the native map is $$(\mathsf E_\mu z)_i:=\sum_{t\in\mathcal L_i}\mu(t)z_{i,t}.  \label{eq:tpc58-intro-erasure}$$ The entire discrepancy between resolved and native energies is $$\|\mathsf E_\mu z\|^2-\|z\|^2  =2\operatorname{Re}\sum_i    \sum_{t<t'}\mu(t)\overline{\mu(t')}       z_{i,t}\overline{z_{i,t'}}.  \label{eq:tpc58-intro-defect}$$ For $k_i=|\mathcal L_i|>0$, the $i$-block has one nonzero singular value $\sqrt{k_i}$ and a $k_i-1$ dimensional kernel. The Möbius signs rotate this decomposition by a diagonal unitary; they do not eliminate the kernel. Hence a support-only lower inequality $$\|\mathsf E_\mu z\|^2\ge c\|z\|^2  \label{eq:tpc58-intro-false-ambient}$$ with $c>0$ is false on the full ambient space whenever any $k_i\ge2$. This negative result is exact, but its quantifier is equally important: it does not show failure on the smaller image traced out by the literal physical coefficients.

## The actual coefficient image

###### ref-29775c791553984f

###### ref-e2080c769dc32384

###### ref-5c2a19a9b5cf0733

###### ref-b4b459d757573e13

###### ref-b3ae9ec3e1e1e20f

Let a fixed baseline carrier be chosen independently of the bounded row coefficient vector $\zeta$, and let $$T:\mathbb C^{\mathcal M}\longrightarrow\mathcal H_{\rm W},  \qquad z=T\zeta,  \qquad E:=\operatorname{Ran}T.  \label{eq:tpc58-intro-T}$$ The best coefficient-uniform comparison is $$\alpha_E  :=\inf_{0\ne z\in E}    \frac{\|\mathsf E_\mu z\|^2}{\|z\|^2}.  \label{eq:tpc58-intro-alpha}$$ In finite dimension it is the least eigenvalue, including zero, of the compression of $\mathsf E_\mu^*\mathsf E_\mu$ to $E$, and $$\alpha_E>0  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad  E\cap\operatorname{Ker}\mathsf E_\mu=\{0\}.  \label{eq:tpc58-intro-transversality}$$ With $$R:=T^*T,  \qquad N:=T^*\mathsf E_\mu^*\mathsf E_\mu T,  \qquad \mathcal S:=\operatorname{Ran}R,  \label{eq:tpc58-intro-two-Grams}$$ the exact relative defect is $$\Theta:=R^{\dagger/2}(N-R)R^{\dagger/2}\big|_{\mathcal S},  \qquad  \alpha_E=1+\lambda_{\min}(\Theta).  \label{eq:tpc58-intro-relative-defect}$$ The restriction to $\mathcal S$ is essential: zero directions in $\operatorname{Ker}T$ must not be counted as physical generalized eigenvectors.

There are therefore three different questions:

1.  the ambient question, which has a sharp dark-space obstruction;

2.  the coefficient-uniform question on the actual image $E$; and

3.  the distinguished-vector question for the one literal coefficient vector inherited at scale $X$.

Failure of (i) does not imply failure of (ii) or (iii). Conversely, a numerically favorable distinguished vector does not prove a uniform bound on $E$.

## The fixed-shift two-stage gate

###### ref-2bf8297759349a02

###### ref-2a7c7f3b62bc5c40

Use the sign convention $$z_H(i,s):=-\sum_{p>y}b_{i,sp},  \qquad  h:=\mathsf E_{\mu,H}z_H=-\sum_{p>y}v_p.  \label{eq:tpc58-intro-high-convention}$$ The TPC–57 terminal residual amplitude already contains the common physical Möbius sign $\mu(s)$. If that vector is denoted by $A$, then the literal interface is $$A=\mathsf U_\mu z_H,  \qquad  \mathsf E_1A=h,  \qquad  \|A\|=\|z_H\|.  \label{eq:tpc58-intro-terminal-interface}$$ Thus no second Möbius factor is inserted when the TPC–57 output is grouped natively.

###### ref-73ba982144fb3da6

###### ref-7aab4fdd17e9146c

For the actual high coefficient image define $\alpha_H$ by the analogue of [\[eq:tpc58-intro-alpha\]](TPC-058.md#ref-e2080c769dc32384). Separately, on the actual paired image of $v_0$ and $h$, define $$\delta_{\rm aff}  :=\inf_{\substack{(b,g)\in\mathcal F_{\rm aff}\\(b,g)\ne(0,0)}}    \frac{\|b+g\|^2}{\|b\|^2+\|g\|^2}.  \label{eq:tpc58-intro-delta}$$ Here $\mathcal F_{\rm aff}$ is the nonzero image of the coupled map driven by the same row vector in both entries, defined precisely in [6](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-058.md#src-1422e6c7cd31b0bd). Then the exact route is $$\mathcal N_{\rm nat}  =\|v_0+h\|^2  \ge \delta_{\rm aff}\alpha_H\|z_H\|^2.  \label{eq:tpc58-intro-two-stage}$$ The constants are independent gates. Residual labels may cancel under native erasure even if the terminal-prime fibers are singletons, and the base vector may cancel the high vector even if native high energy is positive.

There is also an upstream support gate. At the endpoint cutoff $y=X^{267/400+\eta}$ with fixed $\eta>0$, the relation $pr=mj+h_0\le X^{1+o(1)}$ forces $r\le X^{133/400-\eta+o(1)}$. Hence this very-high face is eventually disjoint from the older dyadic block $R\asymp J=X^{133/400+o(1)}$. The interface identities remain exact on their declared face, but they do not prove that the face carries a positive fraction of the inherited energy. A compatible-band relocation and occupancy estimate is therefore charged separately in the endpoint ledger.

## Claim boundary and organization

The finite-dimensional erasure spectrum, compressed Rayleigh quotient, generalized-Gram formula, and counterexamples are unconditional L0 results. The coordinate dictionary at one fixed $h_0\ne0$ is an L1 interface statement on the declared baseline carrier. A positive polynomial lower bound for either $\alpha_H$ or $\delta_{\rm aff}$, relocation to and occupancy of the compatible smaller cofactor band, terminal activation at the endpoint, boundary/tail reconnection, and an estimate for the original arithmetic defect remain open. In particular, no parity improvement and no twin-prime conclusion is asserted; the classical parity boundary remains in force [`\citep{FriedlanderIwaniec2010}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-058.md#cite-c8acfd6f8b1ff9cf).

fixes the two Gram forms and their literal signs. proves the exact erasure spectrum, and [4](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-058.md#src-fd388e77434bb7e4) gives the actual-image and generalized-Gram theorems. The fixed-shift coordinate match is proved in [5](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-058.md#src-a7ea8d33a79eba44); the full affine completion follows in [6](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-058.md#src-1422e6c7cd31b0bd). record the transport restrictions, endpoint budget, kill criteria, and next arithmetic gate.

# Two inherited Gram forms and their common label dictionary

The comparison problem starts with two exact identities from TPC–45, not with two names for the same quadratic form. We restate the identities on one side of the equality column, retain the shaped terminal coefficient, and insert the two successive quotient maps that are needed later. The right side is obtained by interchanging the two source systems; the full column is their orthogonal direct sum. No estimate in this section mixes the two sides before that direct sum is taken.

## The very-high cutoff and the baseline atoms

Fix the one physical intercept $$h_0\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}.  \label{eq:tpc58-fixed-intercept-dictionary}$$ Let $N_\pm,D_\pm,J_+$, and $\Delta_M$ be the support endpoints in the TPC–45 equality packet. The very-high cutoff $y=y_X$ is required to satisfy both inherited conditions $$\begin{aligned}  y&>\max\{|h_0|,D_+,J_+,\Delta_M,\sqrt{N_+}\},  \label{eq:tpc58-cutoff-geometric-condition}\\  \frac{D_+N_+}{y}&<D_-N_-.  \label{eq:tpc58-cutoff-label-separation}\end{aligned}$$ At the endpoint one may take $y=X^{267/400+\eta}$ for fixed $0<\eta<133/400$, but no exponent calculation below uses more than [\[eq:tpc58-cutoff-geometric-condition\]](TPC-058.md#ref-56db4eadd25575b8)– [\[eq:tpc58-cutoff-label-separation\]](TPC-058.md#ref-b594c299c7c706b0).

On the physical equality support, $$pr=mj+h_0\le N_+=X^{1+o(1)},  \qquad p>y,  \label{eq:tpc58-endpoint-product-ceiling}$$ and therefore $$\boxed{r\le \frac{N_+}{y}  =X^{133/400-\eta+o(1)}.}  \label{eq:tpc58-compatible-cofactor-ceiling}$$ For fixed $\eta>0$, this is eventually disjoint from the inherited block $R\asymp J=X^{133/400+o(1)}$. Write $i\in\mathcal I$ for one native output coordinate. On the left side, $i=(L,\gamma,j)$; on the right side, $i=(R,\alpha,j)$. TPC–45 associates to each actually occurring squarefree equality label $u$ a stripped coefficient $b_{i,u}\in\mathbb C$. The word *stripped* means that the Möbius character $\mu(u)$ is not contained in $b_{i,u}$; the literal stripped source amplitude, mask, smooth profiles, and shaped terminal weight are retained. We use the canonical active label set $$\mathcal U_i^\circ:=\{u:b_{i,u}\text{ is not identically zero on the declared  baseline carrier}\}.  \label{eq:tpc58-baseline-native-labels}$$ For one distinguished coefficient vector some entries on this fixed carrier may vanish. They remain zero coordinates; they do not change the definition of the carrier or create a new representation.

The cutoff conditions give the unique factorization $$u=sq,  \qquad P^+(s)\le y,  \qquad q=1\ \text{or}\ q=p\in\mathcal P_y,  \label{eq:tpc58-low-high-factorization}$$ where $\mathcal P_y$ is the set of occurring primes $p>y$. If $q=p$, then $s=dr$ on the literal equality support. For each fixed pair $(i,p)$ there is at most one source row on the chosen side. This last statement is the inherited fixed-$(j,p)$ singleton incidence; it does not say that a fixed $(i,s)$ contains at most one terminal prime.

> **Proposition: Exact two-Gram dictionary** \[prop:tpc58-two-gram-dictionary\] Under [\[eq:tpc58-cutoff-geometric-condition\]](TPC-058.md#ref-56db4eadd25575b8)– [\[eq:tpc58-cutoff-label-separation\]](TPC-058.md#ref-b594c299c7c706b0), the native and residual very-high faces are related by $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_p\|v_p\|^2  &=\sum_p\|x_p\|^2=\mathcal D_H,  \label{eq:tpc58-two-gram-common-diagonal}\\  \left\|-\sum_pv_p\right\|^2  &=\|\mathsf EA_H\|^2,  \label{eq:tpc58-two-gram-native-high}\\  \left\|\sum_px_p\right\|^2  &=\|A_H\|^2.  \label{eq:tpc58-two-gram-residual-high}\end{aligned}$$ For $p\ne q$, however, $$\begin{aligned}  \langle v_p,v_q\rangle_{\mathcal H_{\rm nat}}  &=\sum_i\sum_{s,t}    \mu(s)\mu(t)b_{i,sp}\overline{b_{i,tq}},  \label{eq:tpc58-native-cross-product}\\  \langle x_p,x_q\rangle_{\mathcal H_{\rm res}^{H}}  &=\sum_i\sum_s b_{i,sp}\overline{b_{i,sq}}.  \label{eq:tpc58-residual-cross-product}\end{aligned}$$ Thus the residual Gram retains only $s=t$, whereas the native Gram also contains every $s\ne t$ alias pair sharing the same output $i$.

Equations [\[eq:tpc58-two-gram-native-high\]](TPC-058.md#ref-8cbb6bcd782f0b72) and [\[eq:tpc58-two-gram-residual-high\]](TPC-058.md#ref-44fb81dbb3e626ab) follow from [\[eq:tpc58-high-residual-sign-convention\]](TPC-058.md#ref-0f0373e5bba97f75)– [\[eq:tpc58-high-native-erasure\]](TPC-058.md#ref-d4d83b0a47de71f8). Expanding the two inner products gives [\[eq:tpc58-native-cross-product\]](TPC-058.md#ref-eae9f9aa79e32829)– [\[eq:tpc58-residual-cross-product\]](TPC-058.md#ref-0d844dffc645859b).

The proposition is an exact L1 dictionary for the inherited face. It does not give either inequality between $\|\mathsf EA_H\|^2$ and $\|A_H\|^2$. Nor may the nonnegative base term $\mathcal D_0-\mathcal D_H$ in [\[eq:tpc58-low-averaged-residual-gram\]](TPC-058.md#ref-2c2429653d16f11e) be replaced by $\|v_0\|^2$: the former is obtained after low-sign orthogonalization, while the latter retains the native base–base cross terms.

## The two quotient maps are not interchangeable

###### ref-682f66098304d693

###### ref-76ab0063431d0b0a

For a literal terminal atom write its prime-resolved key as $$(i,s,p),  \qquad i=(L,\gamma,j)\ \text{or}\ (R,\alpha,j),  \qquad s=dr.  \label{eq:tpc58-prime-resolved-key}$$ The two maps in the physical chain are $$\boxed{  (i,s,p)\longmapsto(i,s)  \longmapsto i.}  \label{eq:tpc58-two-grouping-maps}$$ The first arrow coherently sums terminal primes in a fixed residual label; it is the $p\to(i,s)$ grouping studied in TPC–55–57 [`\citep{WangTPC55,WangTPC56,WangTPC57}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-058.md#cite-3087863e74e38aea); [\[WangTPC56\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-058.md#cite-8eb3ab4efaf1630e); [\[WangTPC57\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-058.md#cite-d87f234f729af8b2). The second arrow is the unsigned erasure $\mathsf E$; it aliases different residual labels $s$ in the same native output coordinate. A singleton for the first arrow need not be a singleton for the second. In particular, a lower square proved after the first grouping is an input to the native-alias problem, not its solution.

All displayed sums use raw counting measure on their stated canonical coordinates. Prime-resolved injectivity is used before the first arrow, and the $p$-sum is formed exactly once in each $(i,s)$. Duplicating a prime-resolved atom, adjoining an ambient affine-prime solution, or inserting cancelling null pairs would change the Hilbert space and is not part of this dictionary.

# Canonical residual erasure and its exact spectrum

This section isolates the finite-dimensional linear algebra of forgetting a low label after its Möbius phase has been restored. No arithmetic distribution statement is used. The distinction between a canonical coefficient array and its erased physical image is essential: the first is unique in its declared orthogonal basis, whereas the second generally has a large dark space.

## The block erasure operator

###### ref-6fe6ab1d6537c184

###### ref-60b225bc891c2d3a

###### ref-9777782d3ae66eb6

Let $\mathcal I$ be a finite set. For each $i\in\mathcal I$, let $\mathcal T_i$ be a nonempty finite label set of size $k_i$, and put $$\mathcal Z:=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathcal I}\mathbb C^{\mathcal T_i},  \qquad  \mathcal Y:=\mathbb C^{\mathcal I}.  \label{eq:tpc58-erasure-spaces}$$ Both spaces carry their raw counting-measure inner products. Attach a possibly complex unit gauge $$\bm\mu_i=(\mu_{i,t})_{t\in\mathcal T_i},  \qquad |\mu_{i,t}|=1,  \label{eq:tpc58-unit-mobius-gauge}$$ and define the erasure operator $$(C_\mu z)_i:=\sum_{t\in\mathcal T_i}\mu_{i,t}z_{i,t}.  \label{eq:tpc58-erasure-operator}$$ The classical squarefree Möbius choice is real, but allowing a unitary gauge makes the conjugation in the spectral projector visible.

###### ref-289629bebb7ffbad

For each fiber define $$\Pi_i z_i  :=\frac{\overline{\bm\mu_i}}{k_i}        \sum_{t\in\mathcal T_i}\mu_{i,t}z_{i,t}.  \label{eq:tpc58-bright-projector}$$ Thus $\Pi_i$ is the orthogonal projector onto $\operatorname{span}\{\overline{\bm\mu_i}\}$, not in general onto $\operatorname{span}\{\bm\mu_i\}$.

###### ref-7955d01f61a40ea5

###### ref-dd6d3382ef2d301f

###### ref-e340076a25db966b

###### ref-c53a360f173a96aa

###### ref-bfe87d01b5fb0fe0

> **Theorem: Exact erasure spectrum** \[thm:tpc58-exact-erasure-spectrum\] Let $C_{\mu,i}:\mathbb C^{\mathcal T_i}\to\mathbb C$ denote the $i$-th block of $C_\mu$. Then $$\begin{aligned}  C_{\mu,i}^*C_{\mu,i}&=k_i\Pi_i,  \label{eq:tpc58-fiber-erasure-spectrum}\\  C_\mu^*C_\mu&=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathcal I}k_i\Pi_i,  \label{eq:tpc58-global-erasure-spectrum}\\  \operatorname{Ker}C_\mu  &=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathcal I}    \left\{z_i:\sum_{t\in\mathcal T_i}\mu_{i,t}z_{i,t}=0\right\}.  \label{eq:tpc58-exact-dark-space}\end{aligned}$$ In particular, $$\operatorname{rank}C_\mu=|\mathcal I|,  \qquad  \dim\operatorname{Ker}C_\mu=\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}(k_i-1).  \label{eq:tpc58-erasure-rank-nullity}$$ The nonzero singular value on the $i$-th fiber is $\sqrt{k_i}$.

###### ref-e699a6460a71705a

> **Proof** In the standard coordinate convention the row of $C_{\mu,i}$ is $\bm\mu_i^{\mathsf T}$, so $$C_{\mu,i}^*C_{\mu,i}  =\overline{\bm\mu_i}\,\bm\mu_i^{\mathsf T}.  \label{eq:tpc58-rank-one-outer-product}$$ Its range is the line spanned by $\overline{\bm\mu_i}$, and $\|\bm\mu_i\|^2=k_i$ by [\[eq:tpc58-unit-mobius-gauge\]](TPC-058.md#ref-60b225bc891c2d3a). This proves [\[eq:tpc58-fiber-erasure-spectrum\]](TPC-058.md#ref-dd6d3382ef2d301f). Distinct fibers have disjoint domain and range coordinates, hence the direct-sum identity [\[eq:tpc58-global-erasure-spectrum\]](TPC-058.md#ref-e340076a25db966b). Each nonzero row has rank one, and its kernel has dimension $k_i-1$, which proves the remaining claims.

###### ref-612c16662af52512

###### ref-cd1727fc8ccf5c5a

> **Remark: The hypotheses behind the scalar $k_i$** \[rem:tpc58-erasure-weight-scope\] For a nonzero general row $g_i=(g_{i,t})_t$, the exact identity is $$C_i^*C_i  =\overline g_i g_i^{\mathsf T}  =\|g_i\|^2\Pi_{\operatorname{span}(\overline g_i)}.  \label{eq:tpc58-general-erasure-row}$$ Thus the eigenvalue is $k_i$ only when all $k_i$ gauges have unit modulus. In particular, nonsquarefree labels carrying Möbius value zero cannot be counted among the $k_i$ active unit-gauge labels. Also, when the numbers $k_i$ vary, there is no global scalar $k$ for which $C_\mu^*C_\mu=k\Pi$.

###### ref-ae9198c45f89127c

###### ref-df801995b8d6cc01

> **Proposition: Sharp ambient obstruction** \[prop:tpc58-sharp-ambient-obstruction\] The best lower erasure constant on the entire canonical space is $$\alpha_{\rm amb}  :=\inf_{0\ne z\in\mathcal Z}    \frac{\|C_\mu z\|^2}{\|z\|^2}  =  \begin{cases}   1,&k_i=1\text{ for every }i,\\   0,&k_i\ge2\text{ for at least one }i.  \end{cases}  \label{eq:tpc58-sharp-ambient-constant}$$ Both alternatives are sharp.

###### ref-2dba2cf724d734ad

> **Proof** If every $k_i=1$, then $C_\mu$ is diagonal with unit-modulus entries and is therefore unitary. If $k_i\ge2$, choose distinct labels $t,u\in\mathcal T_i$, set $$z_{i,t}=\mu_{i,u},  \qquad z_{i,u}=-\mu_{i,t},  \label{eq:tpc58-two-label-dark-vector}$$ and set every other coordinate to zero. This is a nonzero vector in $\operatorname{Ker}C_\mu$, so the infimum vanishes.

###### ref-f223180eb0b38cbc

The complex conjugation in [\[eq:tpc58-bright-projector\]](TPC-058.md#ref-289629bebb7ffbad) is not cosmetic. For example, if $$C=[\,1\ \ i\,],  \label{eq:tpc58-complex-gauge-counterexample}$$ then $\operatorname{Ran}C^*=\operatorname{span}\{(1,-i)\}$. Projecting instead onto $\operatorname{span}\{(1,i)\}$ reverses both off-diagonal signs and does not give $C^*C$. Likewise, the row $[\,1\ \ 0\,]$ has two displayed coordinates but nonzero eigenvalue one, not two; this is the smallest counterexample to omitting the unit-gauge condition.

## Canonical aggregation is not physical uniqueness

###### ref-f864cfd283bbf124

###### ref-d6e7e6f254ea0bf0

###### ref-a90380b54a0fbfeb

###### ref-cfc103f84125e65f

###### ref-78d70a04c52fb194

We record the exact sense in which the residual coordinate system is canonical. For each $i$, let $$\mathcal T_i=\mathcal T_i^0\mathbin{\dot\cup}\mathcal S_i  \label{eq:tpc58-canonical-label-partition}$$ be a declared disjoint partition into no-high labels and residual labels. For $t\in\mathcal T_i^0$ let $b_{i,t}\in\mathbb C$, and for $s\in\mathcal S_i$ let $\mathcal P_{i,s}$ be a finite set with coefficients $b_{i,s,p}$. Put $$B_{i,s}:=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_{i,s}}b_{i,s,p},  \qquad  z_{i,t}:=b_{i,t},  \qquad  z_{i,s}:=-B_{i,s}.  \label{eq:tpc58-canonical-residual-vector}$$ Define $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal D_0  &:=\sum_i\left(       \sum_{t\in\mathcal T_i^0}|b_{i,t}|^2       +\sum_{s\in\mathcal S_i}\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_{i,s}}        |b_{i,s,p}|^2\right),  \label{eq:tpc58-ambient-diagonal}\\  \mathcal D_H  &:=\sum_i\sum_{s\in\mathcal S_i}       \sum_{p\in\mathcal P_{i,s}}|b_{i,s,p}|^2,  \label{eq:tpc58-high-diagonal}\\  \mathcal G_{\rm res}  &:=\sum_i\sum_{s\in\mathcal S_i}|B_{i,s}|^2.  \label{eq:tpc58-abstract-residual-grouped-energy}\end{aligned}$$

###### ref-44ad07ded90a8c16

###### ref-5e6ad83c39c8e71d

###### ref-f96878983fd18a44

> **Proposition: Canonical residual identity** \[prop:tpc58-canonical-residual-identity\] For the coefficient vector in [\[eq:tpc58-canonical-residual-vector\]](TPC-058.md#ref-d6e7e6f254ea0bf0), $$\boxed{  \mathcal R_{\rm low}:=\|z\|^2  =\mathcal D_0-\mathcal D_H+\mathcal G_{\rm res}.}  \label{eq:tpc58-canonical-low-energy}$$ Equivalently, $$\mathcal R_{\rm low}  =\mathcal D_0+2\operatorname{Re}    \sum_i\sum_{s\in\mathcal S_i}    \sum_{\substack{p,q\in\mathcal P_{i,s}\\p<q}}    b_{i,s,p}\overline{b_{i,s,q}},  \label{eq:tpc58-residual-signed-offdiagonal}$$ after fixing any order on each finite prime set.

> **Proof** The declared partition [\[eq:tpc58-canonical-label-partition\]](TPC-058.md#ref-f864cfd283bbf124) is orthogonal in $\mathcal Z$. Hence $$\|z\|^2  =\sum_i\sum_{t\in\mathcal T_i^0}|b_{i,t}|^2   +\sum_i\sum_{s\in\mathcal S_i}|B_{i,s}|^2,$$ which is exactly [\[eq:tpc58-canonical-low-energy\]](TPC-058.md#ref-5e6ad83c39c8e71d). Expanding every $|B_{i,s}|^2$ and cancelling its diagonal contribution against $-\mathcal D_H$ gives [\[eq:tpc58-residual-signed-offdiagonal\]](TPC-058.md#ref-f96878983fd18a44).

###### ref-4fdd7d2e0fa722fe

The vector $z$ is unique as the coefficient vector in the fixed orthonormal coordinate family $(i,t)$. This statement requires the partition in [\[eq:tpc58-canonical-label-partition\]](TPC-058.md#ref-f864cfd283bbf124) to have been fixed first; it neither recovers the separate coefficients inside a sum $B_{i,s}$ nor makes $z$ unique from $C_\mu z$. Its support is the smallest subset of this particular orthonormal coordinate family that represents $z$, but the fixed baseline label bank need not be minimal for one realized coefficient vector. Indeed, $$b_{i,s,p}=1,  \qquad b_{i,s,q}=-1  \label{eq:tpc58-cancelled-residual-counterexample}$$ gives $z_{i,s}=0$, even though two baseline atoms occur. At the physical erasure level, the one-fiber vectors $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$ have the same image under $[\,1\ \ 1\,]$. Thus “canonical”, “minimal”, and “physically identifiable” are three different assertions.

## The signed native off-diagonal

###### ref-6cdba923b8f12695

###### ref-3c312fc71dc51196

###### ref-7dbcc5fa6fd17544

Put $$(v_0)_i:=\sum_{t\in\mathcal T_i^0}\mu_{i,t}b_{i,t},  \qquad  w_i:=\sum_{s\in\mathcal S_i}\mu_{i,s}B_{i,s}.  \label{eq:tpc58-vzero-and-w}$$ Then the erasure of the canonical residual vector is $$\boxed{C_\mu z=v_0-w.}  \label{eq:tpc58-erased-affine-difference}$$ The exact coordinate expansion is $$\begin{aligned}  \|C_\mu z\|^2  ={}&\|z\|^2  +2\operatorname{Re}\sum_i    \sum_{\substack{t,u\in\mathcal T_i^0\\t<u}}    \mu_{i,t}\overline{\mu_{i,u}}    b_{i,t}\overline{b_{i,u}}  \notag\\  &+2\operatorname{Re}\sum_i    \sum_{\substack{s,r\in\mathcal S_i\\s<r}}    \mu_{i,s}\overline{\mu_{i,r}}    B_{i,s}\overline{B_{i,r}}  \notag\\  &-2\operatorname{Re}\sum_i    \sum_{\substack{t\in\mathcal T_i^0\\s\in\mathcal S_i}}    \mu_{i,t}\overline{\mu_{i,s}}    b_{i,t}\overline{B_{i,s}}.  \label{eq:tpc58-complete-signed-offdiagonal}\end{aligned}$$ Thus erasure restores all unequal-low-label cross terms. The mixed no-high/residual terms have a minus sign, while the no-high/no-high and residual/residual terms have a plus sign. For a complex gauge the factor is $\mu_{i,t}\overline{\mu_{i,u}}$; it cannot be replaced by a formal $\mu(tu)$ without additional coprimality and reality hypotheses.

###### ref-431adce5bbfca1f0

###### ref-513ad3b856ff523b

###### ref-45a8f74999903a02

If $$(v_p)_i  :=\sum_{\substack{s\in\mathcal S_i\\p\in\mathcal P_{i,s}}}    \mu_{i,s}b_{i,s,p},  \label{eq:tpc58-individual-high-vectors}$$ then $w=\sum_pv_p$ and $$\boxed{  \|v_0-w\|^2  =\|v_0\|^2+\sum_p\|v_p\|^2   +2\operatorname{Re}\sum_{p<q}\langle v_p,v_q\rangle   -2\operatorname{Re}\sum_p\langle v_0,v_p\rangle.}  \label{eq:tpc58-affine-signed-expansion}$$ No positive lower comparison with $\|z\|^2$ follows from the diagonal terms alone. The minimal sharp example is $$C=[\,1\ \ 1\,],  \qquad z=(1,-1),  \qquad \|z\|^2=2,  \qquad \|Cz\|^2=0.  \label{eq:tpc58-sharp-native-cancellation}$$ The restricted-range analysis in the next section identifies exactly what additional coefficient geometry is needed to exclude this dark direction.

# Restricted-range transfer and the three quantifiers

The ambient erasure constant vanishes as soon as one fiber contains two labels. A physical coefficient family may nevertheless occupy a smaller subspace transverse to the dark kernel. This section gives the exact finite-dimensional criterion, its Moore–Penrose form, and the distinction between an ambient theorem, a canonical-range theorem, and a statement for one distinguished vector.

## Compression to the actual attainable range

###### ref-f466ca916d01f6da

###### ref-e553cd31db9ae007

Let $\mathcal A$ be a finite-dimensional Hilbert space, let $$T:\mathcal A\longrightarrow\mathcal Z,  \qquad  E:=\operatorname{Ran}T,  \label{eq:tpc58-attainable-range}$$ and assume $E\ne\{0\}$. The map $T$ is part of the data: replacing it by a free ambient coefficient array changes the quantifier and may create dark directions that the declared family never attains. Define $$\alpha_E  :=\inf_{0\ne z\in E}    \frac{\|C_\mu z\|^2}{\|z\|^2}.  \label{eq:tpc58-restricted-transfer-constant}$$

###### ref-0bdcb7b7b0105309

###### ref-5a9f1c8f2f0ef225

###### ref-8d68b1571d90342e

> **Theorem: Exact restricted-range criterion** \[thm:tpc58-exact-restricted-range\] Let $P_E$ be the orthogonal projector onto $E$. Then $$\boxed{  \alpha_E  =\lambda_{\min}\!\left(    (P_EC_\mu^*C_\mu P_E)|_E\right).}  \label{eq:tpc58-compressed-minimum-eigenvalue}$$ Here $\lambda_{\min}$ is the ordinary smallest eigenvalue, including zero. Moreover, $$\boxed{  \alpha_E>0  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad  E\cap\operatorname{Ker}C_\mu=\{0\}.}  \label{eq:tpc58-transversality-criterion}$$

###### ref-5055c26cca22c92c

> **Proof** For $z\in E$, $$\langle z,P_EC_\mu^*C_\mu P_Ez\rangle  =\langle z,C_\mu^*C_\mu z\rangle  =\|C_\mu z\|^2.  \label{eq:tpc58-compression-rayleigh-identity}$$ The compression in [\[eq:tpc58-compressed-minimum-eigenvalue\]](TPC-058.md#ref-5a9f1c8f2f0ef225) is a positive self-adjoint operator on the finite-dimensional space $E$. The Rayleigh–Ritz principle proves the eigenvalue formula. Its smallest eigenvalue is zero exactly when some nonzero $z\in E$ has $C_\mu z=0$, which is [\[eq:tpc58-transversality-criterion\]](TPC-058.md#ref-8d68b1571d90342e).

###### ref-9da288f758255e5d

###### ref-cd37e46f66d27e55

The finite-dimensional hypothesis is essential for the reverse implication in [\[eq:tpc58-transversality-criterion\]](TPC-058.md#ref-8d68b1571d90342e). On $\ell^2(\mathbb N)$, the injective diagonal operator $$C(x_1,x_2,\ldots)=(x_1,x_2/2,x_3/3,\ldots)  \label{eq:tpc58-infinite-dimensional-counterexample}$$ has trivial kernel but is not bounded below. Even in finite dimension one must use the smallest eigenvalue rather than the smallest *positive* eigenvalue: for $$C_\mu=[\,1\ \ 1\,],  \qquad E=\mathbb C^2,  \label{eq:tpc58-positive-eigenvalue-counterexample}$$ the smallest positive eigenvalue of $C_\mu^*C_\mu$ is two, while $\alpha_E=0$.

## The support-restricted pseudoinverse formula

###### ref-eeb42006647d9dda

Set $$R:=T^*T,  \qquad  N:=T^*C_\mu^*C_\mu T,  \qquad  S:=\operatorname{Ran}R=(\operatorname{Ker}T)^\perp.  \label{eq:tpc58-R-N-S}$$ Let $R^\dagger$ be the Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse and write $R^{\dagger/2}:=(R^\dagger)^{1/2}$.

###### ref-3565f901c2722322

###### ref-64a78cb2c730f5ca

###### ref-e2a98181fa0deeee

###### ref-2b9c24a6ac85962f

###### ref-f351b5b6747469a3

> **Theorem: Range-normalized Gram formula** \[thm:tpc58-range-normalized-gram\] The operator $$U:=T R^{\dagger/2}|_S:S\longrightarrow E  \label{eq:tpc58-polar-unitary}$$ is unitary. Consequently, $$\boxed{  \alpha_E  =\lambda_{\min}\!\left(    (R^{\dagger/2}NR^{\dagger/2})|_S\right).}  \label{eq:tpc58-pseudoinverse-minimum}$$ If $$\Theta  :=R^{\dagger/2}(N-R)R^{\dagger/2},  \label{eq:tpc58-relative-defect}$$ then the equivalent defect formula is $$\boxed{  \alpha_E  =1+\lambda_{\min}(\Theta|_S).}  \label{eq:tpc58-relative-defect-minimum}$$ The restriction to $S$ in both formulas is mandatory.

###### ref-1140f1d6da32f550

###### ref-ac8a1df384746dfe

###### ref-51dad80e2ff387bc

> **Proof** On $S$, functional calculus for the positive operator $R$ gives $$U^*U  =R^{\dagger/2}T^*TR^{\dagger/2}|_S  =R^{\dagger/2}RR^{\dagger/2}|_S  =I_S.  \label{eq:tpc58-polar-isometry}$$ The range of $U$ equals $\operatorname{Ran}T=E$, either by the polar decomposition or directly on each positive eigenspace of $R$. Hence $U$ is unitary from $S$ onto $E$. Furthermore, $$U^*C_\mu^*C_\mu U  =(R^{\dagger/2}NR^{\dagger/2})|_S.  \label{eq:tpc58-unitary-gram-equivalence}$$ The right side is therefore unitarily equivalent to the compressed operator in [\[eq:tpc58-compressed-minimum-eigenvalue\]](TPC-058.md#ref-5a9f1c8f2f0ef225), proving [\[eq:tpc58-pseudoinverse-minimum\]](TPC-058.md#ref-e2a98181fa0deeee). Finally, $$R^{\dagger/2}RR^{\dagger/2}|_S=I_S,  \label{eq:tpc58-pseudoinverse-support-identity}$$ so subtracting this identity gives [\[eq:tpc58-relative-defect-minimum\]](TPC-058.md#ref-f351b5b6747469a3).

###### ref-85744bdb86e7a997

###### ref-4c30e5eeef4960dc

###### ref-f8802a2e7a48ce8b

For reference, the same calculation gives the standard exact formulas $$T^\dagger=R^\dagger T^*,  \qquad  P_E=TT^\dagger=TR^\dagger T^*.  \label{eq:tpc58-moore-penrose-projector}$$ Taking the minimum in [\[eq:tpc58-relative-defect-minimum\]](TPC-058.md#ref-f351b5b6747469a3) on the full parameter space $\mathcal A$ is generally false, because $\Theta$ vanishes on $\operatorname{Ker}R$. The smallest counterexample is $$T(a,b)=\frac{a}{\sqrt2}(1,1),  \qquad C_\mu=[\,1\ \ 1\,].  \label{eq:tpc58-pseudoinverse-support-counterexample}$$ Here $$R=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},  \qquad  N=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},  \qquad  \Theta=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.  \label{eq:tpc58-pseudoinverse-counterexample-matrices}$$ The true restricted constant is two, whereas $1+\lambda_{\min}(\Theta)=1$ on all of $\mathbb C^2$.

## Dimension kill and its nonconverse

###### ref-93c0df3e1ff5aeba

###### ref-9d7f20386263ffb4

> **Proposition: One-way dimension kill** \[prop:tpc58-dimension-kill\] If $$\dim E>\operatorname{rank}C_\mu,  \label{eq:tpc58-dimension-kill-hypothesis}$$ then $\alpha_E=0$. The converse is false.

> **Proof** The restriction $C_\mu|_E:E\to\mathcal Y$ has rank at most $\operatorname{rank}C_\mu$. Under [\[eq:tpc58-dimension-kill-hypothesis\]](TPC-058.md#ref-9d7f20386263ffb4), rank–nullity supplies a nonzero element of $E\cap\operatorname{Ker}C_\mu$, and [\[thm:tpc58-exact-restricted-range\]](TPC-058.md#ref-0bdcb7b7b0105309) gives $\alpha_E=0$.
>
> For the nonconverse, take $C_\mu=[\,1\ \ 1\,]$ and $E=\operatorname{span}\{(1,-1)\}$. Then $\dim E=\operatorname{rank}C_\mu=1$, but $E\subseteq\operatorname{Ker}C_\mu$ and $\alpha_E=0$.

In the erasure geometry of [\[thm:tpc58-exact-erasure-spectrum\]](TPC-058.md#ref-7955d01f61a40ea5), $\operatorname{rank}C_\mu=|\mathcal I|$. If the raw grouping map is surjective onto the full canonical space, then $E=\mathcal Z$, and any fiber with $k_i>1$ activates the dark space. Thus a positive restricted theorem must use a genuinely smaller *actual attainable range*; it cannot be obtained by declaring all canonical coordinates to be free and then forgetting that enlargement.

## Ambient, canonical-range, and distinguished statements

###### ref-c865f972475a6a0f

###### ref-2ffbcd6b40c2a65b

###### ref-c2b78b4e07dd6036

###### ref-434d9664e82b0a69

###### ref-bd72fd07fc018027

There are three different quantifiers: $$\begin{aligned}  \alpha_{\rm amb}  &:=\inf_{0\ne z\in\mathcal Z}    \frac{\|C_\mu z\|^2}{\|z\|^2},  \label{eq:tpc58-ambient-quantifier}\\  \alpha_E  &:=\inf_{0\ne z\in E}    \frac{\|C_\mu z\|^2}{\|z\|^2},  \label{eq:tpc58-canonical-range-quantifier}\\  \rho(z_*)  &:=\frac{\|C_\mu z_*\|^2}{\|z_*\|^2}  \qquad(0\ne z_*\in E).  \label{eq:tpc58-distinguished-quantifier}\end{aligned}$$ They obey only the one-way chain $$\boxed{  \alpha_{\rm amb}\le\alpha_E\le\rho(z_*).}  \label{eq:tpc58-three-quantifier-chain}$$ The two reverse implications fail sharply. With $C=[\,1\ \ 1\,]$, the ambient constant is zero, while for $$E=\operatorname{span}\{(1,1)\}  \label{eq:tpc58-canonical-beats-ambient}$$ one has $\alpha_E=2$. On the other hand, with the same $C$ and $E=\mathbb C^2$, one has $\alpha_E=0$, while the distinguished vector $z_*=(1,1)$ satisfies $\rho(z_*)=2$. Consequently:

1.  an ambient lower bound controls every canonical family and every distinguished vector;

2.  a canonical-range lower bound controls only vectors proved to lie in that one declared range;

3.  a positive distinguished ratio does not imply a uniform theorem on its containing range.

Calling one of these statements by the name of another is a quantifier change, not a harmless normalization.

## The independent affine gate

###### ref-cad63de72bb9610e

###### ref-f5ef95712819fc4e

The restricted-range constant resolves only the erasure from a residual coefficient vector to its native high component. It does not prevent that component from cancelling a no-high vector. Abstractly, let $\mathcal Z_H\subseteq\mathcal Z$, let $E_H=\operatorname{Ran}T_H\subseteq\mathcal Z_H$ be nonzero, and define $$\alpha_H  :=\inf_{0\ne r\in E_H}    \frac{\|C_\mu r\|^2}{\|r\|^2}.  \label{eq:tpc58-high-restricted-constant}$$ For one signed residual vector $r\in E_H$, put $$h:=C_\mu r,  \qquad  \mathcal G_{\rm res}:=\|r\|^2,  \qquad  \mathcal N_H:=\|h\|^2.  \label{eq:tpc58-abstract-native-high-energy}$$

###### ref-fb477df901241a70

###### ref-4230c0c8f9c0e35b

###### ref-4a3b0036660697e6

> **Proposition: Two independent transfer gates** \[prop:tpc58-two-independent-gates\] Let $v_0\in\mathcal Y$. Suppose $r\ne0$ and, independently, that $$\|v_0+h\|^2  \ge\delta_{\rm aff}       (\|v_0\|^2+\|h\|^2)  \qquad(\delta_{\rm aff}\ge0).  \label{eq:tpc58-affine-angle-gate}$$ Then $$\boxed{  \|v_0+h\|^2  \ge\delta_{\rm aff}\mathcal N_H  \ge\delta_{\rm aff}\alpha_H\mathcal G_{\rm res}.}  \label{eq:tpc58-two-stage-transfer}$$ Neither inequality supplies the hypothesis of the other.

###### ref-d1e91b9753008d60

> **Proof** The first inequality follows from $\|v_0\|^2+\|h\|^2\ge\|h\|^2=\mathcal N_H$. The definition of $\alpha_H$ gives $\mathcal N_H\ge\alpha_H\mathcal G_{\rm res}$, proving the chain.
>
> For independence, take the one-dimensional example $$r=1,  \qquad C_\mu r=h=1,  \qquad v_0=-1.  \label{eq:tpc58-independent-gate-counterexample}$$ The restricted erasure constant and both high energies equal one, but $\|v_0+h\|^2=0$, so no positive affine constant follows. Conversely, an affine separation for one chosen pair says nothing about the infimum of $C_\mu$ on all of $E_H$.

###### ref-57c5e8167b6103db

For a class $\mathcal F\subseteq\mathcal Y\oplus\mathcal Y$ of admissible pairs, the exact affine constant is $$\delta_{\rm aff}  =\inf_{\substack{(v_0,h)\in\mathcal F\\                    \|v_0\|^2+\|h\|^2>0}}    \frac{\|v_0+h\|^2}{\|v_0\|^2+\|h\|^2}.  \label{eq:tpc58-exact-affine-constant}$$ It lies in $[0,2]$, and a positive value is precisely a uniform exclusion of approach to the cancellation direction $v_0=-h$. For an arbitrary, possibly nonclosed class $\mathcal F$, absence of one exact anti-diagonal pair does not suffice: normalized admissible pairs may still converge to the anti-diagonal. If $\mathcal F$ is a nonzero finite-dimensional linear subspace, as is the actual coupled image used below, compactness of its unit sphere makes positivity equivalent to trivial intersection with the anti-diagonal. This is a separate signed off-diagonal problem; the spectrum of the residual erasure operator alone does not solve it.

## Raw norms and the second grouping gate

###### ref-097542db466fab91

###### ref-6c11696c3f6b70e0

###### ref-d7b96a085454fcd8

The terminal atomic, residual grouped, and native-high energies on this face are three different raw counting-measure quantities: $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal D_{h_0}^{>y}(G_X^L)  &:=\sum_{\mathfrak u\in\mathcal U_{h_0,>y}^{\rm term}}       |X_{\mathfrak u}|^2,  \label{eq:tpc58-very-high-terminal-diagonal}\\  \mathcal G_{h_0}^{>y}(G_X^L)  &:=\sum_{i,s}       \left|\sum_{\substack{\mathfrak u:\,                     i(\mathfrak u)=i,\ s(\mathfrak u)=s}}              X_{\mathfrak u}\right|^2    =\|A_H\|_{\mathcal H_{\rm res}^{H}}^2,  \label{eq:tpc58-very-high-residual-grouped-energy}\\  \mathcal N_{H}^{>y}(h_0)  &:=\sum_i\left|\sum_s A_H(i,s)\right|^2    =\|\mathsf EA_H\|_{\mathcal H_{\rm nat}}^2.  \label{eq:tpc58-very-high-native-energy}\end{aligned}$$ Equation [\[eq:tpc58-very-high-residual-grouped-energy\]](TPC-058.md#ref-6c11696c3f6b70e0) already forms the coherent terminal-prime sum at fixed $(i,s)$. The input to the native Gram gate is therefore $\mathcal G_{h_0}^{>y}$, not $\mathcal D_{h_0}^{>y}$. The passage from [\[eq:tpc58-very-high-residual-grouped-energy\]](TPC-058.md#ref-6c11696c3f6b70e0) to [\[eq:tpc58-very-high-native-energy\]](TPC-058.md#ref-d7b96a085454fcd8) is the second grouping $(i,s)\to i$.

The singleton and phase-sector results of TPC–56–57 concern the first grouping $p\to(i,s)$ [`\citep{WangTPC56,WangTPC57}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-058.md#cite-8eb3ab4efaf1630e); [\[WangTPC57\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-058.md#cite-d87f234f729af8b2). They do not constrain the cross-$s$ sum in [\[eq:tpc58-very-high-native-energy\]](TPC-058.md#ref-d7b96a085454fcd8). In particular:

1.  a terminal-prime singleton at $(i,s)$ may coexist with many other singleton residual labels $t\ne s$ in the same native coordinate;

2.  the adaptive phase sector of TPC–56 deletes terminal coordinates before the first grouping and is not the undeleted vector $A_H$;

3.  the conditional undeleted floor of TPC–57 can provide a lower bound for $\|A_H\|^2$, but no lower bound for $\|\mathsf EA_H\|^2$ follows without a new residual-label comparison.

###### ref-e58ba722fdc2db90

Finally, [\[eq:tpc58-very-high-native-energy\]](TPC-058.md#ref-d7b96a085454fcd8) is only the very-high part of the native affine column on the declared direct slice. Its complete native affine energy contains $$v_0+\mathsf EA_H=v_0-\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_y}v_p.  \label{eq:tpc58-full-affine-sign-interface}$$ Neither nonnegativity nor a triangle inequality permits a lower bound for $\|\mathsf EA_H\|^2$ to be returned to $\|v_0+\mathsf EA_H\|^2$. Base completion is a separate affine gate.

The fixed-shift interface proved here is therefore exact but deliberately limited: it aligns literal coefficients, signs, supports, and raw coordinates through both grouping maps. It proves no positive lower singular value for $\mathsf E$, no terminal activation, no residual-label occupancy estimate, and no native affine lower bound.

# High-block transfer and completion of the affine face

The preceding residual construction and the native affine column use two equivalent Möbius conventions. We first align them exactly. ## The restricted high-transfer constant

###### ref-7078cf19a8eb2cab

###### ref-c07988deaddd161b

For coefficient-uniform statements, fix once and for all a baseline carrier independent of the bounded row coefficient vector $\zeta=(\zeta_m)$. Remove atoms whose baseline factor is identically zero, but do not remove an atom merely because a particular $\zeta_m$ vanishes. On this fixed carrier, let $$T_H:\mathcal K_X\longrightarrow\mathcal H_H,  \qquad  T_H\zeta=z_H(\zeta),  \qquad  E_H:=\operatorname{Ran}T_H,  \label{eq:tpc58-high-coefficient-map}$$ where $\mathcal K_X$ is the finite row-coefficient space and $\mathcal H_H$ is the canonical high-label Hilbert space. The exact literal factorization $x_u=c_u\zeta_{m(u)}$ makes $T_H$ linear. For the coefficient-uniform gate, assume $E_H\ne\{0\}$ and define $$\alpha_H  :=\inf_{\substack{z\in E_H\\z\ne0}}  \frac{\|C_{\mu,H}z\|_2^2}{\|z\|_2^2}.  \label{eq:tpc58-alpha-H}$$ If $E_H=\{0\}$, the declared high carrier is inactive for the whole coefficient class. The pointwise identities above still hold, but no least-eigenvalue constant $\alpha_H$ is assigned and the two-stage lower chain has no nonzero high input.

> **Proposition: Optimal high-block transfer** \[prop:tpc58-optimal-high-transfer\] Let $P_{E_H}$ be the orthogonal projection onto $E_H$. Then $$\boxed{  \alpha_H  =\lambda_{\min}\!\left(  P_{E_H}C_{\mu,H}^{*}C_{\mu,H}P_{E_H}\big|_{E_H}  \right).}  \label{eq:tpc58-alpha-H-eigenvalue}$$ Moreover, $$\alpha_H>0  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad  E_H\cap\operatorname{Ker}C_{\mu,H}=\{0\}.  \label{eq:tpc58-alpha-H-kernel-test}$$ In particular, $$\dim E_H>\operatorname{rank}C_{\mu,H}  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad  \alpha_H=0.  \label{eq:tpc58-alpha-H-dimension-test}$$ The same value is obtained if the infimum is restricted to any bounded coefficient cube containing a nonzero scalar multiple of every direction in $\mathcal K_X$.

> **Proof** The quadratic form of the compressed positive operator in [\[eq:tpc58-alpha-H-eigenvalue\]](TPC-058.md#ref-6bf959b21a836a59) is exactly $\|C_{\mu,H}z\|_2^2$ on $E_H$. The Rayleigh–Ritz principle therefore gives the first assertion. In finite dimension its least eigenvalue is zero exactly when the restriction of $C_{\mu,H}$ to $E_H$ has a nonzero kernel. Rank–nullity proves [\[eq:tpc58-alpha-H-dimension-test\]](TPC-058.md#ref-bb8561ad500a6cae). Finally, every nonzero coefficient vector can be multiplied by a sufficiently small positive scalar to enter the bounded cube, and the quotient [\[eq:tpc58-alpha-H\]](TPC-058.md#ref-c07988deaddd161b) is homogeneous of degree zero.

## Two independent sharp obstructions

###### ref-5ede2bb70bc7a851

> **Theorem: Sharp independence of the two transfer gates** \[thm:tpc58-two-independent-no-go\] Neither stage in [\[thm:tpc58-two-stage-affine-transfer\]](TPC-058.md#ref-347f217cfbcd5422) follows from positivity at the other stage.
>
> 1.  A residual packet may have positive energy, with every terminal-prime fiber a singleton, while its native high vector is zero.
>
> 2.  A native high vector may be nonzero while the complete affine vector is zero.
>
> Both statements are sharp finite-dimensional assertions with raw counting measure.

> **Proof** Both examples use one common coefficient space $\mathcal K_X=\mathbb C$, so the two affine entries are genuinely coupled rather than selected independently.
>
> For (i), take one native coordinate $i$, two distinct residual labels $s_1,s_2$, and one terminal atom in each residual fiber. Let $z_d\ne0$ be the dark vector $$(z_d)_{i,s_1}=\overline{\mu(s_1)},  \qquad  (z_d)_{i,s_2}=-\overline{\mu(s_2)},  \qquad C_{\mu,H}z_d=0.$$ Define $T_H\zeta=\zeta z_d$ and, for a fixed nonzero $g\in\mathcal H_{\rm out}$, define $B_0\zeta=\zeta g$. Then $E_H=\operatorname{span}\{z_d\}$, so $\alpha_H=0$, whereas $$\mathcal F_{\rm aff}=\operatorname{span}\{(g,0)\},  \qquad \delta_{\rm aff}=1.$$ Thus a strictly positive affine gate does not prevent total loss at native erasure, even with singleton terminal-prime fibers.
>
> For (ii), choose a bright vector $z_b$ with $C_{\mu,H}z_b=g\ne0$, and set $T_H\zeta=\zeta z_b$, $B_0\zeta=-\zeta g$. Then $$\alpha_H=\frac{\|g\|_2^2}{\|z_b\|_2^2}>0,  \qquad  \mathcal F_{\rm aff}=\operatorname{span}\{(-g,g)\},  \qquad \delta_{\rm aff}=0.$$ Hence a positive high-transfer gate does not prevent exact base–high cancellation. These are complete finite-dimensional coupled-map models; they do not assert that either bad direction occurs in the distinguished arithmetic image.

The last sentence is part of the theorem boundary. In particular, a dark direction on the unrestricted coordinate space is not evidence that the distinguished arithmetic coefficient occupies it. Conversely, an upper bound for fiber multiplicity does not bound its angle away from that dark space.

# Transport boundaries, the signed native exchange, and the endpoint ledger

The exact transfer theorem does not make either restricted-range constant positive. This section explains why the two preceding papers do not supply that positivity, identifies the literal signed quadratic form that would supply it, and records the complete endpoint charge.

## Why the adaptive phase sector does not transport

TPC–56 extracts a constant-energy subpacket after the terminal phases have been observed. Its selector is a genuine coordinate projection once it is chosen, but it is not a coefficient-independent pre-terminal criterion [`\citep{WangTPC56}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-058.md#cite-8eb3ab4efaf1630e). More basically, squared norms are not monotone under putting the deleted coordinates back into a coherent sum.

###### ref-720057fe78deedff

> **Proposition: Sharp adaptive nontransport** \[prop:tpc58-adaptive-nontransport\] There is no constant $c>0$ for which the grouped energy of the undeleted residual packet, or the complete native affine energy of the declared slice, is bounded below by $c$ times the grouped energy of every phase-adaptively selected terminal subpacket. This failure already occurs in one two-atom residual fiber.

> **Proof** Take two terminal coordinates in one residual fiber with amplitudes $1$ and $-1$. They lie in distinct phase sectors. A sector containing either coordinate has terminal diagonal and selected grouped energy equal to one, whereas restoring the other coordinate makes the undeleted grouped amplitude zero. After a common phase rotation avoiding sector boundaries, the two antipodal amplitudes occupy distinct members of the five equal sectors of TPC–56. Since the native and affine maps are downstream of this already vanishing undeleted residual amplitude, no positive lower bound for either downstream quantity can follow. The zero lower endpoint is attained, so the obstruction is sharp.

The proposition does not weaken the TPC–56 selected-square theorem. It states that the selected theorem may enter a physical chain only if the same selection is realized before terminal primality and is preserved by every later operation, or if a separate lower-reassembly theorem is proved. No such realization is assumed here.

# Audit, claim boundary, and the next Gram gate

###### ref-2f6919e12f9962e1

The point of the preceding construction is to locate, rather than conceal, the two comparisons that are still missing. With the final sign convention of [\[prop:tpc58-exact-gauge-alignment\]](TPC-058.md#ref-956c1ec192bf9742), $$z_H=-\sum_p b,  \qquad  h=C_{\mu,H}z_H=-\sum_pv_p,  \qquad  A=U_\mu z_H,  \qquad  C_1A=h.  \label{eq:tpc58-final-sign-ledger}$$ Thus the physical affine column is $v_0+h$. ## Six-point physical audit

The native-affine bridge passes the following bookkeeping audit. Passing the audit certifies that the operators act on the intended physical object; it does not certify positivity of either restricted singular value.

1.  *Literal coefficients.* The terminal amplitude is the inherited physical coefficient $x_u=c_u\zeta_{m(u)}$, including its output factor, source shape, masks, profiles, cofactor sign, and terminal multiplier. In the TPC–56–57 common gauge, $x_u$ already contains the factor $\mu(s(u))$. Equation [\[eq:tpc58-final-sign-ledger\]](TPC-058.md#ref-2f6919e12f9962e1) therefore uses the unsigned second grouping $C_1A$; no second Möbius sign is inserted.

2.  *Fixed physical shift.* Every terminal atom satisfies $p_ur_u-m(u)j_u=h_0$ for the one declared nonzero physical shift. The identities in \[[prop:tpc58-exact-gauge-alignment](TPC-058.md#ref-956c1ec192bf9742),[prop:tpc58-native-exchange-identity](TPC-058.md#ref-c0e1cb15cdacea78)\] are pointwise at this $h_0$. No intercept average, frozen-shift family, or projective reassembly is substituted for the physical column.

3.  *Physical atomic normalization.* The terminal diagonal energy, the first grouped residual energy, and the native high energy use the inherited raw counting-measure atoms. Repeated cofactor labels remain distinct terminal atoms until the declared grouping, and the terminal multiplier is not moved into a renormalized input norm. Consequently the two grouping maps compare actual physical energies rather than support cardinalities.

4.  *Canonical representation and minimality scope.* The first grouping uses the full residual key $q(u)=(i,s(u))$; the second grouping erases only $s$. The restricted space $E_H=\operatorname{Ran}T_H$ and the feasible affine pair space $\mathcal F_{\rm aff}$ are the canonical images of one declared coefficient map. Moore–Penrose reductions are restricted to the active Gram range, so null coefficient directions are not counted as physical generalized eigenvectors.

5.  *Actual active support.* The baseline carrier used to define $T_H$ is fixed independently of the row coefficient vector. Only factors that vanish identically on that baseline are removed. Coefficient-dependent zeros may shrink a distinguished support, but they do not redefine $E_H$, its rank, or the uniform constants. The high residual identification also uses the same $p>y$ cutoff on both the Walsh and terminal sides. At the endpoint this cutoff forces the smaller cofactor range [\[eq:tpc58-compatible-cofactor-ceiling\]](TPC-058.md#ref-121e1b73e62dbbe8); occupancy of that range is not inferred from the old $R\asymp J$ block.

6.  *Endpoint budget.* The two new losses are recorded separately as $\lambda_H$ and $\lambda_{\rm aff}$. Together with the separately recorded compatible-band relocation, selection, cofactor-block, terminal, singleton, boundary, and tail losses, they enter the exact ledger [\[eq:tpc58-total-endpoint-exponent\]](TPC-058.md#ref-d5e58d00cda497f9). The safe condition is the strict inequality $\Lambda_{\rm tot}<1/400$; equality is not accepted without a constant-level comparison and quantified remainder.

## The next gates and their kill tests

The remaining work begins with one support door and then separates into two spectral doors. Compatible-band activation must be tested before either spectral constant: a Gram theorem on an empty inherited block carries no endpoint energy. After activation, the native door precedes the affine door because an affine estimate cannot reconstruct a high component already lost in residual-label erasure.

1.  *Compatible-band relocation and occupancy.* With $y=X^{267/400+\eta}$, replace the incompatible old block $R\asymp J$ by dyadic blocks contained in $$r\le X^{133/400-\eta+o(1)}.$$ Prove a lower transfer from the inherited parent diagonal into these blocks, retain one fixed $h_0$, and then verify terminal activation and the hypotheses of the TPC–57 undeleted singleton bridge. The relocation loss $\lambda_{\rm reloc}$ and subsequent block loss $\lambda_{\rm blk}$ remain distinct.

    The immediate kill test is asymptotic emptiness or a best possible parent-to-band exponent already exhausting the $1/400$ slack. In either case the endpoint route stops before any Gram computation.

2.  *Literal restricted-Gram assembly.* Assemble the pair $$R_H=T_H^*T_H,         \qquad         N_H=T_H^*C_{\mu,H}^*C_{\mu,H}T_H$$ from the actual fixed-carrier coefficients, after quotienting only $\operatorname{Ker}T_H$. The first target is a certified census of the negative spectrum of $\Theta_H$, not merely a floating-point plot. Exact sparse elimination, interval eigenvalue bounds, and blockwise Schur-complement certificates may be used, provided every block keeps the literal normalization and active support.

    The immediate kill test is $$E_H\cap\operatorname{Ker}C_{\mu,H}\ne\{0\}.$$ If it holds on the actual image, then $\alpha_H=0$ and the coefficient-uniform native door is closed. A positive numerical eigenvalue at finitely many scales is scouting evidence only; a proof requires a scale-uniform lower certificate with an explicit loss exponent.

3.  *Distinguished signed exchange.* If the uniform image contains a dark vector, retain the one inherited coefficient vector and estimate the weakest exact target $$2\Re\sum_i\sum_{s<t}A_{i,s}\overline{A_{i,t}}         \ge -(1-\delta_H)\sum_{i,s}|A_{i,s}|^2         \label{eq:tpc58-next-distinguished-exchange}$$ with $\delta_H\ge X^{-\lambda_H+o(1)}$. 4.  *Base–high affine transversality.* Conditional on surviving Door B or C, assemble the feasible pair image $\mathcal F_{\rm aff}$ and test $$\mathcal F_{\rm aff}\cap\{(g,-g):g\in\mathcal H_{\rm out}\}=\{0\}.         \label{eq:tpc58-next-affine-kill-test}$$ A nonzero intersection proves $\delta_{\rm aff}=0$ for the uniform route. Otherwise the target is a quantitative principal-angle lower bound $\delta_{\rm aff}\ge X^{-\lambda_{\rm aff}+o(1)}$. This analysis must retain the joint dependence of $v_0$ and $h$ on the same coefficient vector; treating them as independent ambient vectors can only recover the sharp no-go of [\[thm:tpc58-two-independent-no-go\]](TPC-058.md#ref-5ede2bb70bc7a851).

5.  *Distinguished affine ratio.* If uniform affine transversality fails but the physical coefficient vector avoids the anti-diagonal, study the exact pointwise ratio [\[eq:tpc58-distinguished-delta-aff\]](TPC-058.md#ref-774186a55d35e1b7). 6.  *Budget stop and route closure.* At each scale record $\lambda_{\rm Gram}=\lambda_H+\lambda_{\rm aff}$ separately from the five upstream and two downstream losses. The most economical next paper is therefore a compatible-band census at the literal fixed shift: determine whether the smaller $r$-range forced by $p>y$ carries a quantitatively useful part of the parent energy. Only on a surviving nonzero carrier should the subsequent paper construct $R_H$, $N_H$, and the affine pair Gram and perform exact rank and kernel tests. This order permits a positive theorem or a sharp route-closure theorem without performing spectral analysis on an empty face.

#### Conclusion.

The missing native bridge is now an exact signed alias problem. TPC–57 can, subject to its own unresolved arithmetic hypotheses, deliver energy after the first grouping, but residual-label erasure introduces the new unequal-residual exchange form. Its optimal restricted constant is $\alpha_H$. Even if that gate is positive, completion of the physical column encounters the independent affine constant $\delta_{\rm aff}$. Both constants have exact spectral descriptions and sharp independent zero mechanisms; neither is proved positive on the endpoint carrier. The paper therefore closes an L1 interface and exposes one upstream activation gate and the next two auditable spectral gates, while making no parity improvement and no twin-prime claim.
