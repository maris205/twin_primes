## Abstract

The native-affine interface at a fixed shift isolates a very-high-prime face by imposing a cutoff $p>y$. Choosing $y=X^{267/400+\eta}$ with fixed $\eta>0$ forces its target cofactor into $r\le X^{133/400-\eta+o(1)}$, apparently separating it from the inherited $R\asymp J=X^{133/400+o(1)}$ packet. We prove that this positive-power displacement is a feature of that convenient cutoff, not a requirement of the literal very-high decomposition. The exact cutoff hypotheses already admit $$y=C_{\rm vh}Q,  \qquad Q=X^{267/400+o(1)},$$ with a fixed support-dependent constant. Writing $\omega_X=y/Q$, the safe and maximal cofactor scales are both $R\asymp J/\omega_X$. Thus a critical cutoff $\omega_X\asymp1$ preserves the $R\asymp J$ exponent, a subpower cutoff shift has zero fixed-power cost, and only $\omega_X=X^{\eta+o(1)}$ creates the polynomial gap.

Geometric compatibility does not imply energy occupancy. For the literal pre-terminal lift, whose coefficient has no cofactor dependence, we compute the optimal coefficient-uniform projection constant exactly: $$\beta_{\mathcal R}  =\min_{m:B_mN(m)>0}\frac{N_{\mathcal R}(m)}{N(m)}.$$ It can vanish under the inherited hypotheses. We give the analogous exact terminal-activation minimax, a largest-prime census of the very-high face, and an exact dyadic decomposition. Conditional on nonzero very-high atomic energy, one compatible dyadic block retains an $X^{-o(1)}$ fraction, so block localization has no positive polynomial loss. It does not prove parent-to-face occupancy. The exact Walsh decomposition also exposes high mass outside the represented terminal image; restoring it is a separate nonmonotone reassembly gate. Finally, under the declared $\Delta_M=O(Q)$ support information and among certified fixed-power schedules, we show that the critical cutoff is support-optimal, while all current support-only multiplicity and automatic-singleton bounds remain far outside the $1/400$ endpoint budget. These are exact L0 results and an L1 fixed-shift interface advance; no parity improvement or twin-prime conclusion is claimed.

TPC–58 therefore recorded a separate parent-to-compatible-band loss $\lambda_{\rm reloc}$ [`\citep{WangTPC58}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-059.md#cite-578b76b7099ebcf1).

That conclusion is correct on the face declared in [\[eq:tpc59-intro-fixed-eta\]](TPC-059.md#ref-7e60b649a0131480), but it leaves a prior optimization question: is a fixed $\eta$ forced by the unique-prime dictionary? The answer is no. TPC–45 uses only the literal inequalities $$\begin{aligned}  y&>\max\{|h_0|,D_+,J_+,\Delta_M,\sqrt{N_+}\},  \label{eq:tpc59-intro-cutoff-geometry}\\  \frac{D_+N_+}{y}&<D_-N_-.  \label{eq:tpc59-intro-cutoff-separation}\end{aligned}$$ At the endpoint, $\Delta_M=O(Q)$, every other growing term in the maximum is $o(Q)$, and the second inequality only imposes a bounded lower threshold on $y$. A sufficiently large fixed multiple of $Q$ therefore satisfies both conditions. The corresponding cofactor ceiling is again a fixed multiple of $J$.

This cutoff retuning removes a geometric positive-power loss, but it does not move any atom and does not prove that the physical carrier occupies the newly compatible window. The distinction is central: $$\boxed{  \text{cutoff compatibility}\ne  \text{cofactor occupancy}\ne  \text{terminal activation}.}  \label{eq:tpc59-three-gates}$$ The first is a support theorem. ## Main results

###### ref-5701dc6db267a449

###### ref-abffa2df5ebfd51f

Our first theorem introduces the dimensionless cutoff ratio $$\omega_X:=\frac yQ  \label{eq:tpc59-intro-omega}$$ and proves the exact reciprocity $$R_{\rm safe}=\frac{N_-}{2y}\asymp\frac J{\omega_X},  \qquad  R_{\rm max}=\frac{N_+}{y}\asymp\frac J{\omega_X}.  \label{eq:tpc59-intro-reciprocity}$$ A dyadic block below $R_{\rm safe}$ is automatically contained in the very-high face whenever it terminalizes, while a block above $R_{\rm max}$ is empty. Only $O(1)$ boundary shells lie between these two scales.

###### ref-f3ece6c984bd7ccc

###### ref-39baa9f138b16efe

###### ref-4e99c126cc7a294c

Our second result makes occupancy exact. In the literal lift, $$_{m,r,j,\gamma}=c_{m,j,\gamma}\zeta_m,  \label{eq:tpc59-intro-row-factorization}$$ with no $r$-dependence. For a fixed cofactor set $\mathcal R$, let $N(m)$ and $N_{\mathcal R}(m)$ be the total and retained cofactor multiplicities in row $m$, and put $$B_m:=\sum_{j,\gamma}|c_{m,j,\gamma}|^2.  \label{eq:tpc59-intro-Bm}$$ Then the sharp uniform energy-transfer constant is $$\boxed{  \beta_{\mathcal R}  =\inf_{\mathcal E_{\rm par}(\zeta)>0}    \frac{\mathcal E_{\mathcal R}(\zeta)}{\mathcal E_{\rm par}(\zeta)}  =\min_{m:B_mN(m)>0}\frac{N_{\mathcal R}(m)}{N(m)}.}  \label{eq:tpc59-intro-beta}$$ This is a rowwise minimax law, not an asymptotic estimate. The inherited carrier assumptions allow $N_{\mathcal R}(m)=0$ on an active row, so $\beta_{\mathcal R}=0$ is possible.

Our third result identifies the terminal face exactly using the largest prime factor of $mj+h_0$, and partitions its raw atomic energy into dyadic cofactor blocks. If that face has positive energy, a maximal block retains an $X^{-o(1)}$ fraction. This proves $\lambda_{\rm blk}=0$ after high-face activation. It does not prove any parent-to-face energy transfer, nor does it permit a restricted coherent norm to be returned monotonically to the undeleted output.

# Critical cutoff admissibility and cutoff–cofactor reciprocity

We first separate the literal cutoff inequalities from one convenient power-scale choice. This distinction is what permits the endpoint support door to be reopened without changing the physical shift or the coefficient packet.

Let the positive target and source supports satisfy $$\begin{aligned}  D_-&\le d_m\le D_+,&  M_-&\le m\le M_+,&  J_-&\le j\le J_+,&  N_-&\le n_{m,j}:=mj+h_0\le N_+,  \label{eq:tpc59-support-endpoints}\end{aligned}$$ with fixed support-ratio bounds $$D_\pm\asymp D,  \qquad M_\pm\asymp Q,  \qquad J_\pm\asymp J,  \qquad N_\pm\asymp X,  \qquad \Delta_M:=M_+-M_-\ll Q.  \label{eq:tpc59-support-ratios}$$ At the endpoint, $$Q=X^{267/400+o(1)},  \qquad  J=X^{133/400+o(1)},  \qquad  D=X^{10049/52500+o(1)},  \qquad QJ\asymp X.  \label{eq:tpc59-endpoint-QJD}$$ In particular, $$|h_0|+D_++J_++\sqrt{N_+}=o(Q).  \label{eq:tpc59-smaller-than-Q}$$ We retain the inherited literal one-side row system and assume that each retained source row has its declared unique representation $$m=d_m\ell_m  \label{eq:tpc59-unique-source-row}$$ inside that system. This is the row-factorization hypothesis used when a fixed-$(j,p)$ incidence is lifted back from the integer $m$ to the full source coordinate.

###### ref-78d77cba0057f203

Call a real cutoff $y=y_X$ *very-high admissible* if $$\begin{aligned}  y&>\max\{|h_0|,D_+,J_+,\Delta_M,\sqrt{N_+}\},  \label{eq:tpc59-admissible-geometric}\\  \frac{D_+N_+}{y}&<D_-N_-.  \label{eq:tpc59-admissible-separation}\end{aligned}$$ These are the exact TPC–45 hypotheses; no exponent has yet been assigned to the amount by which $y$ exceeds $Q$ [`\citep{WangTPC45}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-059.md#cite-1802a7e8047e595c).

###### ref-084b16c1dee0462e

###### ref-0b7b7fe8dcce50d5

> **Theorem: Critical very-high cutoff** \[thm:tpc59-critical-cutoff\] Under [\[eq:tpc59-support-endpoints\]](TPC-059.md#ref-12ab712241de7b58)– [\[eq:tpc59-smaller-than-Q\]](TPC-059.md#ref-eab8986cc2659691) and [\[eq:tpc59-unique-source-row\]](TPC-059.md#ref-39d66ea54036de2f), there is a fixed constant $C_{\rm vh}>0$, depending only on the support-ratio constants, such that $$\boxed{y=C_{\rm vh}Q}  \label{eq:tpc59-critical-y}$$ is very-high admissible for all sufficiently large $X$. Consequently the unique very-high-prime factorization, the residual identity $s=d_mr$, and fixed-$(j,p)$ row singleton incidence all hold already at the critical exponent $267/400$.

> **Proof** Choose $C_{\rm vh}$ strictly larger than the fixed implied constant in $\Delta_M\ll Q$. Equation [\[eq:tpc59-smaller-than-Q\]](TPC-059.md#ref-eab8986cc2659691) then proves [\[eq:tpc59-admissible-geometric\]](TPC-059.md#ref-dceb0d137125372c) for large $X$. The ratio $$\frac{D_+N_+}{D_-N_-}$$ is bounded by [\[eq:tpc59-support-ratios\]](TPC-059.md#ref-7aebbd2959b9801f), whereas $C_{\rm vh}Q\to\infty$. Hence $y>D_+N_+/(D_-N_-)$, which is equivalent to [\[eq:tpc59-admissible-separation\]](TPC-059.md#ref-78d77cba0057f203).
>
> For completeness, $p>D_+$ prevents a very-high prime from lying in $d_m$, and $n_{m,j}<y^2$ permits at most one prime divisor $p>y$ of $n_{m,j}$. If $n_{m,j}=pr$, the fused label is $d_mn_{m,j}=p(d_mr)$, so the residual label is $s=d_mr$. Finally, if the same $(j,p)$ meets two rows, then $p\mid(m-m')j$. The alternative $p\mid j$ would imply $p\mid h_0$, impossible because $p>|h_0|$. Thus $p\mid m-m'$, but $|m-m'|\le\Delta_M<p$, so $m=m'$. The unique representation [\[eq:tpc59-unique-source-row\]](TPC-059.md#ref-39d66ea54036de2f) then recovers the same full source row.

> **Remark: What the theorem does not say** The constant $C_{\rm vh}$ need not place a previously chosen dyadic cofactor block inside the very-high face. It proves that blocks of exponent $133/400$ are geometrically compatible after a fixed-factor retuning. A positive atomic-energy intersection is a separate occupancy statement.

Put $$\omega_X:=\frac yQ,  \qquad  R_{\rm safe}(y):=\frac{N_-}{2y},  \qquad  R_{\rm max}(y):=\frac{N_+}{y}.  \label{eq:tpc59-omega-safe-max}$$ The names refer to dyadic cofactor blocks $[R,2R)$.

> **Theorem: Cutoff–cofactor reciprocity** \[thm:tpc59-cutoff-cofactor-reciprocity\] Let $y$ be very-high admissible, and let a terminal atom satisfy $$n=mj+h_0=pr,  \qquad N_-\le n\le N_+.  \label{eq:tpc59-terminal-npr}$$ For a dyadic block $R\le r<2R$, one has $$\frac{N_-}{2R}<p\le\frac{N_+}{R}.  \label{eq:tpc59-prime-window-from-R}$$ Consequently:
>
> 1.  if $R<R_{\rm safe}(y)$, every terminal prime in the block is automatically greater than $y$;
>
> 2.  if $R>R_{\rm max}(y)$, the block contains no very-high atom;
>
> 3.  the interval between $R_{\rm safe}(y)$ and $R_{\rm max}(y)$ meets only $O(1)$ dyadic scales.
>
> Moreover, $$\boxed{  R_{\rm safe}(y)\asymp R_{\rm max}(y)  \asymp\frac J{\omega_X}.}  \label{eq:tpc59-cutoff-band-reciprocity}$$ All implied constants depend only on the fixed support ratios.

> **Proof** Since $r<2R$ and $n\ge N_-$, one has $p=n/r>N_-/(2R)$. Since $r\ge R$ and $n\le N_+$, one has $p\le N_+/R$, proving [\[eq:tpc59-prime-window-from-R\]](TPC-059.md#ref-7056b468e563afa5). If $R<N_-/(2y)$, the lower endpoint is greater than $y$. If $R>N_+/y$, then $p>y$ and $r\ge R$ would give $n=pr>yR>N_+$, a contradiction. Finally, $$\frac{R_{\rm max}(y)}{R_{\rm safe}(y)}  =2\frac{N_+}{N_-}\asymp1,$$ so only a bounded number of dyadic scales lies between them. Using $N_\pm\asymp X\asymp QJ$ and $y=\omega_XQ$ proves [\[eq:tpc59-cutoff-band-reciprocity\]](TPC-059.md#ref-4a724869c0b1c385).

###### ref-a94d8febbe976105

###### ref-8e55dbfe61675cc9

> **Corollary: Three cutoff regimes** \[cor:tpc59-three-cutoff-regimes\] Suppose $\omega_X=X^{\xi+o(1)}$ with $\xi\ge0$. Then the top compatible cofactor exponent is $$\theta_R=\frac{133}{400}-\xi+o(1).  \label{eq:tpc59-compatible-theta-R}$$ In particular:
>
> 1.  if $\omega_X\asymp1$, compatible blocks have $R\asymp J$, with no geometrically forced positive-power relocation;
>
> 2.  if $\omega_X=X^{o(1)}$, the displacement is subpower and has zero fixed exponent;
>
> 3.  if $\omega_X=X^{\eta+o(1)}$ for fixed $\eta>0$, every compatible block satisfies $R\le X^{133/400-\eta+o(1)}$ and is eventually disjoint from any fixed dyadic block $R\asymp J$.

The last case is precisely the face audited in TPC–58. The first case is not a contradiction; it uses the same literal unique-prime theorem with a sharper admissible cutoff. No coefficient, norm, or physical shift has been changed.

# Exact cofactor occupancy and terminal activation

The critical cutoff makes a cofactor window possible at the desired exponent. We now determine exactly what is needed for the literal carrier to place energy in that window. This is an orthogonal projection problem before terminal grouping and therefore admits a sharp rowwise solution.

## Literal pre-terminal energy

###### ref-1eb73535041b0018

On one declared source system, let $\mathcal I_X$ be the fixed retained set of $(m,r)$-pairs, $\mathcal J_{X,a}$ the fixed orbit class, and $\mathcal O_X$ the complete opposite-row cell. The TPC–53/57 factorization can be written $$_{m,r,j,\gamma}  =c_{m,j,\gamma}\zeta_m,  \qquad  (m,r)\in\mathcal I_X,\quad j\in\mathcal J_{X,a},\quad\gamma\in\mathcal O_X,  \label{eq:tpc59-literal-preterminal-factorization}$$ where $c_{m,j,\gamma}$ contains the source shape with its Möbius and prime-logarithm factors, literal mask, opposite-row factor, orbit phase, and four-factor profile, but not the bounded row coefficient $\zeta_m$. > **Theorem: Sharp rowwise occupancy minimax** \[thm:tpc59-rowwise-occupancy-minimax\] Assume $\mathcal E_{\rm par}$ is not identically zero on the coefficient cube $|\zeta_m|\le1$. The optimal coefficient-uniform projection constant is $$\boxed{  \begin{aligned}  \beta_{\mathcal R}  &:={}  \inf_{\substack{|\zeta_m|\le1\\\mathcal E_{\rm par}(\zeta)>0}}  \frac{\mathcal E_{\mathcal R}(\zeta)}{\mathcal E_{\rm par}(\zeta)}\\  &=\min_{m:B_mN(m)>0}\frac{N_{\mathcal R}(m)}{N(m)}.  \end{aligned}}  \label{eq:tpc59-beta-R-minimax}$$ In particular, $$\beta_{\mathcal R}>0  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad  N_{\mathcal R}(m)>0  \quad\text{for every row with }B_mN(m)>0.  \label{eq:tpc59-beta-positive-test}$$ The formula is sharp at every finite $X$.

###### ref-0791ddef6631df36

> **Proof** For $B_mN(m)>0$, write $$\rho_m:=\frac{N_{\mathcal R}(m)}{N(m)},  \qquad  w_m:=B_mN(m)|\zeta_m|^2.$$ Whenever the denominator is positive, $$\frac{\mathcal E_{\mathcal R}(\zeta)}{\mathcal E_{\rm par}(\zeta)}  =\frac{\sum_mw_m\rho_m}{\sum_mw_m},  \label{eq:tpc59-occupancy-convex-combination}$$ which is a convex combination of the row ratios. It is at least their minimum. Conversely, choose a minimizing row $m_*$, set $\zeta_{m_*}=1$, and set every other row coefficient to zero. Then the ratio equals $\rho_{m_*}$. The positivity test follows immediately.

> **Corollary: Sharp zero-occupancy obstruction** \[cor:tpc59-zero-occupancy-obstruction\] If one active row has retained cofactors but none in $\mathcal R$, then $\beta_{\mathcal R}=0$. A fixed-factor cutoff retuning can change which existing labels are compatible, but it cannot create their energy.

###### ref-13b155672d3b50f3

> **Proposition: Exact terminal-activation minimax** \[prop:tpc59-terminal-activation-minimax\] Assume $\mathcal E_{\mathcal R}$ is not identically zero. The optimal uniform lower constant from compatible pre-terminal energy to its very-high terminal diagonal is $$\boxed{  \tau_{\mathcal R}  :=\inf_{\substack{|\zeta_m|\le1\\\mathcal E_{\mathcal R}(\zeta)>0}}    \frac{\mathcal D_{\mathcal R}^{\rm term}(\zeta)}{\mathcal E_{\mathcal R}(\zeta)}  =\min_{m:B_mN_{\mathcal R}(m)>0}    \frac{T_{\mathcal R}(m)}{B_mN_{\mathcal R}(m)}.}  \label{eq:tpc59-tau-R-minimax}$$ The exact combined parent-to-terminal constant is $$\boxed{  \chi_{\mathcal R}  :=\inf_{\substack{|\zeta_m|\le1\\\mathcal E_{\rm par}(\zeta)>0}}    \frac{\mathcal D_{\mathcal R}^{\rm term}(\zeta)}{\mathcal E_{\rm par}(\zeta)}  =\min_{m:B_mN(m)>0}\frac{T_{\mathcal R}(m)}{B_mN(m)},}  \label{eq:tpc59-combined-minimax}$$ with the convention $T_{\mathcal R}(m)=0$ if $N_{\mathcal R}(m)=0$. Moreover, $$\chi_{\mathcal R}\ge\beta_{\mathcal R}\tau_{\mathcal R}.  \label{eq:tpc59-combined-product-lower}$$ None of these exact formulas asserts that the displayed constant is positive.

> **Proof** The two minimax formulas follow from the same weighted-average argument as [\[thm:tpc59-rowwise-occupancy-minimax\]](TPC-059.md#ref-5fd8e2f7e332cdd0), concentrating $\zeta$ on one row for sharpness. If an active parent row has $N_{\mathcal R}(m)=0$, then $\beta_{\mathcal R}=\chi_{\mathcal R}=0$, and [\[eq:tpc59-combined-product-lower\]](TPC-059.md#ref-13b155672d3b50f3) is immediate. Otherwise every active parent row has $N_{\mathcal R}(m)>0$, and $$\frac{T_{\mathcal R}(m)}{B_mN(m)}  =\frac{N_{\mathcal R}(m)}{N(m)}   \frac{T_{\mathcal R}(m)}{B_mN_{\mathcal R}(m)}.$$ Taking rowwise minima proves [\[eq:tpc59-combined-product-lower\]](TPC-059.md#ref-13b155672d3b50f3).

The inherited hypotheses impose no prime-distribution lower bound on $T_{\mathcal R}(m)$. Thus $\tau_{\mathcal R}$ and $\chi_{\mathcal R}$ may vanish even when $\beta_{\mathcal R}>0$. Conversely, a positive terminal face in selected rows does not repair a row with zero compatible occupancy. This formally separates the last two gates in [\[eq:tpc59-three-gates\]](TPC-059.md#ref-120ab46a9d4fcdd7).

> **Remark: Activation constants are not probabilities** The literal multiplier $|\Lambda_X(n)|^2$ remains inside $T_{\mathcal R}(m)$. Consequently $\tau_{\mathcal R}$ and $\chi_{\mathcal R}$ are energy-transfer constants and can exceed one; only their possible vanishing and their polynomial lower scale are relevant to the loss ledger.

# Largest-prime census and zero-cost dyadic localization

The preceding minimax constants are exact but still refer to a terminal indicator. We now identify that indicator arithmetically and show that, once very-high terminal energy exists, localizing it to one cofactor block costs only a logarithm.

## The exact largest-prime face

###### ref-2e6d9950dd9c7249

Adopt the convention $P^{+}(1)=1$. Let $\mathcal U_{h_0}^{\rm term}$ be the complete literal terminal universe of TPC–57, and define $$\mathcal U_{h_0,>y}^{\rm term}  :=\{u=(m,r,j,\gamma)\in\mathcal U_{h_0}^{\rm term}:p_u>y\}.  \label{eq:tpc59-very-high-terminal-universe}$$

###### ref-850b5a712f7c8185

###### ref-c94db4d1bc7782f2

###### ref-2de781ca4213ae47

> **Theorem: Largest-prime census** \[thm:tpc59-largest-prime-census\] Assume $y>\sqrt{N_+}$, and fix $(m,j,\gamma)$ with $n=mj+h_0\in[N_-,N_+]$. There is at most one terminal atom over this triple with $p>y$. Such an atom exists exactly when $$p_*:=P^{+}(n)>y,  \qquad  r_*:=\frac n{p_*},  \qquad  (m,r_*)\in\mathcal I_X,  \label{eq:tpc59-largest-prime-retained-test}$$ and $(m,r_*,j,\gamma)$ satisfies the remaining inherited terminal masks, squarefree conditions, coprimality conditions, and $\Lambda_X(n)\ne0$. In that case $$p_u=p_* = P^{+}(mj+h_0),  \qquad  r_u=\frac{mj+h_0}{P^{+}(mj+h_0)}.  \label{eq:tpc59-largest-prime-formula}$$

> **Proof** If two distinct primes greater than $y$ divided $n$, their product would exceed $y^2>N_+\ge n$. Thus a prime divisor above $y$ is unique and necessarily equals $P^{+}(n)$. The equality $p_ur=mj+h_0=n$ then forces $r=r_*$. The retained-pair and remaining terminal conditions are plainly necessary. Conversely, if all displayed and inherited conditions hold, $p_*$ is prime by definition of largest prime factor, the equality $p_*r_*=n$ holds, and the corresponding coordinate belongs to $\mathcal U_{h_0,>y}^{\rm term}$.

###### ref-7c9691061585f7e9

###### ref-6520abdc24edf8a3

###### ref-594e1fce2b44afbc

###### ref-79bb18e6da3e3655

For the literal terminal amplitude $$x_u=\mu(r_u)\Lambda_X(p_ur_u)[G_X^L]_u,  \label{eq:tpc59-literal-terminal-amplitude-repeat}$$ define the very-high atomic diagonal $$\mathcal D_H(y;\zeta)  :=\sum_{u\in\mathcal U_{h_0,>y}^{\rm term}}|x_u|^2.  \label{eq:tpc59-very-high-terminal-diagonal}$$ The theorem gives the exact weighted census $$\boxed{  \mathcal D_H(y;\zeta)  =\sum_{m,j,\gamma}    \mathbf 1_{\rm vh}(m,j,\gamma;y)    |\Lambda_X(mj+h_0)|^2    |c_{m,j,\gamma}|^2|\zeta_m|^2,}  \label{eq:tpc59-largest-prime-energy-census}$$ where $\mathbf 1_{\rm vh}$ is precisely the test in [\[thm:tpc59-largest-prime-census\]](TPC-059.md#ref-850b5a712f7c8185). Under the sign calibration of TPC–58, $x_u=-\mu(d_mr_u)b_{i,d_mr_up_u}$, so $$|x_u|^2=|b_{i,d_mr_up_u}|^2.  \label{eq:tpc59-terminal-Walsh-atomic-match}$$ Thus every matched terminal/Walsh coordinate has the same raw atomic weight on both sides of the dictionary [`\citep{WangTPC58}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-059.md#cite-578b76b7099ebcf1). This coordinate injection is not asserted to be surjective onto the full Walsh carrier.

The census is an identity, not a density theorem. It permits $\mathcal D_H(y;\zeta)=0$ while the parent or full Walsh diagonal is positive: the targets may all be $y$-smooth, the required complementary cofactors may be absent from $\mathcal I_X$, terminal masks may fail, or the distinguished row coefficients may vanish on every surviving row.

## Exact dyadic partition

###### ref-6c241bbbb089fb4d

###### ref-ce61a2c40e5236ab

###### ref-f68dcb5d220175f0

###### ref-500d799318c22199

Put $$R_+(y):=\frac{N_+}{y}.  \label{eq:tpc59-dyadic-R-plus}$$ If $R_+(y)<1$, the very-high terminal face is empty. Otherwise, for integers $$0\le k\le K_X:=\left\lfloor\log_2R_+(y)\right\rfloor,  \label{eq:tpc59-dyadic-index-range}$$ define the half-open cofactor cells $$\mathcal B_k(y)  :=\{u\in\mathcal U_{h_0,>y}^{\rm term}:2^k\le r_u<2^{k+1}\}  \label{eq:tpc59-dyadic-terminal-cells}$$ and their atomic energies $$\mathcal D_k(y;\zeta):=\sum_{u\in\mathcal B_k(y)}|x_u|^2.  \label{eq:tpc59-dyadic-terminal-energy}$$

###### ref-5f3fc72550923123

###### ref-36204d0d0e597049

###### ref-b81c58da03945e48

###### ref-cf25f6fe0660c65d

> **Theorem: Exact dyadic localization** \[thm:tpc59-exact-dyadic-localization\] The cells $\mathcal B_k(y)$ form a disjoint partition of the very-high terminal face and $$\boxed{  \mathcal D_H(y;\zeta)=\sum_{k=0}^{K_X}\mathcal D_k(y;\zeta).}  \label{eq:tpc59-dyadic-energy-partition}$$ If $\mathcal D_H(y;\zeta)>0$, let $k_*$ be the least index attaining the maximum dyadic energy. Then $$\boxed{  \mathcal D_{k_*}(y;\zeta)  \ge\frac{\mathcal D_H(y;\zeta)}{B_X(y)},  \qquad  B_X(y):=1+\left\lfloor\log_2R_+(y)\right\rfloor.}  \label{eq:tpc59-dyadic-pigeonhole}$$ For every admissible endpoint cutoff $y\ge C_{\rm vh}Q$, $$B_X(y)=O(\log X)=X^{o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc59-dyadic-count-subpower}$$ Hence dyadic localization inside an already activated very-high terminal face has zero fixed-power loss.

> **Proof** Every very-high atom has positive integral $r_u\le N_+/p_u<N_+/y$, so it lies in exactly one half-open cell in the displayed index range. Raw atomic energy is additive across disjoint coordinate sets, proving [\[eq:tpc59-dyadic-energy-partition\]](TPC-059.md#ref-36204d0d0e597049). The maximum of $B_X(y)$ nonnegative numbers is at least their average. Finally, $R_+(y)\le N_+/(C_{\rm vh}Q)=X^{133/400+o(1)}$, proving [\[eq:tpc59-dyadic-count-subpower\]](TPC-059.md#ref-cf25f6fe0660c65d).

###### ref-a9df072199377b45

In the TPC–58 loss notation, the theorem proves $$\boxed{\lambda_{\rm blk}=0}  \label{eq:tpc59-zero-block-exponent}$$ conditional on a lower bound for $\mathcal D_H$. It does not prove a parent-to-high-face energy transfer: the ratio $\mathcal D_H/\mathcal E_{\rm par}$, or even $\mathcal D_H/\mathcal D_0$, remains uncontrolled.

## The base/represented/missing decomposition and its limit

###### ref-d5a045a73888a19b

###### ref-985a4ab821586f51

###### ref-63ab3ace08cc4bd8

###### ref-abf635f6ee72df76

###### ref-cfc0dacb163ba4b0

###### ref-fad30341c76de4d6

Let $\mathcal W_0$ be the complete declared Walsh slice and define its raw atomic diagonal by $$\mathcal D_0:=\sum_{w\in\mathcal W_0}|b_w|^2.  \label{eq:tpc59-full-declared-Walsh-diagonal}$$ Let $\mathcal W_H(y)\subseteq\mathcal W_0$ be the set of high Walsh atoms in that slice, namely those whose unique very-high prime exceeds $y$, and let $\iota:\mathcal U_{h_0,>y}^{\rm term}\to\mathcal W_H(y)$ be the injective terminal/Walsh coordinate map. Its image retains exactly the retained-pair and terminal-mask conditions in [\[thm:tpc59-largest-prime-census\]](TPC-059.md#ref-850b5a712f7c8185). Define $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal D_{\rm base}(y)  &:=\sum_{w\in\mathcal W_0\setminus\mathcal W_H(y)}|b_w|^2,  \label{eq:tpc59-base-Walsh-energy}\\  \mathcal D_{\rm miss}(y)  &:=\sum_{w\in\mathcal W_H(y)\setminus\operatorname{Ran}\iota}|b_w|^2.  \label{eq:tpc59-missing-high-Walsh-energy}\end{aligned}$$ The second term is the high Walsh mass whose complementary cofactor is absent from the retained carrier or whose remaining terminal conditions fail. Unique very-high factorization and the atomic norm match [\[eq:tpc59-terminal-Walsh-atomic-match\]](TPC-059.md#ref-79bb18e6da3e3655) now give the exact three-part decomposition $$\boxed{  \mathcal D_0  =\mathcal D_{\rm base}(y)+\mathcal D_{\rm miss}(y)   +\sum_{k=0}^{K_X}\mathcal D_k(y).}  \label{eq:tpc59-base-high-partition}$$ In particular, with $\mathcal D_{\rm off}(y):=\mathcal D_{\rm base}(y)+\mathcal D_{\rm miss}(y)$, either $$\mathcal D_{\rm off}(y)\ge\frac12\mathcal D_0  \label{eq:tpc59-base-dominates}$$ or some represented high cofactor block satisfies $$\mathcal D_k(y)\ge\frac{\mathcal D_0}{2B_X(y)}.  \label{eq:tpc59-high-block-dominates}$$ This dichotomy is unconditional, but its favorable alternative is not. A proof requiring the represented high face cannot discard either the base mass or the unrepresented high mass. In particular, the possibility $\mathcal D_{\rm miss}>0$ is exactly why the largest-prime census may vanish while the full Walsh diagonal is positive.

## Why coherent energy is not a dyadic measure

###### ref-5cfc8d44f486055b

###### ref-e82a6022b77a85bd

Let $q(u)=(i,s(u))$ be the residual output key and define $$A_k(i,s)  :=\sum_{\substack{u\in\mathcal B_k(y)\\q(u)=(i,s)}}x_u.  \label{eq:tpc59-dyadic-grouped-vector}$$ The complete very-high residual vector is $A_H=\sum_kA_k$, and therefore $$\mathcal G_H=\left\|\sum_kA_k\right\|_2^2,  \label{eq:tpc59-grouped-not-additive}$$ not $\sum_k\|A_k\|_2^2$.

###### ref-3851dda96ff18a8c

###### ref-32f5ab4ea2933481

###### ref-f4ceb786de792a60

There is nevertheless one exact locality statement. If two atoms have the same residual key, then $$d_mr=d_{m'}r',  \qquad d_m,d_{m'}\in[D,2D],  \qquad \frac12\le\frac{r'}r\le2.  \label{eq:tpc59-factor-exchange-locality}$$ Thus $$\langle A_k,A_\ell\rangle=0  \qquad(|k-\ell|\ge2),  \label{eq:tpc59-dyadic-orthogonality}$$ and $$\boxed{  \mathcal G_H  =\sum_k\|A_k\|_2^2   +2\operatorname{Re}\sum_k\langle A_k,A_{k+1}\rangle.}  \label{eq:tpc59-block-tridiagonal-grouping}$$ Adjacent blocks can still cancel exactly. After the native erasure $(i,s)\mapsto i$, even this residual-label locality is lost because different $s$ values share the same native output. Hence the atomic pigeonhole theorem cannot be applied to a coherent norm without a new lower-reassembly estimate.

# The selected cofactor core and the undeleted singleton bridge

The dyadic theorem localizes raw terminal energy, not a coherent output norm. We now connect one selected cell to the complete undeleted residual vector by the full-fiber singleton bridge of TPC–57. The distinction between a very-high root and its full terminal fiber is essential: a collision partner of a root with $p>y$ need not itself remain on the very-high face [`\citep{WangTPC57}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-059.md#cite-d87f234f729af8b2).

## Core energy and full active multiplicity

###### ref-746e70018798c1d1

###### ref-2d37705a4997a981

###### ref-324c827264e530a8

###### ref-21a29d975e1aaa8f

Let $$\mathcal U_{h_0}^{\rm phys}(\zeta)  :=\{u\in\mathcal U_{h_0}^{\rm term}:x_u\ne0\},  \qquad q(u)=(i(u),s(u)),  \label{eq:tpc59-full-physical-terminal-set}$$ and define the complete active degree of a residual fiber by $$n_a^{\rm phys}(\zeta)  :=\bigl|q^{-1}(a)\cap\mathcal U_{h_0}^{\rm phys}(\zeta)\bigr|.  \label{eq:tpc59-full-physical-degree}$$ The degree in [\[eq:tpc59-full-physical-degree\]](TPC-059.md#ref-2d37705a4997a981) is computed in the whole terminal vector, without imposing $p>y$ or a cofactor block. The complete undeleted residual energy is $$\mathcal G_{\rm full}(\zeta)  :=\sum_a\left|       \sum_{\substack{u\in\mathcal U_{h_0}^{\rm term}\\q(u)=a}}x_u      \right|^2.  \label{eq:tpc59-complete-grouped-energy}$$ The high-only residual energy used by the TPC–58 high-column interface is $$\mathcal G_H(y;\zeta)  :=\sum_a\left|       \sum_{\substack{u\in\mathcal U_{h_0,>y}^{\rm term}\\q(u)=a}}x_u      \right|^2.  \label{eq:tpc59-high-only-grouped-energy}$$ This is the same quantity as $\mathcal G_H$ in [\[eq:tpc59-grouped-not-additive\]](TPC-059.md#ref-e82a6022b77a85bd). The full degree below is deliberately stronger than the active degree inside the high-only vector.

###### ref-e22e7c806409f20f

###### ref-1c5602baf4589fd6

###### ref-0d4ce02598dc0535

For a half-open cofactor core $$\mathcal B_R(y)  :=\{u\in\mathcal U_{h_0,>y}^{\rm term}:R\le r_u<2R\},  \label{eq:tpc59-very-high-core}$$ put $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal D_R(y;\zeta)  &:=\sum_{u\in\mathcal B_R(y)}|x_u|^2,  \label{eq:tpc59-core-terminal-diagonal}\\  \mathcal I_R^{\rm full}(y;\zeta)  &:=\sum_{u\in\mathcal B_R(y)\cap\mathcal U_{h_0}^{\rm phys}(\zeta)}        |x_u|^2\bigl(n_{q(u)}^{\rm phys}(\zeta)-1\bigr).  \label{eq:tpc59-core-full-incidence}\end{aligned}$$ Thus $\mathcal I_R^{\rm full}$ is a rooted, coefficient-weighted incidence, not an unweighted pair count and not a multiplicity computed after restricting the vector to $\mathcal B_R(y)$.

###### ref-39206bf189cc941c

###### ref-f1bbd418c4878878

###### ref-46132539b7c8ce80

###### ref-2a1ac73195e46bd5

###### ref-63de951bedc707fd

> **Theorem: Very-high core to high-only and full-output singleton bridge** \[thm:tpc59-core-full-singleton-bridge\] For every fixed $h_0\ne0$, every admissible cutoff $y$, every cofactor scale $R$, and every distinguished coefficient vector, $$\boxed{  \min\{\mathcal G_H(y;\zeta),\mathcal G_{\rm full}(\zeta)\}  \ge  \bigl(\mathcal D_R(y;\zeta)-\mathcal I_R^{\rm full}(y;\zeta)\bigr)_+.}  \label{eq:tpc59-core-full-singleton-floor}$$ Consequently, if $\mathcal D_R(y;\zeta)>0$ and $$\mathcal I_R^{\rm full}(y;\zeta)  \le(1-\delta_R)\mathcal D_R(y;\zeta),  \qquad 0<\delta_R\le1,  \label{eq:tpc59-core-incidence-criterion}$$ then $$\boxed{  \min\{\mathcal G_H(y;\zeta),\mathcal G_{\rm full}(\zeta)\}  \ge\delta_R\mathcal D_R(y;\zeta).}  \label{eq:tpc59-core-positive-full-output}$$ If $R=2^{k_*}$ is the maximal-energy cell selected in [\[thm:tpc59-exact-dyadic-localization\]](TPC-059.md#ref-5f3fc72550923123), then $$\min\{\mathcal G_H(y;\zeta),\mathcal G_{\rm full}(\zeta)\}  \ge\frac{\delta_R}{B_X(y)}\mathcal D_H(y;\zeta)  \label{eq:tpc59-selected-block-singleton-transfer}$$ whenever [\[eq:tpc59-core-incidence-criterion\]](TPC-059.md#ref-46132539b7c8ce80) holds.

> **Proof** If $n_a^{\rm phys}=1$, the unique active atom in the full fiber $a$ survives with exactly its atomic energy both in [\[eq:tpc59-complete-grouped-energy\]](TPC-059.md#ref-324c827264e530a8) and, when it is a very-high root, in [\[eq:tpc59-high-only-grouped-energy\]](TPC-059.md#ref-21a29d975e1aaa8f). Hence each of $\mathcal G_H$ and $\mathcal G_{\rm full}$ dominates the energy of all core roots whose full active degree is one. A core root with full degree at least two contributes at most $|x_u|^2(n_{q(u)}^{\rm phys}-1)$ to the energy removed from this singleton subcollection. Summing over core roots proves $\min\{\mathcal G_H,\mathcal G_{\rm full}\}\ge \mathcal D_R-\mathcal I_R^{\rm full}$; nonnegativity gives [\[eq:tpc59-core-full-singleton-floor\]](TPC-059.md#ref-f1bbd418c4878878). Equation [\[eq:tpc59-core-positive-full-output\]](TPC-059.md#ref-2a1ac73195e46bd5) follows from [\[eq:tpc59-core-incidence-criterion\]](TPC-059.md#ref-46132539b7c8ce80). For the selected dyadic cell, combine it with [\[eq:tpc59-dyadic-pigeonhole\]](TPC-059.md#ref-b81c58da03945e48).

No comparison between the restricted grouped norm $$\sum_a\left|    \sum_{u\in q^{-1}(a)\cap\mathcal B_R(y)}x_u  \right|^2$$ and either $\mathcal G_H(y;\zeta)$ or $\mathcal G_{\rm full}(\zeta)$ occurs in the proof. Restoring omitted coordinates can cancel a restricted coherent sum. The theorem instead retains only roots that are singletons in the complete active fiber system. Those roots survive in particular in the high-only residual vector needed by the downstream native-erasure map.

## Factor-exchange locality of every full partner

###### ref-9695858490b626aa

Define the enlarged, unrestricted terminal neighborhood $$\mathcal B_R^+  :=\{v\in\mathcal U_{h_0}^{\rm term}:R/2\le r_v<4R\}.  \label{eq:tpc59-enlarged-full-terminal-block}$$ Unlike $\mathcal B_R(y)$, this set does not impose $p_v>y$.

###### ref-6c281a394eb1888e

###### ref-b77c27e21b7ec0f5

###### ref-421880fc4c260a5c

###### ref-9c77bf33c2021296

> **Proposition: Every full partner is cofactor-local** \[prop:tpc59-full-partner-locality\] If $u\in\mathcal B_R(y)$ and $v\in\mathcal U_{h_0}^{\rm term}$ satisfies $q(v)=q(u)$, then $$\boxed{R/2\le r_v<4R.}  \label{eq:tpc59-full-partner-range}$$ Consequently, for every physical core root, $$n_{q(u)}^{\rm phys}(\zeta)  =\bigl|q^{-1}(q(u))\cap        \mathcal U_{h_0}^{\rm phys}(\zeta)\cap\mathcal B_R^+\bigr|,  \label{eq:tpc59-full-degree-localized}$$ and the exact incidence is $$\boxed{  \mathcal I_R^{\rm full}  =\sum_{\substack{u\in\mathcal B_R(y)\cap\mathcal U_{h_0}^{\rm phys}(\zeta)\\                    v\in\mathcal B_R^+\cap\mathcal U_{h_0}^{\rm phys}(\zeta)\\                    v\ne u,\ q(v)=q(u)}}|x_u|^2.}  \label{eq:tpc59-local-rooted-pair-identity}$$ When $R$ is dyadic, the enlarged range is the union of three adjacent half-open dyadic cells, so localization of all partners has $O(1)$ covering cost.

###### ref-f0d06c1d56fc497b

> **Proof** Write the two source rows as $m=d\ell$ and $m'=d'\ell'$. Equality of the residual keys gives $$dr=d'r'.  \label{eq:tpc59-partner-factor-exchange}$$ Both source divisors belong to the same declared cell $[D,2D]$. Therefore $$r'=\frac d{d'}r,  \qquad \frac12\le\frac d{d'}\le2.$$ Together with $R\le r<2R$, this proves [\[eq:tpc59-full-partner-range\]](TPC-059.md#ref-b77c27e21b7ec0f5). Thus every full physical partner is already present in $\mathcal B_R^+$, proving [\[eq:tpc59-full-degree-localized\]](TPC-059.md#ref-421880fc4c260a5c); expanding $n_{q(u)}^{\rm phys}-1$ then gives [\[eq:tpc59-local-rooted-pair-identity\]](TPC-059.md#ref-9c77bf33c2021296).

The proposition is stronger than merely checking collisions inside the selected very-high cell, but weaker than an occupancy estimate. A carrier made entirely of active doubletons obeys the same $O(1)$ partner locality and still has no singleton energy. Thus bounded scale displacement does not make $\delta_R$ in [\[eq:tpc59-core-incidence-criterion\]](TPC-059.md#ref-46132539b7c8ce80) positive.

## Critical compatibility does not create occupancy

###### ref-926b133166adfc90

At the critical cutoff $y=C_{\rm vh}Q$, [\[thm:tpc59-cutoff-cofactor-reciprocity\]](TPC-059.md#ref-6f925b3a61f55d1c) gives $$R_{\rm safe}(y)\asymp R_{\rm max}(y)\asymp J.  \label{eq:tpc59-critical-singleton-compatible-scale}$$ Hence the exponent $R\asymp J$ is once again compatible with the very-high dictionary. This removes the fixed positive-power support gap created by $y=X^{267/400+\eta}$; it proves none of the following:

1.  that the fixed retained-pair carrier contains a cofactor in the critical window on every active row;

2.  that such a coordinate terminalizes or has nonzero physical energy;

3.  that the energy-rich dyadic cell $k_*$ from [\[thm:tpc59-exact-dyadic-localization\]](TPC-059.md#ref-5f3fc72550923123) lies at scale $J$; or

4.  that a positive weighted fraction of its full fibers are singletons.

The first two failures are measured by the occupancy and terminal minimax constants of [3](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-059.md#src-506bc48ccecc3bc5); the fourth is precisely [\[eq:tpc59-core-incidence-criterion\]](TPC-059.md#ref-46132539b7c8ce80).

###### ref-81eb0450ab464f9f

###### ref-72738fc30809cfff

###### ref-171d752fd65f8c97

For comparison, retain the fixed-power cutoff $$y=X^{267/400+\eta},  \qquad \eta>0.  \label{eq:tpc59-fixed-eta-singleton-face}$$ Then every very-high atom satisfies $$r\le R_{\rm max}(y)  =X^{133/400-\eta+o(1)},  \label{eq:tpc59-fixed-eta-compatible-ceiling}$$ whereas an inherited root with $R\asymp J$ and every one of its factor-exchange partners lie in a constant-factor neighborhood of $J$. Thus, for large $X$, $$=\varnothing.  \label{eq:tpc59-fixed-eta-factor-exchange-gap}$$ The factor-exchange identity does not transport an old atom across this polynomial gap: it only locates other atoms already sharing the same full residual key. In particular, it cannot turn geometric incompatibility into cofactor occupancy or create terminal energy in a missing block.

## The remaining arithmetic door

###### ref-f3f40d3593975426

After high-face activation and dyadic localization, the exact remaining singleton target is $$\boxed{  \mathcal I_{R_*}^{\rm full}(y;\zeta)  \le(1-\delta_{R_*})\mathcal D_{R_*}(y;\zeta),  \qquad  \delta_{R_*}\ge X^{-\lambda_{\rm sing}+o(1)},}  \label{eq:tpc59-remaining-weighted-incidence-gate}$$ for the distinguished energy-rich block $R_*=2^{k_*}$, or a stronger coefficient-uniform analogue on a fixed carrier. By [\[eq:tpc59-local-rooted-pair-identity\]](TPC-059.md#ref-9c77bf33c2021296), this is a local, weighted one-atom-versus-two-atom factor-exchange estimate retaining the literal terminal coefficient and the one physical shift $h_0$. A support-only upper bound for the maximal fiber degree does not imply it.

The bridge theorem and partner localization are unconditional finite-dimensional and literal-support statements (L0/L1). Positivity or a polynomial lower bound for $\delta_{R_*}$ is a new arithmetic input; neither critical cutoff compatibility nor dyadic pigeonholing supplies it. No undeleted asymptotic lower square, native-affine Gram transfer, parity improvement, or twin-prime consequence is claimed here.

# Endpoint exponent audit and the revised loss ledger

## Retiring relocation: feasibility before occupancy

###### ref-0d969efa99622317

TPC–58 used one symbol $\lambda_{\rm reloc}$ for the passage from a parent carrier to the smaller cofactor range produced by its fixed- $\eta$ cutoff [`\citep{WangTPC58}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-059.md#cite-578b76b7099ebcf1). That notation mixed a support feasibility question with an energy-transfer estimate. They are not additive loss exponents. The correct replacement is the ordered pair $$\boxed{  \lambda_{\rm reloc}  \quad\rightsquigarrow\quad  \{\text{cutoff feasibility prerequisite}\}  \ \text{followed by}\ \lambda_{\rm occ}.}  \label{eq:tpc59-relocation-split}$$ The feasibility prerequisite is the trichotomy in [\[thm:tpc59-cutoff-cofactor-reciprocity\]](TPC-059.md#ref-6f925b3a61f55d1c): a candidate block is safe, lies in the bounded boundary region, or is empty. It has no associated lower-energy inequality. At the critical cutoff, blocks at exponent $133/400$ are feasible, so there is no separate geometric exponent to enter in an energy ledger. For the fixed-$\eta$ cutoff, by contrast, the old $R\asymp J$ block is eventually empty; this is a closed gate, not an energy loss equal to $\eta$.

Once feasibility is established, the only parent-to-window energy charge is the actual occupancy exponent. It is an orthogonal coordinate projection onto cofactor labels already present in the parent carrier. Moreover, factor exchange inside one residual fiber satisfies $r'/r\in[1/2,2]$. Every squarefree, coprimality, support, and shaped-terminal factor must stay in $T_{\mathcal R}(m)$. An active compatible row with $T_{\mathcal R}(m)=0$ kills the coefficient-uniform route. If the best activation loss consumes the remaining endpoint slack, the route stops before dyadic or Gram analysis.

3.  *Weighted full-fiber singleton collision.* After $\mathcal D_H>0$, select the maximal atomic block and prove the literal one-sided estimate $$\mathcal I_{R_*}^{\rm full}(y;\zeta)         \le(1-\delta_{R_*})\mathcal D_{R_*}(y;\zeta),         \qquad         \delta_{R_*}\ge X^{-\lambda_{\rm sing}+o(1)}.         \label{eq:tpc59-next-weighted-singleton}$$ The degree is computed in the complete terminal fiber, and every partner in the enlarged block $[R_*/2,4R_*)$ is retained whether or not its prime exceeds $y$. Unweighted degree bounds, the coefficient-blind determinant capacity, and singleton counts inside the selected face alone do not prove this inequality. If $\delta_{R_*}=0$, or if every valid lower exponent already exceeds the remaining budget, the present singleton route closes.

4.  *Native residual-label erasure: $\alpha_H$.* Only after the undeleted residual energy has been obtained should one form the nonzero actual image $E_H=\operatorname{Ran}T_H$ and seek $$\alpha_H\ge X^{-\lambda_H+o(1)}.         \label{eq:tpc59-next-native-Gram}$$ The exact uniform kill test remains $$E_H\cap\operatorname{Ker}C_{\mu,H}\ne\{0\}.         \label{eq:tpc59-next-native-kernel-kill}$$ A positive singleton fraction controls the first grouping $p\mapsto(i,s)$; it does not control the second grouping $(i,s)\mapsto i$. A finite-scale numerical eigenvalue is scouting evidence, not a scale-uniform lower certificate.

5.  *Base–high affine transversality: $\delta_{\rm aff}$.* Conditional on the native gate, form the nonzero represented feasible pair image, retain the joint dependence of the base and represented high columns on the same coefficient vector, and prove $$\delta_{\rm aff}         \ge X^{-\lambda_{\rm aff}+o(1)}.         \label{eq:tpc59-next-affine-angle}$$ The exact uniform kill test is a nonzero intersection of the actual feasible pair image with the anti-diagonal, $$\mathcal F_{\rm aff}\cap\{(g,-g):g\in\mathcal H_{\rm out}\}         \ne\{0\}.         \label{eq:tpc59-next-affine-kernel-kill}$$ Treating the base and high vectors as independent, or replacing the physical coefficient vector by a favorable adaptive pair, changes the problem. If the uniform image meets the anti-diagonal, only an explicitly distinguished-vector route may remain.

6.  *Missing-high/off-carrier reassembly.* Let $g_{\rm rep}(\zeta)$ be the represented affine output after Door E and let $h_{\rm miss}(\zeta)$ be the native contribution of the high Walsh coordinates in $\mathcal W_H(y)\setminus\operatorname{Ran}\iota$. Both are driven by the same row coefficient vector. The required lower transfer has the form $$\|g_{\rm rep}(\zeta)+h_{\rm miss}(\zeta)\|_2^2         \ge         X^{-\lambda_{\rm reasm}+o(1)}         \|g_{\rm rep}(\zeta)\|_2^2.         \label{eq:tpc59-next-missing-high-reassembly}$$ Its coefficient-uniform zero test is a nonzero actual pair $(g_{\rm rep},h_{\rm miss})=(g,-g)$. The scalar $\mathcal D_{\rm miss}$ records omitted atomic mass but cannot rule out such vector cancellation. If the anti-diagonal is met, a distinguished-vector estimate or a different carrier is required; simply restoring the omitted coordinates is not norm-monotone.

7.  *Strict endpoint budget and route decision.* Insert every proved exponent into $$\begin{aligned}         \Lambda_{\rm tot}={}&         \lambda_{\rm sel}+\lambda_{\rm occ}         +\lambda_{\rm blk}         +\lambda_{\rm term}+\lambda_{\rm sing}\\         &+\lambda_H+\lambda_{\rm aff}         +\lambda_{\rm reasm}         +\lambda_{\rm bdry}+\lambda_{\rm tail},         \end{aligned}         \label{eq:tpc59-next-complete-budget}$$ retaining $\lambda_{\rm blk}=0$ only with the distinguished-vector scope in [\[eq:tpc59-final-two-zero-costs\]](TPC-059.md#ref-0401913203befb8f); cutoff feasibility is checked before this sum. Continue the endpoint route only if $\Lambda_{\rm tot}<1/400$. At equality, stop unless leading constants and all remainders are closed. If a kernel or zero-occupancy test fires, or a necessary loss alone exhausts the slack, record the resulting route-specific obstruction rather than replacing the physical vector, shift, normalization, or carrier.

#### Conclusion.

The critical-cutoff analysis is a genuine L1 route correction. It proves that the earlier positive-power cofactor displacement was caused by a nonoptimal cutoff schedule, splits geometry from actual occupancy, and shows that dyadic localization costs no positive power after the very-high face has been activated. The surviving route is narrower and auditable: critical-window occupancy, terminal activation, weighted singleton survival, native erasure, represented affine transversality, missing-high reassembly, and the strict endpoint ledger must be passed in that order. None is bypassed here. In particular, this paper contains no L2 parity improvement, prime-pair asymptotic, or twin-prime implication.
