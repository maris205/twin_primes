## Abstract

The critical-cutoff analysis of TPC–59 separates the complete high-prime Walsh face into coordinates represented by the terminal census and high coordinates absent from that image. Restoring the latter is not a monotone operation: their native output may cancel the already represented affine vector. We formulate this reassembly problem on the literal finite row-coefficient space at one fixed nonzero shift.

For linear maps $A,M:\mathcal K\to\mathcal H$, representing respectively the represented affine output and the missing-high correction, put $B=A+M$ and $$\delta_{\rm reasm}  =\inf_{Az\ne0}\frac{\|Bz\|^2}{\|Az\|^2}.$$ The denominator does not see $N=\operatorname{Ker}A$, while $B(N)=M(N)$ can cancel the full output. We prove that the exact quotient is obtained only after shorting $B^*B$ against these nuisance directions. If $S=N^\perp$, $B_1=B|_S$, $B_0=B|_N$, and $P$ projects onto $B(N)^\perp$, then $$\delta_{\rm reasm}  =\inf_{s\in S\setminus\{0\}}    \frac{\|PB_1s\|^2}{\|A s\|^2}.$$ Equivalently, it is the least generalized eigenvalue of the pair $(Q,A_S^*A_S)$, where the shorted numerator is $$Q=B_1^*B_1-B_1^*B_0(B_0^*B_0)^\dagger B_0^*B_1.$$ This yields the exact finite-dimensional positivity test $\operatorname{Ker}(A+M)\subseteq\operatorname{Ker}A$, an anti-diagonal image test, and a rank kill criterion. A naive compression to $N^\perp$ is shown by sharp examples to overestimate the transfer.

We also give a distinguished-vector signed-defect identity and a sharp raw-mass certificate. If each native output receives at most $d_{\rm miss}$ missing atoms, then $$\|Mz\|^2\le d_{\rm miss}\mathcal D_{\rm miss}(z).$$ Consequently a missing-mass saving $X^{-\sigma_{\rm miss}}$ automatically defeats a fiber-degree cost $X^{\mu_{\rm miss}}$ under the strict exponent inequality $\sigma_{\rm miss}>\mu_{\rm miss}$, relative to the represented output. At exponent equality, finer constant or subpower information decides whether this certificate still has zero fixed-power cost. The terminal image and its complement are identified on the same physical Walsh carrier and raw normalization. No positive bound for the resulting arithmetic constant is proved. Thus the work closes the L0 linear-algebra interface and the L1 fixed-shift dictionary, but proves no parity improvement, prime-pair asymptotic, or twin-prime consequence.

# Introduction

###### ref-e58ada408a735708

The large-prime branch of the TPC program begins with an exact Walsh expansion, resolves a unique very-high prime, transports the resulting atoms through residual fibers, and finally recombines them into the native affine output at a fixed physical shift $h_0\ne0$ [`\citep{WangTPC45,WangTPC53,WangTPC57,WangTPC58}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-060.md#cite-1802a7e8047e595c); [\[WangTPC53\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-060.md#cite-d85e91fc7a3dbaf8); [\[WangTPC57\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-060.md#cite-d87f234f729af8b2); [\[WangTPC58\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-060.md#cite-578b76b7099ebcf1). TPC–59 makes a necessary correction to that chain. Its largest-prime census injects the retained terminal atoms into the high Walsh carrier, but it need not cover the full carrier. With the notation of that paper, $$\mathcal D_0  =\mathcal D_{\rm base}+\mathcal D_{\rm miss}   +\mathcal D_H^{\rm rep}.  \label{eq:tpc60-inherited-three-part-diagonal}$$ Here $\mathcal D_H^{\rm rep}$ is the represented high diagonal, while $\mathcal D_{\rm miss}$ is high Walsh mass outside the terminal image [`\citep{WangTPC59}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-060.md#cite-4f045ca3a3f537f2).

###### ref-35088ad042b62a1d

This distinction survives the final grouping. Let $g_{\rm rep}$ be the native affine vector built from the base column and the represented high column, and let $h_{\rm miss}$ be the output of the missing high coordinates. The full physical vector is $$g_{\rm full}=g_{\rm rep}+h_{\rm miss}.  \label{eq:tpc60-full-sum-intro}$$ There is no inequality $\|g_{\rm full}\|\ge\|g_{\rm rep}\|$: the omitted vector can be destructively aligned. Nor does small raw missing mass by itself solve the problem, because many small missing atoms may enter one native output with the same phase.

The purpose of this paper is to identify exactly what must be proved at this reassembly arrow. The answer contains a subtle quotient. Two row coefficient vectors may have the same represented output while having different missing output. Therefore the full map need not descend to the quotient by the kernel of the represented map. The correct uniform constant minimizes over that entire kernel fiber. In matrix language this is a shorted operator, or equivalently a generalized Schur complement [`\citep{AndersonTrapp1975,BenIsraelGreville2003}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-060.md#cite-b8c5a03e9fd5d376); [\[BenIsraelGreville2003\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-060.md#cite-c226866f1a965f0f).

## The three questions

###### ref-d2bfe9b10ed100d2

Fix finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces $\mathcal K$ and $\mathcal H$, and linear maps $$A,M:\mathcal K\longrightarrow\mathcal H,  \qquad B:=A+M.  \label{eq:tpc60-abstract-maps-intro}$$ The TPC specialization is:

-   $A$ is the base plus represented-high affine map;

-   $M$ is the missing-high native grouping;

-   $B$ is the full declared affine output on the same fixed slice.

We answer three separate questions.

###### ref-31a31a0a82b99dd6

1.  What is the optimal coefficient-uniform ratio $$\delta_{\rm reasm}   :=\inf_{Az\ne0}\frac{\|Bz\|^2}{\|Az\|^2}?   \label{eq:tpc60-delta-intro}$$

2.  What exact kernel, image, or rank condition kills that uniform gate?

3.  What arithmetic estimate on missing raw mass and native fiber degree is sufficient for a distinguished-vector or uniform lower transfer?

These questions must not be conflated. A distinguished physical vector can reassemble safely even when the uniform constant vanishes. Conversely, the qualitative kernel condition can hold while the least ratio tends to zero with $X$. Finally, a raw atomic estimate does not determine the sign of the represented–missing cross term.

The first result is the exact nuisance-elimination formula $$\boxed{  \delta_{\rm reasm}  =\inf_{s\in S\setminus\{0\}}    \frac{\|P_W^\perp Bs\|^2}{\|As\|^2}.}  \label{eq:tpc60-main-short-formula-intro}$$ Thus the effective map is not $B|_S$, but its projection away from the output that can be generated freely by $N$. We derive the equivalent shorted-Gram and least-singular-value formulas and prove $$\boxed{  \delta_{\rm reasm}>0  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad  \operatorname{Ker}B\subseteq\operatorname{Ker}A.}  \label{eq:tpc60-main-kernel-intro}$$ The equivalence is finite-dimensional. It is an exact L0 statement; it does not assert the condition for the TPC coefficient family.

For the literal missing atomic lift, let $\mathcal F_i^{\rm miss}$ be the missing atoms entering native output $i$, and put $$d_{\rm miss}:=\max_i|\mathcal F_i^{\rm miss}|,  \qquad  \mathcal D_{\rm miss}(z)  :=\sum_i\sum_{u\in\mathcal F_i^{\rm miss}}|m_u(z)|^2.  \label{eq:tpc60-degree-diagonal-intro}$$ Then the grouping operator has squared norm $d_{\rm miss}$, and hence $$\|Mz\|^2\le d_{\rm miss}\mathcal D_{\rm miss}(z).  \label{eq:tpc60-mass-degree-intro}$$ Together with the reverse triangle inequality this gives the sharp certificate $$\frac{\|Bz\|^2}{\|Az\|^2}  \ge  \left(   1-\sqrt{\frac{d_{\rm miss}\mathcal D_{\rm miss}(z)}{\|Az\|^2}}  \right)_+^2.  \label{eq:tpc60-pointwise-certificate-intro}$$ We exhibit a family saturating the threshold: $d_{\rm miss}=n$, raw missing mass $1/n$, and exact cancellation of a unit represented output.

## Claim boundary and route position

The paper deliberately stops at the first honest boundary. The shorted-Gram theorem, signed-defect identity, and mass–degree estimate are L0. Their construction from the complete high Walsh carrier, the represented terminal image, and its complement at one fixed $h_0$ is L1. No estimate here proves that the missing-mass saving exceeds the fiber-degree cost, that the effective map is injective uniformly in $X$, or that the distinguished physical vector avoids cancellation. Those are new arithmetic gates.

Accordingly, no statement in this paper is an L2 parity advance. In particular, we prove neither a positive fixed-$h_0$ prime-pair lower bound nor an asymptotic for twin primes. The value of the result is diagnostic: it replaces an informal “restore the omitted coordinates” arrow by an optimal constant, an exact zero test, and two quantitatively auditable routes to positivity.

# The represented, missing, and full physical maps

We first place all three outputs on one coefficient space. This prevents two common changes of problem: choosing the missing vector independently of the represented vector, and comparing norms formed from different atomic normalizations.

> **Proposition: Literal three-map identity** \[prop:tpc60-literal-three-map\] There are linear maps $$A_X,M_X,B_X:\mathcal K_X\longrightarrow\mathcal H_{\rm out}  \label{eq:tpc60-three-map-domains}$$ such that, for every physical row coefficient vector $\zeta$, $$\begin{aligned}  A_X\zeta&=v_0(\zeta)+h_R(\zeta),  \label{eq:tpc60-represented-map}\\  M_X\zeta&=h_M(\zeta),  \label{eq:tpc60-missing-map}\\  B_X\zeta&=v_0(\zeta)+h_R(\zeta)+h_M(\zeta)             =(A_X+M_X)\zeta.  \label{eq:tpc60-full-map}\end{aligned}$$ Moreover $h_R=C_{\mu,H}T_H^{\rm rep}\zeta$ is exactly the TPC–58 high map restricted to $\mathcal W_R=\operatorname{Ran}\iota$, while $h_M$ is the same physical grouping restricted to $\mathcal W_M$. Consequently $$\mathcal N_R(\zeta):=\|A_X\zeta\|_2^2,  \qquad  \mathcal N_{\rm full}(\zeta):=\|B_X\zeta\|_2^2  \label{eq:tpc60-represented-full-energies}$$ are the represented and full native affine energies on one and the same declared slice.

> **Proof** Linearity follows from [\[eq:tpc60-literal-atom-linear\]](TPC-060.md#ref-bdbd3141cd42fbe0) and the fixed carrier sums. The gauge alignment of TPC–58 identifies the represented terminal output with $C_{\mu,H}T_H^{\rm rep}\zeta$. Restricting the identical atom-to-native map to the complementary high set defines $M_X$. The disjoint coordinate partition [\[eq:tpc60-W0-partition\]](TPC-060.md#ref-4bf8d935a1a8a538) then gives the last identity before any output norm is taken.

> **Remark: Why the atom partition does not imply output monotonicity** The equality $\mathcal D_0=\mathcal D_B+\mathcal D_R+\mathcal D_M$ is taken before native grouping. After grouping, $$\mathcal N_{\rm full}-\mathcal N_R  =\|h_M\|^2+2\operatorname{Re}\langle A_X\zeta,h_M\rangle.$$ The cross term has no fixed sign. Thus restoring a disjoint atomic coordinate set can decrease the physical output norm.

## Prime-resolved alias structure

###### ref-fdbc4a1c4c63a2ef

###### ref-a0c3836f4b7e3c5c

###### ref-a085135e007944ee

> **Proposition: Same-prime separation and unequal-prime alias** \[prop:tpc60-same-prime-separation\] For every fixed pair $(i,p)$, there is at most one active high Walsh atom. Consequently $$\langle v_p^R,v_p^M\rangle=0  \label{eq:tpc60-same-prime-orthogonality}$$ for every $p$, and $$\sum_p\bigl(\|v_p^R\|^2+\|v_p^M\|^2\bigr)  =\mathcal D_R+\mathcal D_M.  \label{eq:tpc60-prime-resolved-Pythagoras}$$ The exact reassembly defect is $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal N_{\rm full}-\mathcal N_R  ={}&\left\|\sum_qv_q^M\right\|^2  -2\operatorname{Re}\sum_q\langle v_0,v_q^M\rangle  \notag\\  &+2\operatorname{Re}    \sum_{\substack{p,q>y\\p\ne q}}        \langle v_p^R,v_q^M\rangle.  \label{eq:tpc60-exact-prime-alias-defect}\end{aligned}$$ Thus represented–missing cancellation cannot occur at the same prime; the remaining dangers are base–missing interaction and unequal-prime alias at a common native output.

> **Proof** Fix $(i,p)$. Since $i$ fixes $j$, suppose that $p$ meets two source rows $m,m'$. Then $p\mid(m-m')j$. The alternative $p\mid j$ is excluded by $p>J_+$; hence $p\mid m-m'$. But $|m-m'|\le\Delta_M<p$, so $m=m'$, and the inherited unique row representation recovers the same full source atom. This is exactly the TPC–59 critical-cutoff fixed-$(j,p)$ argument [`\citep{WangTPC59}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-060.md#cite-4f045ca3a3f537f2). Thus a fixed $(i,p)$ contains at most one high atom. Such an atom belongs to exactly one of $\mathcal W_R,\mathcal W_M$, proving disjoint coordinate support and [\[eq:tpc60-same-prime-orthogonality\]](TPC-060.md#ref-a55a0d68fdeee6df). Summing the coordinate squares gives [\[eq:tpc60-prime-resolved-Pythagoras\]](TPC-060.md#ref-a085135e007944ee). Finally expand $$\|v_0-\sum_pv_p^R-\sum_qv_q^M\|^2  -\|v_0-\sum_pv_p^R\|^2$$ and delete the same-prime cross terms using [\[eq:tpc60-same-prime-orthogonality\]](TPC-060.md#ref-a55a0d68fdeee6df).

The proposition is useful but does not close reassembly. Different high primes can land at the same native output, and degree two already permits exact cancellation. Prime resolution is therefore a diagnostic layer, not a substitute for the full native Gram.

## The six-item physical audit

The construction above passes the following interface audit.

1.  The coefficients are the literal $b_w=\beta_w\zeta_{m(w)}$.

2.  One fixed physical $h_0$ appears in $n=mj+h_0$.

3.  Every atomic norm is the raw counting norm; no fiber average is inserted.

4.  The atom representation is nonduplicated, and the row space removes only identically null columns. A quotient by $\operatorname{Ker}A_X$ is not taken unless the full map actually descends to it.

6.  Any eventual reassembly loss remains an explicit term in the strict endpoint budget $<1/400$.

# Exact nuisance elimination and the shorted Gram

This section is finite-dimensional linear algebra. It applies at every fixed scale $X$, after which one may ask whether the resulting constants are uniform or polynomially controlled as $X\to\infty$.

###### ref-8dafcc8020998368

###### ref-161bb6bf962d9712

> **Theorem: Exact nuisance elimination** \[thm:tpc60-exact-nuisance-elimination\] For the maps in [\[eq:tpc60-abstract-AMB\]](TPC-060.md#ref-b83484c2fe70a0e9), $$\boxed{  \delta(A,M)  =\inf_{s\in S\setminus\{0\}}    \frac{\|PB_Ss\|^2}{\|A_Ss\|^2}.}  \label{eq:tpc60-exact-nuisance-quotient}$$ For fixed $s$, all minimizing nuisance vectors are $$k_s=-B_N^\dagger B_Ss+k_0,  \qquad k_0\in\operatorname{Ker}B_N.  \label{eq:tpc60-minimizing-nuisance}$$

###### ref-aaf78112056f8208

> **Proof** For fixed $s$, minimizing [\[eq:tpc60-nuisance-numerator\]](TPC-060.md#ref-cff53e51bb931a28) over $k\in N$ is the least-squares problem of approximating $-B_Ss$ by $\operatorname{Ran}B_N$. Hence $$\begin{aligned}  \inf_{k\in N}\|B_Ss+B_Nk\|^2  &=\operatorname{dist}(B_Ss,W)^2  \notag\\  &=\|PB_Ss\|^2.  \label{eq:tpc60-distance-identity}\end{aligned}$$ The Moore–Penrose least-squares formula gives [\[eq:tpc60-minimizing-nuisance\]](TPC-060.md#ref-161bb6bf962d9712). Since $A_S$ is injective, $A_Ss\ne0$ precisely when $s\ne0$. Taking the infimum over $s$ proves [\[eq:tpc60-exact-nuisance-quotient\]](TPC-060.md#ref-8dafcc8020998368).

## Schur complement and shorted operator

###### ref-e6999fa92fd9669a

###### ref-0055047fc5a0ec54

Let $G:=B^*B$, and write its block matrix relative to $\mathcal K=S\oplus N$: $$G=  \begin{pmatrix}   G_{SS}&G_{SN}\\   G_{NS}&G_{NN}  \end{pmatrix}  =  \begin{pmatrix}   B_S^*B_S&B_S^*B_N\\   B_N^*B_S&B_N^*B_N  \end{pmatrix}.  \label{eq:tpc60-G-blocks}$$ Because $G\ge0$, its blocks satisfy the standard range compatibility needed for the generalized Schur complement. Define $$Q  :=G_{SS}-G_{SN}G_{NN}^\dagger G_{NS}.  \label{eq:tpc60-Q-Schur}$$

###### ref-0543927e7794c92b

###### ref-97c109fad4781a6d

###### ref-3d45025bc785d20f

> **Proposition: Shorted-Gram identity** \[prop:tpc60-shorted-Gram-identity\] One has $$\boxed{  Q=B_S^*PB_S\ge0.}  \label{eq:tpc60-Q-projected-Gram}$$ Moreover the short of $G$ to $S$ is $$\operatorname{short}_S(G)  =  \begin{pmatrix}Q&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.  \label{eq:tpc60-shorted-G}$$

###### ref-0285e9ebfe38d745

> **Proof** The Moore–Penrose projection identity gives $$B_N(B_N^*B_N)^\dagger B_N^*  =B_NB_N^\dagger=P_W.  \label{eq:tpc60-BN-projection-identity}$$ Substitution into [\[eq:tpc60-Q-Schur\]](TPC-060.md#ref-0055047fc5a0ec54) yields $$Q=B_S^*(I-P_W)B_S=B_S^*PB_S.$$ The block formula for the finite-dimensional shorted positive operator is then exactly [\[eq:tpc60-shorted-G\]](TPC-060.md#ref-3d45025bc785d20f); see [`\citet{AndersonTrapp1975}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-060.md#cite-b8c5a03e9fd5d376).

###### ref-4368843bd9de7fd6

Let $$R:=A^*A,  \qquad R_S:=A_S^*A_S.  \label{eq:tpc60-RRS}$$ The operator $R_S$ is positive definite on $S$.

###### ref-44503029052531a6

###### ref-2312332cc46ebd96

###### ref-da4329b73fd3daa5

> **Theorem: Exact shorted generalized eigenvalue** \[thm:tpc60-shorted-generalized-eigenvalue\] The optimal reassembly constant is $$\boxed{  \delta(A,M)  =\lambda_{\min}\!\left(   R_S^{-1/2}QR_S^{-1/2}  \right).}  \label{eq:tpc60-delta-generalized-eigenvalue}$$ Equivalently, $$\delta(A,M)  =\lambda_{\min}\!\left(  \left.  R^{\dagger/2}\operatorname{short}_S(B^*B)R^{\dagger/2}  \right|_S  \right).  \label{eq:tpc60-delta-global-shorted}$$

> **Proof** By \[[thm:tpc60-exact-nuisance-elimination](TPC-060.md#ref-b0cccf1de5e4a019),[prop:tpc60-shorted-Gram-identity](TPC-060.md#ref-0543927e7794c92b)\], $$\delta(A,M)  =\inf_{s\ne0}    \frac{\langle Qs,s\rangle}{\langle R_Ss,s\rangle}.$$ Set $x=R_S^{1/2}s$ and apply the Rayleigh–Ritz principle [`\citep{HornJohnson2013}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-060.md#cite-25e2af110ba05633). This proves [\[eq:tpc60-delta-generalized-eigenvalue\]](TPC-060.md#ref-2312332cc46ebd96). On $S$, the restriction of $R^{\dagger/2}$ is $R_S^{-1/2}$, while it vanishes on $N$, giving [\[eq:tpc60-delta-global-shorted\]](TPC-060.md#ref-da4329b73fd3daa5).

## Effective output map

###### ref-59c38ab554185ed2

###### ref-02b2d167173e6347

Let $E:=\operatorname{Ran}A$. The map $$A_S:S\longrightarrow E  \label{eq:tpc60-AS-bijection}$$ is a Hilbert-space isomorphism, with inverse $A^\dagger|_E$. Define $$C_{\rm eff}:=PB_SA_S^{-1}:E\longrightarrow W^\perp.  \label{eq:tpc60-effective-map}$$

###### ref-e8323b62e456e4dd

###### ref-c58f923ce003beef

> **Corollary: Effective singular value** \[cor:tpc60-effective-singular-value\] One has $$\boxed{  \delta(A,M)=\sigma_{\min}(C_{\rm eff})^2.}  \label{eq:tpc60-effective-singular-value}$$ Thus reassembly consists of choosing a represented output $y\in E$, taking its canonical coefficient preimage $A_S^{-1}y$, forming the full output, and projecting away everything producible by an $A$-invisible coefficient direction.

> **Proof** Substitute $y=A_Ss$ in [\[eq:tpc60-exact-nuisance-quotient\]](TPC-060.md#ref-8dafcc8020998368).

## Why ordinary compression is wrong

###### ref-58d219a9b8ce249f

###### ref-edc3196cb2396c91

If one fixes the canonical representative $s\in S$ and forbids motion along $N$, one instead computes $$\delta_{\rm can}  :=\inf_{s\ne0}\frac{\|B_Ss\|^2}{\|A_Ss\|^2}  =\lambda_{\min}    \bigl(R_S^{-1/2}G_{SS}R_S^{-1/2}\bigr).  \label{eq:tpc60-naive-canonical-constant}$$ Since $P$ is a contraction, $$0\le\delta(A,M)\le\delta_{\rm can},  \label{eq:tpc60-short-less-compression}$$ and the inequality may be strict.

###### ref-10974a0f9f63fee6

###### ref-34c5588680da0a1d

\[ex:tpc60-naive-compression-fails\] Take $\mathcal K=\mathbb C^2$, $\mathcal H=\mathbb C$, and $$A(x,k)=x,  \qquad M(x,k)=k,  \qquad B(x,k)=x+k.  \label{eq:tpc60-basic-counterexample}$$ Here $S=\{(x,0)\}$, so the canonical ratio is identically one. However $N=\{(0,k)\}$ and $B(N)=\mathbb C$, hence $P=0$ and $\delta(A,M)=0$. Explicitly, choosing $k=-x$ annihilates the full output without changing $Ax=x$.

###### ref-5660eccbf9258eea

###### ref-ae33a70d57260f12

###### ref-f719c82ece1a0df6

The compressed Gram equals the shorted Gram precisely when the Schur correction vanishes: $$G_{SN}G_{NN}^\dagger G_{NS}=0.  \label{eq:tpc60-zero-Schur-correction}$$ Because this operator is positive, the condition is equivalent to $$\operatorname{Ran}B_S\perp\operatorname{Ran}B_N.  \label{eq:tpc60-orthogonal-SN-output}$$ This operator identity is sufficient, but not necessary, for equality of the two least generalized eigenvalues: a nonzero correction may act only away from the minimizing eigenspace. Thus [\[eq:tpc60-zero-Schur-correction\]](TPC-060.md#ref-5660eccbf9258eea) must not be advertised as an exact criterion for equality of the scalar minima. A particularly safe case is $$B|_{\operatorname{Ker}A}=0.  \label{eq:tpc60-B-descends}$$ Then the full map genuinely descends to $\mathcal K/\operatorname{Ker}A$, $W=\{0\}$, and no shorting is needed. Without [\[eq:tpc60-B-descends\]](TPC-060.md#ref-f719c82ece1a0df6), replacing a coefficient vector by its minimum-norm representative changes the physical full output and is not an admissible simplification.

# Kernel, anti-diagonal, and rank kill tests

The shorted formula gives an exact qualitative test at each finite scale. It also shows why qualitative injectivity is weaker than the polynomial lower bound needed by an endpoint argument.

###### ref-4c86d54edf994038

> **Theorem: Exact positivity test** \[thm:tpc60-exact-positivity-test\] For finite-dimensional $\mathcal K,\mathcal H$ and $A\ne0$, the following are equivalent:
>
> 1.  $\delta(A,M)>0$;
>
> 2.  $PB_S:S\to W^\perp$ is injective;
>
> 3.  $\operatorname{Ker}(A+M)\subseteq\operatorname{Ker}A$;
>
> 4.  there is no $z\in\mathcal K$ with $Az\ne0$ and $(A+M)z=0$.

> **Proof** By [\[cor:tpc60-effective-singular-value\]](TPC-060.md#ref-e8323b62e456e4dd), positivity is equivalent to injectivity of $PB_S$ on $S$. If that map is not injective, there is $s\in S\setminus\{0\}$ with $B_Ss\in W=\operatorname{Ran}B_N$. Choose $k\in N$ so that $B_Nk=-B_Ss$. Then $z=s+k\in\operatorname{Ker}B$, while $Az=A_Ss\ne0$. Conversely, any $z=s+k\in\operatorname{Ker}B\setminus\operatorname{Ker}A$ has $s\ne0$ and $PB_Ss=-PB_Nk=0$. This proves all equivalences.

###### ref-569a4339556254d3

###### ref-9b87635efeb34243

> **Corollary: Dimension kill criterion** \[cor:tpc60-dimension-kill\] If $$\dim S>\dim\mathcal H-\operatorname{rank}B_N,  \label{eq:tpc60-dimension-kill}$$ then $\delta(A,M)=0$.

> **Proof** The codomain $W^\perp$ of $PB_S$ has dimension $\dim\mathcal H-\operatorname{rank}B_N$. Under [\[eq:tpc60-dimension-kill\]](TPC-060.md#ref-9b87635efeb34243), that map cannot be injective.

For the TPC specialization this supplies a finite-scale kill test which can be checked exactly from the literal matrices. If it fires, no uniform loss exponent $\lambda_{\rm reasm}$ exists on the declared coefficient class: the ratio is exactly zero, not merely poorly estimated. If it does not fire, no quantitative asymptotic lower bound follows automatically.

## The pair-image formulation

###### ref-44d8c516896b6df5

###### ref-5778c4cabb533de8

###### ref-3c43b3941d858fba

###### ref-4fc8a4a903c8b524

Define the coupled represented–missing map $$J:\mathcal K\longrightarrow\mathcal H\oplus\mathcal H,  \qquad Jz:=(Az,Mz),  \label{eq:tpc60-coupled-AM-map}$$ and its actual feasible pair image $$\mathcal F_{A,M}:=\operatorname{Ran}J.  \label{eq:tpc60-feasible-pair-image}$$ The physical sum map is $$D_+(g,h):=g+h.  \label{eq:tpc60-pair-sum-map}$$ When $\mathcal F_{A,M}\ne\{0\}$, put $$\delta_{\rm pair}  :=\inf_{(g,h)\in\mathcal F_{A,M}\setminus\{0\}}    \frac{\|g+h\|^2}{\|g\|^2+\|h\|^2}.  \label{eq:tpc60-pair-angle}$$ Here $\mathcal F_{A,M}$ carries the norm inherited from the Hilbert direct sum $\mathcal H\oplus\mathcal H$.

###### ref-4fdd48982517d686

###### ref-eae0d113049acb5a

###### ref-b87573f1c1434ac3

###### ref-d97e374f0793a5ab

> **Proposition: Anti-diagonal test** \[prop:tpc60-anti-diagonal-test\] One has $$\delta_{\rm pair}  =\sigma_{\min}\!\left(D_+|_{\mathcal F_{A,M}}\right)^2,  \qquad 0\le\delta_{\rm pair}\le2,  \label{eq:tpc60-pair-singular-value}$$ and $$\begin{aligned}  \delta_{\rm pair}>0  &\Longleftrightarrow  \mathcal F_{A,M}\cap\{(g,-g):g\in\mathcal H\}=\{(0,0)\}  \label{eq:tpc60-pair-antidiagonal}\\  &\Longleftrightarrow  \operatorname{Ker}(A+M)\subseteq\operatorname{Ker}A.  \notag\end{aligned}$$ Furthermore $$\boxed{\delta(A,M)\ge\delta_{\rm pair}.}  \label{eq:tpc60-delta-ge-pair}$$

> **Proof** The first identity is the Rayleigh quotient of $D_+^*D_+$ on the actual pair image. Its kernel is the anti-diagonal. If $Jz=(g,-g)\ne(0,0)$, then $Bz=0$ and $Az=g\ne0$; the converse is immediate. Finally, for $Az\ne0$, $$\frac{\|Bz\|^2}{\|Az\|^2}  \ge  \frac{\|Bz\|^2}{\|Az\|^2+\|Mz\|^2}  \ge\delta_{\rm pair}.$$ Taking the infimum proves [\[eq:tpc60-delta-ge-pair\]](TPC-060.md#ref-d97e374f0793a5ab).

The pair formulation is often convenient for an exact matrix computation, but it must use $\operatorname{Ran}J$, not an independently chosen product of the represented and missing ranges. Enlarging the feasible set to $\operatorname{Ran}A\times\operatorname{Ran}M$ can manufacture an anti-diagonal pair which no physical coefficient vector produces.

## Qualitative and quantitative separation

###### ref-b204063641a395ba

###### ref-1740adf06eefed58

\[ex:tpc60-kernel-no-quantitative\] Let $A=I$ and $M=-(1-\varepsilon)I$, where $0<\varepsilon<1$. Then $B=\varepsilon I$, so $$\operatorname{Ker}B=\operatorname{Ker}A=\{0\},  \qquad  \delta(A,M)=\varepsilon^2.  \label{eq:tpc60-epsilon-example}$$ The exact kernel test passes for every $\varepsilon>0$, but the constant can be arbitrarily small. For an $X$-dependent family, injectivity at each finite $X$ does not imply a polynomial lower bound.

###### ref-91882b3fd21a2b9d

###### ref-741aae1b4d6c12ce

###### ref-6135cf433fdab31e

\[ex:tpc60-same-mass-opposite-phase\] Let $A=I$ and $M=\pm tI$, with $t\ge0$. In both cases $$\|Mz\|^2=t^2\|Az\|^2,  \label{eq:tpc60-same-raw-mass}$$ but $$\delta(A,tI)=(1+t)^2,  \qquad  \delta(A,-tI)=(1-t)^2.  \label{eq:tpc60-opposite-phase-constants}$$ Thus the missing norm alone cannot distinguish constructive from destructive interference.

###### ref-4d76c6f4af4b14da

###### ref-f4c909495a54d91f

\[ex:tpc60-pointwise-not-uniform\] Return to [\[ex:tpc60-naive-compression-fails\]](TPC-060.md#ref-10974a0f9f63fee6). The distinguished vector $z_*=(1,0)$ satisfies $$\frac{\|Bz_*\|^2}{\|Az_*\|^2}=1,  \label{eq:tpc60-distinguished-success-example}$$ while the uniform constant is zero because $(1,-1)$ cancels exactly. Any distinguished-vector claim must therefore remain explicitly labeled as such.

## Scale-dependent interpretation

###### ref-5457bb1cf5832fc8

###### ref-e9cc02ae7d843d62

For a family $(A_X,M_X)$, call $\lambda_{\rm reasm}\ge0$ an admissible uniform loss exponent if $$\delta(A_X,M_X)  \ge X^{-\lambda_{\rm reasm}+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc60-uniform-loss-exponent}$$ If the exact kernel test fails along an unbounded sequence of scales, no such finite exponent exists on that coefficient class. If the test passes, [\[eq:tpc60-uniform-loss-exponent\]](TPC-060.md#ref-5457bb1cf5832fc8) is still a separate quantitative assertion. For one distinguished physical vector $\zeta_*(X)$, the analogous ratio $$\delta_*(X)  :=\frac{\|B_X\zeta_*(X)\|^2}{\|A_X\zeta_*(X)\|^2}  \label{eq:tpc60-distinguished-ratio}$$ is a different object and may remain positive after the uniform gate is killed.

# Signed defects and two routes to reassembly

The Gram formulation is optimal for a coefficient-uniform theorem. For a distinguished physical coefficient vector, the exact remaining task is often clearer as a signed cross-term estimate.

## The pointwise signed defect

###### ref-db4bc1ebdca02f87

###### ref-7aacdd2666b22614

###### ref-04e72a4151625ec6

###### ref-945abbb34566528b

###### ref-bdfdd03614c4660b

Define $$\mathfrak X(z)  :=2\operatorname{Re}\langle Az,Mz\rangle+\|Mz\|^2.  \label{eq:tpc60-signed-defect}$$ Then $$\boxed{  \|Bz\|^2=\|Az\|^2+\mathfrak X(z).}  \label{eq:tpc60-signed-defect-identity}$$ For $Az\ne0$, this becomes $$\boxed{  \frac{\|Bz\|^2}{\|Az\|^2}  =1+\frac{\mathfrak X(z)}{\|Az\|^2}.}  \label{eq:tpc60-signed-ratio}$$ Consequently a desired pointwise transfer $$\|Bz\|^2\ge\delta_*\|Az\|^2  \label{eq:tpc60-desired-pointwise-transfer}$$ is exactly equivalent to $$\boxed{  \mathfrak X(z)  \ge-(1-\delta_*)\|Az\|^2.}  \label{eq:tpc60-exact-signed-target}$$ This is neither a positivity statement about $\|Mz\|^2$ nor an unsigned estimate for the atomic missing mass. The cross term is part of the physical problem.

In the prime-resolved notation of [\[prop:tpc60-same-prime-separation\]](TPC-060.md#ref-83b52c08cf7431a1), $\mathfrak X$ is exactly the right side of [\[eq:tpc60-exact-prime-alias-defect\]](TPC-060.md#ref-f80a4997f37b9196). Hence its negative part can arise only from base–missing and unequal-prime native aliasing. The same-prime orthogonality is a genuine simplification, but it leaves the two surviving sums uncontrolled.

## The nuisance-optimized defect

###### ref-bc65f7187fbb07cf

For $s\in S$, define the best defect obtainable by moving inside its $A$-fiber: $$\mathfrak X_{\rm sh}(s)  :=\inf_{k\in N}  \left(   \|B(s+k)\|^2-\|A_Ss\|^2  \right).  \label{eq:tpc60-shorted-signed-defect}$$

###### ref-2a0d1d5e4b6140ee

###### ref-18cc7b03cabd9100

###### ref-f0b57531485db5fb

###### ref-ab958b2bb012d84b

> **Proposition: Shorted defect operator** \[prop:tpc60-shorted-defect-operator\] With $Q$ and $R_S$ as in [3](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-060.md#src-e175481cefa68c59), $$\boxed{  \mathfrak X_{\rm sh}(s)  =\langle(Q-R_S)s,s\rangle.}  \label{eq:tpc60-shorted-defect-form}$$ If $$\Theta_{\rm reasm}  :=R_S^{-1/2}(Q-R_S)R_S^{-1/2},  \label{eq:tpc60-Theta-reassembly}$$ then $$\boxed{  \delta(A,M)  =1+\lambda_{\min}(\Theta_{\rm reasm}).}  \label{eq:tpc60-delta-Theta}$$

> **Proof** The least-squares identity [\[eq:tpc60-distance-identity\]](TPC-060.md#ref-aaf78112056f8208) gives $$\inf_{k\in N}\|B(s+k)\|^2  =\langle Qs,s\rangle.$$ Subtracting $\|A_Ss\|^2=\langle R_Ss,s\rangle$ proves [\[eq:tpc60-shorted-defect-form\]](TPC-060.md#ref-18cc7b03cabd9100). Conjugating by $R_S^{-1/2}$ and using [\[thm:tpc60-shorted-generalized-eigenvalue\]](TPC-060.md#ref-44503029052531a6) proves [\[eq:tpc60-delta-Theta\]](TPC-060.md#ref-ab958b2bb012d84b).

Thus a coefficient-uniform signed route must control the negative spectrum of the *shorted* defect. Bounding the unshorted cross term only on the canonical sector $S$ is insufficient when $B(N)\ne0$.

## An angle certificate

###### ref-63b99371a263c455

###### ref-e8ec84eb937f1155

###### ref-975ec4c23d1e3858

For $Az\ne0$, put $$t(z):=\frac{\|Mz\|}{\|Az\|},  \label{eq:tpc60-size-ratio-t}$$ and, when $Mz\ne0$, define the destructive cosine $$\rho(z)  :=-\frac{\operatorname{Re}\langle Az,Mz\rangle}           {\|Az\|\,\|Mz\|}.  \label{eq:tpc60-destructive-cosine}$$ Set $\rho(z)=0$ if $Mz=0$. Then $-1\le\rho(z)\le1$, and $$\frac{\|Bz\|^2}{\|Az\|^2}  =1+t(z)^2-2\rho(z)t(z).  \label{eq:tpc60-angle-size-identity}$$

###### ref-7035b04b366a0b48

###### ref-f922fcb4e3b5be6a

###### ref-ea4ee5d52bd1425d

> **Proposition: Uniform angle certificate** \[prop:tpc60-uniform-angle-certificate\] Suppose there is $0\le\rho_0<1$ such that $$\rho(z)\le\rho_0  \qquad\text{whenever }Az\ne0.  \label{eq:tpc60-uniform-angle-hypothesis}$$ Then $$\boxed{  \delta(A,M)\ge1-\rho_0^2>0.}  \label{eq:tpc60-uniform-angle-bound}$$ In particular, if $\operatorname{Re}\langle Az,Mz\rangle\ge0$ for all $z$, then $\delta(A,M)\ge1$.

> **Proof** If $\rho(z)\ge0$, complete the square: $$1+t^2-2\rho t=(t-\rho)^2+1-\rho^2  \ge1-\rho_0^2.$$ If $\rho(z)<0$, the left side is at least one. Take the infimum over $z$.

This angle route may succeed even when the missing norm is not small. It is also substantially more arithmetic: one must retain the phases in the base–missing and unequal-prime alias sums. The mass–degree route in the next section is cruder, but it replaces phase control by a quantitative smallness condition.

# Raw missing mass times native fiber degree

We now give a phase-blind sufficient condition. It is sharp at the level of information it uses.

## The exact grouping norm

###### ref-ea8ced785d43ff45

###### ref-e17d037e892076ec

###### ref-bde0db4fe107b83a

###### ref-726ab87abb6e40aa

###### ref-7d4ba9f61ffd048c

Let $\mathcal W_M$ be a finite missing atomic carrier, let $\mathcal I_{\rm out}$ be the active native output set, and let $$\mathcal F_i^{\rm miss}  :=\{w\in\mathcal W_M:i(w)=i\}.  \label{eq:tpc60-missing-native-fibers}$$ Absorb every fixed unit Möbius or high-character phase into the atomic amplitude $m_w(z)$, and define $$\begin{aligned}  L_M:\mathcal K&\longrightarrow\ell^2(\mathcal W_M),  &(L_Mz)_w&:=m_w(z),  \label{eq:tpc60-missing-atomic-lift}\\  C_M:\ell^2(\mathcal W_M)&\longrightarrow\ell^2(\mathcal I_{\rm out}),  &(C_Ma)_i&:=\sum_{w\in\mathcal F_i^{\rm miss}}a_w.  \label{eq:tpc60-missing-grouping}\end{aligned}$$ Then $$M=C_ML_M,  \qquad  \mathcal D_{\rm miss}(z):=\|L_Mz\|_2^2.  \label{eq:tpc60-M-factorization-Dmiss}$$ In the fixed-shift specialization, every absorbed phase has modulus one, so $\mathcal D_{\rm miss}(z)=\mathcal D_M(z)$ with the raw diagonal of [\[eq:tpc60-BRM-diagonals\]](TPC-060.md#ref-4b15ee8a8504c7d5). Put $$d_{\rm miss}:=\max_{i\in\mathcal I_{\rm out}}                    |\mathcal F_i^{\rm miss}|,  \label{eq:tpc60-native-missing-degree}$$ with $d_{\rm miss}=0$ when the missing carrier is empty.

###### ref-923a1b63e554170e

###### ref-35acbd8fceb8e802

###### ref-bb78de9d8a1890ff

> **Proposition: Exact native grouping norm** \[prop:tpc60-exact-grouping-norm\] One has $$\boxed{  \|C_M\|^2=d_{\rm miss},}  \label{eq:tpc60-CM-norm}$$ and therefore, for every $z\in\mathcal K$, $$\boxed{  \|Mz\|^2\le d_{\rm miss}\mathcal D_{\rm miss}(z).}  \label{eq:tpc60-mass-degree-upper}$$

> **Proof** Fiberwise Cauchy–Schwarz gives $$|(C_Ma)_i|^2  \le|\mathcal F_i^{\rm miss}|     \sum_{w\in\mathcal F_i^{\rm miss}}|a_w|^2.$$ Summing over the disjoint output fibers proves $\|C_M\|^2\le d_{\rm miss}$. On a fiber of maximal size, choose all coordinates equal with a common phase and set all other coordinates to zero. Equality follows. Applying the operator norm bound to $L_Mz$ proves [\[eq:tpc60-mass-degree-upper\]](TPC-060.md#ref-bb78de9d8a1890ff).

The exact norm concerns the ambient grouping map. The physical range $\operatorname{Ran}L_M$ may have a smaller restricted norm, but proving that is another Gram problem. Conversely, replacing $d_{\rm miss}$ by an average degree is invalid for a coefficient-uniform estimate.

## Distinguished-vector certificate

###### ref-f1a72f7a6f022005

###### ref-1c885738f17ee864

###### ref-ba50cfddf24d1664

> **Theorem: Sharp mass–degree lower certificate** \[thm:tpc60-pointwise-mass-degree\] For every $z$ with $Az\ne0$, $$\boxed{  \frac{\|(A+M)z\|^2}{\|Az\|^2}  \ge  \left(   1-   \sqrt{\frac{d_{\rm miss}\mathcal D_{\rm miss}(z)}{\|Az\|^2}}  \right)_+^2.}  \label{eq:tpc60-pointwise-mass-degree-bound}$$ In particular, if $$d_{\rm miss}\mathcal D_{\rm miss}(z)  \le\eta\|Az\|^2,  \qquad 0\le\eta<1,  \label{eq:tpc60-eta-mass-degree}$$ then the ratio is at least $(1-\sqrt\eta)^2$.

> **Proof** The reverse triangle inequality and [\[prop:tpc60-exact-grouping-norm\]](TPC-060.md#ref-923a1b63e554170e) give $$\|(A+M)z\|  \ge(\|Az\|-\|Mz\|)_+  \ge  \left(   \|Az\|-\sqrt{d_{\rm miss}\mathcal D_{\rm miss}(z)}  \right)_+.$$ Divide by $\|Az\|$ and square.

###### ref-4c03be9f04deb4d4

###### ref-a4b469d15028dcfa

\[ex:tpc60-sharp-mass-degree-threshold\] Take one represented native output $Az=1$. Let one native fiber contain $n$ missing atoms, each of amplitude $-1/n$. Then $$d_{\rm miss}=n,  \qquad  \mathcal D_{\rm miss}(z)=\frac1n,  \qquad  Mz=-1.  \label{eq:tpc60-threshold-example-data}$$ Thus $d_{\rm miss}\mathcal D_{\rm miss}/\|Az\|^2=1$ and the full output vanishes. The same construction with $n=2$ shows that very small combinatorial degree is already compatible with exact cancellation. Therefore neither $\mathcal D_{\rm miss}=o(1)$ nor a bounded degree alone is a sufficient reassembly theorem.

## Uniform and canonical-sector versions

###### ref-9ca291767effc86e

###### ref-a17a7989e9255781

###### ref-517251490d971f97

> **Corollary: Uniform mass–degree certificate** \[cor:tpc60-uniform-mass-degree\] Suppose $\varepsilon\ge0$ and $$\mathcal D_{\rm miss}(z)\le\varepsilon\|Az\|^2  \qquad\text{for every }z\in\mathcal K.  \label{eq:tpc60-uniform-missing-mass-hypothesis}$$ Then $M|_{\operatorname{Ker}A}=0$ and $$\boxed{  \delta(A,M)  \ge(1-\sqrt{d_{\rm miss}\varepsilon})_+^2.}  \label{eq:tpc60-uniform-mass-degree-bound}$$

> **Proof** If $z\in\operatorname{Ker}A$, [\[eq:tpc60-uniform-missing-mass-hypothesis\]](TPC-060.md#ref-a17a7989e9255781) forces $\|L_Mz\|=0$, hence $Mz=0$. Apply [\[thm:tpc60-pointwise-mass-degree\]](TPC-060.md#ref-f1a72f7a6f022005) to every $z$ with $Az\ne0$ and take the infimum.

###### ref-443f596fb4d794ca

The hypothesis in [\[cor:tpc60-uniform-mass-degree\]](TPC-060.md#ref-9ca291767effc86e) is strong: it automatically removes the nuisance image. When only the canonical sector can be controlled, the following version retains the missing transversality explicitly. With the notation of [3](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-060.md#src-e175481cefa68c59), define $$\gamma_A  :=\inf_{s\in S\setminus\{0\}}    \frac{\|PA_Ss\|^2}{\|A_Ss\|^2}.  \label{eq:tpc60-gamma-A}$$

###### ref-8babc381b3250960

###### ref-d939a49fd2f902cc

###### ref-fbe5ae7147232fb4

> **Proposition: Canonical mass plus nuisance transversality** \[prop:tpc60-canonical-mass-transversality\] Suppose $\varepsilon\ge0$ and $$\mathcal D_{\rm miss}(s)  \le\varepsilon\|A_Ss\|^2  \qquad(s\in S).  \label{eq:tpc60-canonical-missing-bound}$$ Then $$\boxed{  \delta(A,M)  \ge  \left(   \sqrt{\gamma_A}-\sqrt{d_{\rm miss}\varepsilon}  \right)_+^2.}  \label{eq:tpc60-canonical-transversality-bound}$$

> **Proof** For $s\in S$, $$\begin{aligned}  \|PB_Ss\|  &=\|P(A_Ss+Ms)\|\\  &\ge\|PA_Ss\|-\|PMs\|\\  &\ge  \left(\sqrt{\gamma_A}-\sqrt{d_{\rm miss}\varepsilon}\right)  \|A_Ss\|.\end{aligned}$$ Use [\[thm:tpc60-exact-nuisance-elimination\]](TPC-060.md#ref-b0cccf1de5e4a019) and take the positive part.

This estimate exposes two independent risks: the missing atomic output can be large, and the represented output can already lie close to the nuisance range $B(\operatorname{Ker}A)$. An estimate for only one of them does not close the uniform gate.

## Exponent form

###### ref-fabbe5ae76b72c33

###### ref-9d0517f60505dae5

###### ref-500824d272ec3e8c

###### ref-2229563f00d5aa27

###### ref-c6eb0f2e7b7354f6

###### ref-94a5ef92682d0359

For a distinguished vector $z_*(X)$, write $$\frac{\|B_Xz_*(X)\|^2}{\|A_Xz_*(X)\|^2}  \ge X^{-\lambda_{\rm reasm,*}+o(1)}  \label{eq:tpc60-pointwise-reassembly-exponent}$$ when the denominator is nonzero. The star distinguishes this pointwise exponent from the coefficient-uniform $\lambda_{\rm reasm}$ of [4](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-060.md#src-3503895394bb30db). Suppose now, for that distinguished vector or uniformly on a stated class, $$d_{\rm miss}=X^{\mu_{\rm miss}+o(1)},  \qquad  \frac{\mathcal D_{\rm miss}(z)}{\|Az\|^2}  \le X^{-\sigma_{\rm miss}+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc60-degree-saving-exponents}$$ Writing $$q_X(z):=  \frac{d_{\rm miss}\mathcal D_{\rm miss}(z)}{\|Az\|^2},  \label{eq:tpc60-qX-product}$$ the triangle inequality gives the two-sided estimate $$\bigl(1-\sqrt{q_X(z)}\bigr)_+^2  \le\frac{\|Bz\|^2}{\|Az\|^2}  \le\bigl(1+\sqrt{q_X(z)}\bigr)^2.  \label{eq:tpc60-qX-two-sided}$$ Consequently $$\sigma_{\rm miss}>\mu_{\rm miss}  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad  \frac{\|Bz\|^2}{\|Az\|^2}=1+o(1)  \label{eq:tpc60-zero-cost-exponent-condition}$$ Thus the mass–degree route gives $\lambda_{\rm reasm,*}=0$ in the distinguished-vector scope and $\lambda_{\rm reasm}=0$ when the hypotheses hold uniformly on the declared class. At equality of the exponents, the exact sufficient condition furnished by this certificate is $$\bigl(1-\sqrt{q_X(z)}\bigr)_+^2\ge X^{-o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc60-equality-subpower-test}$$ This bound must hold pointwise for the distinguished vector, or uniformly over the declared coefficient class, according to the claimed scope. The simpler condition $\limsup q_X(z)<1$ is sufficient but not necessary; for example, $q_X=1-1/\log X$ still gives a subpower lower bound. Thus exponent equality requires finer constant or subpower control. If $\sigma_{\rm miss}<\mu_{\rm miss}$, the certificate is silent; it does not prove failure.

###### ref-23285b690eb29982

###### ref-2d7bdab0c6e4b733

More generally, if a common reference diagonal satisfies $$\mathcal D_{\rm miss}(z)  \le X^{-\sigma+o(1)}\mathcal D_{\rm ref}(z),  \qquad  \|Az\|^2  \ge X^{-\lambda_{\rm rep}+o(1)}\mathcal D_{\rm ref}(z),  \label{eq:tpc60-common-reference-bounds}$$ then the zero-cost sufficient condition becomes $$\boxed{  \sigma>\mu_{\rm miss}+\lambda_{\rm rep}.}  \label{eq:tpc60-common-reference-threshold}$$ Every exponent in this comparison refers to the same physical vector and the same raw reference measure.

# Arithmetic localization of the missing carrier

The previous sections are exact for any pair of finite-dimensional maps. We now return to the fixed-$h_0$ Walsh carrier and record what the literal arithmetic dictionary does and does not add.

## Boolean signatures and ordered first failure

###### ref-df0866f332a03d0e

###### ref-78d26b066ec95ee9

###### ref-68d5c3a3d2f383cd

###### ref-75ed3abc7c3a68fb

###### ref-94771a7b07032eed

###### ref-96141e709a2b79e0

For $w\in\mathcal W_H(y)$, recover $m,j,\gamma,n,p,r,s$ by [\[eq:tpc60-target-n\]](TPC-060.md#ref-15600d33f6af30ed)–[\[eq:tpc60-prs-recovery\]](TPC-060.md#ref-47e508718cb7b611). Membership in the represented terminal image is a finite conjunction of declared tests. For definiteness, use the dependency order $$\begin{aligned}  T_1(w)&:=\mathbf 1_{\{(m,r)\in\mathcal I_X\}},  \label{eq:tpc60-test-retained-pair}\\  T_2(w)&:=\mathbf 1_{\{j,\gamma,p\text{ lie in the declared terminal scopes}\}},  \label{eq:tpc60-test-terminal-scopes}\\  T_3(w)&:=\mathbf 1_{\{\Lambda_X(n)\ne0\}},  \label{eq:tpc60-test-terminal-weight}\\  T_4(w)&:=\mathbf 1_{\{d_m,r,s\text{ squarefree and }(d_m,r)=1\}},  \label{eq:tpc60-test-squarefree}\\  T_5(w)&:=\mathbf 1_{\{\text{all remaining terminal-only masks pass}\}}.  \label{eq:tpc60-test-remaining-masks}\end{aligned}$$ The lists inside $T_2$ and $T_5$ are the inherited literal declarations, not new favorable supports. By construction, $$w\in\mathcal W_R  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad  T_1(w)=\cdots=T_5(w)=1.  \label{eq:tpc60-represented-all-tests}$$

###### ref-d6ac73470aaf46e1

###### ref-8587fb5f9b1a0228

The Boolean signature $(T_1,\ldots,T_5)$ is order independent. A “first failure” is not: it becomes well defined only after the order above is declared. Define $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal F_1&:=\{w\in\mathcal W_H:T_1(w)=0\},  \label{eq:tpc60-first-failure-1}\\  \mathcal F_\nu&:=\{w\in\mathcal W_H:  T_1(w)=\cdots=T_{\nu-1}(w)=1, T_\nu(w)=0\},  \quad2\le\nu\le5.  \label{eq:tpc60-first-failure-nu}\end{aligned}$$

###### ref-8d968168150845f7

###### ref-fe9cf8e82469e5ac

###### ref-55f412cb34e3f0ea

###### ref-280cde0f76d14968

> **Proposition: Exact ordered missing partition** \[prop:tpc60-exact-missing-partition\] The first-failure sets are disjoint and $$\boxed{  \mathcal W_M=\bigsqcup_{\nu=1}^5\mathcal F_\nu.}  \label{eq:tpc60-missing-first-failure-partition}$$ If $$\mathcal D_\nu(\zeta):=\sum_{w\in\mathcal F_\nu}|b_w(\zeta)|^2,  \label{eq:tpc60-first-failure-energy}$$ then $$\boxed{  \mathcal D_M=\sum_{\nu=1}^5\mathcal D_\nu.}  \label{eq:tpc60-DM-failure-partition}$$

> **Proof** Every missing atom fails at least one test by [\[eq:tpc60-represented-all-tests\]](TPC-060.md#ref-96141e709a2b79e0); its least failing index is unique. Conversely every atom in a first-failure set fails terminal admission and is missing. Raw atomic additivity gives the energy identity.

The adjective “ordered” is essential. Reordering the tests changes the first-failure labels, although it does not change $\mathcal W_M$ or the full Boolean signature.

## Empty pseudo-failure layers

Several apparent failure mechanisms are already excluded on the common active Walsh carrier.

###### ref-3ed6cc4f6e3e7dbb

> **Proposition: Inherited automatic tests** \[prop:tpc60-inherited-automatic-tests\] For an actual nonzero high Walsh atom on the common TPC–45/TPC–53 physical carrier:
>
> 1.  $p=P^+(n)>y$, $pr=mj+h_0$, and $r=n/p$ hold by the unique high-prime decoding;
>
> 2.  the nonzero Walsh amplitude already enforces $\Lambda_X(n)\ne0$ and its shared literal profile masks;
>
> 3.  the inherited squarefree and coprimality support implies that $r$ and $s=d_mr$ are squarefree and $(d_m,r)=1$.
>
> Consequently the first-failure layers created solely by $T_3$ and $T_4$ are empty. The part of $T_5$ consisting only of masks shared with the Walsh carrier is empty as well.

> **Proof** Part $a$ is the largest-prime census and the assumption $y>\sqrt{N_+}$. For $b$, coordinates with zero shaped weight or zero shared profile were removed as identically inactive when the common Walsh carrier was formed. For $c$, the TPC–45 support makes $d_mn$ squarefree and $(d_m,n)=1$. Since $n=pr$, every divisor $r$ is squarefree, is coprime to $d_m$, and therefore $s=d_mr$ is squarefree.

###### ref-9bf284957fb9db0a

###### ref-3c6319f3fbd422c2

> **Corollary: Identical-support specialization** \[cor:tpc60-identical-support-missing\] Suppose the Walsh and terminal constructions use identical row, orbit, opposite-row, terminal-prime, literal-mask, and profile supports, and there is no additional terminal-only pruning. Then $$\boxed{  \mathcal W_M  =\{w\in\mathcal W_H(y):(m(w),r(w))\notin\mathcal I_X\}.}  \label{eq:tpc60-missing-only-cofactor}$$ Thus the only missing-high mechanism in this specialization is failure of the complementary cofactor to enter the retained-pair carrier.

This is a support-alignment theorem, not an occupancy theorem. It does not show that the set in [\[eq:tpc60-missing-only-cofactor\]](TPC-060.md#ref-3c6319f3fbd422c2) has small mass, or even that some active row contains a represented atom.

## Zero-cost localization of the diagnostic branch

###### ref-542fe2128b639415

###### ref-ab986da23dc559a7

###### ref-0ca18071e110f415

###### ref-609510d6f90ef377

Let $q\le5$ be the number of nonempty first-failure layers, and write $$N_{\rm dyad}(y)  :=1+\left\lfloor\log_2\frac{N_+}{y}\right\rfloor  =O(\log X)  \label{eq:tpc60-dyadic-block-count}$$ for the number of represented cofactor cells. If $\mathcal D_M>0$, one of them satisfies $$\max_\nu\mathcal D_\nu\ge\frac{\mathcal D_M}{q}.  \label{eq:tpc60-failure-pigeonhole}$$ Combining this constant partition with the $O(\log X)$ dyadic partition of the represented terminal face gives the exact dichotomy $$\mathcal D_H^W:=\mathcal D_R+\mathcal D_M>0  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad  \begin{cases}   \exists k:\displaystyle   \mathcal D_k\ge\dfrac{\mathcal D_H^W}{2N_{\rm dyad}(y)},   &\mathcal D_R\ge\mathcal D_H^W/2,\\[8pt]   \exists\nu:\displaystyle   \mathcal D_\nu\ge\dfrac{\mathcal D_H^W}{2q},   &\mathcal D_M\ge\mathcal D_H^W/2,  \end{cases}  \label{eq:tpc60-represented-missing-dichotomy}$$ Hence localization has zero fixed-power cost in either branch: $$\boxed{\lambda_{\rm part}=0.}  \label{eq:tpc60-zero-partition-cost}$$ The conclusion is diagnostic. It either finds a large represented block or identifies a dominant reason for missing mass. It does not force the represented alternative and does not imply $\lambda_{\rm reasm}=0$.

## Exact rowwise represented-mass minimax

###### ref-d3b5b8920bee5019

###### ref-1ff632053668c727

Write $$W_E(m):=\sum_{\substack{w\in\mathcal W_E\\m(w)=m}}|\beta_w|^2,  \qquad E\in\{R,M,H\},  \qquad W_H:=W_R+W_M.  \label{eq:tpc60-row-weights}$$ Then literal factorization gives $$\mathcal D_E(\zeta)=\sum_mW_E(m)|\zeta_m|^2.  \label{eq:tpc60-row-diagonal-form}$$

###### ref-070b59fe937ba840

###### ref-e596f1ee14cc7820

> **Proposition: Sharp represented-mass minimax** \[prop:tpc60-rowwise-represented-minimax\] The optimal constant uniform over any bounded coefficient cube containing a nonzero multiple of each coordinate direction is $$\boxed{  \inf_{\mathcal D_H^W(\zeta)>0}  \frac{\mathcal D_R(\zeta)}{\mathcal D_H^W(\zeta)}  =\min_{m:W_H(m)>0}\frac{W_R(m)}{W_H(m)}.}  \label{eq:tpc60-rowwise-represented-minimax}$$ In particular, if one active row has $W_R(m)=0<W_H(m)$, the uniform represented-mass transfer is exactly zero.

> **Proof** The quotient is a weighted average of the row ratios $W_R(m)/W_H(m)$, with nonnegative weights $W_H(m)|\zeta_m|^2$. It is at least their minimum. Concentrating $\zeta$ on a minimizing row attains that minimum after a harmless scalar rescaling into the bounded cube.

Thus a geometric density statement about the union of rows cannot replace a rowwise lower bound when the coefficient class has only upper bounds on $|\zeta_m|$.

## Residual ladders and ambient degree

###### ref-bc1d0e6221d15516

###### ref-d4a932a10d34fb78

Fix a common source-prime cell of diameter $O(L)$, a residual key $(i,s)$, and a dyadic cofactor block $R\le r<2R$. Write $$m=d\ell,  \qquad r=\frac sd.  \label{eq:tpc60-d-ell-r}$$ The fixed-shift equality becomes $$rp-dj\ell=h_0.  \label{eq:tpc60-determinant-equation}$$

###### ref-8a9a7f401dd58c48

###### ref-7df20d448d58a03f

###### ref-498acbe5c5306140

> **Proposition: Ambient residual ladder bound** \[prop:tpc60-ambient-residual-degree\] On one common $D,L$ cell, the number of full, represented, missing, or first-failure high atoms with fixed residual key $(i,s)$ and $R\le r<2R$ satisfies $$\boxed{  d_{\max}^{\rm res}(R)  \ll_{h_0}\tau(s)\left(1+\frac LR\right)  =X^{o(1)}\left(1+\frac LR\right).}  \label{eq:tpc60-residual-degree-bound}$$ Moreover the prime-resolved degrees at $(i,p)$ and $(i,s,p)$ are at most one. The direct native degree has only the inherited crude bound $$d_{\rm miss}\ll X^{o(1)}Q.  \label{eq:tpc60-crude-native-degree}$$

###### ref-0c35c4226b4c4c64

> **Proof** Fix $d\mid s$, hence $r=s/d$, and put $g=(r,dj)=(r,j)$, using $(d,r)=1$. If [\[eq:tpc60-determinant-equation\]](TPC-060.md#ref-d4a932a10d34fb78) has one integral solution $(p_0,\ell_0)$, all integral solutions lie on the determinant ladder $$p=p_0+\frac{dj}{g}t,  \qquad  \ell=\ell_0+\frac rg t,  \qquad t\in\mathbb Z.  \label{eq:tpc60-determinant-ladder}$$ Solvability forces $g\mid h_0$, and therefore $g\le|h_0|$. Since $\ell$ lies in an interval of diameter $O(L)$, the ladder contributes $O_{h_0}(1+L/R)$ candidates. Summing over $d\mid s$ gives [\[eq:tpc60-residual-degree-bound\]](TPC-060.md#ref-7df20d448d58a03f); primality, retained-pair, residue, mask, and profile tests only delete candidates. The fixed- $(i,s,p)$ assertion is the TPC–55/TPC–58 prime-resolved injection, while the stronger fixed-$(i,p)$ assertion is the TPC–59 critical-cutoff row singleton proved again in [\[prop:tpc60-same-prime-separation\]](TPC-060.md#ref-83b52c08cf7431a1).
>
> For the native bound, fixing $i$ fixes the side, opposite row, and $j$. Fixed-coordinate fused-label injectivity leaves at most one active high atom per source row, and the inherited source-system support bound is $\#\mathcal A_\sigma\ll X^{o(1)}Q$, $\sigma\in\{1,2\}$. This proves [\[eq:tpc60-crude-native-degree\]](TPC-060.md#ref-498acbe5c5306140). A wider union of $D,L$ cells must pay explicitly for the additional cells.

The residual estimate is not the native estimate used directly in [\[eq:tpc60-mass-degree-upper\]](TPC-060.md#ref-bb78de9d8a1890ff): erasing $s$ can merge many residual fibers. Prime-resolved singleton structure therefore does not imply native singleton structure.

# Endpoint audit and the reassembly ledger

The exact constants above identify the reassembly problem. We now compare the available coefficient-blind degree certificate with the endpoint budget and state the admissible ways to close the new gate.

## Critical-cutoff exponent dictionary

###### ref-63d36e4b17906bbb

###### ref-e82a754a5ced373e

###### ref-6ed1c5a47527c41f

###### ref-7b9ebd466cd31e3b

Assume that $y$ is very-high admissible and write $$\frac yQ=X^{\xi+o(1)},  \qquad 0\le\xi<\frac{133}{400}.  \label{eq:tpc60-xi-cutoff}$$ At $\xi=0$, admissibility retains the constant-scale requirement $y\ge C_{\rm vh}Q$ for a sufficiently large support-dependent constant; the exponent identity alone is not enough. The displayed range is the one in which a polynomially nonempty cofactor scale remains compatible. The compatible cofactor ceiling is $$r\le\frac{N_+}{y}  =X^{\theta_c(\xi)+o(1)},  \qquad  \theta_c(\xi):=\frac{133}{400}-\xi.  \label{eq:tpc60-compatible-cofactor-ceiling}$$ The inherited source-prime and divisor exponents are $$\lambda_L  :=\frac{267}{400}-\frac{10049}{52500}  =\frac{99979}{210000},  \qquad  \lambda_D:=\frac{10049}{52500}.  \label{eq:tpc60-LD-exponents}$$ Thus the residual label $s=d_mr$ obeys $$s\le  X^{\lambda_D+\theta_c(\xi)+o(1)}  =X^{110021/210000-\xi+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc60-residual-label-ceiling}$$

###### ref-b1d2ccd0db6c31f0

###### ref-2ab250b20614b8f8

###### ref-34c51baf23a92d30

###### ref-8d472d92d4c23e29

Let $R=X^{\theta_R+o(1)}$ be any compatible dyadic cofactor block, so $$0\le\theta_R\le\theta_c(\xi).  \label{eq:tpc60-compatible-thetaR}$$ The determinant-ladder estimate [\[eq:tpc60-residual-degree-bound\]](TPC-060.md#ref-7df20d448d58a03f) supplies the coefficient-blind capacity exponent $$\mu_{\rm cap}(R)  :=\lambda_L-\theta_R.  \label{eq:tpc60-capacity-exponent}$$ It satisfies $$\boxed{  \mu_{\rm cap}(R)  \ge\frac{15077}{105000}+\xi,}  \label{eq:tpc60-capacity-floor}$$ and hence $$\boxed{  \mu_{\rm cap}(R)-\frac1{400}  \ge\frac{29629}{210000}+\xi.}  \label{eq:tpc60-capacity-budget-gap}$$ At the critical choice $\xi=0$, the lower floor is approximately $0.143590$, whereas the entire endpoint allowance is $0.0025$.

This calculation must be interpreted in the correct direction. $\mu_{\rm cap}$ is the exponent of an ambient *upper* bound for a fiber size; it is not a proved energy loss and does not say that actual fibers attain that size. It does show that a crude route which pays the complete ambient degree through coloring, a coefficient-blind transversal, or Cauchy–Schwarz cannot fit inside the $1/400$ ledger. That support-only closure is therefore unavailable. A successful argument must use actual missing-mass sparsity, restricted-range geometry, or signed phase information.

## Three admissible reassembly certificates

The following routes preserve the physical coefficient vector and the fixed shift.

###### ref-cd76676b63c672cd

###### ref-bc45a6aa95bac227

###### ref-2594711b040adf9a

1.  *Shorted-Gram route.* Prove directly that $$\lambda_{\min}\left(    R_S^{-1/2}Q R_S^{-1/2}   \right)   \ge X^{-\lambda_{\rm reasm}+o(1)}.   \label{eq:tpc60-route-shorted-Gram}$$ This is the optimal coefficient-uniform statement. Before estimating an eigenvalue, apply the exact kernel and dimension kill tests.

2.  *Signed distinguished-vector route.* For the physical coefficient vector $\zeta_*$, prove $$\mathfrak X(\zeta_*)   \ge-\left(1-X^{-\lambda_{\rm reasm,*}+o(1)}\right)        \|A_X\zeta_*\|^2.   \label{eq:tpc60-route-signed}$$ This may remain open after the uniform gate is killed, but it cannot be promoted to a uniform claim.

3.  *Mass–degree route.* Prove a common-reference saving $\sigma$, a represented loss $\lambda_{\rm rep}$, and a native degree exponent $\mu_{\rm miss}$ satisfying $$\sigma>\mu_{\rm miss}+\lambda_{\rm rep}.   \label{eq:tpc60-route-mass-degree}$$ Then [\[thm:tpc60-pointwise-mass-degree\]](TPC-060.md#ref-f1a72f7a6f022005) yields $\lambda_{\rm reasm}=0$ for a uniform scope, or $\lambda_{\rm reasm,*}=0$ for the distinguished-vector scope. At equality of exponents, finer constant or subpower control must verify [\[eq:tpc60-equality-subpower-test\]](TPC-060.md#ref-94a5ef92682d0359); the exponent comparison alone is inconclusive.

The angle certificate of [\[prop:tpc60-uniform-angle-certificate\]](TPC-060.md#ref-7035b04b366a0b48) is a fourth presentation of the signed route. Orthogonal full represented-affine and missing output ranges, $\operatorname{Ran}A\perp\operatorname{Ran}M$, give the safe special case $\delta(A,M)\ge1$, but the fixed-$h_0$ maps here do not currently satisfy such an orthogonality theorem.

## Complete ledger

###### ref-06da4a6ea742c953

The prospective fixed-shift chain remains $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal D_{\rm up}  &\longrightarrow\mathcal E_{\rm par}  \longrightarrow\mathcal E_{\mathcal R}  \longrightarrow\mathcal D_{\mathcal R}^{\rm term}  \longrightarrow\mathcal D_H^{\rm rep}  \longrightarrow\mathcal D_{k_*}\\  &\longrightarrow\mathcal G_H  \longrightarrow\mathcal N_H  \longrightarrow\mathcal N_{\rm aff}^{\rm rep}  \xrightarrow{\rm reassembly}  \mathcal N_{\rm slice}^{\rm full}  \xrightarrow{\rm boundary/tail}  \text{parent arithmetic target}.  \end{aligned}  \label{eq:tpc60-full-transfer-chain}$$ Every arrow is a different assertion. TPC–60 replaces the displayed reassembly arrow by the exact constant $\delta(A_X,M_X)$; it does not estimate that constant from below for the arithmetic family.

###### ref-8399e7366699de46

###### ref-98cd454e2f1f8e1f

If all arrows eventually receive polynomial lower bounds, the energy loss is $$\boxed{  \begin{aligned}  \Lambda_{\rm tot}:={}&  \lambda_{\rm sel}+\lambda_{\rm occ}  +\lambda_{\rm blk}  +\lambda_{\rm term}+\lambda_{\rm sing}\\  &+\lambda_H+\lambda_{\rm aff}  +\lambda_{\rm reasm}  +\lambda_{\rm bdry}+\lambda_{\rm tail}.  \end{aligned}}  \label{eq:tpc60-complete-ledger}$$ The endpoint requirement is the strict inequality $$\boxed{  \Lambda_{\rm tot}<\frac1{400}.}  \label{eq:tpc60-strict-endpoint}$$ For a distinguished-vector chain, $\lambda_{\rm reasm}$ in [\[eq:tpc60-complete-ledger\]](TPC-060.md#ref-8399e7366699de46) is replaced consistently by $\lambda_{\rm reasm,*}$. The two exponents are never interchanged: a pointwise certificate cannot be entered as a uniform one. Equality is rejected unless leading constants and all remainders are quantified. The cutoff $y\asymp Q$ is a feasibility choice and is not an additive energy loss. TPC–59 proves $\lambda_{\rm blk}=0$ only after represented high-face activation and with its distinguished-block scope. The new identity $\lambda_{\rm part}=0$ is a diagnostic finite/logarithmic partition and does not replace either reassembly exponent in its matching version of [\[eq:tpc60-complete-ledger\]](TPC-060.md#ref-8399e7366699de46).

## Stop rules

The current route must stop or change scope under any of the following events.

1.  A literal vector satisfies $A_X\zeta\ne0$ and $B_X\zeta=0$; then the uniform reassembly gate is exactly dead.

2.  An active row satisfies $W_R(m)=0<W_H(m)$; then uniform represented raw-mass transfer is exactly zero.

3.  A necessary proved loss by itself reaches $1/400$, or the sum of necessary losses reaches it without a constant-level endpoint argument.

4.  The only available proof pays the full ambient capacity exponent in [\[eq:tpc60-capacity-floor\]](TPC-060.md#ref-34c51baf23a92d30); then that proof architecture exceeds the endpoint allowance even at the critical cutoff.

None of these events may be repaired by changing $h_0$, averaging shifts, renormalizing atoms by their fiber sizes, deleting coefficient-dependent zeros, or selecting represented and missing vectors independently. Such operations define different problems.

## What is not proved

The following statements do not follow from this paper:

1.  $\mathcal D_M=o(\mathcal D_R)$, either rowwise or for the distinguished coefficient vector;

2.  a polynomial lower bound for $\delta(A_X,M_X)$;

3.  a favorable sign for the base–missing or unequal-prime cross terms;

4.  $\lambda_{\rm reasm}=0$ for the physical TPC family;

5.  a positive fixed-$h_0$ parity gain, a prime-pair lower bound, a prime-pair asymptotic, or a twin-prime consequence.

The first-failure partition can identify where missing mass lies, but does not make that mass small. The same-prime orthogonality deletes one class of cross terms, but different primes still alias at the native output. A finite-scale positive eigenvalue is numerical scouting evidence unless its scale dependence is controlled.

## The next ordered attack

The next paper should not introduce another abstract angle. The exact reassembly theorem leaves a concrete arithmetic decision tree.

1.  *Rowwise census.* Compute or bound the literal weights $$W_R(m),\qquad W_M(m),\qquad   \frac{W_R(m)}{W_R(m)+W_M(m)}   \label{eq:tpc60-next-row-census}$$ on every active row. If an active missing-only row occurs, record the exact death of the uniform represented-mass route.

2.  *Cofactor-defect estimate.* Under the identical-support specialization, replace the vague terminal failure by the single event $$(m,r)\notin\mathcal I_X.   \label{eq:tpc60-next-cofactor-defect}$$ Estimate its coefficient-weighted mass relative to the represented affine reference, not merely its unweighted cardinality.

3.  *Restricted native degree.* Factor the missing map through residual keys and estimate its norm on the actual coefficient image. The ambient bound $X^{o(1)}Q$ is too large, while the residual ladder bound alone does not survive erasure of $s$. A successful argument must prove either a smaller restricted degree exponent or destructive phase cancellation inside the missing map itself.

4.  *Shorted kernel audit.* Assemble the literal matrices $A_X,M_X$ at finite scales and test $$\operatorname{Ker}(A_X+M_X)\subseteq\operatorname{Ker}A_X,   \qquad   \dim\bigl((\operatorname{Ker}A_X)^\perp\bigr)   \le\dim\mathcal H_{\rm out}        -\operatorname{rank}\!\left(B_X|_{\operatorname{Ker}A_X}\right).   \label{eq:tpc60-next-kernel-audit}$$ Repeated exact failure closes the uniform door. Repeated numerical success does not prove an asymptotic lower singular value, but it can identify the correct subspace for a theorem.

5.  *Signed fallback.* If the uniform door is killed, retain the physical distinguished vector and estimate $$-2\operatorname{Re}     \langle A_X\zeta_*,M_X\zeta_*\rangle   \label{eq:tpc60-next-signed-cross-term}$$ using the exact base–missing and unequal-prime decomposition. This is a legitimate narrower route, provided it is not relabeled uniform.

The mass–degree route advances automatically at zero fixed-power cost when its arithmetic saving strictly beats the degree plus all represented-reference losses, as in [\[eq:tpc60-common-reference-threshold\]](TPC-060.md#ref-2d7bdab0c6e4b733). At equality, the finer test [\[eq:tpc60-equality-subpower-test\]](TPC-060.md#ref-94a5ef92682d0359) decides whether this certificate still closes at subpower cost. If every provable lower bound already spends the remaining $1/400$ slack, the correct result is a route-closure theorem, not an adjusted normalization.

#### Conclusion.

TPC–59 exposed a high Walsh boundary omitted by the represented terminal census. The present paper closes the corresponding interface: it writes the physical maps on one coefficient space, identifies the precise same-prime simplification, eliminates invisible coefficient directions by shorting the full Gram, and gives sharp kernel and mass–degree certificates. The route has therefore moved from an unspecified *off-carrier restoration* to a finite list of falsifiable arithmetic tasks. It has not crossed the parity barrier.
