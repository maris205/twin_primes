## Abstract

TPC–60 reduces restoration of the missing very-high Walsh face to a raw missing-mass estimate and an upper bound for the missing native grouping on the actual row-coefficient image. We identify both quantities exactly at the critical cutoff $y\asymp Q$ and one fixed nonzero shift.

Under the explicit identical-support specialization, each row $m$ has a largest-prime cofactor exposure measure $\nu_m$. Represented and missing raw masses are precisely $\nu_m(\mathcal I_m)$ and $\nu_m(\mathcal I_m^c)$. The optimal coefficient-uniform missing fraction is therefore the worst row defect, not an average cofactor density. We prove the fixed-shift cofactor ladder $$j=j_0+rt,  \qquad p=p_0+mt,$$ and a Brun–Titchmarsh refinement. It yields a harmonic weighted defect certificate and shows why one omitted small cofactor can be more dangerous than its global cardinality suggests. Since the inherited interface leaves $\mathcal I_X$ as an arbitrary retained relation, it does not imply any nontrivial uniform missing-mass saving; this is a hypothesis-insufficiency theorem, not a counterexample to a more specific upstream carrier.

For the native map $M_X$, let $\mathbf D_M=\operatorname{diag}(W_M(m))$. The optimal physical upper degree is $$\kappa_M=  \lambda_{\max}\!\left(  \mathbf D_M^{\dagger/2}M_X^*M_X\mathbf D_M^{\dagger/2}  \big|_{\operatorname{Ran}\mathbf D_M}\right),$$ so $\|M_X\zeta\|^2\le\kappa_M\mathcal D_M(\zeta)$ sharply. On a nonempty missing carrier, fixed $(i,p)$ incidence gives an exact unit diagonal and $\kappa_M=1+\lambda_{\max}(\Xi_{\ne p})$; all excess comes from unequal high primes. After a fixed row gauge, the off-diagonal entries are weighted two-affine-form Möbius correlations of $\mu(mj+h_0)\mu(m'j+h_0)$, equivalently sums on $m'pr-mp'r'=h_0(m'-m)$. This is the explicit missing-high parity kernel. We give graph, stable-rank, Schur-complement, and block certificates for its positive spectrum and a residual-level endpoint wedge. No bound $\kappa_M=X^{o(1)}$, missing-mass saving, parity improvement, prime-pair lower bound, or twin-prime consequence is asserted.

# Introduction

###### ref-e13471df79f04928

###### ref-5ea5739b7b92efd5

The critical-cutoff correction of TPC–59 reopens the geometric cofactor scale $r\asymp J$, but it does not say that the terminal retained-pair carrier meets the largest-prime cofactors actually exposed by the complete Walsh face. TPC–60 consequently splits that face into represented and missing atoms and writes their physical native outputs on one row-coefficient space. If $A_X$ is the base plus represented-high map and $M_X$ is the missing-high correction, the exact full map is $$B_X=A_X+M_X.  \label{eq:tpc61-intro-full-map}$$ Restoring the missing atoms is not monotone after grouping. TPC–60 proves the sharp pointwise certificate $$\frac{\|B_X\zeta\|^2}{\|A_X\zeta\|^2}  \ge  \left(   1-\sqrt{    \frac{d_{\rm miss}\mathcal D_M(\zeta)}{\|A_X\zeta\|^2}}  \right)_+^2,  \label{eq:tpc61-intro-ambient-certificate}$$ but its ambient degree $d_{\rm miss}$ is far too expensive at the endpoint. The next task is therefore not another abstract lower angle. It is to determine, on the literal carrier,

1.  how much raw high mass lies outside the retained cofactor relation;

2.  how large the missing grouping is on the actual row-coefficient image.

This paper answers both questions at the level of exact objects and sharp finite-dimensional certificates. It does not estimate the final arithmetic constants. The distinction matters: replacing an unknown quantity by its correct formula is progress only if the formula produces new tests, new closures, or a narrower surviving target. Here it does all three.

## From cofactor counts to exposure matching

###### ref-d29cb3bd8efc6a4c

###### ref-b852eb1397a8e0bb

On the very-high face, every target has the unique factorization $$mj+h_0=p_m(j)r_m(j),  \qquad p_m(j)=P^{+}(mj+h_0)>y.  \label{eq:tpc61-intro-largest-prime}$$ For a row $m$, we retain the complete literal weight of all opposite rows and define the cofactor exposure $\nu_m(r)$ at those orbit times for which $r_m(j)=r$. Under identical Walsh and terminal supports, the retained set $\mathcal I_m=\{r:(m,r)\in\mathcal I_X\}$ captures exactly $$W_R(m)=\nu_m(\mathcal I_m),  \qquad  W_M(m)=\nu_m(\mathcal I_m^c).  \label{eq:tpc61-intro-exposure-split}$$ Thus the problem is a weighted matching problem between a deterministic retained relation and a largest-prime exposure profile. Marginal knowledge of $\#\mathcal I_m$, or an average over rows, does not determine this match.

###### ref-da1a1dfcff7317b6

There is, however, new arithmetic structure inside one row. The terminal support forces $(m,r)=1$. Fixing $(m,r)$ in [\[eq:tpc61-intro-largest-prime\]](TPC-061.md#ref-d29cb3bd8efc6a4c) gives $$j=j_0+rt,  \qquad  p=p_0+mt.  \label{eq:tpc61-intro-row-ladder}$$ Consequently a fixed critical cofactor $r\asymp J$ occurs at only $O(1)$ orbit times. For smaller $r$, the relevant defect is harmonic: the candidate load is $1+J/r$, with a logarithmic Brun–Titchmarsh improvement. A single omitted $r=1$ may therefore have a length-$J$ candidate ladder even though it is a negligible fraction of the global cofactor universe.

## The optimal physical upper degree

###### ref-a09cf2c3ac16b115

###### ref-69dc3960eb69997b

###### ref-b6035396d062314c

The raw missing diagonal is a quadratic form on the row space: $$\mathcal D_M(\zeta)=\langle\mathbf D_M\zeta,\zeta\rangle,  \qquad  \mathbf D_M=\operatorname{diag}(W_M(m)).  \label{eq:tpc61-intro-DM}$$ The least constant in $$\|M_X\zeta\|^2\le\kappa\mathcal D_M(\zeta)  \label{eq:tpc61-intro-kappa-inequality}$$ is $$\kappa_M  =\lambda_{\max}\!\left(  \mathbf D_M^{\dagger/2}M_X^*M_X\mathbf D_M^{\dagger/2}  \big|_{\operatorname{Ran}\mathbf D_M}\right).  \label{eq:tpc61-intro-kappa}$$ This is not the ambient native fiber degree. It is the exact operator norm after the atomic lift is restricted to the coupled physical row image.

###### ref-5f999b804349aba3

The fixed-$(i,p)$ singleton incidence makes each prime block an isometry relative to its raw diagonal. When $S_M\ne\{0\}$, normalization gives $$\mathbf D_M^{\dagger/2}M_X^*M_X\mathbf D_M^{\dagger/2}  =I+\Xi_{\ne p},  \qquad  \kappa_M=1+\lambda_{\max}(\Xi_{\ne p}).  \label{eq:tpc61-intro-prime-alias}$$ All excess above one is therefore unequal-prime alias. The matrix further splits into same-residual determinant-ladder collisions and the new unequal-residual alias created by native erasure. Stable rank, weighted collision graphs, Schur complements, and dyadic block decompositions give rigorous ways either to certify a small positive spectrum or to kill that possibility before attempting a delicate number-theoretic proof.

## The parity kernel is literal

###### ref-08d9df08d0f286f5

###### ref-f84c794ea21f21dc

###### ref-52e9b8df768a446c

The remaining Gram is not an unnamed “random-sign” object. Since $mj+h_0=p r$ is squarefree, $$-\mu(r)=\mu(mj+h_0).  \label{eq:tpc61-intro-mu-n}$$ The fixed factor $\mu(d_m)$ can be removed by a diagonal unitary gauge. For two distinct rows, the normalized Gram entry is then a weighted correlation of $$\mu(mj+h_0)\mu(m'j+h_0)  \label{eq:tpc61-intro-two-affine}$$ on the simultaneous missing very-high support. Writing $mj+h_0=pr$ and $m'j+h_0=p'r'$, the common-orbit condition is exactly $$m'pr-mp'r'=h_0(m'-m).  \label{eq:tpc61-intro-determinant}$$ Thus the surviving route is an operator-norm problem for a truncated two-affine-form Möbius kernel, equivalently a signed determinant-surface sum. Known fixed-form or averaged cancellation cannot simply be inserted: the slopes range with $X$, the support includes a largest-prime cutoff and cofactor mismatch, and the desired quantity is a matrix norm rather than a single unrestricted average.

## Main consequences and boundary

###### ref-5680168e8ba995e9

###### ref-ca8f88b7bcd1670a

###### ref-2fc4cc0d1b01150f

For $A_X\zeta\ne0$, the exact replacement of [\[eq:tpc61-intro-ambient-certificate\]](TPC-061.md#ref-5ea5739b7b92efd5) is $$\boxed{  \frac{\|B_X\zeta\|^2}{\|A_X\zeta\|^2}  \ge  \left(   1-\sqrt{    \frac{\kappa_M\mathcal D_M(\zeta)}{\|A_X\zeta\|^2}}  \right)_+^2.}  \label{eq:tpc61-intro-restricted-certificate}$$ For a distinguished vector, $\kappa_M$ may be replaced by its one-vector Rayleigh quotient; that conclusion remains pointwise. If $$\kappa_M\le X^{\nu_M+o(1)},  \quad  \mathcal D_M(\zeta)\le X^{-\sigma_M+o(1)}                    \mathcal D_{\rm ref}(\zeta),  \quad  \|A_X\zeta\|^2\ge  X^{-\lambda_{\rm rep}+o(1)}\mathcal D_{\rm ref}(\zeta),  \label{eq:tpc61-intro-three-exponents}$$ then the strict condition $$\sigma_M>\nu_M+\lambda_{\rm rep}  \label{eq:tpc61-intro-zero-cost-condition}$$ gives zero fixed-power reassembly loss in the same declared scope.

What is proved here is the exposure disintegration, the row ladder, the harmonic defect certificate, the optimal restricted Gram and its exact parity-kernel representation. What is not proved is a positive exposure match, a subpower bound for $\kappa_M$, or any lower square at one fixed shift. The result is L0/L1. It neither claims nor hints at an L2 parity advance, a prime-pair asymptotic, or a twin-prime consequence.

The paper is organized as follows. freezes the literal carrier. treat the missing-mass side. identify and test the restricted native Gram. The endpoint ledger and exact next gate appear in \[[sec:tpc61-endpoint-ledger](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-061.md#src-0c2e4fc4b163e4dd),[sec:tpc61-claims-next](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-061.md#src-6e63d4081462718f)\].

We begin on the same one-side direct slice as TPC–53–60. The purpose of this section is to freeze the arithmetic carrier before defining either an exposure profile or a normalized Gram. None of the sets below may depend on the coefficient vector.

## Scales, rows, and the critical cutoff

###### ref-db7e6ff9a31d73d8

The inherited source row has the unique factorization $$m=d_m\ell_m,  \qquad d_m\asymp D,  \qquad \ell_m\asymp L,  \qquad m\asymp Q=DL,  \label{eq:tpc61-source-scales}$$ inside one declared row system. The orbit time satisfies $j\asymp J$, and at the endpoint $$\begin{aligned}  Q&=X^{267/400+o(1)},  &J&=X^{133/400+o(1)},\\  D&=X^{10049/52500+o(1)},  &L&=X^{99979/210000+o(1)}.  \end{aligned}  \label{eq:tpc61-endpoint-scales}$$ The physical shift $h_0\ne0$ is fixed. The equality support is squarefree and obeys $$(d_m,mj+h_0)=(d_m,h_0)=1.  \label{eq:tpc61-source-coprimality}$$

Throughout, the cutoff $y$ is *very-high admissible*: $$y>\max\{|h_0|,D_+,J_+,\Delta_M,\sqrt{N_+}\},  \qquad  \frac{D_+N_+}{y}<D_-N_-.  \label{eq:tpc61-very-high-admissible}$$ In particular, $y=C_{\rm vh}Q$ is allowed for a sufficiently large fixed support-dependent constant [`\citep{WangTPC59}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-061.md#cite-4f045ca3a3f537f2). ## Recovered labels and raw amplitudes

###### ref-9822c4f93a36aec7

###### ref-c067e3ad90944a6b

###### ref-f7a7f088f14cf24b

Let $\mathcal W_H(y)$ be the complete nonzero very-high Walsh carrier on this slice. A coordinate $w\in\mathcal W_H(y)$ uniquely recovers $$w=(m,j,\gamma),  \qquad  n(w)=mj+h_0,  \qquad  p(w)=P^{+}(n(w))>y,  \label{eq:tpc61-recovered-mjp}$$ and, because $p(w)^2>n(w)$, $$r(w)=\frac{n(w)}{p(w)},  \qquad  s(w)=d_mr(w).  \label{eq:tpc61-recovered-rs}$$ The native key is $$i(w)=(L,\gamma,j)  \label{eq:tpc61-native-key-left}$$ on the displayed left slice. The right slice exchanges the two row systems and is added by orthogonal direct sum. Thus a fixed native key fixes the side, opposite row, and the same physical orbit time $j$.

It is the stripped TPC–45 coefficient: the physical high character is applied separately. No atom-dependent free phase is introduced.

###### ref-70512f76d1d08a75

The represented terminal census injects into $\mathcal W_H(y)$. Write $$\mathcal W_H=\mathcal W_R\,\dot\cup\,\mathcal W_M  \label{eq:tpc61-RM-partition}$$ for its image and complement. The raw diagonals are $$\mathcal D_E(\zeta)  :=\sum_{w\in\mathcal W_E}|\beta_w|^2|\zeta_{m(w)}|^2,  \qquad E\in\{R,M,H\},  \label{eq:tpc61-raw-RMH-diagonals}$$ so $\mathcal D_H=\mathcal D_R+\mathcal D_M$ exactly.

On squarefree support, $\mu(s)=\mu(d_m)\mu(r)$. The missing native map is therefore $$(M_X\zeta)_i  :=-\sum_{\substack{w\in\mathcal W_M\\i(w)=i}}        \mu(s(w))\beta_w\zeta_{m(w)}.  \label{eq:tpc61-missing-native-map}$$ This is the same sign convention as TPC–58 and TPC–60; inserting another Möbius sign would change the physical map [`\citep{WangTPC58,WangTPC60}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-061.md#cite-578b76b7099ebcf1); [\[WangTPC60\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-061.md#cite-cf407ea6a063759a).

## The two singleton incidences

###### ref-c058fcd104e3172b

The very-high inequalities have two consequences used repeatedly. First, for fixed $(i,p)$, there is at most one high atom. Indeed, $i$ fixes $j$; if $p$ meets rows $m,m'$, then $p\mid(m-m')j$. The alternative $p\mid j$ is excluded by $p>J_+$, and $|m-m'|\le\Delta_M<p$ then gives $m=m'$; unique row factorization recovers the atom. Second, for fixed $(i,m)$, the target $mj+h_0$, hence $p,r,s$, is already determined. Consequently $$\#\{w\in\mathcal W_H:(i(w),p(w))=(i,p)\}\le1,  \qquad  \#\{w\in\mathcal W_H:(i(w),m(w))=(i,m)\}\le1.  \label{eq:tpc61-ip-im-singletons}$$ These are coordinate incidences, not statements that a native output has only one prime or only one row.

## When missing means cofactor mismatch

The exposure theorem below uses the following explicit specialization.

###### ref-767ddcf16a27ea4d

###### ref-d4a0f9f24660e552

###### ref-9f27981543a1dc8c

> **Definition: Identical-support specialization** \[def:tpc61-identical-support\] The Walsh and terminal constructions have identical row, orbit, opposite-row, terminal-prime, literal-mask, and profile supports, with no additional terminal-only pruning. If $$\mathcal I_m:=\{r:(m,r)\in\mathcal I_X\},  \label{eq:tpc61-retained-cofactor-row}$$ then $$w\in\mathcal W_M  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad  r(w)\notin\mathcal I_{m(w)}.  \label{eq:tpc61-missing-iff-cofactor}$$

TPC–60 proves this conditional support identity. Outside this specialization one must retain the full first-failure partition; the cofactor complement alone need not equal $\mathcal W_M$.

## Six-item physical audit

The construction satisfies the required interface audit.

1.  Every coefficient is the literal $\beta_w\zeta_{m(w)}$.

2.  Every target is $mj+h_0$ for one fixed nonzero physical shift.

3.  All atomic energies use raw counting measure, with no fiber average.

4.  The row, largest prime, cofactor, residual label, and native key are canonically recovered; only identically inactive rows are removed.

5.  The carriers $\mathcal W_H,\mathcal W_R,\mathcal W_M$ and $\mathcal I_X$ are fixed before $\zeta$ is chosen.

6.  Exposure and Gram identities have zero structural cost, but every eventual reassembly loss remains in the strict $1/400$ ledger.

## The realized cofactor measure

###### ref-4b590e5834ebd02b

Work in the identical-support specialization of [\[def:tpc61-identical-support\]](TPC-061.md#ref-767ddcf16a27ea4d). Thus a high Walsh atom is represented precisely when its recovered pair $(m,r)$ belongs to the fixed retained relation $\mathcal I_X$. Let $\mathcal M_X$ be the declared source-row set and retain the notation $$\mathcal I_m=\{r\in\mathbb N:(m,r)\in\mathcal I_X\},  \qquad m\in\mathcal M_X,  \label{eq:tpc61-row-retained-cofactors}$$ from [\[eq:tpc61-retained-cofactor-row\]](TPC-061.md#ref-d4a0f9f24660e552). The set $\mathcal I_m$ is fixed independently of the coefficient vector.

###### ref-c4e5673024289a4a

Every $w\in\mathcal W_H(y)$ has the unique recovered labels in [\[eq:tpc61-recovered-mjp\]](TPC-061.md#ref-9822c4f93a36aec7)–[\[eq:tpc61-recovered-rs\]](TPC-061.md#ref-c067e3ad90944a6b). In particular, $$n(w)=m(w)j(w)+h_0,  \qquad  p(w)=P^{+}(n(w))>y,  \qquad  r(w)=\frac{n(w)}{p(w)}.  \label{eq:tpc61-recovered-prime-cofactor}$$ Literal factorization is $b_w(\zeta)=\beta_w\zeta_{m(w)}$, as in [\[eq:tpc61-literal-row-amplitude\]](TPC-061.md#ref-ca4495297a48d009); $\beta_w$ contains every fixed physical factor but not the bounded row coefficient $\zeta_m$.

> **Definition: Realized row cofactor measure** \[def:tpc61-realized-cofactor-measure\] For $m\in\mathcal M_X$, define the finite positive measure on $\mathbb N$ $$\nu_m(r)  :=\sum_{\substack{w\in\mathcal W_H(y)\\m(w)=m,\ r(w)=r}}        |\beta_w|^2.  \label{eq:tpc61-nu-m-r}$$ Write $$W_H(m):=\sum_r\nu_m(r),\qquad  W_R(m):=\sum_{r\in\mathcal I_m}\nu_m(r),\qquad  W_M(m):=\sum_{r\notin\mathcal I_m}\nu_m(r).  \label{eq:tpc61-row-HRM-weights}$$ Thus $W_H=W_R+W_M$ row by row.

The measure $\nu_m$ is not counting measure on possible cofactors. It counts only cofactors actually realized by the largest-prime factorization of $mj+h_0$, with the complete coefficient-free physical square attached.

> **Proposition: Exact row disintegration** \[prop:tpc61-exact-row-disintegration\] For every row-coefficient vector $\zeta$, $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal D_H(\zeta)  &=\sum_mW_H(m)|\zeta_m|^2,  \label{eq:tpc61-DH-row-disintegration}\\  \mathcal D_R(\zeta)  &=\sum_mW_R(m)|\zeta_m|^2,  \label{eq:tpc61-DR-row-disintegration}\\  \mathcal D_M(\zeta)  &=\sum_mW_M(m)|\zeta_m|^2.  \label{eq:tpc61-DM-row-disintegration}\end{aligned}$$ In particular, the represented/missing split is an orthogonal coordinate projection before residual or native grouping.

No coherent sum occurs in this calculation.

## Sharp uniform and distinguished exposure constants

###### ref-1aaade3bd069847e

For every row with $W_H(m)>0$, define $$\varepsilon_m  :=\frac{W_M(m)}{W_H(m)},  \qquad  \rho_m:=\frac{W_R(m)}{W_H(m)}=1-\varepsilon_m.  \label{eq:tpc61-row-defect-and-exposure}$$

###### ref-7b04e25a85510453

###### ref-a52c9085c1749718

###### ref-71d65f58d1c74f66

> **Theorem: Sharp rowwise exposure minimax** \[thm:tpc61-rowwise-exposure-minimax\] Assume the bounded coefficient class contains a nonzero multiple of every coordinate direction. If $\mathcal D_H$ is not identically zero, then $$\boxed{  \sup_{\mathcal D_H(\zeta)>0}  \frac{\mathcal D_M(\zeta)}{\mathcal D_H(\zeta)}  =\max_{m:W_H(m)>0}\varepsilon_m,}  \label{eq:tpc61-sharp-missing-minimax}$$ and $$\boxed{  \inf_{\mathcal D_H(\zeta)>0}  \frac{\mathcal D_R(\zeta)}{\mathcal D_H(\zeta)}  =\min_{m:W_H(m)>0}\rho_m  =1-\max_{m:W_H(m)>0}\varepsilon_m.}  \label{eq:tpc61-sharp-represented-minimax}$$ Both identities are sharp at every finite $X$.

###### ref-e3e64e84660d0f80

> **Proof** Whenever $\mathcal D_H(\zeta)>0$, $$\frac{\mathcal D_M(\zeta)}{\mathcal D_H(\zeta)}  =\sum_{m:W_H(m)>0}    \frac{W_H(m)|\zeta_m|^2}{\mathcal D_H(\zeta)}\,    \varepsilon_m.  \label{eq:tpc61-defect-convex-combination}$$ This is a convex combination of the row defects. It is at most their maximum, and concentrating $\zeta$ on a maximizing row attains that maximum after a harmless scalar rescaling into the coefficient class. The represented identity follows from $\rho_m=1-\varepsilon_m$, or by the same argument with a minimum.

###### ref-c4c97b2cea405dc2

For one distinguished vector, the exact and generally weaker quantity is $$\varepsilon_*(\zeta)  :=\frac{\mathcal D_M(\zeta)}{\mathcal D_H(\zeta)}  =\sum_m    \frac{W_H(m)|\zeta_m|^2}{\mathcal D_H(\zeta)}\varepsilon_m.  \label{eq:tpc61-distinguished-defect}$$ A favorable value of $\varepsilon_*(\zeta)$ does not imply the uniform bound in [\[eq:tpc61-sharp-missing-minimax\]](TPC-061.md#ref-a52c9085c1749718).

###### ref-c5fbc4bb7dac2bde

###### ref-fa7764f55900de6a

> **Corollary: Exact missing-only-row kill test** \[cor:tpc61-missing-only-row-kill\] If one active row satisfies $$W_R(m_0)=0<W_M(m_0),  \label{eq:tpc61-missing-only-row}$$ then the optimal uniform represented-mass constant is zero and the optimal uniform missing fraction is one.

## What the inherited relation does not determine

###### ref-c6ffefa1f01d1d70

The papers preceding TPC–61 call $\mathcal I_X$ the retained or supported pair relation, but impose no rowwise completion rule on it; arbitrary finite cofactor multiplicity is explicitly allowed [`\citep{WangTPC53,WangTPC54}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-061.md#cite-d85e91fc7a3dbaf8); [\[WangTPC54\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-061.md#cite-6f1a8ec95b6b34b5). Consequently the displayed inherited hypotheses do not force $$\mathcal I_m\cap\operatorname{supp}\nu_m\ne\varnothing  \quad\text{on every active row}.  \label{eq:tpc61-unproved-row-intersection}$$

###### ref-335fbc094fc32226

###### ref-e2c6c6e147c4abac

> **Proposition: Interface-insufficiency theorem** \[prop:tpc61-interface-insufficiency\] Within the finite-relation hypothesis class stated in TPC–53–TPC–60, there is no universal constant $c>0$, and no exponent $\sigma>0$, for which either $$\mathcal D_R\ge c\mathcal D_H  \qquad\text{or}\qquad  \mathcal D_M\le X^{-\sigma+o(1)}\mathcal D_H  \label{eq:tpc61-unavailable-uniform-conclusions}$$ follows from scale compatibility, finiteness of $\mathcal I_X$, and boundedness of the row coefficients alone.

> **Proof** Those assumptions do not exclude a retained relation which omits every realized cofactor on one otherwise active row. Such a row satisfies [\[eq:tpc61-missing-only-row\]](TPC-061.md#ref-fa7764f55900de6a), and [\[cor:tpc61-missing-only-row-kill\]](TPC-061.md#ref-c5fbc4bb7dac2bde) gives both failures. This is a non-implication for the stated interface, not a claim that the upstream physical provenance necessarily chooses such a relation.

###### ref-b0d29ae9c20b2aa9

\[ex:tpc61-average-row-trap\] Take any number of completed rows with $\varepsilon_m=0$ and one active missing-only row with $\varepsilon_{m_0}=1$. The unweighted average of the row defects can tend to zero as the number of completed rows grows, whereas [\[eq:tpc61-sharp-missing-minimax\]](TPC-061.md#ref-a52c9085c1749718) remains equal to one. The coefficient vector supported on $m_0$ detects the bad row exactly. Thus an average census can support a distinguished-vector statement only after the physical coefficient weights in [\[eq:tpc61-distinguished-defect\]](TPC-061.md#ref-c4c97b2cea405dc2) are retained.

The result above closes only a proof architecture: it says that the opaque “retained relation” interface is insufficient. It is not a negative theorem about the actual upstream carrier. A genuine arithmetic advance must either reopen the provenance of $\mathcal I_X$, or directly estimate the realized weighted measures $\nu_m(I_m^c)$.

# The fixed-shift cofactor ladder

We now use the literal equation to bound how much of one row can be captured by a specified missing cofactor. The resulting weight is harmonic in $r$, rather than uniform over the cofactor set.

## Coprimality of a realized cofactor

###### ref-51cd73dfa7d8902e

Write every source row in its inherited unique form $$m=d\ell,  \qquad d\in[D,2D],\quad \ell\in[L,2L]\cap\mathbb P,  \label{eq:tpc61-row-factorization}$$ where $d$ is squarefree, $(d,h_0)=1$, and $L>|h_0|$ for large $X$. On the common high carrier, the TPC–45 support gives $(d,r)=1$ and squarefreeness of $dr$ [`\citep{WangTPC45,WangTPC59,WangTPC60}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-061.md#cite-1802a7e8047e595c); [\[WangTPC59\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-061.md#cite-4f045ca3a3f537f2); [\[WangTPC60\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-061.md#cite-cf407ea6a063759a).

###### ref-efcc2d82eb4ef442

###### ref-eb5c1a4b20343fa2

> **Lemma: Full row–cofactor coprimality** \[lem:tpc61-full-row-cofactor-coprimality\] For every actual high atom, $$\boxed{(m,r)=1.}  \label{eq:tpc61-m-r-coprime}$$ Moreover $(m,h_0)=1$.

> **Proof** It remains only to exclude $\ell\mid r$. If this held, then $\ell\mid rp=mj+h_0$, while $\ell\mid mj$; hence $\ell\mid h_0$, contradicting $\ell>|h_0|$. Thus $(\ell,r)=1$, and the inherited relation $(d,r)=1$ proves [\[eq:tpc61-m-r-coprime\]](TPC-061.md#ref-eb5c1a4b20343fa2). Finally $(d,h_0)=1$ and $\ell>|h_0|$ imply $(m,h_0)=1$.

## Exact rowwise progression

Let $\mathcal J_X$ denote the fixed orbit slice. It is contained in an integer interval of diameter at most $C_JJ$, where $C_J$ depends only on the declared compact normalized orbit interval. Direct residue splitting may delete points from this interval and therefore only improves the upper bounds below.

###### ref-5cd630806b9af799

###### ref-f48939f23071cd0f

###### ref-3021c2afa6ed1c8f

###### ref-3871c59ccf0263d1

> **Proposition: Exact cofactor ladder** \[prop:tpc61-exact-cofactor-ladder\] Fix a source row $m$ and a positive integer $r$ which is realized by at least one high atom. There are integers $j_0,p_0$ such that every integral solution of $$rp-mj=h_0  \label{eq:tpc61-fixed-row-equation}$$ has the unique form $$\boxed{  j=j_0+rt,  \qquad  p=p_0+mt,  \qquad t\in\mathbb Z.}  \label{eq:tpc61-row-cofactor-ladder}$$ In addition, $$p\equiv h_0r^{-1}\pmod m,  \label{eq:tpc61-prime-residue-class}$$ and this is a reduced residue class modulo $m$.

> **Proof** By [\[lem:tpc61-full-row-cofactor-coprimality\]](TPC-061.md#ref-efcc2d82eb4ef442), multiplication by $m$ is invertible modulo $r$. Hence $mj\equiv-h_0\pmod r$ has one residue class $j\equiv j_0\pmod r$. Substitution of $j=j_0+rt$ into [\[eq:tpc61-fixed-row-equation\]](TPC-061.md#ref-f48939f23071cd0f) gives $p=p_0+mt$, and every such pair is an integral solution. Conversely, subtracting two solutions gives $r(p-p')=m(j-j')$; coprimality of $m,r$ recovers the same parameterization. Reducing the original equation modulo $m$ proves [\[eq:tpc61-prime-residue-class\]](TPC-061.md#ref-3871c59ccf0263d1). The residue is reduced because both $r$ and $h_0$ are coprime to $m$.

###### ref-80a6144ff279eab6

For the next estimates, let $$N_m(r)  :=\#\left\{j\in\mathcal J_X:  \begin{array}{l}  p=(mj+h_0)/r\text{ is prime},\ p>y,\\[-2pt]  p=P^{+}(mj+h_0),\text{ and there is a }\gamma\\[-2pt]  \text{for which the common high masks pass}  \end{array}\right\}.  \label{eq:tpc61-Nmr}$$ All conditions after integrality only delete points from the ladder.

###### ref-cf2fdd87fcdbf756

###### ref-00e8138b34eb7bfd

> **Corollary: Deterministic rowwise capacity** \[cor:tpc61-deterministic-row-capacity\] Uniformly in every realized $m,r$, $$\boxed{  N_m(r)\le 1+C_J\frac Jr.}  \label{eq:tpc61-trivial-row-capacity}$$

> **Proof** The admissible $j$’s lie in one residue class modulo $r$ inside an interval of diameter at most $C_JJ$.

## The available one-dimensional sieve improvement

The prime coordinate in [\[eq:tpc61-row-cofactor-ladder\]](TPC-061.md#ref-3021c2afa6ed1c8f) lies in one reduced residue class modulo $m$. Brun–Titchmarsh therefore improves [\[eq:tpc61-trivial-row-capacity\]](TPC-061.md#ref-00e8138b34eb7bfd) by a logarithm when the ladder is long. We include the short-ladder case explicitly so that no expression with $\log(J/r)\le0$ is used.

###### ref-71f03ef2402e3012

###### ref-81e613e069864efe

###### ref-1552ae530a94b191

> **Proposition: Brun–Titchmarsh row bound** \[prop:tpc61-brun-titchmarsh-row-bound\] There is a constant depending only on the fixed orbit interval such that $$\boxed{  N_m(r)  \ll  \frac m{\varphi(m)}  \left(   1+\frac{J/r}{\log(2+J/r)}  \right).}  \label{eq:tpc61-BT-row-bound}$$ On the inherited row support, $$\frac m{\varphi(m)}=X^{o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc61-m-over-phi-subpower}$$ Thus the prime condition saves at most a logarithm in this coefficient-blind row census; it does not change its fixed-power exponent.

###### ref-ff5f7fb3ebeda41b

> **Proof** Put $T=J/r$. If $T$ is bounded by a sufficiently large constant depending on $C_J$, [\[eq:tpc61-trivial-row-capacity\]](TPC-061.md#ref-00e8138b34eb7bfd) is $O(1)$, which is dominated by the right side of [\[eq:tpc61-BT-row-bound\]](TPC-061.md#ref-81e613e069864efe) because $m/\varphi(m)\ge1$.
>
> Suppose now that $T$ exceeds that constant. The prime values $p=p_0+mt$ lie in an interval of length at most $C_JmT+O(m)$ and in the reduced class [\[eq:tpc61-prime-residue-class\]](TPC-061.md#ref-3871c59ccf0263d1). The interval form of the Brun–Titchmarsh theorem gives $$\#\{p\text{ on this ladder}\}  \ll  \frac{mT}{\varphi(m)\log T};  \label{eq:tpc61-BT-long-case}$$ enlarging the interval by a fixed factor changes only the implied constant. The largest-prime and remaining physical conditions delete primes, so the same upper bound holds for $N_m(r)$. Combining the long and short cases and replacing $\log T$ by the uniformly positive $\log(2+T)$ proves [\[eq:tpc61-BT-row-bound\]](TPC-061.md#ref-81e613e069864efe); see, for example, [`\citet{MontgomeryVaughan2007}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-061.md#cite-3c91c7e57f203d4b).
>
> Finally the standard maximal-order estimate $n/\varphi(n)\ll\log\log(3n)$, together with $m=X^{O(1)}$, proves [\[eq:tpc61-m-over-phi-subpower\]](TPC-061.md#ref-1552ae530a94b191).

###### ref-a1f7017ad2a92f00

###### ref-27b22817a69bd60d

###### ref-05524eed44dd38fa

###### ref-75bbe4b6cba758ce

> **Corollary: Dyadic missing-cofactor census** \[cor:tpc61-dyadic-missing-census\] For a set $E_m(R)\subseteq[R,2R]\cap\mathbb N$, the number of high orbit times on row $m$ whose cofactor lies in $E_m(R)$ is at most $$\ll  \frac m{\varphi(m)}|E_m(R)|  \left(   1+\frac{J/R}{\log(2+J/R)}  \right).  \label{eq:tpc61-dyadic-missing-count}$$ In fixed-power notation, if $$J=X^{\theta_J+o(1)},\quad  R=X^{\theta+o(1)},\quad  |E_m(R)|=X^{e(\theta)+o(1)},  \label{eq:tpc61-dyadic-count-exponents}$$ then the exponent in [\[eq:tpc61-dyadic-missing-count\]](TPC-061.md#ref-27b22817a69bd60d) is at most $$e(\theta)+(\theta_J-\theta)_+.  \label{eq:tpc61-dyadic-capacity-exponent}$$

> **Proof** Sum [\[eq:tpc61-BT-row-bound\]](TPC-061.md#ref-81e613e069864efe) over $r\in E_m(R)$, using $r\asymp R$. Equations [\[eq:tpc61-m-over-phi-subpower\]](TPC-061.md#ref-1552ae530a94b191) and [\[eq:tpc61-dyadic-count-exponents\]](TPC-061.md#ref-05524eed44dd38fa) give the exponent formula.

The estimate is an upper capacity, not a lower bound for actual missing mass. Nevertheless it identifies the strongest conclusion obtainable from the fixed congruence and a one-dimensional upper-bound sieve alone. Any further fixed-power improvement must use the actual retained relation, the physical weights, a lower exposure estimate, or cancellation.

# Harmonic defect, conditional savings, and the native wall

The row ladder shows that one omitted small cofactor is more dangerous than many omitted cofactors near the critical ceiling. We now turn this fact into a literal weighted estimate and audit its endpoint strength.

## A weighted harmonic certificate

###### ref-41df73f4002fdcfd

###### ref-0dc33d2e1c7ebd76

###### ref-8efe6628dec560f6

For each row and orbit time define the coefficient-free high weight $$\Omega_m(j)  :=\sum_{\substack{w\in\mathcal W_H(y)\\m(w)=m,\ j(w)=j}}        |\beta_w|^2.  \label{eq:tpc61-Omega-mj}$$ Every summand at a fixed $(m,j)$ has the same recovered cofactor $$r_m(j):=\frac{mj+h_0}{P^{+}(mj+h_0)}  \label{eq:tpc61-realized-row-cofactor}$$ whenever $P^{+}(mj+h_0)>y$. Different opposite-row atoms remain distinct in [\[eq:tpc61-Omega-mj\]](TPC-061.md#ref-41df73f4002fdcfd). Thus $$W_H(m)=\sum_j\Omega_m(j),  \qquad  W_M(m)=\sum_{\substack{j\\r_m(j)\notin\mathcal I_m}}\Omega_m(j).  \label{eq:tpc61-row-weight-orbit-form}$$

###### ref-53b0b8fd30eb99de

###### ref-ca5d357ce2e96161

###### ref-5d9ac319601ed462

For an active row put $$U_m:=\max_j\Omega_m(j),  \qquad  H_m^{\rm eff}:=\frac{W_H(m)}{U_m}.  \label{eq:tpc61-effective-high-exposure}$$ The second quantity is a weighted effective number of exposed orbit times; it lies in $(0,\#\mathcal J_X]$. Let $$E_m:=\{r\in\mathbb N:r\le N_+/y,\ r\notin\mathcal I_m\}  \label{eq:tpc61-missing-cofactor-set}$$ and define its rowwise sieve-harmonic capacity by $$\mathfrak h_m(E_m)  :=\frac m{\varphi(m)}    \sum_{r\in E_m}    \left(     1+\frac{J/r}{\log(2+J/r)}    \right).  \label{eq:tpc61-harmonic-defect}$$ Including unrealized $r$’s in $E_m$ only enlarges this upper certificate.

###### ref-f7ed12cc03664faa

###### ref-90b0723f63669438

###### ref-8b543a19110a4977

###### ref-0bf8171f8e1d1b19

###### ref-19863a7d06b68bc3

> **Theorem: Literal harmonic-defect bound** \[thm:tpc61-harmonic-defect-bound\] For every active row, $$\boxed{  W_M(m)\ll U_m\mathfrak h_m(E_m),}  \label{eq:tpc61-row-missing-harmonic-bound}$$ and hence $$\boxed{  \varepsilon_m  \ll\frac{\mathfrak h_m(E_m)}{H_m^{\rm eff}}.}  \label{eq:tpc61-row-defect-harmonic-bound}$$ Consequently, if a number $\epsilon_X\ge0$ satisfies $$\sup_{m:W_H(m)>0}  \frac{\mathfrak h_m(E_m)}{H_m^{\rm eff}}  \le\epsilon_X,  \label{eq:tpc61-uniform-harmonic-hypothesis}$$ then, with an absolute fixed-carrier constant, $$\mathcal D_M(\zeta)\ll\epsilon_X\mathcal D_H(\zeta)  \label{eq:tpc61-uniform-harmonic-transfer}$$ for every coefficient vector in the declared uniform class.

###### ref-46ad2a11821279d7

> **Proof** Group the second sum in [\[eq:tpc61-row-weight-orbit-form\]](TPC-061.md#ref-8efe6628dec560f6) by the recovered cofactor. For fixed $r$, there are $N_m(r)$ eligible orbit times, and each aggregate weight is at most $U_m$. Therefore $$W_M(m)  \le U_m\sum_{r\in E_m}N_m(r).  \label{eq:tpc61-WM-before-BT}$$ Apply [\[prop:tpc61-brun-titchmarsh-row-bound\]](TPC-061.md#ref-71f03ef2402e3012) and use [\[eq:tpc61-harmonic-defect\]](TPC-061.md#ref-5d9ac319601ed462); this proves [\[eq:tpc61-row-missing-harmonic-bound\]](TPC-061.md#ref-90b0723f63669438). Division by $W_H(m)=U_mH_m^{\rm eff}$ gives [\[eq:tpc61-row-defect-harmonic-bound\]](TPC-061.md#ref-8b543a19110a4977). The uniform conclusion follows from [\[thm:tpc61-rowwise-exposure-minimax\]](TPC-061.md#ref-7b04e25a85510453).

The theorem is unconditional as an inequality, but a useful numerical value of its right side requires two arithmetic inputs: completion of the retained relation in harmonic capacity and a lower bound for the actual effective high exposure.

## Dyadic exponent law

###### ref-1d00a495ba2a0e24

###### ref-f5bb67250af26c68

###### ref-4bf3241cfece3052

###### ref-235f0326ee045cc7

At the critical endpoint, $$\theta_J:=\frac{133}{400},  \qquad  J=X^{\theta_J+o(1)},  \qquad  \frac{N_+}{y}=X^{\theta_J+o(1)}  \label{eq:tpc61-critical-J-exponent}$$ when $y=C_{\rm vh}Q$ with the required fixed support constant [`\citep{WangTPC59}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-061.md#cite-4f045ca3a3f537f2). Suppose a dyadic block $R=X^{\theta+o(1)}$, $0\le\theta\le\theta_J$, satisfies uniformly on the claimed rows $$|E_m\cap[R,2R]|\le X^{e(\theta)+o(1)},  \qquad  H_m^{\rm eff}\ge X^{\eta_H+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc61-dyadic-defect-exposure-hypotheses}$$ For this block write $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal W_{M,R}  &:=\{w\in\mathcal W_M:R\le r(w)<2R\},  \label{eq:tpc61-missing-block-carrier}\\  W_{M,R}(m)  &:=\sum_{\substack{R\le r<2R\\r\notin\mathcal I_m}}\nu_m(r),  \qquad  \mathcal D_{M,R}(\zeta):=\sum_mW_{M,R}(m)|\zeta_m|^2.  \label{eq:tpc61-missing-block-diagonal}\end{aligned}$$

###### ref-a3f0b49773647f1b

###### ref-29888b532c173fb5

###### ref-d412cf91eec7f452

###### ref-42b2b05030eb0c58

> **Corollary: Dyadic cofactor-saving exponent** \[cor:tpc61-dyadic-saving-exponent\] The contribution of this block to the row defect obeys $$\frac{W_{M,R}(m)}{W_H(m)}  \le  X^{-\sigma_{\rm cof}(\theta)+o(1)},  \label{eq:tpc61-block-defect-saving}$$ where $$\boxed{  \sigma_{\rm cof}(\theta)  :=\eta_H-e(\theta)-(\theta_J-\theta).}  \label{eq:tpc61-general-cofactor-saving}$$ In the balanced-exposure case $\eta_H=\theta_J$, this simplifies to $$\boxed{  \sigma_{\rm cof}(\theta)=\theta-e(\theta).}  \label{eq:tpc61-balanced-cofactor-saving}$$ The conclusion is useful only when the displayed saving is positive. An $O(\log X)$ dyadic union changes no fixed-power exponent.

> **Proof** By [\[cor:tpc61-dyadic-missing-census\]](TPC-061.md#ref-a1f7017ad2a92f00), the harmonic numerator from the block has exponent at most $e(\theta)+\theta_J-\theta$. Divide by the effective-exposure lower bound in [\[eq:tpc61-dyadic-defect-exposure-hypotheses\]](TPC-061.md#ref-f5bb67250af26c68). Summing a logarithmic number of nonnegative block bounds contributes $X^{o(1)}$.

###### ref-7214cb0c58b905df

###### ref-9d508ecb85c9bc59

\[ex:tpc61-one-small-cofactor\] Suppose all integers up to the critical ceiling are retained except $r=1$. Its unweighted global missing fraction is $J^{-1+o(1)}=X^{-133/400+o(1)}$, but the row ladder is $$p=mj+h_0.  \label{eq:tpc61-r-one-ladder}$$ The support capacity has $J$ candidates and Brun–Titchmarsh removes only a logarithm. Hence this cardinal statement certifies no positive fixed-power saving. If the actual high weight on a row is supported on the realized points of this ladder, then $\varepsilon_m=1$. The current retained-relation interface contains no exposure hypothesis excluding that possibility.

###### ref-48ef9ae8031e7471

\[ex:tpc61-weight-concentration\] Even if the missing set has much smaller cardinality than the represented set, the exact measure can satisfy $\nu_m(\mathcal I_m)=0<\nu_m(\mathcal I_m^c)$ when all represented profile squares vanish and one missing profile square is nonzero. Less extremely, represented weights may be arbitrarily smaller than a missing weight. Thus cardinality can replace $\nu_m$ only after a two-sided physical-weight or effective- exposure estimate has been proved on the same carrier.

These examples explain why the correct support statistic is harmonic and why even harmonic smallness must be divided by actual exposure. In particular, a proof based only on this certificate must cover every cofactor $r<X^{\sigma-o(1)}$ exactly, or supply an independent prime/weight saving on the omitted small-cofactor ladders, before claiming an $X^{-\sigma}$ missing-mass bound.

## The residual wedge and the direct-native closure

###### ref-1e63efad68861a1e

###### ref-d03c45a8eb364371

###### ref-fc987be9b2c2652e

The preceding exponent can now be compared with the grouping costs of TPC–60. Write $$\lambda_L:=\frac{99979}{210000}.  \label{eq:tpc61-lambda-L}$$ On a block $R=X^{\theta+o(1)}$, the ambient residual-key degree from the fixed-determinant ladder is $$d_{\rm res}(R)\le X^{\lambda_L-\theta+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc61-residual-degree-exponent}$$ In the balanced-exposure scenario with missing-cardinality exponent $e(\theta)$, the phase-blind residual mass–degree exponent is therefore $$\lambda_L-2\theta+e(\theta).  \label{eq:tpc61-residual-product-exponent}$$

###### ref-951e87968269c93d

###### ref-8954c624e407c8bd

At the critical block $\theta=133/400$, $$\lambda_L-\theta  =\frac{15077}{105000},  \label{eq:tpc61-critical-residual-degree}$$ whereas $$\boxed{  2\theta-\lambda_L  =\frac{39671}{210000}  =0.1889095\ldots.}  \label{eq:tpc61-residual-wedge}$$ Thus the residual-key exponents themselves leave a genuine positive window. They do not close the physical map, because the latter also erases the residual label $s$.

###### ref-51a5ae86e55ea12a

###### ref-6cdd5b17dc1b7e1f

###### ref-c3f85f58479f85f1

###### ref-eeee4bbb11bf53aa

###### ref-55022c35894b919e

###### ref-74691e2378a3f6ac

###### ref-435de0e6e0ee6606

###### ref-94a942f35d54feb0

To state the remaining requirement in the notation of the restricted-Gram section, factor the missing map on one block as $$\mathcal K_X  \xrightarrow{\ L_{M,R}\ }  \ell^2(\mathcal W_{M,R})  \xrightarrow{\ C_{{\rm res},R}\ }  \ell^2(i,s)  \xrightarrow{\ \mathsf E\ }  \ell^2(i),  \label{eq:tpc61-two-stage-missing-map}$$ where $T_{M,R}:=C_{{\rm res},R}L_{M,R}$ is the residual map on the block and $\mathsf E$ is the canonical residual-label erasure. Define the blockwise restricted erasure constant $$\beta_{M,R}  :=\left\|  \mathsf E\big|_{\operatorname{Ran}T_{M,R}}  \right\|^2  =X^{\mu_{\rm erase}+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc61-restricted-erasure-exponent}$$ This is the block analogue of $\beta_M$ in [\[thm:tpc61-restricted-erasure-norm\]](TPC-061.md#ref-3f562dd780c1e495); it is not the ambient number of residual labels at one native output. Likewise [\[eq:tpc61-residual-degree-exponent\]](TPC-061.md#ref-d03c45a8eb364371) gives $$\kappa_{{\rm res},R}  :=\sup_{\mathcal D_{M,R}(\zeta)>0}    \frac{\|T_{M,R}\zeta\|^2}{\mathcal D_{M,R}(\zeta)}  \le d_{\rm res}(R)  \le X^{\lambda_L-\theta+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc61-block-residual-kappa}$$ If the represented reference satisfies $$\|A_X\zeta\|^2  \ge X^{-\lambda_{\rm rep}+o(1)}\mathcal D_H(\zeta),  \label{eq:tpc61-represented-reference-loss}$$ then the critical-block contribution has zero fixed-power reassembly cost under the strict condition $$\boxed{  e+\mu_{\rm erase}+\lambda_{\rm rep}  <\frac{39671}{210000}.}  \label{eq:tpc61-critical-residual-route-condition}$$ Indeed, the two-stage inequality $$\|M_{X,R}\zeta\|^2  \le\beta_{M,R}\kappa_{{\rm res},R}        \mathcal D_{M,R}(\zeta)  \label{eq:tpc61-block-two-stage-upper}$$ shows that the product of missing mass, residual degree, restricted erasure, and represented-reference loss is then $o(1)$. The reverse triangle certificate of TPC–60 yields the blockwise conclusion $$\frac{\|(A_X+M_{X,R})\zeta\|^2}{\|A_X\zeta\|^2}=1+o(1).  \label{eq:tpc61-zero-cost-reassembly-consequence}$$ For the full missing map, the same conclusion holds only if every other nonempty dyadic block is empty or satisfies its analogous strict estimate uniformly with a fixed positive margin. Since there are $O(\log X)$ blocks, those estimates give $$\sum_R\|M_{X,R}\zeta\|=o(\|A_X\zeta\|),  \label{eq:tpc61-all-blocks-small}$$ and then $M_X=\sum_RM_{X,R}$ yields the full version of [\[eq:tpc61-zero-cost-reassembly-consequence\]](TPC-061.md#ref-435de0e6e0ee6606). A favorable critical block alone does not control missing mass on smaller cofactors. The condition is uniform or distinguished according to the scope of [\[eq:tpc61-represented-reference-loss\]](TPC-061.md#ref-eeee4bbb11bf53aa) and the cofactor-exposure hypotheses; the two scopes are not interchangeable.

###### ref-05ab755c8852ff5c

###### ref-7f6dd2fb967d969f

By contrast, the only inherited direct native-degree bound has exponent $$\mu_{\rm native}=\frac{267}{400}.  \label{eq:tpc61-crude-native-exponent}$$ Even the best nonempty pure-cardinality certificate at the critical scale, one missing cofactor with balanced exposure, saves at most $133/400$. Hence the direct-native phase-blind architecture has the fixed exponent deficit $$\boxed{  \frac{267}{400}-\frac{133}{400}  =\frac{67}{200}>0.}  \label{eq:tpc61-direct-native-deficit}$$ It cannot prove reassembly by paying the full native degree. This is a route-specific closure, not a lower bound for the actual restricted norm and not an endpoint loss to be inserted into the ledger.

#### Claim boundary.

The exact row measure, cofactor ladder, Brun–Titchmarsh estimate, harmonic certificate, and exponent audit are L0/L1 results at one fixed $h_0$. No estimate in this section proves that the actual retained relation has a small harmonic complement, that every active row has polynomial high exposure, or that $\mu_{\rm erase}$ satisfies [\[eq:tpc61-critical-residual-route-condition\]](TPC-061.md#ref-55022c35894b919e). Consequently there is no L2 fixed-shift lower square, parity improvement, prime-pair asymptotic, or twin-prime implication. The positive conclusion is narrower and falsifiable: the residual wedge is open, the coefficient-blind direct native door is closed, and restricted Möbius erasure is the next arithmetic gate.

# The restricted native Gram of the missing carrier

The ambient native degree from TPC–60 is the norm of a grouping operator on a free atomic space [`\citep{WangTPC60}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-061.md#cite-cf407ea6a063759a). The physical missing vector does not range over that free space: every atom in one source row is driven by the same row coefficient. This section computes the optimal grouping constant on that actual coefficient image. It also separates the same-residual determinant-ladder collisions from the new collisions created when the residual label is erased.

## Atomic, residual, and native maps

Let $\mathcal W_M$ be the fixed missing-high carrier, let $\mathcal M_X$ be the declared finite source-row set, and put $$\mathcal M_M:=\{m\in\mathcal M_X:  \text{some }w\in\mathcal W_M\text{ satisfies }m(w)=m\}.$$ All maps below act on the original coefficient space $\mathcal K_X=\ell^2(\mathcal M_X)$; rows outside $\mathcal M_M$ remain zero directions of the missing diagonal rather than being deleted from the represented map. On an active missing atom $w$, write the literal coefficient as $$b_w(\zeta)=\beta_w\zeta_{m(w)},  \qquad  m(w)\in\mathcal M_M,  \label{eq:tpc61-literal-missing-coefficient}$$ and absorb the physical high-character sign and the residual Möbius gauge into $$c_w:=-\mu(s(w))\beta_w.  \label{eq:tpc61-sign-compatible-atom}$$ The active squarefree support gives $|c_w|=|\beta_w|$. Define the atomic lift $$L_M:\mathcal K_X\longrightarrow\ell^2(\mathcal W_M),  \qquad  (L_M\zeta)_w=c_w\zeta_{m(w)}.  \label{eq:tpc61-atomic-lift}$$ Its Gram is diagonal: $$\begin{aligned}  \mathbf D_M  &:=L_M^*L_M    =\operatorname{diag}\bigl(W_M(m)\bigr)_{m\in\mathcal M_X},  \label{eq:tpc61-missing-diagonal-matrix}\\  W_M(m)  &:=\sum_{\substack{w\in\mathcal W_M\\m(w)=m}}|\beta_w|^2,  \qquad  \mathcal D_M(\zeta)=\langle\mathbf D_M\zeta,\zeta\rangle.  \label{eq:tpc61-row-missing-weight}\end{aligned}$$ Thus $\mathcal D_M$ is exactly the raw missing atomic diagonal, with no fiber normalization.

> **Theorem: Exact restricted native degree** \[thm:tpc61-optimal-kappa\] If $S_M\ne\{0\}$, define $$\boxed{  \kappa_M:=  \lambda_{\max}\!\left(   \mathbf D_M^{\dagger/2}M_X^*M_X   \mathbf D_M^{\dagger/2}\big|_{S_M}  \right).}  \label{eq:tpc61-kappa-definition}$$ Then $$\begin{aligned}  \kappa_M  &=\sup_{\mathcal D_M(\zeta)>0}    \frac{\|M_X\zeta\|^2}{\mathcal D_M(\zeta)}  \label{eq:tpc61-kappa-rayleigh}\\  &=\inf\{\kappa\ge0:        M_X^*M_X\preceq\kappa\mathbf D_M\}.  \label{eq:tpc61-kappa-loewner}\end{aligned}$$ Consequently $$\boxed{  \|M_X\zeta\|^2\le\kappa_M\mathcal D_M(\zeta)}  \qquad(\zeta\in\mathcal K_X),  \label{eq:tpc61-optimal-native-upper}$$ and $\kappa_M$ is the least possible coefficient-uniform constant in [\[eq:tpc61-optimal-native-upper\]](TPC-061.md#ref-cb7eeca5d0b1458b). If $S_M=\{0\}$, then $M_X=0$, and we set $\kappa_M=0$.

> **Proof** For $\mathcal D_M(\zeta)>0$, put $x=\mathbf D_M^{1/2}\zeta\in S_M$. Since $M_X$ vanishes on $\operatorname{Ker}\mathbf D_M$, $$M_X\zeta=M_X\mathbf D_M^{\dagger/2}x.$$ The quotient in [\[eq:tpc61-kappa-rayleigh\]](TPC-061.md#ref-63c26d166ee0f401) is therefore the Rayleigh quotient of the positive operator in [\[eq:tpc61-kappa-definition\]](TPC-061.md#ref-eeadbc1ea5744789). Rayleigh–Ritz proves [\[eq:tpc61-kappa-rayleigh\]](TPC-061.md#ref-63c26d166ee0f401). A quadratic-form inequality for every $\zeta$ is equivalent to the Loewner inequality in [\[eq:tpc61-kappa-loewner\]](TPC-061.md#ref-bfafe8f361440c02), which proves optimality.

Let $C_M:\ell^2(\mathcal W_M)\to\ell^2(\mathcal I_{\rm out})$ be the ambient native grouping $$(C_Ma)_i=\sum_{\substack{w\in\mathcal W_M\\i(w)=i}}a_w,  \qquad  d_{\rm miss}:=\max_i|\{w\in\mathcal W_M:i(w)=i\}|.  \label{eq:tpc61-ambient-native-grouping}$$ Then $M_X=C_ML_M$ after the unit gauges have been included in [\[eq:tpc61-sign-compatible-atom\]](TPC-061.md#ref-7d857e3e27c60a4b).

###### ref-4b24d926ef16faa0

> **Corollary: Compression improves the ambient degree** \[cor:tpc61-kappa-below-degree\] One has $$\boxed{\kappa_M\le d_{\rm miss}.}  \label{eq:tpc61-kappa-ambient-bound}$$ The inequality can be sharp, or the two sides can differ by an arbitrarily large factor.

> **Proof** The map $$U_M:=L_M\mathbf D_M^{\dagger/2}|_{S_M}$$ is an isometry because $U_M^*U_M=I_{S_M}$. Also $\|C_M\|^2=d_{\rm miss}$ by the exact fiberwise Cauchy–Schwarz calculation of TPC–60. Hence $$\kappa_M  =\|C_MU_M\|^2  \le\|C_M\|^2=d_{\rm miss}.$$ Sharp examples are given in \[[ex:tpc61-coherent-incidence](TPC-061.md#ref-456bbaf1d81b5aee),[ex:tpc61-fourier-incidence](TPC-061.md#ref-3988ba0af7916329)\].

## Prime blocks and the unequal-prime alias operator

###### ref-770c08807baf03dd

###### ref-15c83ae0a88ecf54

For $p>y$, let $\mathcal W_{M,p}$ be the missing atoms with terminal prime $p$, and let $M_p$ be their contribution to the native map. > **Theorem: Prime-block isometry and exact alias Gram** \[thm:tpc61-prime-block-identity\] Assume $S_M\ne\{0\}$. On the literal fixed-$h_0$ carrier, $$\begin{aligned}  M_p^*M_p&=\mathbf D_p,  \label{eq:tpc61-prime-block-isometry}\\  \sum_{p>y}\mathbf D_p&=\mathbf D_M.  \label{eq:tpc61-prime-diagonal-sum}\end{aligned}$$ Define $$\widehat M_p  :=M_p\mathbf D_M^{\dagger/2}|_{S_M},  \qquad  \Xi_{\ne p}  :=\sum_{\substack{p,q>y\\p\ne q}}      \widehat M_p^*\widehat M_q.  \label{eq:tpc61-unequal-prime-operator}$$ Then $\Xi_{\ne p}$ is Hermitian and $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_p\widehat M_p^*\widehat M_p&=I_{S_M},  \label{eq:tpc61-normalized-prime-resolution}\\  \mathbf D_M^{\dagger/2}M_X^*M_X  \mathbf D_M^{\dagger/2}\big|_{S_M}  &=I_{S_M}+\Xi_{\ne p},  \label{eq:tpc61-native-gram-prime-split}\\  \kappa_M&=\boxed{1+\lambda_{\max}(\Xi_{\ne p})}.  \label{eq:tpc61-kappa-unequal-prime}\end{aligned}$$ Every nonzero matrix entry of $\Xi_{\ne p}$ is an unequal-prime alias at a common native output.

> **Proof** For a fixed $p$, the singleton property makes the summands at distinct atoms occupy distinct native coordinates. Therefore $$\|M_p\zeta\|^2  =\sum_{w\in\mathcal W_{M,p}}    |\beta_w|^2|\zeta_{m(w)}|^2  =\langle\mathbf D_p\zeta,\zeta\rangle,$$ which proves [\[eq:tpc61-prime-block-isometry\]](TPC-061.md#ref-2d6c390fc5abbc31). The prime carrier partition proves [\[eq:tpc61-prime-diagonal-sum\]](TPC-061.md#ref-6a42e05721eab42f). Normalize this identity by $\mathbf D_M^{\dagger/2}$ to obtain [\[eq:tpc61-normalized-prime-resolution\]](TPC-061.md#ref-6c944015552a7adf). Expanding the square of $\sum_p\widehat M_p$ gives [\[eq:tpc61-native-gram-prime-split\]](TPC-061.md#ref-42f4a026c58bee31); the ordered terms $(p,q)$ and $(q,p)$ show that $\Xi_{\ne p}$ is Hermitian. Equation [\[eq:tpc61-kappa-unequal-prime\]](TPC-061.md#ref-e73f15cc94034e47) follows from [\[thm:tpc61-optimal-kappa\]](TPC-061.md#ref-a78f47e14d146804).

The operator $\Xi_{\ne p}$ is generally indefinite; only $I+\Xi_{\ne p}$ is positive. This causes no loss in [\[eq:tpc61-kappa-unequal-prime\]](TPC-061.md#ref-e73f15cc94034e47): adding the identity shifts every eigenvalue by exactly one, so $\lambda_{\max}(I+\Xi_{\ne p}) =1+\lambda_{\max}(\Xi_{\ne p})$ without any positivity assumption on $\Xi_{\ne p}$.

The diagonal of $\Xi_{\ne p}$ is zero. Indeed, $i$ fixes the orbit time $j$, while $i$ and a source row $m$ fix $n=mj+h_0$, its unique prime $p=P^{+}(n)>y$, the cofactor $r=n/p$, and the residual label $s=d_mr$. Thus two different terminal primes from one source row cannot meet at the same native output. In particular, if $r_M:=\dim S_M>0$, the normalized native Gram has trace $r_M$.

> **Theorem: Residual/native positive-spectrum split** \[thm:tpc61-residual-native-split\] The two operators in [\[def:tpc61-two-alias-operators\]](TPC-061.md#ref-56554c90c9bcdde0) are Hermitian, have zero diagonal, and satisfy $$\begin{aligned}  \mathbf D_M^{\dagger/2}T_M^*T_M  \mathbf D_M^{\dagger/2}\big|_{S_M}  &=I+\Xi_{=s},  \label{eq:tpc61-residual-normalized-gram}\\  \mathbf D_M^{\dagger/2}M_X^*M_X  \mathbf D_M^{\dagger/2}\big|_{S_M}  &=I+\Xi_{=s}+\Xi_{\ne s}.  \label{eq:tpc61-native-normalized-gram-split}\end{aligned}$$ Consequently, if $$\kappa_{\rm res}  :=\sup_{\mathcal D_M(\zeta)>0}  \frac{\|T_M\zeta\|^2}{\mathcal D_M(\zeta)},  \label{eq:tpc61-residual-effective-degree}$$ then $$\begin{aligned}  \kappa_{\rm res}  &=1+\lambda_{\max}(\Xi_{=s}),  \label{eq:tpc61-residual-positive-spectrum}\\  \boxed{  \kappa_M  \le1+\lambda_{\max}(\Xi_{=s})        +\lambda_{\max}(\Xi_{\ne s}).}  \label{eq:tpc61-two-positive-spectrum-bound}\end{aligned}$$

The arithmetic roles of the two terms differ. The support of $\Xi_{=s}$ is governed by the determinant ladder $$rp-dj\ell=h_0$$ inside one residual label. The operator $\Xi_{\ne s}$ is created only after $s$ is erased; its terms share the native output $i$ but have different residual labels. Thus the residual ladder does not by itself control $\Xi_{\ne s}$. Also, the present upper-norm problem needs only the positive spectra of these signed operators. Bounding their full absolute norms is sufficient but can discard favorable cancellation.

## Uniform and distinguished quantifiers

###### ref-f029c42d858630ac

For one coefficient vector with $\mathcal D_M(\zeta)>0$, define $$\kappa_M(\zeta)  :=\frac{\|M_X\zeta\|^2}{\mathcal D_M(\zeta)}.  \label{eq:tpc61-pointwise-kappa}$$

> **Proposition: Pointwise Rayleigh identity** \[prop:tpc61-pointwise-rayleigh\] Let $x=\mathbf D_M^{1/2}\zeta$. Then $$\boxed{  \kappa_M(\zeta)  =\frac{\langle(I+\Xi_{\ne p})x,x\rangle}{\|x\|^2},  \qquad  0\le\kappa_M(\zeta)\le\kappa_M.}  \label{eq:tpc61-pointwise-rayleigh}$$ The pointwise value may be zero even when the uniform constant is large.

> **Proof** Insert $x=\mathbf D_M^{1/2}\zeta$ into [\[eq:tpc61-native-gram-prime-split\]](TPC-061.md#ref-42f4a026c58bee31). Positivity gives the lower bound and [\[thm:tpc61-optimal-kappa\]](TPC-061.md#ref-a78f47e14d146804) gives the upper bound.

###### ref-cc5829edb4ba41cd

For the represented map $A_X$ and every vector with $A_X\zeta\ne0$, the reverse triangle inequality now gives the optimal mass-to-native certificate available from $\mathcal D_M$: $$\begin{aligned}  \frac{\|(A_X+M_X)\zeta\|^2}{\|A_X\zeta\|^2}  &\ge  \left(   1-\sqrt{    \frac{\kappa_M\mathcal D_M(\zeta)}         {\|A_X\zeta\|^2}}  \right)_+^2  \label{eq:tpc61-uniform-reassembly-certificate}\\  &\ge  \left(   1-\sqrt{    \frac{d_{\rm miss}\mathcal D_M(\zeta)}         {\|A_X\zeta\|^2}}  \right)_+^2.  \notag\end{aligned}$$ For one distinguished physical vector, $\kappa_M$ in the first line can be replaced by the exact number $\kappa_M(\zeta)$. This replacement is pointwise only: a small Rayleigh quotient for one vector does not imply the Loewner inequality [\[eq:tpc61-kappa-loewner\]](TPC-061.md#ref-bfafe8f361440c02).

# The literal parity kernel

The normalized Gram in [6](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-061.md#src-dcd573fb4d489682) is exact, but its off-diagonal operator still appears there as an unequal-prime alias. We now calibrate every sign in that operator. The result is a weighted correlation of the two affine values $$mj+h_0\quad\hbox{and}\quad m'j+h_0$$ on the actual missing carrier. No randomness hypothesis for the Möbius function is made, and no estimate for the resulting operator norm is asserted.

## Row gauge and exact sign calibration

###### ref-da84e6227732eb66

Retain the active row space $S_M=\operatorname{Ran}\mathbf D_M$ and the unequal-prime alias operator $\Xi_{\ne p}$ from [\[eq:tpc61-unequal-prime-operator\]](TPC-061.md#ref-0eb6013708505c88). Let $$U_d:S_M\longrightarrow S_M,  \qquad  (U_dz)_m:=\mu(d_m)z_m.  \label{eq:tpc61-source-mobius-gauge}$$ The squarefree support and $(d_m,h_0)=1$ make $U_d$ a diagonal self-adjoint unitary. Since $\mathbf D_M$ is diagonal in the same row basis, $U_d$ commutes with every power and Moore–Penrose power of $\mathbf D_M$.

###### ref-03e13018ab27c8ef

For two active rows define the literal common-output form $$\mathfrak C_{m,m'}  :=  \sum_{\substack{w,w'\in\mathcal W_M\\                    m(w)=m,\ m(w')=m'\\                    i(w)=i(w')}}  \mu(s(w))\mu(s(w'))  \overline{\beta_w}\beta_{w'}.  \label{eq:tpc61-common-output-form}$$ For $m=m'$, the fixed-$(i,m)$ singleton in [\[eq:tpc61-ip-im-singletons\]](TPC-061.md#ref-c058fcd104e3172b) gives $\mathfrak C_{m,m}=W_M(m)$. For $m\ne m'$, the same expression is the off-diagonal entry of $M_X^*M_X$.

###### ref-22a3fc9368059e7a

###### ref-c01823fb76ca07c1

###### ref-ddb0c6a420e04316

###### ref-dc8a6ee5fe8197b8

> **Proposition: Exact row-gauge calibration** \[prop:tpc61-exact-row-gauge\] Let $m\ne m'$ be active missing rows. Then $$\boxed{  \bigl(U_d\Xi_{\ne p}U_d\bigr)_{m,m'}  =\frac{1}{\sqrt{W_M(m)W_M(m')}}  \sum_{\substack{w,w'\in\mathcal W_M\\                    m(w)=m,\ m(w')=m'\\                    i(w)=i(w')}}  \mu(n(w))\mu(n(w'))  \overline{\beta_w}\beta_{w'}.}  \label{eq:tpc61-two-affine-abstract-kernel}$$ The diagonal of $U_d\Xi_{\ne p}U_d$ is zero, and $$\lambda_{\max}(\Xi_{\ne p})  =\lambda_{\max}(U_d\Xi_{\ne p}U_d).  \label{eq:tpc61-row-gauge-spectrum}$$ Consequently $$\kappa_M  =1+\lambda_{\max}(U_d\Xi_{\ne p}U_d).  \label{eq:tpc61-kappa-parity-gauge}$$

###### ref-b9abb55ad5387a29

> **Proof** On every high atom, $$n(w)=p(w)r(w)$$ is squarefree, $p(w)$ is prime, and $(p(w),r(w))=1$. Hence $$\mu(r(w))=-\mu(n(w)),  \qquad  \mu(s(w))  =\mu(d_{m(w)})\mu(r(w))  =-\mu(d_{m(w)})\mu(n(w)).  \label{eq:tpc61-high-sign-calibration}$$ The two minus signs cancel in a Gram product. Conjugating its row indices by $U_d$ removes the remaining fixed factors $\mu(d_m)\mu(d_{m'})$, leaving exactly [\[eq:tpc61-two-affine-abstract-kernel\]](TPC-061.md#ref-c01823fb76ca07c1). The normalization by $W_M(m)^{-1/2}$ is the action of $\mathbf D_M^{\dagger/2}$ on the active row basis. The diagonal statement was proved after [\[eq:tpc61-native-gram-prime-split\]](TPC-061.md#ref-42f4a026c58bee31); it also follows directly because one pair $(i,m)$ determines one target and one very-high prime. Finally, unitary conjugation preserves eigenvalues, which gives [\[eq:tpc61-row-gauge-spectrum\]](TPC-061.md#ref-ddb0c6a420e04316); combine this with [\[eq:tpc61-kappa-unequal-prime\]](TPC-061.md#ref-e73f15cc94034e47).

The source-divisor sign has therefore been removed exactly; it has not been discarded statistically. The remaining arithmetic sign is the product of the Möbius values of two actual affine targets. This is the parity-bearing part of the native alias Gram.

## The direct-cell two-affine formula

###### ref-49d0767ea29d3dae

On the displayed left direct cell, write a high atom as $w=(m,j,\gamma)$, extending its coefficient by zero when the declared literal support fails. The factor dictionary of TPC–53 gives the exact coefficient-free separation $$\beta_{m,j,\gamma}  =a_m(j)K_{m\gamma}q_\gamma(j),  \label{eq:tpc61-direct-cell-factorization}$$ where $a_m(j)$ contains the source and terminal shapes, $q_\gamma(j)$ contains the opposite-row shape and its fixed output phase, and $K_{m\gamma}\in\{0,1\}$ is the literal static mask. The fixed residue/orbit phase may be placed in either factor; the product in [\[eq:tpc61-direct-cell-factorization\]](TPC-061.md#ref-49d0767ea29d3dae) is the literal coefficient.

###### ref-0a6ba2c6d80d68a2

###### ref-fa16a4eab54c8145

Let $$\mathfrak m_m(j)  :=\mathbf 1_{\{(m,j,\gamma)\in\mathcal W_M                  \text{ for some }\gamma\}},  \label{eq:tpc61-missing-time-indicator}$$ where coefficient zeros caused only by $K_{m\gamma}$ or $q_\gamma(j)$ remain in the factors rather than in the indicator. In the identical-support specialization, $$\mathfrak m_m(j)=1  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad  P^{+}(mj+h_0)>y,  \qquad  r_m(j)\notin\mathcal I_m,  \label{eq:tpc61-missing-time-cofactor-test}$$ where $r_m(j)$ is defined in [\[eq:tpc61-realized-row-cofactor\]](TPC-061.md#ref-0dc33d2e1c7ebd76), and all common high-support tests are understood. Outside that specialization one must replace the last test by the full first-failure signature.

###### ref-3245b8dd80584ca6

Put $$\Gamma_{m,m'}(j)  :=\sum_\gamma  K_{m\gamma}K_{m'\gamma}|q_\gamma(j)|^2.  \label{eq:tpc61-two-row-output-overlap}$$

###### ref-bd0e6d0f6b1937d8

###### ref-0776edee59e34ee6

> **Corollary: Literal two-affine Möbius kernel** \[cor:tpc61-two-affine-mobius-kernel\] For two distinct active rows $m,m'$, $$\boxed{  \begin{aligned}  \bigl(U_d\Xi_{\ne p}U_d\bigr)_{m,m'}  ={}&\frac{1}{\sqrt{W_M(m)W_M(m')}}  \sum_j  \mathfrak m_m(j)\mathfrak m_{m'}(j)\\  &\times  \mu(mj+h_0)\mu(m'j+h_0)  \overline{a_m(j)}a_{m'}(j)  \Gamma_{m,m'}(j).  \end{aligned}}  \label{eq:tpc61-two-affine-mobius-kernel}$$ The sum is over the one fixed physical orbit slice. The right direct slice has the symmetric formula, and the two sides are joined only by orthogonal direct sum.

> **Proof** A common native output on the left is precisely a common pair $(\gamma,j)$. Substitute [\[eq:tpc61-direct-cell-factorization\]](TPC-061.md#ref-49d0767ea29d3dae) into [\[eq:tpc61-two-affine-abstract-kernel\]](TPC-061.md#ref-c01823fb76ca07c1), sum first over $\gamma$, and use [\[eq:tpc61-two-row-output-overlap\]](TPC-061.md#ref-3245b8dd80584ca6). The missing and high tests depend on $m,j$, not on the surviving opposite coordinate, in the identical-support specialization.

Equation [\[eq:tpc61-two-affine-mobius-kernel\]](TPC-061.md#ref-0776edee59e34ee6) is an identity, not a Chowla estimate. In particular, cancellation for a single pair of rows would not by itself control the largest eigenvalue when the number of rows grows polynomially with $X$. The required statement is a weighted matrix estimate for the complete family of growing slopes $m\asymp Q$.

## Affine gcd separation

The two affine targets in [\[eq:tpc61-two-affine-mobius-kernel\]](TPC-061.md#ref-0776edee59e34ee6) have a simple exact resultant.

###### ref-7d77155580c99ac1

###### ref-c8e1f1344c32613d

###### ref-f925fffc19860375

###### ref-4ede5316e6b6d226

> **Lemma: Gcd and very-high-prime separation** \[lem:tpc61-affine-gcd-separation\] Let $m\ne m'$ and suppose both targets at the same physical time $j$ are active very-high values: $$n=mj+h_0=pr,  \qquad  n'=m'j+h_0=p'r',  \qquad p,p'>y.  \label{eq:tpc61-two-high-factorizations}$$ Then $$\boxed{\gcd(n,n')\mid h_0(m-m').}  \label{eq:tpc61-affine-gcd-resultant}$$ Moreover $$p\ne p',  \qquad p\nmid n',  \qquad p'\nmid n,  \label{eq:tpc61-no-cross-high-prime}$$ and hence neither very-high prime divides the other target’s cofactor.

> **Proof** Every common divisor of $n$ and $n'$ divides the integer linear combination $$m'n-mn'=h_0(m'-m),$$ which proves [\[eq:tpc61-affine-gcd-resultant\]](TPC-061.md#ref-f925fffc19860375). If $p\mid n'$, then, because $p\mid n$, one has $p\mid(m-m')j$. The inequality $p>J_+$ excludes $p\mid j$, and $|m-m'|\le\Delta_M<p$ excludes $p\mid m-m'$. Thus $p\nmid n'$. Interchanging the two rows gives $p'\nmid n$, and in particular $p\ne p'$. Since $r'\mid n'$ and $r\mid n$, the final assertion follows.

The gcd restriction controls local common factors, but it does not assign a sign to $\mu(n)\mu(n')$. It therefore cannot be entered as a cancellation estimate for $\Xi_{\ne p}$.

## Determinant reindexing

For fixed distinct rows $m,m'$, let $\mathscr S_{m,m'}$ consist of quadruples $(p,r,p',r')$ satisfying all of the following:

###### ref-e4b70d278b6dc222

###### ref-0341bf33d39154fa

1.  $p,p'>y$ are primes and $pr,p'r'$ lie on the declared squarefree high support;

2.  $$m'pr-mp'r'=h_0(m'-m);         \label{eq:tpc61-double-prime-determinant}$$

3.  the common integer $$j=\frac{pr-h_0}{m}          =\frac{p'r'-h_0}{m'}         \label{eq:tpc61-determinant-common-time}$$ belongs to the fixed physical orbit slice; and

4.  $p=P^{+}(pr)$, $p'=P^{+}(p'r')$, and both recovered cofactors satisfy the literal missing tests.

All inherited residue, profile, and endpoint conditions remain in this definition; the determinant equation does not enlarge the carrier.

###### ref-ae79163cbeb2a498

###### ref-83db3bd554347899

###### ref-b1aff99bb54f6bef

> **Proposition: Exact determinant form of one parity entry** \[prop:tpc61-determinant-parity-entry\] The map $$j\longmapsto  \bigl(p_m(j),r_m(j),p_{m'}(j),r_{m'}(j)\bigr)  \label{eq:tpc61-time-to-determinant-solution}$$ is a bijection from the jointly missing times in [\[eq:tpc61-two-affine-mobius-kernel\]](TPC-061.md#ref-0776edee59e34ee6) onto $\mathscr S_{m,m'}$. Consequently $$\boxed{  \begin{aligned}  \bigl(U_d\Xi_{\ne p}U_d\bigr)_{m,m'}  ={}&\frac{1}{\sqrt{W_M(m)W_M(m')}}  \sum_{(p,r,p',r')\in\mathscr S_{m,m'}}  \mu(r)\mu(r')\\  &\times  \overline{a_m(j)}a_{m'}(j)  \Gamma_{m,m'}(j),  \end{aligned}}  \label{eq:tpc61-determinant-parity-kernel}$$ where $j$ is given by [\[eq:tpc61-determinant-common-time\]](TPC-061.md#ref-0341bf33d39154fa).

> **Proof** For a jointly missing time, unique largest-prime factorization supplies $(p,r)$ and $(p',r')$. Multiplying the first equality in [\[eq:tpc61-two-high-factorizations\]](TPC-061.md#ref-c8e1f1344c32613d) by $m'$, the second by $m$, and subtracting gives [\[eq:tpc61-double-prime-determinant\]](TPC-061.md#ref-e4b70d278b6dc222). Thus the displayed map lands in $\mathscr S_{m,m'}$, and it is injective because either product $pr$ recovers $j$.
>
> Conversely, conditions (ii)–(iii) imply both affine equalities at the same physical time, while (i) and (iv) give exactly the active high and missing tests. Hence every quadruple comes from one jointly missing time. Finally, squarefreeness gives $$\mu(pr)\mu(p'r')  =(-\mu(r))(-\mu(r'))  =\mu(r)\mu(r'),$$ so [\[eq:tpc61-two-affine-mobius-kernel\]](TPC-061.md#ref-0776edee59e34ee6) becomes [\[eq:tpc61-determinant-parity-kernel\]](TPC-061.md#ref-b1aff99bb54f6bef).

This reformulation exposes a possible bilinear or dispersion route, but it does not prove that the signed determinant sum is smaller than its absolute majorant. The primes $p,p'$ are distinct, yet distinctness alone gives no Möbius cancellation in $r,r'$.

## Why the opposite-row mask does not diagonalize the kernel

###### ref-c5271d6194066349

###### ref-d50cd57654db179e

For a fixed time $j$, put $$B(j):=\sum_\gamma|q_\gamma(j)|^2  \label{eq:tpc61-opposite-row-mass}$$ and, when $B(j)>0$, define $$\eta_m(j)  :=\frac{1}{B(j)}    \sum_{\gamma:K_{m\gamma}=0}|q_\gamma(j)|^2.  \label{eq:tpc61-row-mask-defect}$$ Set $\eta_m(j)=0$ when $B(j)=0$.

###### ref-826cdc0442ed782b

###### ref-8e70c2d8b8be76a7

###### ref-1063ef21b00f4f73

> **Proposition: Exact two-row mask overlap** \[prop:tpc61-two-row-mask-overlap\] For every $m,m',j$, $$\boxed{  \bigl(1-\eta_m(j)-\eta_{m'}(j)\bigr)_+B(j)  \le\Gamma_{m,m'}(j)\le B(j).}  \label{eq:tpc61-two-row-mask-overlap-bound}$$ On a nondegenerate complete direct opposite-row cell to which the TPC–50 weighted mask theorem applies uniformly, $$\sup_{m,j}\eta_m(j)=o(1)  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad  \Gamma_{m,m'}(j)=(1-o(1))B(j)  \label{eq:tpc61-mask-overlap-asymptotic}$$ uniformly for all active $m,m',j$.

> **Proof** The product $K_{m\gamma}K_{m'\gamma}$ vanishes only on the union of the two forbidden opposite-row sets. The weight of that union is at most the sum of their weights, which gives the lower bound. The upper bound is immediate from $0\le K_{m\gamma}K_{m'\gamma}\le1$. The final statement follows from the uniform one-row defect estimate of TPC–50 and the same union bound [`\citep{WangTPC50}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-061.md#cite-0d8078e2652e9912).

Thus static-mask survival has zero positive-power cost on the declared interior cell, but it points in the opposite direction from an orthogonality argument: two source rows retain almost the same opposite-row mass. The mask does not supply the open spectral estimate for $\Xi_{\ne p}$. Any such estimate must use the signed affine correlation in [\[eq:tpc61-two-affine-mobius-kernel\]](TPC-061.md#ref-0776edee59e34ee6), the signed determinant form in [\[eq:tpc61-determinant-parity-kernel\]](TPC-061.md#ref-b1aff99bb54f6bef), or additional literal structure not used here.

## The exact open spectral target

###### ref-df31b78692ce5567

###### ref-9c17e19b2ef4800a

The preceding results identify, but do not estimate, the remaining uniform quantity. Assume $S_M\ne\{0\}$; when $S_M=\{0\}$, the missing map vanishes and this gate is vacuous. Put $$\rho_{\rm par}(X)  :=\lambda_{\max}(\Xi_{\ne p})  =\lambda_{\max}(U_d\Xi_{\ne p}U_d)\ge0.  \label{eq:tpc61-parity-spectral-target}$$ The nonnegativity follows because $\Xi_{\ne p}$ is Hermitian with zero trace. By [\[eq:tpc61-kappa-unequal-prime\]](TPC-061.md#ref-e73f15cc94034e47), $$\boxed{\kappa_M=1+\rho_{\rm par}(X).}  \label{eq:tpc61-kappa-parity-target}$$ For one distinguished vector, only the corresponding Rayleigh quotient in [\[eq:tpc61-pointwise-rayleigh\]](TPC-061.md#ref-cf11fd9bb14e7ffb) is needed. A bound for that one quadratic form is strictly weaker than [\[eq:tpc61-parity-spectral-target\]](TPC-061.md#ref-df31b78692ce5567) and must remain labeled distinguished.

Neither the gcd lemma, the determinant bijection, Brun–Titchmarsh on one cofactor ladder, nor the static-mask overlap proves $\rho_{\rm par}(X)=X^{o(1)}$. Treating $\mu(mj+h_0)$ as an independent random sign would add an unproved hypothesis and is not part of the TPC interface.

# Weighted collision certificates and sharp tests

The constant $\kappa_M$ is an exact spectral target, but a direct eigenvalue computation is not always the best proof. We now express it as the Gram of a literal weighted incidence matrix and give several rigorous certificates: collision-graph Schur tests, stable-rank obstructions, exact Schur complements, and blockwise estimates. These statements retain the fixed physical coefficients. Replacing their signed entries by support cardinalities is explicitly identified as a sufficient relaxation.

## The normalized physical incidence matrix

###### ref-3ff16a474f14ca2d

###### ref-afd2b78eb1f35f9a

###### ref-0e105a649ab1a573

###### ref-ce0bd3219247f481

Let $$F_M:=M_X\mathbf D_M^{\dagger/2}|_{S_M}  :S_M\longrightarrow\ell^2(\mathcal I_{\rm out})  \label{eq:tpc61-normalized-incidence-map}$$ and write its matrix as $$f_{i,m}  =\frac{1}{\sqrt{W_M(m)}}    \sum_{\substack{w\in\mathcal W_M\\i(w)=i,\ m(w)=m}}c_w  \qquad(W_M(m)>0).  \label{eq:tpc61-normalized-incidence-entry}$$ The fixed-$(i,m)$ decoding discussed after [\[thm:tpc61-prime-block-identity\]](TPC-061.md#ref-c610657b932f3e81) makes the sum in [\[eq:tpc61-normalized-incidence-entry\]](TPC-061.md#ref-afd2b78eb1f35f9a) either empty or a single literal atom. Define the row-collision Gram $$\mathbf K_M:=F_M^*F_M.  \label{eq:tpc61-collision-gram}$$ Then $$(\mathbf K_M)_{m,n}  =\sum_i\overline{f_{i,m}}f_{i,n},  \qquad  \kappa_M=\lambda_{\max}(\mathbf K_M).  \label{eq:tpc61-collision-gram-entries}$$ Every off-diagonal summand represents two different source rows landing at the same native output. By the prime-block singleton, their terminal primes are unequal. According to whether their residual labels agree, the same summand belongs to $\Xi_{=s}$ or $\Xi_{\ne s}$.

###### ref-737e9b57b800fbdf

###### ref-5c46d6a22b7aa740

###### ref-c04b874c324593c8

###### ref-9eefc1c677cd67ad

###### ref-10c27e8dbabc8373

###### ref-2d6fff96e173fed2

> **Theorem: Unit columns, stable rank, and dimension obstruction** \[thm:tpc61-stable-rank\] Let $$r_M:=\dim S_M,  \qquad  d_M^{\rm out}:=\operatorname{rank}F_M.  \label{eq:tpc61-active-dimensions}$$ If $r_M>0$, then every column of $F_M$ has norm one and $$\begin{aligned}  (\mathbf K_M)_{m,m}&=1  \qquad(m\in\mathcal M_M),  \label{eq:tpc61-unit-gram-diagonal}\\  \|F_M\|_{\rm HS}^2  =\operatorname{tr}\mathbf K_M&=r_M,  \label{eq:tpc61-gram-trace}\\  \boxed{  \kappa_M  =\frac{r_M}{\operatorname{srank}(F_M)}  \ge\frac{r_M}{d_M^{\rm out}},}  \label{eq:tpc61-stable-rank-identity}\\  \boxed{  1\le\kappa_M\le\min\{d_{\rm miss},r_M\}.}  \label{eq:tpc61-kappa-basic-range}\end{aligned}$$ Moreover:
>
> 1.  $\kappa_M=1$ if and only if the normalized physical row images are orthonormal;
>
> 2.  $\kappa_M=r_M$ if and only if $F_M$ has rank one.

> **Proof** Fix an active source row $m$. Distinct atoms driven by $m$ cannot occupy the same native coordinate: $i$ fixes $j$, and $m,j,h_0$ fix $n$, its unique very-high prime, its cofactor, and its residual label. Therefore $$\sum_i|f_{i,m}|^2  =\frac{1}{W_M(m)}    \sum_{\substack{w\in\mathcal W_M\\m(w)=m}}|c_w|^2  =1.$$ This proves [\[eq:tpc61-unit-gram-diagonal\]](TPC-061.md#ref-c04b874c324593c8) and [\[eq:tpc61-gram-trace\]](TPC-061.md#ref-9eefc1c677cd67ad). By definition, $$\operatorname{srank}(F_M)  =\frac{\|F_M\|_{\rm HS}^2}{\|F_M\|^2}  =\frac{r_M}{\kappa_M},$$ and $\operatorname{srank}(F_M)\le\operatorname{rank}F_M$, proving [\[eq:tpc61-stable-rank-identity\]](TPC-061.md#ref-10c27e8dbabc8373). The lower bound one follows from the trace, the upper bound $r_M$ follows because the largest eigenvalue of a positive matrix is at most its trace, and the $d_{\rm miss}$-bound is [\[cor:tpc61-kappa-below-degree\]](TPC-061.md#ref-d03b9dda297e2e0e). If the largest eigenvalue is one, the trace forces all $r_M$ eigenvalues to equal one, hence $\mathbf K_M=I$. If it equals $r_M$, every other eigenvalue is zero, which is equivalent to rank one.

###### ref-15c51c02dd29b02e

###### ref-19cf2a49fc37228c

###### ref-e68d912b9d8fa282

> **Corollary: Asymptotic rank kill for a subpower degree** \[cor:tpc61-rank-kill\] Suppose $$r_M=X^{\rho_M+o(1)},  \qquad  d_M^{\rm out}\le X^{\omega_M+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc61-rank-exponents}$$ Then $$\boxed{  \kappa_M\ge  X^{(\rho_M-\omega_M)_++o(1)}.}  \label{eq:tpc61-rank-exponent-lower}$$ In particular, a proof of $\kappa_M=X^{o(1)}$ requires the actual native image rank to be within a subpower factor of the number of active missing rows.

This is a kill test for the *mass–degree certificate*, not for the full reassembly problem. A distinguished coefficient vector can avoid the large-singular-value direction even when [\[eq:tpc61-rank-exponent-lower\]](TPC-061.md#ref-e68d912b9d8fa282) is polynomial.

## The weighted collision graph

###### ref-621732cb20d1f6a5

Let $\mathscr G_M$ be the graph on active source rows with $$m\sim n  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad  m\ne n\ \text{ and }\ (\mathbf K_M)_{m,n}\ne0,  \label{eq:tpc61-collision-graph}$$ and attach the signed edge weight $(\mathbf K_M)_{m,n}$. A larger support graph may instead join two rows whenever they share some output; the signed graph is obtained after all literal phases on the shared outputs have been summed.

###### ref-cbea5bb1cab1a43a

###### ref-a410979facb50254

###### ref-5305a699c5af1018

###### ref-9bfc73bf7ba721b4

###### ref-ca286abba842e642

> **Theorem: Component reduction and weighted Schur certificate** \[thm:tpc61-graph-schur\] Let $\mathscr C$ be the connected components of $\mathscr G_M$. Then $$\boxed{  \kappa_M  =\max_{C\in\mathscr C}    \lambda_{\max}\bigl((\mathbf K_M)_{C,C}\bigr).}  \label{eq:tpc61-component-maximum}$$ For every family of positive row weights $t_m>0$, $$\boxed{  \kappa_M  \le  \max_m\left\{   1+\sum_{n\ne m}   |(\mathbf K_M)_{m,n}|\frac{t_n}{t_m}  \right\}.}  \label{eq:tpc61-weighted-graph-schur}$$ In particular, if $$\Delta_{\rm coh}  :=\max_m\sum_{n\ne m}|(\mathbf K_M)_{m,n}|,  \label{eq:tpc61-coherent-degree}$$ then $$\kappa_M\le1+\Delta_{\rm coh}.  \label{eq:tpc61-unweighted-coherent-degree}$$

> **Proof** There is no matrix entry between different connected components, so $\mathbf K_M$ is their orthogonal direct sum, proving [\[eq:tpc61-component-maximum\]](TPC-061.md#ref-a410979facb50254). Entrywise absolute value and the Schur test give $$\|\mathbf K_M\|  \le\bigl\||\mathbf K_M|\bigr\|  \le  \max_m\frac{1}{t_m}  \sum_n|(\mathbf K_M)_{m,n}|t_n.$$ Use the unit diagonal from [\[eq:tpc61-unit-gram-diagonal\]](TPC-061.md#ref-c04b874c324593c8). Taking $t_m=1$ gives the last claim.

Thus a subpower bound for the *weighted coherent degree*, rather than the raw number of atoms in one output fiber, is sufficient for $\kappa_M=X^{o(1)}$. The weights $t_m$ permit exceptional rows with small physical mass or asymmetric overlap to be handled without replacing the physical atomic norm.

## A phase-blind overlap certificate

###### ref-868652499f499c4c

###### ref-2162ab47fee46891

For an active row $m$, put $$I_m:=\{i:f_{i,m}\ne0\}.  \label{eq:tpc61-row-output-support}$$ For two distinct active rows define the directed weighted overlap $$\theta_{m\to n}  :=\sum_{i\in I_m\cap I_n}|f_{i,m}|^2.  \label{eq:tpc61-directed-overlap}$$ By the unit-column identity, $0\le\theta_{m\to n}\le1$.

###### ref-6f05503cdffd7b77

###### ref-83ee0b8dbc96f9da

###### ref-9387df7f0402465c

> **Proposition: Weighted-codegree bound** \[prop:tpc61-weighted-codegree\] For distinct active rows, $$|(\mathbf K_M)_{m,n}|  \le  \sqrt{\theta_{m\to n}\theta_{n\to m}}.  \label{eq:tpc61-codegree-cauchy}$$ Consequently $$\boxed{  \kappa_M  \le  1+\max_m\sum_{n\ne m}  \sqrt{\theta_{m\to n}\theta_{n\to m}}.}  \label{eq:tpc61-phase-blind-overlap-bound}$$

> **Proof** Only the common outputs $I_m\cap I_n$ occur in $(\mathbf K_M)_{m,n}$. Cauchy–Schwarz on that set gives [\[eq:tpc61-codegree-cauchy\]](TPC-061.md#ref-83ee0b8dbc96f9da); insert it in [\[eq:tpc61-unweighted-coherent-degree\]](TPC-061.md#ref-ca286abba842e642).

The overlap certificate is deliberately phase blind. Its failure does not prove that $\kappa_M$ is large: the exact signed sum in $(\mathbf K_M)_{m,n}$ may be much smaller than its Cauchy–Schwarz envelope.

###### ref-b248c2c7c8fea672

###### ref-0f5a138391e3542a

For completeness, the incidence matrix itself admits the following rectangular Schur test. If positive weights $a_i,b_m$ and constants $A,B$ satisfy $$\sum_m|f_{i,m}|b_m\le A a_i,  \qquad  \sum_i|f_{i,m}|a_i\le B b_m,  \label{eq:tpc61-rectangular-schur-hypothesis}$$ then $$\boxed{\kappa_M=\|F_M\|^2\le AB.}  \label{eq:tpc61-rectangular-schur}$$ This form can be more convenient when row and output weights factor into literal smooth profiles.

## Exact Schur-complement certification

###### ref-5bbbef9599102d2e

The preceding Schur bounds use absolute values. The following criterion retains all signed cancellations and is exact. Split the active row space as $S_M=S_U\oplus S_V$ and write $$\mathbf K_M=  \begin{pmatrix}   K_{UU}&K_{UV}\\   K_{VU}&K_{VV}  \end{pmatrix}.  \label{eq:tpc61-blocked-collision-gram}$$

###### ref-08ee9fcafc421553

###### ref-b7160c172648f922

###### ref-3aebc47b37ef2235

###### ref-b3d09ab6de8507c9

###### ref-caf05af30ff9c8d3

###### ref-183e83956e7ebab2

> **Proposition: Exact recursive upper certificate** \[prop:tpc61-exact-schur-complement\] If $\Lambda>\lambda_{\max}(K_{VV})$, then $$\lambda_{\max}(\mathbf K_M)\le\Lambda  \label{eq:tpc61-lambda-upper-target}$$ if and only if $$\boxed{  K_{UU}  +K_{UV}(\Lambda I-K_{VV})^{-1}K_{VU}  \preceq\Lambda I.}  \label{eq:tpc61-strict-schur-certificate}$$ At the boundary where $C:=\Lambda I-K_{VV}$ may be singular, the exact conditions are $$\begin{aligned}  C&\succeq0,  \label{eq:tpc61-singular-schur-one}\\  \operatorname{Ran}K_{VU}&\subseteq\operatorname{Ran}C,  \label{eq:tpc61-singular-schur-two}\\  \Lambda I-K_{UU}-K_{UV}C^\dagger K_{VU}&\succeq0.  \label{eq:tpc61-singular-schur-three}\end{aligned}$$

> **Proof** The target is equivalent to positivity of $$\Lambda I-\mathbf K_M  =  \begin{pmatrix}   \Lambda I-K_{UU}&-K_{UV}\\   -K_{VU}&\Lambda I-K_{VV}  \end{pmatrix}.$$ The ordinary Schur complement gives [\[eq:tpc61-strict-schur-certificate\]](TPC-061.md#ref-3aebc47b37ef2235) when the lower-right block is positive definite. The Moore–Penrose positivity criterion for a Hermitian block matrix gives [\[eq:tpc61-singular-schur-one\]](TPC-061.md#ref-b3d09ab6de8507c9)– [\[eq:tpc61-singular-schur-three\]](TPC-061.md#ref-183e83956e7ebab2).

This certificate permits a dense exceptional core $V$ to be eliminated exactly while a large sparse part $U$ is treated recursively. A floating-point positive gap at finitely many values of $X$ is not such a certificate; exact arithmetic or interval enclosures must also control its scale dependence.

## Blockwise physical certificates

###### ref-639faee266411bb1

###### ref-286ce0cea2b0a105

Let the missing carrier have a fixed disjoint partition $$\mathcal W_M=\bigsqcup_{b=1}^{B}\mathcal W_{M,b},  \qquad  M_X=\sum_{b=1}^{B}M_b,  \qquad  \mathbf D_M=\sum_{b=1}^{B}\mathbf D_b,  \label{eq:tpc61-missing-block-partition}$$ where $\mathbf D_b$ is the raw diagonal of the $b$-th block. Put $$S_b:=\operatorname{Ran}\mathbf D_b,  \qquad  F_b:=M_b\mathbf D_b^{\dagger/2}|_{S_b},  \qquad  \kappa_b:=\|F_b\|^2.  \label{eq:tpc61-block-normalized-maps}$$

###### ref-e9aefca3b3c18221

###### ref-cd8ad83827d98823

###### ref-4ede9c9c9e3acbdd

###### ref-70b5c2a721f019b7

> **Theorem: Block reassembly upper bounds** \[thm:tpc61-block-upper\] For every fixed carrier partition, $$\boxed{\kappa_M\le\sum_{b=1}^{B}\kappa_b.}  \label{eq:tpc61-sum-block-bound}$$ More precisely, define $$a_{bc}:=\|F_b^*F_c\|,  \label{eq:tpc61-block-cross-norm}$$ where the maps are extended by zero between their active row spaces. Then $$\boxed{  \kappa_M\le  \bigl\|[a_{bc}]_{1\le b,c\le B}\bigr\|_{\ell^2_B\to\ell^2_B}.}  \label{eq:tpc61-block-cross-matrix-bound}$$ In particular:
>
> 1.  if $\operatorname{Ran}F_b\perp\operatorname{Ran}F_c$ for $b\ne c$, then $\kappa_M\le\max_b\kappa_b$;
>
> 2.  without any cross-block orthogonality, $a_{bc}\le\sqrt{\kappa_b\kappa_c}$, which recovers [\[eq:tpc61-sum-block-bound\]](TPC-061.md#ref-cd8ad83827d98823).

> **Proof** For $\zeta$, set $x_b=\mathbf D_b^{1/2}\zeta\in S_b$. Then $$M_X\zeta=\sum_bF_bx_b,  \qquad  \sum_b\|x_b\|^2=\mathcal D_M(\zeta).$$ The triangle inequality followed by Cauchy–Schwarz gives $$\left\|\sum_bF_bx_b\right\|^2  \le  \left(\sum_b\sqrt{\kappa_b}\|x_b\|\right)^2  \le  \left(\sum_b\kappa_b\right)  \left(\sum_b\|x_b\|^2\right),$$ which proves [\[eq:tpc61-sum-block-bound\]](TPC-061.md#ref-cd8ad83827d98823). On the orthogonal direct sum $\bigoplus_bS_b$, the Gram of the row operator $(x_b)_b\mapsto\sum_bF_bx_b$ has blocks $F_b^*F_c$. The norm of a block operator matrix is at most the norm of the scalar matrix of block norms, proving [\[eq:tpc61-block-cross-matrix-bound\]](TPC-061.md#ref-70b5c2a721f019b7). The two special cases follow immediately.

###### ref-a84677b597cd0fe3

###### ref-e6a33e63190e236f

If the carrier is divided into $B=O(\log X)$ dyadic cofactor blocks and one proves $$\kappa_b=X^{o(1)}  \quad\text{uniformly in }b,  \label{eq:tpc61-subpower-block-hypothesis}$$ then [\[eq:tpc61-sum-block-bound\]](TPC-061.md#ref-cd8ad83827d98823) gives $$\boxed{\kappa_M=X^{o(1)}}  \label{eq:tpc61-subpower-global-kappa}$$ without any cross-block orthogonality hypothesis. What is not automatic is [\[eq:tpc61-subpower-block-hypothesis\]](TPC-061.md#ref-a84677b597cd0fe3): on a compatible endpoint block, the ambient determinant-ladder bound still contains the positive-power factor $1+L/R$.

## Sharp examples

###### ref-456bbaf1d81b5aee

###### ref-8d886347f98e1f7f

\[ex:tpc61-coherent-incidence\] Take $r$ source rows and one native output, with one atom of coefficient one in every row. Then $$\mathbf D_M=I_r,  \qquad  F_M=[\,1\ \cdots\ 1\,],  \qquad  d_{\rm miss}=r,  \qquad  \kappa_M=r.  \label{eq:tpc61-coherent-example}$$ Thus the ambient degree bound can be exactly sharp.

###### ref-3988ba0af7916329

###### ref-e3364ac7c5218c8d

###### ref-39e3755b84692251

\[ex:tpc61-fourier-incidence\] Take $r$ rows and $r$ native outputs, with one atom at every pair $(i,m)$, all of modulus $r^{-1/2}$. The raw row weights are one and every native fiber has degree $r$. If all phases are equal, then $$F_M=r^{-1/2}\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T},  \qquad  \kappa_M=r.$$ For the Fourier phases $$(F_M)_{i,m}  =r^{-1/2}\exp(2\pi\mathrm i\,im/r),  \label{eq:tpc61-fourier-matrix}$$ $F_M$ is unitary and $$\boxed{d_{\rm miss}=r,\qquad\kappa_M=1.}  \label{eq:tpc61-fourier-saving}$$ The two arrays have identical support, identical atomic magnitudes, and identical native degrees. Hence support and raw mass do not determine the restricted native norm. This is an abstract sharpness example; it does not assert that the literal arithmetic phases form a Fourier matrix.

###### ref-1572809b3aaea153

\[ex:tpc61-uniform-distinguished\] For $$F_M=[\,1\ \ 1\,]$$ one has $\kappa_M=2$. The distinguished normalized coefficient $x=(1,-1)$ has pointwise ratio zero, whereas $x=(1,1)$ attains the uniform value two. A good distinguished ratio therefore gives no coefficient-uniform bound, while a large uniform norm does not prove that the inherited physical vector occupies its top singular direction.

###### ref-af001bde4ea370e6

\[ex:tpc61-two-stage-sharpness\] If $T_M$ embeds $r$ coefficient coordinates as the $r$ residual labels over one native output, then $\kappa_{\rm res}=1$, $\beta_M=r$, and $\kappa_M=r$. Conversely, if $T_M$ coherently groups $r$ row coordinates into one residual coordinate and $\mathsf E$ is the identity on that coordinate, then $\kappa_{\rm res}=r$, $\beta_M=1$, and again $\kappa_M=r$. Thus neither factor in [\[eq:tpc61-two-stage-upper\]](TPC-061.md#ref-ffbe444944e6535f) can be omitted.

## Exponent consequence for the reassembly ledger

###### ref-8929da9518bd45be

###### ref-ee5e8f1732b0c37a

###### ref-449c7a07de9fe56c

###### ref-79a4c7bd24bbb87b

###### ref-dc6a401982151556

Suppose, in a stated uniform or distinguished scope, $$\begin{aligned}  \kappa_M&\le X^{\nu_M+o(1)},  \label{eq:tpc61-effective-degree-exponent}\\  \mathcal D_M(\zeta)  &\le X^{-\sigma_M+o(1)}\mathcal D_{\rm ref}(\zeta),  \label{eq:tpc61-missing-mass-saving}\\  \|A_X\zeta\|^2  &\ge X^{-\lambda_{\rm rep}+o(1)}        \mathcal D_{\rm ref}(\zeta).  \label{eq:tpc61-represented-reference-lower}\end{aligned}$$ Then $$q_X(\zeta)  :=\frac{\kappa_M\mathcal D_M(\zeta)}           {\|A_X\zeta\|^2}  \le  X^{\nu_M+\lambda_{\rm rep}-\sigma_M+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc61-effective-product}$$ Consequently $$\boxed{  \sigma_M>\nu_M+\lambda_{\rm rep}  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad  \frac{\|(A_X+M_X)\zeta\|^2}{\|A_X\zeta\|^2}  =1+o(1).}  \label{eq:tpc61-zero-cost-effective-degree}$$ The upper half of the last equality follows from the triangle inequality, and the lower half from [\[eq:tpc61-uniform-reassembly-certificate\]](TPC-061.md#ref-cc5829edb4ba41cd).

###### ref-e6d859c9c2d69962

###### ref-647db22d33c9a868

At exponent equality one must retain the actual margin. If $$1-\sqrt{q_X(\zeta)}\ge X^{-a+o(1)}  \label{eq:tpc61-critical-margin}$$ for some $a\ge0$, then the certificate charges at most $$\lambda_{\rm reasm}\le2a  \label{eq:tpc61-critical-reassembly-cost}$$ in the matching scope. This cost must be entered in the strict $1/400$ ledger. If $q_X$ is not bounded below one, the present reverse-triangle certificate is silent; that does not prove failure of a signed or shorted-Gram route.

###### ref-cb67a2fe3e0de23a

The dimension obstruction [\[eq:tpc61-rank-exponent-lower\]](TPC-061.md#ref-e68d912b9d8fa282) gives the necessary lower scale $$\nu_M\ge(\rho_M-\omega_M)_+.$$ Thus a zero-cost mass–degree proof cannot follow from the available savings unless $$\sigma_M>  (\rho_M-\omega_M)_++\lambda_{\rm rep}.  \label{eq:tpc61-rank-necessary-saving}$$ Failure of [\[eq:tpc61-rank-necessary-saving\]](TPC-061.md#ref-cb67a2fe3e0de23a) closes this particular uniform certificate, not the distinguished signed route.

At the critical cutoff, the coefficient-blind residual capacity exponent from TPC–60 is at least $$\frac{15077}{105000}  \approx0.143590,$$ far above $1/400$. The new quantity $\nu_M$ is not that ambient capacity exponent. A proof of $\Delta_{\rm coh}=X^{o(1)}$, of the weighted overlap condition in [\[eq:tpc61-phase-blind-overlap-bound\]](TPC-061.md#ref-9387df7f0402465c), or of the block condition [\[eq:tpc61-subpower-block-hypothesis\]](TPC-061.md#ref-a84677b597cd0fe3) would instead give $\nu_M=0$. None of those arithmetic estimates is asserted here.

# Critical-cutoff ledger and route decisions

The exposure and Gram identities are exact at every finite $X$. Their role in the endpoint argument is determined only after missing mass, restricted grouping, and the represented reference have been measured on the same carrier and with the same quantifier. This section performs that comparison and records which numerical differences are genuine tests rather than additive losses.

## The critical arithmetic wedge

###### ref-98077899e6aed21d

###### ref-f2f1ce1f9b7bbedc

At the smallest very-high cutoff allowed by the fixed support constants, put $$\theta_J:=\frac{133}{400},  \qquad  \lambda_L:=\frac{99979}{210000}.  \label{eq:tpc61-ledger-basic-exponents}$$ Thus $J=X^{\theta_J+o(1)}$, and a critical cofactor block has $R=X^{\theta_J+o(1)}$. The coefficient-blind residual determinant-ladder degree on that block has exponent $$\mu_{\rm res}^{\rm amb}  :=\lambda_L-\theta_J  =\frac{15077}{105000}  =0.143590\ldots .  \label{eq:tpc61-ledger-critical-residual-degree}$$ This number is much larger than $1/400$, but it is not by itself an endpoint loss. In the mass–degree certificate it is multiplied by the missing mass before the reverse triangle inequality is applied.

###### ref-96e255b2f580e144

###### ref-fa7a730859d5ec95

###### ref-7c6858cb229ee003

Suppose the missing set in the critical block has cardinality $$|E_m(R)|\le X^{e+o(1)}  \label{eq:tpc61-ledger-critical-missing-count}$$ uniformly in the stated row class, and suppose the balanced-exposure hypothesis $H_m^{\rm eff}\ge X^{\theta_J+o(1)}$ holds there. Then [\[cor:tpc61-dyadic-saving-exponent\]](TPC-061.md#ref-a3f0b49773647f1b) gives the conditional missing-mass saving $$\sigma_{\rm cof}=\theta_J-e.  \label{eq:tpc61-ledger-critical-missing-saving}$$ Consequently the residual degree and cofactor saving leave the algebraic window $$\boxed{  2\theta_J-\lambda_L  =\frac{39671}{210000}  =0.188909\ldots .}  \label{eq:tpc61-ledger-residual-window}$$

###### ref-24ac85eea04cdfa9

###### ref-e64532674978080c

###### ref-66190faabf53d64f

###### ref-693eb891d8c8e38b

Let the actual residual-label erasure on this block satisfy $$\beta_{M,R}\le X^{\mu_{\rm erase}+o(1)},  \label{eq:tpc61-ledger-erasure-input}$$ and let the represented map obey $$\|A_X\zeta\|^2  \ge X^{-\lambda_{\rm rep}+o(1)}\mathcal D_H(\zeta)  \label{eq:tpc61-ledger-represented-input}$$ in the same uniform or distinguished scope. Paying the ambient residual degree, but only the restricted erasure norm, gives $$\frac{\|M_{X,R}\zeta\|^2}{\|A_X\zeta\|^2}  \le  X^{e+\mu_{\rm erase}+\lambda_{\rm rep}       -(2\theta_J-\lambda_L)+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc61-ledger-residual-product}$$ Hence this certificate has zero fixed-power reassembly cost under the strict condition $$\boxed{  e+\mu_{\rm erase}+\lambda_{\rm rep}  <\frac{39671}{210000}.}  \label{eq:tpc61-ledger-residual-door}$$ For $O(\log X)$ compatible blocks, the same conclusion follows only when the corresponding exponent inequality holds block by block with one fixed positive margin $\delta>0$. Then each block norm is $O(X^{-\delta/2+o(1)}\|A_X\zeta\|)$, and the triangle inequality over the logarithmic family remains $o(\|A_X\zeta\|)$. Mere pointwise strictness with a margin tending to zero is not a uniform asymptotic certificate. None of the three hypotheses in [\[eq:tpc61-ledger-critical-missing-count\]](TPC-061.md#ref-96e255b2f580e144)– [\[eq:tpc61-ledger-represented-input\]](TPC-061.md#ref-e64532674978080c) is proved by the numerical window itself.

## Why the direct ambient door remains closed

###### ref-7bf3da79150e66dc

The inherited coefficient-blind native degree has exponent $267/400$. Even the smallest nonempty missing set at the critical cofactor scale has $e=0$, so the balanced harmonic certificate saves at most $\theta_J=133/400$. A direct phase-blind payment therefore leaves $$\boxed{  \frac{267}{400}-\frac{133}{400}  =\frac{67}{200}>0.}  \label{eq:tpc61-ledger-direct-native-gap}$$ This proves that the particular proof architecture which combines the best pure-cardinality missing saving with the full ambient native degree cannot make the perturbation $o(1)$. It does not lower-bound $\kappa_M$, does not rule out cancellation in the parity kernel, and is not a loss to be entered into the final ledger.

The residual window in [\[eq:tpc61-ledger-residual-window\]](TPC-061.md#ref-7c6858cb229ee003) and the direct gap in [\[eq:tpc61-ledger-direct-native-gap\]](TPC-061.md#ref-7bf3da79150e66dc) therefore have different logical meanings. The first is conditional room for a two-stage proof; the second closes one support-only proof. Neither is an arithmetic lower bound for the full physical map.

## The optimal one-stage effective-product test

###### ref-f02b39b44aba34c1

###### ref-a1c34fd3a0e0d04a

###### ref-1662afb5792d7507

###### ref-da624a5b671e889c

###### ref-f183e5ed846ce4bb

The restricted Gram eliminates the need to commit in advance to the residual factorization. Fix a reference diagonal $\mathcal D_{\rm ref}$ and suppose, in one declared scope, $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal D_M(\zeta)  &\le X^{-\sigma_M+o(1)}\mathcal D_{\rm ref}(\zeta),  \label{eq:tpc61-ledger-missing-input}\\  \kappa_M  &\le X^{\nu_M+o(1)},  \label{eq:tpc61-ledger-kappa-input}\\  \|A_X\zeta\|^2  &\ge X^{-\lambda_{\rm rep}+o(1)}         \mathcal D_{\rm ref}(\zeta).  \label{eq:tpc61-ledger-reference-input}\end{aligned}$$ Then the exact perturbation parameter is bounded by $$q_X(\zeta)  :=\frac{\kappa_M\mathcal D_M(\zeta)}{\|A_X\zeta\|^2}  \le X^{\nu_M+\lambda_{\rm rep}-\sigma_M+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc61-ledger-q-bound}$$ Thus $$\boxed{  \sigma_M>\nu_M+\lambda_{\rm rep}  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad  \lambda_{\rm reasm}=0}  \label{eq:tpc61-ledger-zero-cost-test}$$ for a coefficient-uniform statement, and the analogous conclusion holds with $\kappa_M(\zeta_*)$ and $\lambda_{\rm reasm,*}=0$ for one distinguished physical vector. These scopes cannot be interchanged.

###### ref-ab0f83d2f3244b1d

###### ref-cb5b2eb5eda298c1

At equality of exponents, one must use the actual quantity $q_X$. If $$1-\sqrt{q_X(\zeta)}\ge X^{-a+o(1)},  \label{eq:tpc61-ledger-critical-margin}$$ then the reverse-triangle certificate charges at most $$\lambda_{\rm reasm}\le2a  \label{eq:tpc61-ledger-critical-cost}$$ in the matching scope. If $q_X\ge1$, that certificate is silent. It does not imply destructive interference, because the exact cross term may still be favorable and the TPC–60 shorted-Gram route remains available.

###### ref-b6a21d29ded47998

###### ref-e33183e6ef7658c9

The rank obstruction is a useful early test. If $r_M=X^{\rho_M+o(1)}$ and $\operatorname{rank}F_M\le X^{\omega_M+o(1)}$, then $$\nu_M\ge(\rho_M-\omega_M)_+.  \label{eq:tpc61-ledger-rank-floor}$$ Accordingly, the uniform mass–degree route cannot satisfy [\[eq:tpc61-ledger-zero-cost-test\]](TPC-061.md#ref-f183e5ed846ce4bb) unless $$\sigma_M>(\rho_M-\omega_M)_++\lambda_{\rm rep}.  \label{eq:tpc61-ledger-rank-necessary}$$ Failure closes this certificate but need not close a distinguished vector orthogonal to the top singular directions.

## The complete endpoint ledger

###### ref-26eec3097e4a2730

###### ref-60738a8849245bee

The prospective fixed-shift transfer chain remains the one inherited from TPC–60. Its losses are recorded as $$\boxed{  \begin{aligned}  \Lambda_{\rm tot}:={}&  \lambda_{\rm sel}+\lambda_{\rm occ}  +\lambda_{\rm blk}+\lambda_{\rm term}  +\lambda_{\rm sing}\\  &+\lambda_H+\lambda_{\rm aff}  +\lambda_{\rm reasm}  +\lambda_{\rm bdry}+\lambda_{\rm tail}.  \end{aligned}}  \label{eq:tpc61-complete-endpoint-ledger}$$ The endpoint requirement is $$\boxed{\Lambda_{\rm tot}<\frac1{400}.}  \label{eq:tpc61-strict-endpoint-budget}$$ For a distinguished chain, $\lambda_{\rm reasm}$ is consistently replaced by $\lambda_{\rm reasm,*}$. Equality is rejected unless the leading constants and all remainders are controlled strongly enough to recover a strict final inequality.

###### ref-2b4d54ef4691ae39

In particular, in the equality-scale situation [\[eq:tpc61-ledger-critical-margin\]](TPC-061.md#ref-ab0f83d2f3244b1d)– [\[eq:tpc61-ledger-critical-cost\]](TPC-061.md#ref-cb5b2eb5eda298c1), put $\Lambda_{\rm other}:=\Lambda_{\rm tot}-\lambda_{\rm reasm}$. The reverse-triangle certificate can fit the endpoint only if $$\boxed{   2a+\Lambda_{\rm other}<\frac1{400}.}  \label{eq:tpc61-equality-margin-endpoint-test}$$ Thus the number $a$ is not free slack: it consumes twice its value in the loss ledger.

The quantities $e$, $\sigma_M$, $\nu_M$, $\mu_{\rm erase}$, and $67/200$ are not additional arrows in [\[eq:tpc61-complete-endpoint-ledger\]](TPC-061.md#ref-26eec3097e4a2730). They are inputs or diagnostics used to decide the single reassembly loss. When $\lambda_{\rm rep}$ is assembled from $\lambda_H$, $\lambda_{\rm aff}$, or other arrows already listed in that ledger, it enters the comparison tests above but is not charged a second time. Likewise, the cutoff $y\asymp Q$, exact coordinate changes, Moore–Penrose restriction to the active support, and finite or logarithmic block decompositions have zero fixed-power cost.

## Stop and pivot rules

The current proof architecture has the following exact decisions.

1.  If some active row has $W_R(m)=0<W_M(m)$, the coefficient-uniform represented-mass route is dead. An average over other rows cannot repair it.

2.  If the dimension exponents force $$(\rho_M-\omega_M)_++\lambda_{\rm rep}\ge\sigma_M,$$ then the uniform restricted mass–degree certificate cannot satisfy [\[eq:tpc61-ledger-zero-cost-test\]](TPC-061.md#ref-f183e5ed846ce4bb) at the proposed exponents. The distinguished and signed routes remain separate questions.

3.  If one pays the complete ambient native degree, the positive gap [\[eq:tpc61-ledger-direct-native-gap\]](TPC-061.md#ref-7bf3da79150e66dc) closes that proof even before the other endpoint losses are charged.

4.  If a literal coefficient vector satisfies $A_X\zeta\ne0$ but $(A_X+M_X)\zeta=0$, the TPC–60 uniform shorted-Gram gate is exactly dead.

5.  If a proved necessary loss reaches $1/400$, or the sum in [\[eq:tpc61-complete-endpoint-ledger\]](TPC-061.md#ref-26eec3097e4a2730) reaches it without a quantified constant-level margin, the endpoint route must stop.

None of these failures may be repaired by averaging over shifts, replacing the fixed $h_0$, renormalizing the raw atoms by fiber sizes, deleting coefficient-dependent zeros, or substituting a pointwise Rayleigh quotient for a uniform operator norm. Those operations change the mathematical problem rather than close the stated gate.

# Result ledger, claim boundary, and the next gate

TPC–61 replaces two coarse quantities from TPC–60 by literal arithmetic objects. The missing mass is the realized cofactor measure outside the fixed retained relation, and the effective native degree is the largest eigenvalue of the normalized physical row Gram. The latter has now been calibrated to a two-affine-form Möbius kernel. These are reductions and certificates; the required exposure and spectral estimates remain open.

## Six-item physical audit

The statements above pass the interface audit in the following precise sense. Passing this audit verifies that the right physical object is being studied; it does not prove a favorable exposure or spectral estimate.

1.  *Literal coefficients.* Every atom has the single inherited coefficient $\beta_w\zeta_{m(w)}$. The signs $-\mu(s(w))$ in $M_X$ are the physical high-character signs. The row gauge $U_d$ is a fixed unitary change of coordinates, not a choice of favorable atom phases.

2.  *Fixed physical shift.* Every occurrence of a target, cofactor ladder, gcd identity, and determinant equation comes from $n=mj+h_0$ for the same fixed nonzero $h_0$. No shift average or frozen family is substituted.

3.  *Physical atomic normalization.* The measures $\nu_m$, the weights $W_M(m)$, and the matrix $\mathbf D_M$ use raw counting-measure atoms. No division by a native fiber size is hidden in the input norm.

4.  *Canonical active representation.* The row $m=d_m\ell_m$, largest prime $p=P^{+}(n)$, cofactor $r=n/p$, residual label $s=d_mr$, and native output $i$ are canonically recovered. Moore–Penrose normalization is restricted to $S_M=\operatorname{Ran}\mathbf D_M$; only identically inactive rows disappear.

5.  *Actual support.* The carriers $\mathcal W_H,\mathcal W_R,\mathcal W_M$ and the relation $\mathcal I_X$ are fixed before $\zeta$ is chosen. The identity $w\in\mathcal W_M\Longleftrightarrow r(w)\notin\mathcal I_{m(w)}$ is used only under the explicitly declared identical-support specialization.

6.  *Endpoint ledger.* Exact disintegration, gauge calibration, determinant reindexing, and finite/logarithmic partitions carry no fixed-power loss. A proved critical-margin loss is charged only as $\lambda_{\rm reasm}$, and every other arrow remains in the strict $1/400$ sum.

## What is not proved

The following conclusions do not follow from this paper.

1.  The actual upstream relation $\mathcal I_X$ has not been shown to meet $\operatorname{supp}\nu_m$ on every active row, nor has its harmonic complement been shown small.

2.  No coefficient-uniform or distinguished fixed-power bound $\mathcal D_M\le X^{-\sigma_M+o(1)}\mathcal D_{\rm ref}$ is proved.

3.  No lower bound for the represented reference $\|A_X\zeta\|^2$ is supplied; critical-window occupancy and terminal activation remain separate upstream gates.

4.  The exact parity target $\rho_{\rm par}(X)=\lambda_{\max}(\Xi_{\ne p})$ is not shown to be $X^{o(1)}$, or to satisfy any smaller fixed exponent than the available missing-mass saving.

5.  The gcd divisibility $\gcd(mj+h_0,m'j+h_0)\mid h_0(m-m')$, the distinctness of the two high primes, and the determinant parametrization do not imply cancellation of the Möbius product.

6.  The $1-o(1)$ static-mask overlap is not an orthogonality theorem. In fact it shows that the opposite-row coordinate alone supplies no polynomial separation of two source rows.

7.  No bound for one row pair is promoted to an operator-norm bound over the polynomially growing row family.

8.  No positive physical shorted-Gram lower bound, no value $\lambda_{\rm reasm}=0$, no fixed-shift parity improvement, no prime-pair lower bound, no prime-pair asymptotic, and no twin-prime implication is proved.

In particular, treating $\mu(mj+h_0)$ as an independent random sign would be a new unproved hypothesis. Likewise, a result for fixed coefficients, a logarithmically averaged correlation, or an average over shifts cannot be inserted into [\[eq:tpc61-two-affine-mobius-kernel\]](TPC-061.md#ref-0776edee59e34ee6) without verifying the actual growing-slope, physical-weight, and operator-norm quantifiers.

2.  *Prove a missing-mass saving in one declared scope.* A uniform target has the form $$\mathcal D_M(\zeta)         \le X^{-\sigma_M+o(1)}\mathcal D_{\rm ref}(\zeta)         \quad\text{for every declared }\zeta,         \label{eq:tpc61-next-uniform-missing-saving}$$ while the distinguished version is asserted only at $\zeta=\zeta_*(X)$. Their established losses must be stated before $\lambda_{\rm rep}$ is used.

4.  *Estimate the parity kernel, uniformly or pointwise.* The coefficient-uniform target is $$\kappa_M         =1+\lambda_{\max}(\Xi_{\ne p})         \le X^{\nu_M+o(1)}.         \label{eq:tpc61-next-uniform-parity-target}$$ It may be attacked through the two-affine formula [\[eq:tpc61-two-affine-mobius-kernel\]](TPC-061.md#ref-0776edee59e34ee6), the determinant form [\[eq:tpc61-determinant-parity-kernel\]](TPC-061.md#ref-b1aff99bb54f6bef), or the exact collision certificates. Pairwise correlation estimates must be upgraded to a Schur, block, stable-rank, or direct spectral bound. For the distinguished route, replace $\kappa_M$ only by the exact Rayleigh quotient $\kappa_M(\zeta_*)$.

5.  *Apply the strict effective-product test.* The zero-cost mass–degree door opens only when $$\boxed{\sigma_M>\nu_M+\lambda_{\rm rep}.}         \label{eq:tpc61-next-effective-product-test}$$ At exponent equality one must prove the actual subpower margin $1-\sqrt{q_X}\ge X^{-a+o(1)}$; it then costs $\lambda_{\rm reasm}\le2a$. If the inequality fails, this certificate is silent. It does not prove that signed reassembly fails.

6.  *Retain the shorted-Gram and signed fallbacks.* Independently test the TPC–60 uniform kernel condition $$\operatorname{Ker}(A_X+M_X)\subseteq\operatorname{Ker}A_X.         \label{eq:tpc61-next-shorted-kernel-test}$$ An exact violation kills the uniform reassembly route. If it is killed, the physical distinguished vector may still survive through a direct estimate of $$2\operatorname{Re}           \langle A_X\zeta_*,M_X\zeta_*\rangle         +\|M_X\zeta_*\|^2.         \label{eq:tpc61-next-signed-fallback}$$ This fallback must not be relabeled coefficient-uniform.

7.  *Close the complete endpoint ledger.* The final loss remains $$\begin{aligned}         \Lambda_{\rm tot}:={}&         \lambda_{\rm sel}+\lambda_{\rm occ}         +\lambda_{\rm blk}+\lambda_{\rm term}         +\lambda_{\rm sing}\\         &+\lambda_H+\lambda_{\rm aff}         +\lambda_{\rm reasm}         +\lambda_{\rm bdry}+\lambda_{\rm tail},         \end{aligned}         \label{eq:tpc61-final-complete-ledger}$$ and the endpoint condition is $$\boxed{\Lambda_{\rm tot}<\frac1{400}.}         \label{eq:tpc61-final-endpoint-condition}$$ The exponents $\sigma_M$ and $\nu_M$ are inputs to the reassembly test, not two additional losses to be double-counted. Paying the full coefficient-blind native degree is already closed by the $67/200$ deficit; changing the normalization does not reopen it.

#### Conclusion.

TPC–60 reduced missing-high restoration to a shorted-Gram decision tree. TPC–61 resolves three structural subquestions in that decision tree at the L0/L1 level. It identifies the exact realized cofactor defect, proves explicit one-dimensional cofactor-ladder and Brun–Titchmarsh harmonic certificates without assuming cancellation, and replaces the ambient native degree by the optimal restricted Gram. The remaining unequal-prime alias is now the explicit fixed-$h_0$ two-affine Möbius kernel [\[eq:tpc61-two-affine-mobius-kernel\]](TPC-061.md#ref-0776edee59e34ee6), equivalently the signed determinant sum [\[eq:tpc61-determinant-parity-kernel\]](TPC-061.md#ref-b1aff99bb54f6bef). This is a strict narrowing of the route, not a parity breakthrough. The next arithmetic theorem must control the actual retained-relation exposure and then beat the normalized parity-kernel exponent in the same scope.
