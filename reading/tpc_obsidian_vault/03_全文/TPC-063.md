## Abstract

The fixed-shift packet developed in TPC–47–TPC–62 eventually uses a finite retained relation $\mathcal I_X$ of source rows and cofactors. The earlier residual formulas, however, quantify over already supported $(m,r)$-pairs, while the later coefficient space rectangularizes the relation across orbit and opposite-row labels. No displayed theorem had identified that pair relation with the literal full incidence from which the physical terminal operator is built. Consequently, a favorable cofactor-exposure estimate cannot be inferred from the inherited interface without a circular support assumption.

We repair this logical provenance interface at one fixed nonzero shift. For an arbitrary finite literal incidence, we prove exact criteria for when pair projection and rectangular completion preserve the pre-terminal raw form, the terminal atomic map, or the grouped physical operator. The three criteria are different. We construct the unique minimal terminal-faithful pair relation when it exists, identify the spurious-cross obstruction when it does not, and show that the full incidence hypergraph is then the canonical replacement. Applied to the native very-high carrier, this gives a coefficient-independent maximal eligible relation, an effective-core identity, and the exact row factorization $$\frac{W_R^{\mathcal I}(m)}{W_H(m)}  =\alpha_m\theta_m,$$ with an explicit zero-alignment convention. It separates terminal-support alignment from retained-relation completion and yields sharp uniform and distinguished exposure constants.

For the literal single-band provenance $\sqrt{N_+}<p\le N_-/R_0$, intersection with the critical very-high face $p>y$ is confined to $R_0\le r<N_+/y$, up to one fixed-ratio transition window. At $y\asymp Q$, the balanced band, and in fact every band certified by the inherited freezing inequality, has $R_0\gg J$ and is eventually disjoint from the critical face. Retuning to $R_0\asymp J$ makes the old coefficient-blind freezing loss equal to $15077/105000>1/400$. Even after an independently justified retuning, every omitted full dyadic block below the top window makes the inherited Brun–Titchmarsh harmonic certificate yield zero fixed-power saving. A new multiscale construction or a weighted largest-prime-factor/terminal distribution theorem is therefore necessary for this support-only exposure branch. The result is an L1 provenance-completion criterion and a sharp closure of two support-only certificates; it is not a fixed-shift parity estimate, a prime-pair lower bound, or a twin-prime result.

# Introduction

The fixed-shift route preceding this paper has reached a precise but unresolved reassembly problem. The favorable mass–degree route would require, in matching scope, $$\mathcal D_M(z)\le X^{-\sigma_M+o(1)}\mathcal D_{\rm ref}(z),  \qquad  \sigma_M>\lambda_{\rm rep}+\nu_M.  \label{eq:intro-mass-degree-target}$$ The first inequality is not a formal consequence of the definitions. It depends on which cofactors the inherited relation $\mathcal I_X$ actually retains on every active row.

That relation has an unresolved provenance. TPC–47 and TPC–48 begin with literal sums over already supported $(p,r,m,j)$-atoms and prove coefficientwise signed reassembly and fixed-row $r$-orthogonality [`\citep{WangTPC47,WangTPC48}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-063.md#cite-0276fa4f583cdc92); [\[WangTPC48\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-063.md#cite-b2c88d6d47db0c74). TPC–51 first names a set $\mathcal I_X$ containing the supported $(m,r)$-pairs; TPC–52 and TPC–53 then form a direct sum over that relation, and TPC–54 explicitly permits arbitrary finite cofactor multiplicity [`\citep{WangTPC51,WangTPC52,WangTPC53,WangTPC54}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-063.md#cite-09a584963bbbf4f9); [\[WangTPC52\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-063.md#cite-9d5877ad7648b068); [\[WangTPC53\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-063.md#cite-d85e91fc7a3dbaf8); [\[WangTPC54\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-063.md#cite-6f1a8ec95b6b34b5). None of those displayed interfaces supplies a set-builder membership rule or a row-completion theorem. TPC–61 therefore correctly proves that the finite-relation hypothesis alone implies no positive exposure, but leaves open whether the literal upstream incidence repairs that defect.

This paper opens precisely that box. Its first point is conceptual but mathematically exact: a pair relation and a full four-label incidence are not interchangeable. If $$\mathcal U\subseteq\mathcal M\times\mathcal R\times\mathcal J\times\Gamma$$ is the literal incidence and $I=\operatorname{pr}_{m,r}\mathcal U$, then the later lifted space uses the rectangular completion $$\operatorname{Rect}(I)=\{(m,r,j,\gamma):(m,r)\in I,                  j\in\mathcal J,\ \gamma\in\Gamma\}.$$ It may contain spurious crosses. Some crosses are harmless because the terminal predicate kills them; some alter the raw parent form but not the terminal map; and some alter even the grouped physical operator. We give sharp finite-scale if-and-only-if tests for all three cases.

The second point is an exact repair. Removing the retained-pair test from the literal terminal predicate defines a coefficient-independent eligible universe and its maximal pair projection $\mathcal I_X^{\max}$. Every declared relation selects the same native-high carrier as its effective core $\mathcal I_X\cap\mathcal I_X^{\max}$. This selector identity is not an identity for a rectangular terminal operator, which still requires the terminal-faithfulness test. On the native high carrier we further separate the alignment mass $W_A$, which passes every non-relation terminal test, from the amount retained by $\mathcal I_X$. This produces an exact two-gate factorization and the correct uniform/distinguished minimax laws. Setting $\mathcal I_X=\mathcal I_X^{\max}$ eliminates only relation-induced missing mass. It does not prove parent-to-terminal activation, nor may the terminal-active support be substituted retroactively for the parent support.

The third point decides what the old one-band support can and cannot do. On the large-prime band of TPC–45/47, $$\sqrt{N_+}<p\le N_-/R_0,  \label{eq:intro-old-prime-band}$$ every represented atom has $r\ge R_0$. On the critical very-high face of TPC–59, $p>y\asymp Q$ implies $$r<N_+/y\asymp J.  \label{eq:intro-critical-cofactor-ceiling}$$ The exact comparison is stronger than a mere top-window localization. The balanced inherited threshold has exponent $99979/210000>133/400$, and every band satisfying the inherited freezing inequality remains polynomially above the critical ceiling. Its intersection with the critical face is therefore empty. If one independently retunes $R_0\asymp J$ to create a top-window intersection, the old coefficient-blind freezing loss is $15077/105000>1/400$. Moreover, if the unseen lower blocks are then treated by the TPC–61 ladder and Brun–Titchmarsh certificate, their full cardinality exactly cancels the available cofactor exponent. These are genuine negative theorems for two specified certificates, not evidence that the actual weighted missing mass is large.

The resulting fork is useful. One may prove a new coefficient-specific band/freezing theorem and then seek a weighted distribution theorem for its low-cofactor complement, or canonically realize a multiscale relation and separately pay for the enlarged parent/label-reassembly gates. Either route is now falsifiable. Neither route is described as progress on the fixed-$h=2$ parity kernel until a genuinely signed arithmetic estimate on the literal carrier has been proved.

#### Main results.

The paper proves the following finite-scale statements.

1.  Exact pre-terminal, terminal-atomic, and grouped-operator rectangularization criteria, including explicit spurious-cross witnesses.

2.  A unique least terminal-faithful pair relation when pair-only completion is possible, and a sharp criterion requiring the full incidence hypergraph when it is not.

3.  The maximal-eligible relation, effective-core identity, monotonicity lattice, exact alignment/completion factorization, and sharp uniform/distinguished exposure constants.

4.  Exact cofactor-window inclusions, the disjointness of every band certified by the inherited freezing inequality from the critical face, and the over-budget cost of the top-scale retuning.

5.  A zero-saving theorem for the full omitted dyadic blocks under the inherited support-cardinality/harmonic certificate, even after an independently justified retuning.

6.  A labeled multiscale completion with zero fixed-power bookkeeping cost, together with the proof that erasing its labels is a separate reassembly map.

All exact support and operator statements are L0. Their specialization to one literal fixed shift, coefficient field, and raw atomic norm is L1. No new L2 cancellation or prime-distribution lower bound is claimed.

# The inherited support and the missing provenance arrow

We first state exactly what is inherited and what is not. This section is not a historical survey: it fixes the logical hypotheses used in every later theorem.

## What reassembly preserves

###### ref-9da4d1b173e05150

TPC–47 proves a coefficientwise signed Bochner reassembly for every already supported $(m,r)$, and TPC–48 proves an exact orthogonal $r$-square at fixed $m$ before residual grouping [`\citep{WangTPC47,WangTPC48}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-063.md#cite-0276fa4f583cdc92); [\[WangTPC48\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-063.md#cite-b2c88d6d47db0c74). Both statements preserve the relation $\mathcal I_X^{\rm res}$. They neither select a favorable pair nor add a missing one. More explicitly, at fixed $m$, distinct cofactors occupy the distinct residual coordinates $d_mr$, so $$\left\|\sum_r B_{m,r}\right\|^2  =\sum_r\|B_{m,r}\|^2  \label{eq:audit-fixed-row-r-square}$$ whenever the vectors are evaluated in the ungrouped residual-label space. An empty fixed-row cofactor fiber is compatible with this identity.

###### ref-d7c47728ec5a1c49

The orbit projector acts in the $j$-factor, and the all-band tiling acts on the same orbit-frequency quotient. Neither operator creates a new $(m,r)$-label. By contrast, the later map $$(\gamma,d_mr,j)\longmapsto(\gamma,j)  \label{eq:audit-residual-erasure}$$ groups different residual labels and is not norm-monotone from below. Support preservation before that map is therefore not a lower reassembly estimate after it.

# A finite full-incidence calculus

The retained-pair question is finite at every value of $X$. We therefore begin with a finite-dimensional theorem which keeps all four pre-terminal labels. The theorem distinguishes preservation of the pre-terminal raw form, preservation of the terminal atomic form, and preservation of the grouped physical operator. These notions coincide only under additional incidence hypotheses.

## The ambient incidence and its pair projection

###### ref-8e82d1614b54e95b

Let $\mathcal M,\mathcal R\subset\mathbb N_{\ge1}$ and $\mathcal J\subset\mathbb Z$ be finite, and let $\Gamma$ be a finite label set. > **Definition: Full incidence, pair projection, and rectangle** \[def:incidence-full-pair-rectangle\] Let $\mathcal U\subseteq\Omega$ be a declared full pre-terminal incidence. Let $\mathcal Q$ be a finite output-key set, let $q:\Omega\to\mathcal Q$ be fixed, and define $$(Gx)_a:=\sum_{\substack{u\in\Omega\\q(u)=a}}x_u,  \qquad  G:\ell^2(\Omega)\longrightarrow\ell^2(\mathcal Q).  \label{eq:incidence-grouping-map}$$ For the fixed-shift residual packet the literal key is $$q(m,r,j,\gamma)=(\gamma,d_mr,j).  \label{eq:incidence-physical-key}$$ For $V\subseteq\Omega$, write $$\begin{aligned}  P_V(z)&:=\|C_Vz\|_2^2,  \label{eq:incidence-preterminal-form}\\  A_{h,V}(z)&:=\|D_hC_Vz\|_2^2,  \label{eq:incidence-terminal-atomic-form}\\  F_{h,V}&:=GD_hC_V.  \label{eq:incidence-grouped-operator}\end{aligned}$$ The first two forms use raw counting measure. The last map is coherent on each output fiber, so its squared norm need not be a sum of atomic squares.

## Exact rectangularization identities

###### ref-f1d147d8ca5c51b0

###### ref-660e19db9178a3ac

###### ref-a2291957855c94d8

###### ref-91a9a48779ee6505

###### ref-1ea03e01faf24934

###### ref-a284adb8e596b41f

###### ref-93dc9ec89aebec9f

> **Theorem: Rectangularization difference identities** \[thm:incidence-rectangularization-difference\] Let $\mathcal U\subseteq V\subseteq\Omega$, and put $\mathcal S:=V\setminus\mathcal U$. Then $$\begin{aligned}  C_V-C_{\mathcal U}&=C_{\mathcal S},  \label{eq:incidence-C-difference}\\  D_hC_V-D_hC_{\mathcal U}&=D_hC_{\mathcal S},  \label{eq:incidence-D-difference}\\  F_{h,V}-F_{h,\mathcal U}&=GD_hC_{\mathcal S}.  \label{eq:incidence-F-difference}\end{aligned}$$ Moreover, for every $z\in Z$, $$\begin{aligned}  P_V(z)-P_{\mathcal U}(z)  &=\sum_{u\in\mathcal S}|c_u|^2|z_{m(u)}|^2,  \label{eq:incidence-P-positive-difference}\\  A_{h,V}(z)-A_{h,\mathcal U}(z)  &=\sum_{u\in\mathcal S}|t_h(u)c_u|^2|z_{m(u)}|^2.  \label{eq:incidence-A-positive-difference}\end{aligned}$$ Finally, the $(a,m)$-matrix entry of the grouped difference is $$\boxed{  (F_{h,V}-F_{h,\mathcal U})_{a,m}  =\sum_{\substack{u\in\mathcal S\\q(u)=a,\ m(u)=m}}t_h(u)c_u.}  \label{eq:incidence-grouped-matrix-entry}$$ In particular, the identities apply to $V=\operatorname{Rect}(I(\mathcal U))$ and $\mathcal S=\mathcal S(\mathcal U)$.

> **Proof** Since $V=\mathcal U\,\dot\cup\,\mathcal S$, one has $\mathbf 1_V-\mathbf 1_{\mathcal U}=\mathbf 1_{\mathcal S}$. Substitution in [\[eq:incidence-CV\]](TPC-063.md#ref-dc7e63b172bda2e3) proves [\[eq:incidence-C-difference\]](TPC-063.md#ref-660e19db9178a3ac); applying $D_h$ and $G$ gives the next two identities. The vectors on $\mathcal U$ and $\mathcal S$ have disjoint coordinate supports, before and after the diagonal map $D_h$, so Pythagoras proves the two form identities. Finally, evaluate [\[eq:incidence-F-difference\]](TPC-063.md#ref-91a9a48779ee6505) at the row basis vector $e_m$ and read its $a$-coordinate.

The positive identities [\[eq:incidence-P-positive-difference\]](TPC-063.md#ref-1ea03e01faf24934) and [\[eq:incidence-A-positive-difference\]](TPC-063.md#ref-a284adb8e596b41f) must not be transferred through $G$: terms in [\[eq:incidence-grouped-matrix-entry\]](TPC-063.md#ref-93dc9ec89aebec9f) can cancel.

###### ref-fbfc7538da161065

###### ref-39d0fd0b03d00232

###### ref-a4b00850b75a07c2

###### ref-1ed3a950aaee57df

> **Theorem: Three exact fidelity criteria** \[thm:incidence-three-fidelity-criteria\] Let $I=I(\mathcal U)$, and put $\mathcal S=\operatorname{Rect}(I)\setminus\mathcal U$. Then:
>
> 1.  The rectangular lift preserves the pre-terminal synthesis and raw form exactly if and only if $$\begin{aligned}         C_{\operatorname{Rect}(I)}=C_{\mathcal U}         &\ \Longleftrightarrow\         P_{\operatorname{Rect}(I)}=P_{\mathcal U}\text{ on }Z\\         &\ \Longleftrightarrow\         c_u=0\quad(u\in\mathcal S).         \end{aligned}         \label{eq:incidence-preterminal-iff}$$
>
> 2.  It preserves the terminal atomic synthesis and raw form exactly if and only if $$\begin{aligned}         D_hC_{\operatorname{Rect}(I)}=D_hC_{\mathcal U}         &\ \Longleftrightarrow\         A_{h,\operatorname{Rect}(I)}=A_{h,\mathcal U}\text{ on }Z\\         &\ \Longleftrightarrow\         t_h(u)c_u=0\quad(u\in\mathcal S).         \end{aligned}         \label{eq:incidence-terminal-iff}$$
>
> 3.  It preserves the grouped physical operator exactly if and only if $$\boxed{         \begin{aligned}         F_{h,\operatorname{Rect}(I)}=F_{h,\mathcal U}         \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad&         \sum_{\substack{u\in\mathcal S\\q(u)=a,\ m(u)=m}}t_h(u)c_u=0\\[-2pt]         &\text{for every }(a,m).         \end{aligned}}         \label{eq:incidence-grouped-iff}$$
>
> Consequently, pre-terminal fidelity implies terminal-atomic fidelity, which implies grouped-operator fidelity. Neither converse holds for a general grouping map.

> **Proof** Map equality implies equality of the corresponding squared norms. Conversely, take $z=e_m$ in [\[eq:incidence-P-positive-difference\]](TPC-063.md#ref-1ea03e01faf24934). Equality of the pre-terminal forms gives $$\sum_{\substack{u\in\mathcal S\\m(u)=m}}|c_u|^2=0  \qquad(m\in\mathcal M),$$ so every $c_u$ on $\mathcal S$ vanishes. The terminal argument is identical using [\[eq:incidence-A-positive-difference\]](TPC-063.md#ref-a284adb8e596b41f). The grouped criterion is [\[eq:incidence-grouped-matrix-entry\]](TPC-063.md#ref-93dc9ec89aebec9f), because a linear map from $\mathbb C^{\mathcal M}$ is zero exactly when every matrix entry is zero.

###### ref-229d973267362628

\[ex:incidence-strict-hierarchy\] If a spurious coordinate $u_0$ has $c_{u_0}\ne0$ but $t_h(u_0)=0$, pre-terminal fidelity fails while both later fidelities can hold. Separately, two spurious atoms in the same row and output fiber with terminal coefficients $1$ and $-1$ violate terminal-atomic fidelity but cancel in the grouped operator. The latter is an abstract example; the literal key [\[eq:incidence-physical-key\]](TPC-063.md#ref-6feaaaf034a9e4de) has the singleton property below.

## Spurious-cross witnesses

###### ref-dc486bd78a38b6b5

###### ref-0c8724290e0d443b

###### ref-20e09e3faa09fe0a

###### ref-9b31e6dfcbdb87f4

###### ref-721e8d96a3614d62

> **Proposition: Exact spurious-cross witnesses** \[prop:incidence-spurious-cross-witness\] Let $I=I(\mathcal U)$, let $\mathcal S=\operatorname{Rect}(I)\setminus\mathcal U$, and take $u_0\in\mathcal S$, with $m_0=m(u_0)$ and $a_0=q(u_0)$.
>
> 1.  If $c_{u_0}\ne0$, then $$P_{\operatorname{Rect}(I)}(e_{m_0})-P_{\mathcal U}(e_{m_0})         \ge |c_{u_0}|^2>0.         \label{eq:incidence-preterminal-witness}$$
>
> 2.  If $t_h(u_0)c_{u_0}\ne0$, then $$A_{h,\operatorname{Rect}(I)}(e_{m_0})-A_{h,\mathcal U}(e_{m_0})         \ge |t_h(u_0)c_{u_0}|^2>0.         \label{eq:incidence-terminal-witness}$$
>
> 3.  If $$\Sigma_{a_0,m_0}(\mathcal S)         :=\sum_{\substack{u\in\mathcal S\\q(u)=a_0,\ m(u)=m_0}}                     t_h(u)c_u\ne0,         \label{eq:incidence-spurious-fiber-sum}$$ then $$\|(F_{h,\operatorname{Rect}(I)}-F_{h,\mathcal U})e_{m_0}\|_2         \ge |\Sigma_{a_0,m_0}(\mathcal S)|>0.         \label{eq:incidence-grouped-witness}$$ In particular, this holds when $u_0$ is the only terminal-active spurious atom in its $(a_0,m_0)$-fiber.

> **Proof** The first two assertions are the single-summand lower bounds in [\[eq:incidence-P-positive-difference\]](TPC-063.md#ref-1ea03e01faf24934) and [\[eq:incidence-A-positive-difference\]](TPC-063.md#ref-a284adb8e596b41f). For the last assertion, the $a_0$-coordinate of the displayed difference vector is exactly $\Sigma_{a_0,m_0}(\mathcal S)$.

###### ref-ef0874e2494daa34

###### ref-7de8ef92147c3087

> **Corollary: The literal residual key removes accidental rowwise cancellation** \[cor:incidence-physical-key-singleton\] Assume every row integer $m$ determines $d_m$, and use $q(u)=(\gamma,d_mr,j)$. For fixed $(a,m)$, at most one $u\in\Omega$ satisfies $q(u)=a$ and $m(u)=m$. Hence $$F_{h,\operatorname{Rect}(I)}=F_{h,\mathcal U}  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad  D_hC_{\operatorname{Rect}(I)}=D_hC_{\mathcal U}.  \label{eq:incidence-physical-grouped-atomic-equivalence}$$ Cancellation between different rows cannot repair this uniform equality, because the row variables $z_m$ are independent.

> **Proof** Fix $a=(\gamma,s,j)$ and $m$. The row determines $d_m$, and $s=d_mr$ determines $r$. Thus $m,r,j,\gamma$, hence $u$, are fixed. Every sum in [\[eq:incidence-grouped-iff\]](TPC-063.md#ref-1ed3a950aaee57df) has at most one term.

This corollary does not identify the inherited relation $\mathcal I_X$ with the projection of any particular full incidence. It says only that, after the literal full incidence has been written down, an active spurious cross cannot be hidden by the residual-key grouping $q=(\gamma,d_mr,j)$ in a coefficient-uniform comparison. A later map which erases $d_mr$ can merge distinct cofactors and requires the separate fiber/kernel test of [\[sec:tpc63-labeled-completion\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-063.md#src-2156319fe887177a).

# Canonical faithful completion and the hypergraph alternative

The preceding calculus decides whether one proposed pair projection is faithful. We now solve the inverse problem: given the literal full incidence, when does any pair-only rectangular representation exist, and what is its unique least relation? The answer depends on whether one asks to preserve the parent form or only the terminal atomic field.

## Two canonical active incidences

Continue with the finite data of [3](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-063.md#src-06669209ace5f489). Because the structural support is fixed before $z$, the following sets are also coefficient-independent.

###### ref-76932e96a2c8c743

###### ref-c2f367b0fa0a6fd2

###### ref-90abb94a71f23dec

> **Definition: Pre-terminal and terminal active incidences** \[def:completion-active-incidences\] For a full incidence $\mathcal U\subseteq\Omega$, define $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal U^{\mathrm{pre},+}  &:=\{u\in\mathcal U:c_u\ne0\},  \label{eq:completion-pre-active}\\  \mathcal U_h^+  &:=\{u\in\mathcal U:t_h(u)c_u\ne0\}.  \label{eq:completion-terminal-active}\end{aligned}$$

The second set is contained in the first. It may depend on the fixed physical shift and terminal support, but not on the row coefficient vector. It must not be substituted for $\mathcal U^{\mathrm{pre},+}$ in an upstream parent form: that substitution would make terminal activation true by definition.

###### ref-845c22bb6e84bdce

###### ref-fd73cfebe783463d

###### ref-c0adc713b3b8f4ab

> **Proposition: Unique minimal coordinate supports** \[prop:completion-minimal-coordinate-supports\] Let $V\subseteq\mathcal U$.
>
> 1.  The following are equivalent: $$C_V=C_{\mathcal U},         \qquad         P_V=P_{\mathcal U}\text{ on }Z,         \qquad         \mathcal U^{\mathrm{pre},+}\subseteq V.         \label{eq:completion-pre-minimal-equivalence}$$ Consequently $\mathcal U^{\mathrm{pre},+}$ is the unique least coordinate support preserving the pre-terminal synthesis and form.
>
> 2.  The following are equivalent: $$D_hC_V=D_hC_{\mathcal U},         \qquad         A_{h,V}=A_{h,\mathcal U}\text{ on }Z,         \qquad         \mathcal U_h^+\subseteq V.         \label{eq:completion-terminal-minimal-equivalence}$$ Consequently $\mathcal U_h^+$ is the unique least coordinate support preserving the terminal atomic synthesis and form.

> **Proof** If $V$ contains the stated active set, every omitted coordinate has zero coefficient in the relevant synthesis. Conversely, if $u_0\in\mathcal U^{\mathrm{pre},+}\setminus V$, then, at $z=e_{m(u_0)}$, $$P_{\mathcal U}(z)-P_V(z)\ge |c_{u_0}|^2>0.$$ The terminal statement follows identically with $|t_h(u_0)c_{u_0}|^2$. Thus every preserving support contains the corresponding active set.

There need not be a unique least coordinate support preserving only the grouped operator. Exact cancellations inside a grouped fiber can make different subsets synthesize the same output. The terminal atomic support is canonical because it is defined before that cancellation.

## Active rectangular closure

###### ref-2430c689e9f12dc1

###### ref-a1b1f7fa713cfa21

For $I\subseteq\mathcal M\times\mathcal R$, define $$\begin{aligned}  \operatorname{PRect}(I)  &:=\{u\in\operatorname{Rect}(I):c_u\ne0\},  \label{eq:completion-PRect}\\  \operatorname{TRect}_h(I)  &:=\{u\in\operatorname{Rect}(I):t_h(u)c_u\ne0\}.  \label{eq:completion-TRect}\end{aligned}$$ Both operations are monotone in $I$.

###### ref-f32868c4efc08b19

###### ref-0cf8df4c9582a1ec

###### ref-214b8ddf533d0866

> **Definition: Atomic-faithful pair relation** \[def:completion-atomic-faithful\] A pair relation $I$ is
>
> 1.  *pre-terminal faithful* for $\mathcal U$ if $$\operatorname{PRect}(I)=\mathcal U^{\mathrm{pre},+};         \label{eq:completion-pre-faithful}$$
>
> 2.  *terminal-atomic faithful* for $(\mathcal U,h)$ if $$\operatorname{TRect}_h(I)=\mathcal U_h^+.         \label{eq:completion-terminal-faithful}$$

These definitions demand both completeness and absence of active spurious crosses. They are stronger than equality after grouping and are the appropriate notions for raw exposure forms.

###### ref-8c40b4466088791e

###### ref-5551544c7a2b1b84

###### ref-2ddf44ac42803075

###### ref-5ecc61ac37c7643e

> **Theorem: Unique least terminal-faithful pair relation** \[thm:completion-least-terminal-faithful-relation\] Put $$I_h^{\min}:=\operatorname{pr}_{m,r}\mathcal U_h^+.  \label{eq:completion-Ih-min}$$ There exists a terminal-atomic faithful pair relation for $(\mathcal U,h)$ if and only if $$\boxed{\operatorname{TRect}_h(I_h^{\min})=\mathcal U_h^+.}  \label{eq:completion-terminal-rectangularity-criterion}$$ When this condition holds, $I_h^{\min}$ is the unique least faithful relation. More precisely, $I$ is terminal-atomic faithful if and only if $$I_h^{\min}\subseteq I  \quad\text{and}\quad  \operatorname{TRect}_h(I\setminus I_h^{\min})=\varnothing.  \label{eq:completion-all-terminal-faithful-relations}$$

###### ref-db962b5593032682

> **Proof** Every $u\in\mathcal U_h^+$ has its pair in $I_h^{\min}$, hence $$\mathcal U_h^+\subseteq\operatorname{TRect}_h(I_h^{\min}).  \label{eq:completion-active-contained-in-min-closure}$$ If a faithful relation $I$ exists, it contains the pair of every $u\in\mathcal U_h^+$, so $I_h^{\min}\subseteq I$. Monotonicity gives $$\mathcal U_h^+  \subseteq\operatorname{TRect}_h(I_h^{\min})  \subseteq\operatorname{TRect}_h(I)  =\mathcal U_h^+,$$ which proves [\[eq:completion-terminal-rectangularity-criterion\]](TPC-063.md#ref-2ddf44ac42803075). Conversely, that condition says exactly that $I_h^{\min}$ is faithful, and every faithful relation contains it.
>
> Assume the criterion holds. If $I$ is faithful, any active atom created by a pair in $I\setminus I_h^{\min}$ has a pair absent from the projection of $\mathcal U_h^+$, and so lies outside $\mathcal U_h^+$, a contradiction. This proves necessity of the second condition in [\[eq:completion-all-terminal-faithful-relations\]](TPC-063.md#ref-5ecc61ac37c7643e). The two conditions are sufficient because the first retains all of $\operatorname{TRect}_h(I_h^{\min})=\mathcal U_h^+$, while the second adds no active atom.

###### ref-9236501b65c9b3bd

###### ref-275a07ade73499e3

###### ref-8bdae1fa11eb1996

> **Corollary: Unique least pre-terminal faithful relation** \[cor:completion-least-preterminal-faithful-relation\] Put $$I_{\mathrm{pre}}^{\min}  :=\operatorname{pr}_{m,r}\mathcal U^{\mathrm{pre},+}.  \label{eq:completion-Ipre-min}$$ A pre-terminal faithful pair relation exists if and only if $$\boxed{  \operatorname{PRect}(I_{\mathrm{pre}}^{\min})  =\mathcal U^{\mathrm{pre},+}.}  \label{eq:completion-pre-rectangularity-criterion}$$ When it exists, $I_{\mathrm{pre}}^{\min}$ is the unique least one.

> **Proof** Repeat the proof of [\[thm:completion-least-terminal-faithful-relation\]](TPC-063.md#ref-8c40b4466088791e), replacing $\operatorname{TRect}_h$ by $\operatorname{PRect}$ and $\mathcal U_h^+$ by $\mathcal U^{\mathrm{pre},+}$.

Condition [\[eq:completion-pre-rectangularity-criterion\]](TPC-063.md#ref-8bdae1fa11eb1996) is stronger than [\[eq:completion-terminal-rectangularity-criterion\]](TPC-063.md#ref-2ddf44ac42803075). A crossed coordinate can carry nonzero parent energy and still be terminal-null. Thus exact terminal factorization through a pair relation does not justify using the rectangular lift’s parent form as the upstream parent form.

## Pair projection is not full provenance

###### ref-038e101b5aeeb8a5

###### ref-ed90a9abbb9758bc

###### ref-a81a01c4c54b737b

###### ref-f0f387f70fc58ec6

\[ex:completion-same-pair-different-incidence\] Take one row $m$, two cofactors $r_1,r_2$, and two base labels $b_1,b_2\in\mathcal J\times\Gamma$. In the resulting four-point rectangle let $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal U_{\mathrm{diag}}  &:=\{(m,r_1,b_1),(m,r_2,b_2)\},  \label{eq:completion-diagonal-incidence}\\  \mathcal U_{\mathrm{cross}}  &:=\{(m,r_1,b_2),(m,r_2,b_1)\},  \label{eq:completion-cross-incidence}\\  \mathcal U_{\mathrm{full}}  &:=\{m\}\times\{r_1,r_2\}\times\{b_1,b_2\}.  \label{eq:completion-full-incidence}\end{aligned}$$ All three pair projections equal $\{(m,r_1),(m,r_2)\}$. Let every point of the ambient rectangle be terminal-active and let the output key resolve individual atoms. The projected relation is faithful for $\mathcal U_{\mathrm{full}}$, but no pair relation is terminal-atomic faithful for either $\mathcal U_{\mathrm{diag}}$ or $\mathcal U_{\mathrm{cross}}$: retaining both actual pairs necessarily creates the other two active crosses.

The atom-resolved operators are different even if all coefficients have modulus one. To make the scalar raw forms differ as well, assign terminal coefficient modulus one to the diagonal points and modulus two to the cross points. At $z=e_m$, the diagonal and cross forms are then $2$ and $8$, while their pair projections remain equal.

###### ref-407d8eb7d5664277

\[ex:completion-grouped-concealment\] Suppose $\operatorname{Rect}(I_h^{\min})\setminus\mathcal U_h^+$ contains two active crosses with the same row and grouped output key, having terminal coefficients $1$ and $-1$. The rectangular lift is not terminal-atomic faithful, yet the spurious columns cancel in the grouped operator. Hence grouped equality does not certify an exposure identity. Under $q=(\gamma,d_mr,j)$, this cancellation is excluded by [\[cor:incidence-physical-key-singleton\]](TPC-063.md#ref-ef0874e2494daa34).

These are finite-incidence countermodels, not asserted arithmetic counterexamples inside the actual TPC carrier. Their exact conclusion is that hypotheses recording only the pair projection cannot reconstruct the full incidence.

## When the full incidence hypergraph is mandatory

###### ref-6e3461338010c288

###### ref-6c93fbe062dffbd1

> **Theorem: Sharp hypergraph-necessity criterion** \[thm:completion-hypergraph-necessity\] If $$\operatorname{TRect}_h(I_h^{\min})\setminus\mathcal U_h^+\ne\varnothing,  \label{eq:completion-active-spurious-obstruction}$$ then no pair relation is simultaneously complete for every atom of $\mathcal U_h^+$ and free of terminal-active spurious coordinates. The full incidence hypergraph $$\mathcal U_h^+\subseteq\mathcal M\times\mathcal R\times\mathcal J\times\Gamma$$ is then the unique least terminal-atomic coordinate subcarrier among subsets of the literal carrier $\mathcal U$.
>
> If instead the terminal criterion [\[eq:completion-terminal-rectangularity-criterion\]](TPC-063.md#ref-2ddf44ac42803075) holds but the pre-terminal criterion [\[eq:completion-pre-rectangularity-criterion\]](TPC-063.md#ref-8bdae1fa11eb1996) fails, a pair relation can represent the terminal atomic field exactly but does not preserve the parent raw form. A proof chain using that parent must retain the full pre-terminal incidence or insert a separate proved parent comparison.

> **Proof** The first assertion follows from [\[thm:completion-least-terminal-faithful-relation\]](TPC-063.md#ref-8c40b4466088791e); unique coordinate minimality follows from [\[prop:completion-minimal-coordinate-supports\]](TPC-063.md#ref-845c22bb6e84bdce). The second assertion is the simultaneous application of \[[thm:completion-least-terminal-faithful-relation](TPC-063.md#ref-8c40b4466088791e),[cor:completion-least-preterminal-faithful-relation](TPC-063.md#ref-9236501b65c9b3bd)\], together with the positive discrepancy [\[eq:incidence-P-positive-difference\]](TPC-063.md#ref-1ea03e01faf24934).

The term *hypergraph* records the four-label incidence; it introduces no new averaging device. Keeping it merely refuses an identification which the pair projection cannot reconstruct.

###### ref-04369ac546bfa5bd

###### ref-ad1a963cd024e7ef

###### ref-9b471fb8fb963f70

> **Proposition: Noncircular provenance criterion** \[prop:completion-noncircular-provenance-criterion\] Identification of a later retained relation $\mathcal I_X$ with the literal pair projection is valid at the following respective levels only after the corresponding statements have been proved: $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal I_X=I_X^{\mathrm{lit}}  \quad\text{and}\quad  \operatorname{PRect}(I_X^{\mathrm{lit}})    &=(\mathcal U_X^{\mathrm{lit}})^{\mathrm{pre},+},  \label{eq:completion-required-pre-provenance}\\  \mathcal I_X=I_{X,h}^{\min}  \quad\text{and}\quad  \operatorname{TRect}_h(I_{X,h}^{\min})    &=(\mathcal U_X^{\mathrm{lit}})_h^+,  \label{eq:completion-required-terminal-provenance}\end{aligned}$$ where $$I_{X,h}^{\min}:=\operatorname{pr}_{m,r}(\mathcal U_X^{\mathrm{lit}})_h^+.$$ The first line proves pre-terminal and hence terminal fidelity. > **Proof** Sufficiency and the exact logical strength of the two lines are \[[cor:completion-least-preterminal-faithful-relation](TPC-063.md#ref-9236501b65c9b3bd),[thm:completion-least-terminal-faithful-relation](TPC-063.md#ref-8c40b4466088791e)\]. Conversely, a missing actual pair deletes a nonzero coordinate, while failed rectangularity adds an active spurious cross. In the physical application one must return to the TPC–47 coefficient field, write $\mathcal U_X^{\mathrm{lit}}$ by a literal set-builder formula, and verify the chosen equality atom by atom. If that verification fails, the correct repair is the full-incidence carrier, not a redefinition of $\mathcal I_X$ chosen to make exposure favorable.

On the very-high face $p>\sqrt{N_+}$, largest-prime factorization makes the terminal cofactor unique for fixed $(m,j,\gamma)$. This helps check [\[eq:completion-required-terminal-provenance\]](TPC-063.md#ref-9b471fb8fb963f70), but does not prove the required saturation: one must still show that every base label crossed with a retained pair is present or terminal-null.

# Maximal eligibility and exact row exposure

The incidence calculus separates two questions which were previously hidden inside the phrase “retained pair.” First, does a native high atom pass all terminal tests other than membership in the pair relation? Second, if it does, is its recovered pair retained? This section disintegrates the two questions before any residual or native grouping. All sets are fixed before the row coefficient is chosen.

## The relation-free admissible high carrier

###### ref-b043a295c51e523f

###### ref-494adaeb84163194

Let $\mathcal W_H(y)$ be the complete nonzero native very-high carrier on one fixed side and direct residue slice. Every $w\in\mathcal W_H(y)$ canonically recovers $$m(w),\qquad j(w),\qquad  n(w)=m(w)j(w)+h_0,\qquad  p(w)=P^{+}(n(w))>y,\qquad  r(w)=\frac{n(w)}{p(w)}.  \label{eq:exposure-recovered-labels}$$ Write its literal amplitude as $$b_w(\zeta)=\beta_w\zeta_{m(w)},  \label{eq:exposure-literal-amplitude}$$ where $\beta_w$ contains every coefficient-free source, mask, orbit, opposite-row, and shaped terminal factor. No free atom phase is inserted.

###### ref-d9ecb7dcd5e256d8

###### ref-9c49385df453b554

###### ref-20cb8cc13659eb6e

Remove only the retained-pair membership test from the complete terminal admission predicate. Let $$A_X(w)\in\{0,1\}  \label{eq:exposure-nonrelation-predicate}$$ be the conjunction of all remaining declared tests: terminal-prime and orbit scope, nonvanishing terminal weight, squarefree and coprimality conditions, literal mask and profile support, and every further terminal-only condition. Define $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal W_A(y)&:=\{w\in\mathcal W_H(y):A_X(w)=1\},  \label{eq:exposure-admissible-high-carrier}\\  \mathcal I_X^{\max}  &:=\{(m(w),r(w)):w\in\mathcal W_A(y)\}.  \label{eq:exposure-maximal-eligible-relation}\end{aligned}$$ The superscript “max” refers only to removal of the retained-pair test. It does not enlarge any other literal support. Under the exact terminal–Walsh coordinate injection, $\mathcal I_X^{\max}$ is the pair projection of the coefficient-free terminal incidence. Identifying it with the residual projection $\mathcal I_X^{\rm res}$ from [\[def:audit-residual-incidences\]](TPC-063.md#ref-33c7cf0d5e132d65) additionally requires equality of the two declared support systems; this equality is not inferred from their common scales.

###### ref-69ec8d3e63918764

###### ref-6a6de9749ce8c560

###### ref-16cf52d1833391de

Let $\mathcal I_X\subseteq\mathcal M_X\times\mathbb N$ be any declared finite, coefficient-independent pair relation and put $$\mathcal I_m:=\{r:(m,r)\in\mathcal I_X\},  \qquad  \mathcal I_X^{\rm eff}:=\mathcal I_X\cap\mathcal I_X^{\max}.  \label{eq:exposure-effective-core}$$ Its represented high carrier and its full missing complement are $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal W_R^{\mathcal I}  &:=\{w\in\mathcal W_A(y):(m(w),r(w))\in\mathcal I_X\},  \label{eq:exposure-represented-carrier}\\  \mathcal W_M^{\mathcal I}  &:=\mathcal W_H(y)\setminus\mathcal W_R^{\mathcal I}.  \label{eq:exposure-missing-carrier}\end{aligned}$$ Thus $\mathcal W_M^{\mathcal I}$ includes both non-relation failure and failure of a different terminal test. In the identical-support specialization of TPC–60/61, $A_X=1$ on $\mathcal W_H$, so only the first mechanism remains [`\citep{WangTPC60,WangTPC61}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-063.md#cite-cf407ea6a063759a); [\[WangTPC61\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-063.md#cite-c0f79b0b6cb1358c).

###### ref-e8d7768d42f42c95

###### ref-26b465f8e57fa020

###### ref-418d91aaa2654dad

> **Proposition: Effective core and the eligibility lattice** \[prop:exposure-effective-core\] For every declared relation $\mathcal I_X$, $$\boxed{  \mathcal W_R^{\mathcal I}  =\mathcal W_R^{\mathcal I_X^{\rm eff}}.}  \label{eq:exposure-effective-core-identity}$$ If $\mathcal I_X\subseteq\mathcal J_X$, then $$\mathcal W_R^{\mathcal I}\subseteq\mathcal W_R^{\mathcal J}.  \label{eq:exposure-relation-monotonicity}$$ Moreover, $\mathcal I_X^{\max}$ is the unique least pair relation which represents every atom of $\mathcal W_A(y)$. Any superset of $\mathcal I_X^{\max}$ gives the same represented carrier $\mathcal W_A(y)$.

> **Proof** Membership in $\mathcal W_R^{\mathcal I}$ requires both $A_X(w)=1$ and $(m(w),r(w))\in\mathcal I_X$. The first condition already places the recovered pair in $\mathcal I_X^{\max}$, proving [\[eq:exposure-effective-core-identity\]](TPC-063.md#ref-26b465f8e57fa020). Relation inclusion gives [\[eq:exposure-relation-monotonicity\]](TPC-063.md#ref-418d91aaa2654dad). Finally, every relation which represents all of $\mathcal W_A$ must contain the pair of every one of its atoms, hence must contain their projection $\mathcal I_X^{\max}$. Conversely, that projection represents every admissible atom by definition.

The proposition is a maximal-support identity. It is not a statement that $\mathcal W_A$ has positive mass relative to $\mathcal W_H$, and it is not a theorem that the inherited relation equals $\mathcal I_X^{\max}$.

## Two realized measures and the exact factorization

###### ref-53f787e8c8bdd5fe

###### ref-f9e38b4d4924bcdc

###### ref-96f6fa9ed47b0be6

###### ref-b326edec8e438ed3

For a source row $m$, define finite positive measures on $\mathbb N$ by $$\begin{aligned}  \nu_m^H(r)  &:=\sum_{\substack{w\in\mathcal W_H(y)\\                     m(w)=m,\ r(w)=r}}|\beta_w|^2,  \label{eq:exposure-native-high-measure}\\  \nu_m^A(r)  &:=\sum_{\substack{w\in\mathcal W_A(y)\\                     m(w)=m,\ r(w)=r}}|\beta_w|^2.  \label{eq:exposure-admissible-high-measure}\end{aligned}$$ Put $$\begin{aligned}  W_H(m)&:=\nu_m^H(\mathbb N),  &W_A(m)&:=\nu_m^A(\mathbb N),  \label{eq:exposure-high-admissible-row-weights}\\  W_R^{\mathcal I}(m)&:=\nu_m^A(\mathcal I_m),  &W_M^{\mathcal I}(m)&:=W_H(m)-W_R^{\mathcal I}(m).  \label{eq:exposure-represented-missing-row-weights}\end{aligned}$$ All weights use raw counting-measure atoms. In particular, neither $\nu_m^H$ nor $\nu_m^A$ is divided by a row, orbit, cofactor, or native fiber multiplicity.

###### ref-1109dd664e115b29

###### ref-ef5bfed170e7fc7a

For every row with $W_H(m)>0$, define the alignment factor $$\alpha_m:=\frac{W_A(m)}{W_H(m)}\in[0,1].  \label{eq:exposure-alignment-factor}$$ When $W_A(m)>0$, define the relation-completion factor $$\theta_m(\mathcal I)  :=\frac{\nu_m^A(\mathcal I_m)}{W_A(m)}\in[0,1];  \label{eq:exposure-completion-factor}$$ when $W_A(m)=0$, put $\theta_m(\mathcal I):=0$.

###### ref-cd784eae0134d8c0

###### ref-b56b427353274581

###### ref-ffa19766fc058164

###### ref-be60fbc0dd82b4ac

> **Theorem: Exact alignment–completion factorization** \[thm:exposure-exact-factorization\] For every native-high active row, $$\boxed{  \frac{W_R^{\mathcal I}(m)}{W_H(m)}  =\alpha_m\theta_m(\mathcal I).}  \label{eq:exposure-alpha-theta-factorization}$$ Consequently, $$W_R^{\mathcal I}(m)>0  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad  W_A(m)>0  \quad\text{and}\quad  \mathcal I_m\cap\operatorname{supp}\nu_m^A\ne\varnothing.  \label{eq:exposure-positive-row-criterion}$$ For the canonical maximal relation, $$\theta_m(\mathcal I_X^{\max})=1  \quad\text{whenever }W_A(m)>0,  \qquad  \frac{W_R^{\max}(m)}{W_H(m)}=\alpha_m.  \label{eq:exposure-maximal-relation-value}$$ Thus maximal relation completion removes only relation-induced missing mass; it does not remove the alignment defect $1-\alpha_m$.

> **Proof** If $W_A(m)>0$, substitute $W_R^{\mathcal I}(m)=\nu_m^A(\mathcal I_m)$ and insert $W_A(m)$ between numerator and denominator. If $W_A(m)=0$, both sides of [\[eq:exposure-alpha-theta-factorization\]](TPC-063.md#ref-b56b427353274581) vanish by convention. A positive finite measure charges $\mathcal I_m$ precisely when that set meets its support, proving [\[eq:exposure-positive-row-criterion\]](TPC-063.md#ref-ffa19766fc058164). Finally, $\mathcal I_X^{\max}$ contains every pair in $\operatorname{supp}\nu_m^A$, which gives [\[eq:exposure-maximal-relation-value\]](TPC-063.md#ref-be60fbc0dd82b4ac).

###### ref-f34142febb77216e

Under identical support one has $\nu_m^A=\nu_m^H$ and $\alpha_m=1$. Then [\[eq:exposure-alpha-theta-factorization\]](TPC-063.md#ref-b56b427353274581) reduces exactly to the TPC–61 realized-cofactor formula $$\frac{W_R^{\mathcal I}(m)}{W_H(m)}  =\frac{\nu_m^H(\mathcal I_m)}{\nu_m^H(\mathbb N)}.  \label{eq:exposure-identical-support-specialization}$$ The specialization is useful only after identical support itself has been proved for the declared packet.

## Sharp uniform and distinguished constants

###### ref-5ae9658933992711

###### ref-5d4e3b096fa183e3

For a row-coefficient vector $\zeta$, let $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal D_H(\zeta)  &:=\sum_mW_H(m)|\zeta_m|^2,  \label{eq:exposure-DH}\\  \mathcal D_R^{\mathcal I}(\zeta)  &:=\sum_mW_R^{\mathcal I}(m)|\zeta_m|^2,  \qquad  \mathcal D_M^{\mathcal I}(\zeta)  :=\mathcal D_H(\zeta)-\mathcal D_R^{\mathcal I}(\zeta).  \label{eq:exposure-DR-DM}\end{aligned}$$

###### ref-7a6d3ccf2c1775e1

###### ref-88b80c0aa0d07ef0

###### ref-d09584e141cdc916

###### ref-bc0e27be82a1c146

> **Theorem: Uniform and distinguished exposure minimax** \[thm:exposure-uniform-distinguished-minimax\] Assume the declared uniform coefficient class contains a nonzero multiple of each active row direction. If $\mathcal D_H$ is not identically zero, then $$\begin{aligned}  \inf_{\mathcal D_H(\zeta)>0}  \frac{\mathcal D_R^{\mathcal I}(\zeta)}{\mathcal D_H(\zeta)}  &=\min_{m:W_H(m)>0}\alpha_m\theta_m(\mathcal I),  \label{eq:exposure-uniform-minimum}\\  \sup_{\mathcal D_H(\zeta)>0}  \frac{\mathcal D_M^{\mathcal I}(\zeta)}{\mathcal D_H(\zeta)}  &=\max_{m:W_H(m)>0}    \bigl(1-\alpha_m\theta_m(\mathcal I)\bigr).  \label{eq:exposure-uniform-maximum}\end{aligned}$$ For one distinguished physical vector $\zeta_*$ with $\mathcal D_H(\zeta_*)>0$, the exact represented fraction is instead $$\boxed{  \rho_*(X)  :=\frac{\mathcal D_R^{\mathcal I}(\zeta_*)}{\mathcal D_H(\zeta_*)}  =\sum_{m:W_H(m)>0}  \frac{W_H(m)|\zeta_{*,m}|^2}{\mathcal D_H(\zeta_*)}  \alpha_m\theta_m(\mathcal I).}  \label{eq:exposure-distinguished-average}$$

> **Proof** By [\[thm:exposure-exact-factorization\]](TPC-063.md#ref-cd784eae0134d8c0), each quotient is a convex combination of the row values $\alpha_m\theta_m(\mathcal I)$, with weights $W_H(m)|\zeta_m|^2/\mathcal D_H(\zeta)$. It is bounded below by their minimum, and concentration on a minimizing coordinate attains that value after a harmless scalar rescaling into the declared class. Apply the same argument to $1-\alpha_m\theta_m$ for the missing quotient. Keeping the literal weights of $\zeta_*$ gives [\[eq:exposure-distinguished-average\]](TPC-063.md#ref-bc0e27be82a1c146).

###### ref-6535caf43b574f84

###### ref-886a21e30589be8f

At one finite scale, coefficient-uniform positive exposure is therefore equivalent to $$\min_{m:W_H(m)>0}\alpha_m\theta_m(\mathcal I)>0.  \label{eq:exposure-finite-positive-uniform}$$ A scale-uniform constant requires the infimum of this minimum over all large $X$ to be positive. The much stronger fixed-power missing-mass target requires $$\max_{m:W_H(m)>0}  \bigl(1-\alpha_m\theta_m(\mathcal I)\bigr)  \le X^{-\sigma+o(1)}  \label{eq:exposure-power-target}$$ for some $\sigma>0$. Mere rowwise nonemptiness proves neither statement.

## The sharp support no-go and its scope

###### ref-9dbe226b07cd089c

> **Corollary: Finite-relation insufficiency** \[cor:exposure-finite-relation-no-go\] The inherited hypotheses “$\mathcal I_X$ is finite and fixed before $\zeta$” do not imply a positive value in [\[eq:exposure-uniform-minimum\]](TPC-063.md#ref-88b80c0aa0d07ef0), even under identical support. They also do not imply [\[eq:exposure-power-target\]](TPC-063.md#ref-886a21e30589be8f).

> **Proof** The stated hypotheses permit an otherwise active row $m_0$ for which $\mathcal I_{m_0}\cap\operatorname{supp}\nu_{m_0}^A=\varnothing$. Under identical support, $\alpha_{m_0}=1$, but $\theta_{m_0}=0$. Hence the exact minimum is zero and the exact maximum missing fraction is one. This is an admissible model of the displayed interface, not an assertion that the literal upstream construction necessarily chooses such a row.

The distinguished route is logically different. It may survive a bad row when $\zeta_{*,m}=0$ there, but the upper bound $|\zeta_{*,m}|\ll1$ alone supplies no lower bound for [\[eq:exposure-distinguished-average\]](TPC-063.md#ref-bc0e27be82a1c146). Likewise, defining $\mathcal I_X^{\max}$ from support gives the exact value $\theta_m=1$, but does not prove $\alpha_m>0$, parent-to-high activation, or any post-grouping lower bound.

The lattice and factorization theorems are L0 finite-incidence statements. Their use with [\[eq:exposure-recovered-labels\]](TPC-063.md#ref-b043a295c51e523f), the literal $\beta_w$, one fixed $h_0$, and the raw native atomic norm is an L1 physical-interface specialization. The support no-go closes only an inference from opaque finite relations. It neither lower-bounds actual missing mass nor estimates the signed parity kernel, and it has no L2 prime-pair consequence.

# The single-band completion barrier

We now compare the literal global prime band inherited by TPC–47 with the critical very-high face of TPC–59. The comparison is made at the same fixed shift and on the same target interval. It has two parts. First, an exact constant-factor sandwich identifies which cofactors one band can see. Second, the TPC–61 harmonic certificate is evaluated on every full dyadic block that the band omits. Neither part estimates the actual mass of those blocks.

## An exact constant-factor window

###### ref-842db2bce49ecfad

###### ref-3f26f4c725dcba60

###### ref-697f3005fa6bc571

Let every target satisfy $$N_-\le n=mj+h_0=pr\le N_+,  \qquad  \kappa_N:=\frac{N_+}{N_-}.  \label{eq:band-target-endpoints}$$ The fixed support ratios give $1\le\kappa_N\ll1$. For a parameter $R_0$ in the admissible TPC–45 range, the inherited global prime band is $$\mathcal P_{R_0}^{\sharp}  :=\left\{p\in\mathbb P:       \sqrt{N_+}<p\le\frac{N_-}{R_0}\right\}.  \label{eq:band-single-prime-band}$$ Let $y$ be very-high admissible. In particular, $y>\sqrt{N_+}$, and put $$R_{\rm safe}(y):=\frac{N_-}{2y},  \qquad  R_{\max}(y):=\frac{N_+}{y}  =2\kappa_NR_{\rm safe}(y).  \label{eq:band-safe-max}$$ These are the exact TPC–59 safe and maximal cofactor scales [`\citep{WangTPC59}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-063.md#cite-4f045ca3a3f537f2).

###### ref-2e87d181d4f73720

Define the portion of the native high carrier selected solely by the prime band condition: $$\mathcal W_{R_0}^{\rm band}(y)  :=\{w\in\mathcal W_H(y):p(w)\in\mathcal P_{R_0}^{\sharp}\}.  \label{eq:band-high-intersection}$$ All other row, orbit, opposite-row, mask, profile, and terminal labels are those already present in $\mathcal W_H(y)$; no favorable support is added.

###### ref-d59cadcb4691def4

###### ref-12b2e3c4ea9892a0

###### ref-621f238e93878090

> **Theorem: Exact single-band/high-face sandwich** \[thm:band-exact-window-sandwich\] For every very-high admissible $y$, $$\boxed{  \begin{aligned}  \{w\in\mathcal W_H(y):\ &\kappa_NR_0\le r(w)<R_{\max}(y)\}  \\  &\subseteq \mathcal W_{R_0}^{\rm band}(y)  \\  &\subseteq  \{w\in\mathcal W_H(y):R_0\le r(w)<R_{\max}(y)\}.  \end{aligned}}  \label{eq:band-exact-window-sandwich}$$ Thus the only uncertainty created by the target-width constants lies in the fixed-ratio transition window $$R_0\le r<\kappa_NR_0.  \label{eq:band-transition-window}$$ In particular, every high atom with $r<R_0$ is absent from the single band, while every high atom with $r\ge\kappa_NR_0$ is present in it.

###### ref-b9090aa2a2298188

###### ref-2e4c952ada86d1a5

###### ref-1e0363fc2274df2f

> **Proof** If $w\in\mathcal W_{R_0}^{\rm band}(y)$, then $p(w)\le N_-/R_0$ and $n(w)\ge N_-$, so $$r(w)=\frac{n(w)}{p(w)}\ge R_0.  \label{eq:band-right-inclusion-lower}$$ Also $p(w)>y$ and $n(w)\le N_+$, hence $$r(w)<\frac{N_+}{y}=R_{\max}(y).  \label{eq:band-right-inclusion-upper}$$ This proves the right inclusion.
>
> Conversely, let $w\in\mathcal W_H(y)$ and suppose $r(w)\ge\kappa_NR_0$. Then $$p(w)=\frac{n(w)}{r(w)}  \le\frac{N_+}{\kappa_NR_0}  =\frac{N_-}{R_0}.  \label{eq:band-left-inclusion-upper}$$ The very-high inequality $p(w)>y>\sqrt{N_+}$ supplies the lower endpoint of [\[eq:band-single-prime-band\]](TPC-063.md#ref-3f26f4c725dcba60). Thus $p(w)$ lies in the band. The upper bound $r(w)<R_{\max}(y)$ is automatic on the high face.

###### ref-fc112e7a261e4614

> **Corollary: Empty, top-window, and multiscale regimes** \[cor:band-three-regimes\] The sandwich has the following exact consequences.
>
> 1.  If $R_0\ge R_{\max}(y)$, then $\mathcal W_{R_0}^{\rm band}(y)=\varnothing$.
>
> 2.  If $R_0\asymp R_{\max}(y)$, the intersection is confined to $O(1)$ top dyadic cofactor blocks.
>
> 3.  If $R_0=X^{\theta_0+o(1)}$ and $R_{\max}(y)=X^{\theta_J+o(1)}$ with $0\le\theta_0<\theta_J$, then the band may contain high blocks of exponent $\theta\ge\theta_0$, but it omits every full block with $2R<R_0$.
>
> These are support statements. In cases (ii) and (iii), the permitted blocks may still carry zero actual atomic mass.

## The endpoint mismatch with the inherited freezing certificate

###### ref-1a0b59168945462f

###### ref-7611549e70e487ed

At the critical cutoff of TPC–59, $$y=C_{\rm vh}Q,  \qquad  R_{\max}(y)\asymp J  =X^{\theta_J+o(1)},  \qquad  \theta_J:=\frac{133}{400}.  \label{eq:band-critical-ceiling}$$ The balanced global band of TPC–45 has $$R_0=\ell_+  =X^{\lambda_L+o(1)},  \qquad  \lambda_L:=\frac{99979}{210000},  \label{eq:band-balanced-R0}$$ and therefore $$\lambda_L-\theta_J  =\frac{15077}{105000}>0.  \label{eq:band-balanced-critical-gap}$$

> **Proof** Equation [\[eq:band-certified-exponent-gap\]](TPC-063.md#ref-68bf536b8633144b) gives $R_0/R_{\max}(y)=X^{c+o(1)}$ for a fixed $c>0$. Apply [\[cor:band-three-regimes\]](TPC-063.md#ref-fc112e7a261e4614)(i). For the retuned value, substitution of $\theta_0=\theta_J$ in the first term of [\[eq:band-inherited-freezing-condition\]](TPC-063.md#ref-c161e07416ad45da) gives [\[eq:band-retuned-cost\]](TPC-063.md#ref-9f52e0cbd7ef0163); adding the nonnegative cutoff term cannot improve the inequality.

The theorem closes one simultaneous use of the old support-only freezing certificate and the critical high face. It does not say that a different coefficient-specific freezing theorem is impossible.

There are two quantitatively different targets.  \paragraph{Positive activation target.} For a full-coefficient statement, prove with one fixed \(\lambda_{\rm act}\ge0\) \begin{equation}  \inf_{m:W_H(m)>0}\frac{W_A(m)}{W_H(m)}  \ge X^{-\lambda_{\rm act}+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc63-next-positive-activation}$$ For a declared distinguished coefficient vector $z^*$, the honest alternative is $$\frac{\sum_mW_A(m)|z_m^*|^2}       {\sum_mW_H(m)|z_m^*|^2}  \ge X^{-\lambda_{\rm act,*}+o(1)}.  \label{eq:tpc63-next-distinguished-activation}$$ The second statement must not be promoted to the first.

#### Near-full alignment target.

The perturbative missing-mass route needs the stronger estimate $$\sum_m\bigl(W_H(m)-W_A(m)\bigr)|z_m|^2  \le X^{-\sigma_{\rm align}+o(1)}        \sum_mW_H(m)|z_m|^2,  \qquad \sigma_{\rm align}>0,  \label{eq:tpc63-next-near-full-alignment}$$ in the same uniform or distinguished scope used downstream. A merely positive proportion does not imply [\[eq:tpc63-next-near-full-alignment\]](TPC-063.md#ref-6f950e483775dfcd).

There are then two honest construction routes.

1.  *Preserve the single band.* Prove a genuinely weighted terminal or largest-prime-factor estimate on its low-cofactor complement. Reusing only support cardinality and the old harmonic bound cannot work by [\[eq:tpc63-cardinality-certified-saving\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-063.md#src-09b05b64d6886f3e).

2.  *Realize the multiscale completion.* Construct the literal coefficient and terminal maps on every added dyadic block, retain the block label, and then compute the exact physical erasure on the actual upstream range. The set union and Parseval identity do not supply these arrows.

Either route should begin with an exact zero test. If a required active row has $W_A=0<W_H$, the uniform activation route is closed and that no-go is itself the correct result. If activation survives, the following layer should establish a represented-reference Gram lower bound on the same carrier before returning to determinant collisions and signed alias cancellation.

#### Conclusion.

The provenance gap has now been converted into two separate, testable questions: does the literal terminal predicate activate enough of the native parent mass, and can the completed labeled carrier be mapped back to the physical output without an active-range kernel? TPC–63 answers neither by definition. It proves the exact finite calculus, shows why the old single-band cardinality proof cannot answer the first question, and specifies the next activation theorem without crossing the L1/L2 claim boundary.
