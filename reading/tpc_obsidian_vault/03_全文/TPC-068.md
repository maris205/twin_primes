## Abstract

TPC–61 identifies the exact normalized Gram of the literal missing-native map at a fixed nonzero shift, while TPC–62 shows that support degree and entrywise magnitudes alone lead only to phase-blind Schur certificates. We develop the complementary signed-operator route without selecting a minor, deleting a difficult row, or replacing a complete native output by a reduced collision cell.

Before erasure the normalized residual Gram is $I+\Xi_{=s}$, not generally the identity. Native erasure adds the new unequal-residual gate $\Xi_{\ne s}$, so the full defect is $H=\Xi_{=s}+\Xi_{\ne s}=\Xi_{\ne p}$. After the common residual Möbius gauge is calibrated, every off-diagonal entry of this full defect is exactly a normalized two-affine sum with sign $\mu(mj+h_0)\mu(nj+h_0)$. The gauge change is spectrally inert; it is not a cancellation estimate. We prove six finite-dimensional layers. First, the missing-native norm and lower frame are the extremal eigenvalues of $I+H$, where $H$ is a Hermitian zero-diagonal signed alias operator. Second, sign-reversing partial matchings give exact pair-defect bounds for complex literal weights. Third, the maximum row $\ell^2$-star $s_*$ satisfies $$s_*\le\|H\|\le\sqrt{\Delta}\,s_*,$$ and a local extremizer shows why a scalar signed row sum cannot replace the star norm. Fourth, every entrywise pair majorant $P$ gives the optimal weighted-Schur certificate $\|H\|\le\rho(P)$. Fifth, even operator walks and traces give $$\|H\|\le  \left(\max_m\sum_n|(H^{2q})_{mn}|\right)^{1/(2q)}  \quad\text{and}\quad  \|H\|\le(\operatorname{tr}H^{2q})^{1/(2q)}.$$ Their literal expansions retain unequal-residual and complex-phase closed-walk cancellation that an entrywise majorant discards; on a pure same-residual walk the target signs telescope to one. Sixth, these estimates form a certificate lattice for both extremal eigenvalues of the missing-native Gram.

For the full represented-plus-missing map we retain the nuisance space $\operatorname{Ker}A$. An exact shorted-Gram corollary separates the direct canonical Gram defect from a positive shorting penalty; control of the missing-native Gram alone does not control that penalty. Five explicit countermodels distinguish coordinate gauge, pair, star, support-phase, and nuisance obstructions. All operator identities are L0 and their specialization to the complete fixed-$h_0$ carrier is L1. Uniform pairing defects, star savings, growing operator-walk estimates, missing-mass savings, and shorting control are stated only as unproved L2 premises. No parity, prime-pair, or twin-prime conclusion is claimed.

# Introduction

The missing-high reassembly problem contains two different questions. The first asks how much a family of literal missing atoms can amplify after its residual labels are erased and its contributions are coherently returned to native outputs. The second asks whether the resulting missing map can cancel a represented map along coefficient directions invisible to the represented denominator. The first is a signed native-Gram problem. The second is a shorted-operator problem. Conflating them turns a useful restricted norm estimate into a false uniform reassembly statement.

TPC–60 defined the physical missing-high map and proved exact nuisance elimination by a shorted Gram [`\citep{WangTPC60}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-068.md#cite-cf407ea6a063759a). TPC–61 then fixed the complete missing carrier before the coefficient vector, factored the map through residual coordinates, and identified its optimal normalized native Gram [`\citep{WangTPC61}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-068.md#cite-c0f79b0b6cb1358c). TPC–62 supplied inverse localization and phase-blind Schur bounds: a large operator norm leaves a row, cell, and pair witness, whereas a uniform pair estimate must still beat the actual collision degree [`\citep{WangTPC62}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-068.md#cite-7e6a7d2f67f67940). TPC–67 pursued a different door, using all-row full-output minors, matching holonomy, and determinant mass to certify a lower frame [`\citep{WangTPC67}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-068.md#cite-a737718f0b4af9e0). The present paper returns to the direct operator door advertised there.

## The narrow object of this paper

The main arithmetic object is only the *literal missing-native map* $$M_X:\ell^2(\mathcal M_X)\longrightarrow\ell^2(\mathcal I_X^{\rm nat})$$ from the TPC–61 very-high missing carrier. Its source rows, atoms, external blocks, side labels, orbit labels, residual labels, native labels, masks, profiles, and coefficients are frozen before a coefficient vector is chosen. We neither extend the TPC–61 entry formula to the entire prime packet nor assert that it already describes the cross terms of the full represented-plus-missing Gram.

There are two output levels. Before erasure the residual output is $(i,s)$; after erasure the native output is $i$. Every still-orthogonal physical label, including side, external block, opposite row, the $\gamma$-label, and orbit time $j$, remains inside $i$. Only the residual label $s$ is summed out, because only that erasure occurs in the physical construction. This is stricter than replacing $i$ by a reduced arithmetic collision cell. The pre-erasure normalized Gram is not assumed to be the identity: its same-residual defect remains part of the full native operator.

## The six exact layers

After physical row normalization, write $$K=F^*F=I+H.$$ The six finite-dimensional layers proved below are:

1.  an exact gauge-to-genuine-sign identity for $H$;

2.  complex-weight sign-reversing pair certificates;

3.  exact star-norm bounds and their local sharpness;

4.  an entrywise pair majorant and its Perron/weighted-Schur optimum;

5.  even operator-walk and trace certificates that retain cancellation between complete walks; and

6.  a certificate lattice converting any of these estimates into upper and lower eigenvalue bounds for $K$.

These layers are deterministic. They do not assume random signs, independence, generic phases, or an average over shifts.

## Why operator walks are a genuinely different door

A pair majorant first takes an absolute value of every off-diagonal entry. Once that is done, cancellation between different row pairs is lost. By contrast, $H^{2q}$ expands into complete row walks. Products of the row gauges telescope on closed walks, while unequal-residual target signs and literal complex phases remain. Pure same-residual target signs also telescopically trivialize, a limitation made explicit below. An arithmetic estimate for a whole decorated walk sum could therefore succeed even when every pair-majorant bound is too expensive. This is a logical possibility, not a theorem that such an estimate exists.

## Claim levels and nonclaims

We use the following hierarchy throughout.

-   **L0:** exact finite-dimensional linear algebra for a fixed coefficient array.

-   **L1:** identification of that array with the complete literal fixed-$h_0$ missing carrier and its physical atomic normalization.

-   **L2:** any new uniform cancellation or saving for the two-affine Möbius sums, their stars, or their operator walks.

The paper proves L0 and records the L1 crosswalk. It does not prove the L2 premises needed for a subpower native norm, an endpoint reassembly inequality, a parity improvement, a prime-pair estimate, or a twin-prime result. In particular, all statements hold for an arbitrary fixed nonzero $h_0$; none is specialized to $h_0=2$.

## Organization

## Frozen carrier and complete output labels

###### ref-cf8b46f308aef4cb

Fix a scale $X$, a nonzero integer $h_0$, one direct side, one external block, all source and opposite-row blocks, residue and orbit classes, masks, profiles, endpoint restrictions, and the very-high cutoff before choosing a coefficient vector. The opposite direct side is included by orthogonal direct sum. Let $\mathcal W_M$ be the complete set of actual nonzero missing-high atoms in this scope. Let $\mathcal M_X$ be the complete declared physical row set and $$\mathcal K_X:=\ell^2(\mathcal M_X)  \label{eq:tpc68-full-coefficient-space}$$ the full coefficient space.

Every $w\in\mathcal W_M$ canonically recovers $$m=m(w)=d_m\ell_m,\qquad  j=j(w),\qquad  n(w)=m(w)j(w)+h_0,$$ its largest prime $p(w)>y$, cofactor $r(w)=n(w)/p(w)$, and residual label $$s(w)=d_{m(w)}r(w).$$ The squarefree physical support gives $$\mu(s(w))=\mu(d_{m(w)})\mu(r(w)).$$ The complete native key is denoted by $i(w)$. It retains the side, external block, opposite row, $\gamma$-coordinate, orbit time $j$, and every other label that remains orthogonal after native reassembly. Thus $$i(w)=i(w')\quad\Longrightarrow\quad j(w)=j(w'),$$ but the residual labels $s(w)$ and $s(w')$ may differ.

The literal atom driven by row coefficient $\zeta_m$ is $$\beta_w\zeta_{m(w)},$$ where $\beta_w\ne0$ retains every physical source shape, mask, multiplier, opposite-row factor, profile, and terminal weight. There is no freely chosen atomwise phase. The TPC–61 singleton incidence says that, for a fixed pair $(i,m)$, at most one atom of $\mathcal W_M$ occurs. Define $$\mathcal M_M  :=\{m:\text{some }w\in\mathcal W_M\text{ has }m(w)=m\}.$$ Rows in $\mathcal M_X\setminus\mathcal M_M$ have identically zero missing mass. They remain in $\mathcal K_X$ and in the represented map; only the normalized missing Gram will be restricted to its active range.

Before erasure, multiplication by $$(U_\mu z)_{i,s}:=\mu(s)z_{i,s}$$ is unitary on the residual space. This fact says only that storing the common residual sign in the input or in a signed erasure is a coordinate choice. It does *not* say that the sign disappears from $\mathsf EU_\mu$: after $s$ is erased, different residual coordinates meet and their relative signs are genuine.

> **Proposition: Normalized signed alias Gram** \[prop:tpc68-normalized-signed-Gram\] One has $$K:=F^*F=I_{S_M}+H,  \label{eq:tpc68-K-IH}$$ where $$H=H_{\rm res}+H_{\rm erase}=\Xi_{\ne p}  \label{eq:tpc68-full-H-split}$$ is Hermitian and has zero diagonal. Moreover $$\begin{aligned}  \kappa_M  &:=\sup_{\substack{\zeta\in\mathcal K_X\\                     \langle D_M\zeta,\zeta\rangle>0}}    \frac{\|M_X\zeta\|^2}{\langle D_M\zeta,\zeta\rangle}    =\lambda_{\max}(K)    =1+\lambda_{\max}(H),  \label{eq:tpc68-upper-native-eigenvalue}\\  \alpha_M  &:=\inf_{\substack{\zeta\in\mathcal K_X\\                     \langle D_M\zeta,\zeta\rangle>0}}    \frac{\|M_X\zeta\|^2}{\langle D_M\zeta,\zeta\rangle}    =\lambda_{\min}(K)    =1+\lambda_{\min}(H)\ge0.  \label{eq:tpc68-lower-native-eigenvalue}\end{aligned}$$

> **Proof** For a fixed $m\in\mathcal M_M$, the $(i,m)$-singleton property makes distinct missing atoms occupy distinct native coordinates within that column. Hence $$\|Fe_m\|^2  =\frac1{W_M(m)}    \sum_{\substack{w\in\mathcal W_M\\m(w)=m}}|\beta_w|^2  =1.$$ Thus $K=F^*F$ is positive semidefinite with unit diagonal. Therefore $H=K-I$ is Hermitian with zero diagonal. Substituting $x=D_M^{1/2}\zeta\in S_M$ into the two Rayleigh quotients gives the extremal eigenvalue identities. The map $M_X$ vanishes on $\operatorname{Ker}D_M$, so retaining zero missing rows in $\mathcal K_X$ does not change either quotient.

> **Remark: Notation** The symbol $\alpha_M$ in [\[eq:tpc68-lower-native-eigenvalue\]](TPC-068.md#ref-83699259997f825d) is the lower eigenvalue of the atomic-normalized missing-native Gram. It is not the restricted erasure upper norm denoted by $\beta_M$ in TPC–61.

## Exact removal of the row gauge

Define the diagonal row gauge $$U_d:=\operatorname{diag}\bigl(\mu(d_m)\bigr)_{m\in\mathcal M_M}.  \label{eq:tpc68-row-gauge}$$ It is a self-adjoint unitary on the squarefree active support.

> **Theorem: Gauge-to-genuine-sign identity** \[thm:tpc68-gauge-to-sign\] Put $\widetilde H:=U_dHU_d$. Then $\widetilde H$ is unitarily equivalent to $H$, has the same zero diagonal, spectrum, entry magnitudes, star norms, and collision graph, and for $m\ne n$, $$\boxed{  \widetilde H_{mn}  =  \frac1{\sqrt{W_M(m)W_M(n)}}  \sum_{\substack{w,w'\in\mathcal W_M\\                  m(w)=m,\ m(w')=n\\                  i(w)=i(w')}}  \mu(mj(w)+h_0)\mu(nj(w')+h_0)  \overline{\beta_w}\beta_{w'}.}  \label{eq:tpc68-two-affine-entry}$$ Every label summed in [\[eq:tpc68-two-affine-entry\]](TPC-068.md#ref-5f12ce6332da7e66) is an actual complete native output; no reduced collision cell has been substituted.

> **Proof** The normalized matrix entry is $$K_{mn}  =  \frac1{\sqrt{W_M(m)W_M(n)}}  \sum_{\substack{w,w'\in\mathcal W_M\\                  m(w)=m,\ m(w')=n\\                  i(w)=i(w')}}  \mu(s(w))\mu(s(w'))  \overline{\beta_w}\beta_{w'}.$$ The two minus signs in the definition of $M_X$ cancel. For $m\ne n$, this is $H_{mn}$. Multiplication by the row gauges replaces $\mu(s(w))$ by $$\mu(d_m)\mu(s(w))  =\mu(r(w)).$$ Since $mj(w)+h_0=p(w)r(w)$ is squarefree and $p(w)\nmid r(w)$, $$\mu(mj(w)+h_0)=-\mu(r(w)).$$ The two extra minus signs again cancel in the product for $m,n$, giving [\[eq:tpc68-two-affine-entry\]](TPC-068.md#ref-5f12ce6332da7e66). Unitary conjugation proves every spectral and magnitude assertion.

## Six-item L1 audit

The literal specialization satisfies the following audit.

1.  The entries are the actual coefficients $-\mu(s(w))\beta_w$, with no free atomwise phase.

2.  Every target is $mj+h_0$ for one fixed physical $h_0\ne0$.

3.  The full diagonal $D_M$ is raw physical atomic mass; its zero rows are retained in $\mathcal K_X$.

4.  Source rows and every retained output label are canonical; only the residual label is erased.

5.  The carrier, active rows, complete native outputs, collision graph, and every certificate family below are fixed before the later coefficient vector or extremizer.

6.  The exact identities cost no endpoint exponent. Every eventual arithmetic estimate must still fit the total strict $1/400$ endpoint ledger.

# Literal pair cancellation after gauge removal

Because $H$ and $\widetilde H$ are unitarily equivalent, we work with the gauged matrix and drop the tilde in this and the next three sections. Thus every off-diagonal entry has the exact form [\[eq:tpc68-two-affine-entry\]](TPC-068.md#ref-5f12ce6332da7e66).

## Signed complex pair lists

###### ref-bd09c0cde3eeb65c

###### ref-e20cfd3d0bb5766b

###### ref-9e3765597c03d4f1

Fix distinct active rows $m,n$. Let $$\mathcal C_{mn}  :=  \{(w,w')\in\mathcal W_M^2:  m(w)=m,\ m(w')=n,\ i(w)=i(w')\}$$ be their complete native collision list. For $\xi=(w,w')\in\mathcal C_{mn}$, define $$\begin{aligned}  \varepsilon_\xi^{mn}  &:=  \mu(mj(w)+h_0)\mu(nj(w')+h_0)\in\{-1,1\},  \label{eq:tpc68-pair-sign}\\  v_\xi^{mn}  &:=  \frac{\overline{\beta_w}\beta_{w'}}  {\sqrt{W_M(m)W_M(n)}}\in\mathbb C.  \label{eq:tpc68-pair-complex-weight}\end{aligned}$$ Then $$H_{mn}=\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal C_{mn}}  \varepsilon_\xi^{mn}v_\xi^{mn}.  \label{eq:tpc68-literal-pair-list}$$ The coefficient $v_\xi^{mn}$ is complex and literal. Replacing it by its magnitude before pairing would discard physical phase information.

Write $$\mathcal C_{mn}^{\pm}  :=\{\xi\in\mathcal C_{mn}:\varepsilon_\xi^{mn}=\pm1\}.$$

###### ref-3b8ff5f27432bee5

> **Definition: Admissible sign-reversing matching** \[def:tpc68-admissible-pairing\] An admissible matching for the row pair $(m,n)$ is a bijection $$\tau:A\longrightarrow B$$ between subsets $A\subseteq\mathcal C_{mn}^{+}$ and $B\subseteq\mathcal C_{mn}^{-}$. The matching may depend on the fixed literal carrier, the row pair, and all literal coefficients $v_\xi^{mn}$. It may not depend on a coefficient vector subsequently inserted into the row space or on an extremizing eigenvector of $H$.

###### ref-656cc98dd6b15277

###### ref-e95f9eb7d36d1fa9

###### ref-60da7ef45d4eca29

###### ref-cb71e2a309b42dcd

> **Theorem: Complex sign-reversing pair certificate** \[thm:tpc68-pair-certificate\] For every admissible matching $\tau:A\to B$, $$|H_{mn}|  \le  \sum_{\xi\in A}|v_\xi^{mn}-v_{\tau(\xi)}^{mn}|  +\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal C_{mn}\setminus(A\cup B)}  |v_\xi^{mn}|.  \label{eq:tpc68-pair-matching-bound}$$ Consequently the literal pair defect $$P_{mn}  :=  \min_{\tau\ {\rm admissible}}  \left\{  \sum_{\xi\in\operatorname{dom}\tau}  |v_\xi^{mn}-v_{\tau(\xi)}^{mn}|  +  \sum_{\xi\ {\rm unmatched}}|v_\xi^{mn}|  \right\}  \label{eq:tpc68-optimal-pair-defect}$$ is well defined, symmetric after setting $P_{nm}=P_{mn}$, satisfies $$|H_{mn}|\le P_{mn},  \label{eq:tpc68-entry-below-defect}$$ and is computable from the finite literal coefficient array. If $P_{mn}=0$, then $H_{mn}=0$.

> **Proof** Every matched positive-negative pair contributes $$(+1)v_\xi^{mn}+(-1)v_{\tau(\xi)}^{mn}  =v_\xi^{mn}-v_{\tau(\xi)}^{mn}.$$ The remaining terms are unmatched. The triangle inequality gives [\[eq:tpc68-pair-matching-bound\]](TPC-068.md#ref-e95f9eb7d36d1fa9). There are finitely many partial matchings, including the empty one, so the minimum exists. Swapping $(m,n)$ conjugates every weight and leaves all absolute defects unchanged; hence the symmetric convention is consistent.

> **Remark: Exact orthogonality is stronger than sign balance** Equal cardinalities $|\mathcal C_{mn}^{+}|=|\mathcal C_{mn}^{-}|$ do not imply $P_{mn}=0$. Exact cancellation also requires a matching of the *complex literal weights*. Conversely, unequal cardinalities do not preclude $H_{mn}=0$, because several complex terms may cancel without a pairwise equality. Thus $P_{mn}$ is a sufficient certificate, not an exact formula for $|H_{mn}|$.

## An ordered partial-sum certificate

Some arithmetic decompositions supply an order on the collision list rather than a literal matching. The following exact Abel certificate records what such an order would need to control.

###### ref-ad8c3c9c8188b20a

###### ref-907996ad30c4871d

> **Proposition: Complex Abel certificate** \[prop:tpc68-Abel-certificate\] If $\mathcal C_{mn}=\varnothing$, then $H_{mn}=0$. Otherwise let $\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_N$, with $N\ge1$, be any fixed ordering of $\mathcal C_{mn}$, and abbreviate $$\varepsilon_k:=\varepsilon_{\xi_k}^{mn},  \qquad v_k:=v_{\xi_k}^{mn},  \qquad A_t:=\sum_{k\le t}\varepsilon_k.$$ Then $$|H_{mn}|  \le  \max_{1\le t\le N}|A_t|  \left(  |v_N|+\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}|v_{k+1}-v_k|  \right).  \label{eq:tpc68-Abel-bound}$$

> **Proof** The empty-list assertion follows from [\[eq:tpc68-literal-pair-list\]](TPC-068.md#ref-9e3765597c03d4f1). For $N\ge1$, summation by parts gives the exact identity $$\sum_{k=1}^{N}\varepsilon_kv_k  =A_Nv_N+\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}A_k(v_k-v_{k+1}).$$ Taking absolute values proves [\[eq:tpc68-Abel-bound\]](TPC-068.md#ref-907996ad30c4871d), with no restriction that the complex weights lie on one ray.

Neither the partial sums $A_t$ nor the variation of the actual physical weights are estimated here. Choosing an order after inspecting a later extremizing vector would violate the L1 quantifier; choosing it from the fixed carrier and literal pair coefficients is allowed.

## Pair certificates and their limit

The matrix $$P=(P_{mn})_{m,n\in\mathcal M_M}, \qquad P_{mm}:=0,$$ is a symmetric nonnegative majorant of $|H|$. It contains more signed information than the raw support degree, but it has already taken an absolute value at the level of each completed row pair. Therefore it cannot see cancellation between different edges in a row star or between different complete operator walks. The next two sections distinguish these levels.

# Star norms and the optimal pair majorant

###### ref-2d2b20b5fa7cb921

Let $$\mathcal N_H(m):=\{n\ne m:H_{mn}\ne0\},  \qquad  \Delta_H:=\max_m|\mathcal N_H(m)|$$ be the actual collision graph and its maximum degree. Define the row $\ell^2$-star $$s_m(H):=  \left(\sum_{n\ne m}|H_{mn}|^2\right)^{1/2},  \qquad  s_*(H):=\max_m s_m(H).  \label{eq:tpc68-star-norm}$$ All definitions use the completed literal entries after summing their native collision lists.

###### ref-fffef82dba63a732

###### ref-3bb9f138436d3e02

> **Theorem: Exact star sandwich** \[thm:tpc68-star-sandwich\] For every finite Hermitian zero-diagonal matrix $H$, $$\boxed{  s_*(H)\le\|H\|_{2\to2}  \le\sqrt{\Delta_H}\,s_*(H).}  \label{eq:tpc68-star-sandwich}$$ Both sides are interpreted as zero when $H=0$.

> **Proof** Hermitian symmetry gives $$\|He_m\|_2  =\left(\sum_n|H_{nm}|^2\right)^{1/2}  =s_m(H),$$ so the operator norm is at least $s_*(H)$. On the other hand, Cauchy–Schwarz on every actual row gives $$\sum_n|H_{mn}|  \le\sqrt{|\mathcal N_H(m)|}\,s_m(H)  \le\sqrt{\Delta_H}\,s_*(H).$$ For a Hermitian matrix, the maximum absolute column sum equals the maximum absolute row sum. Therefore $$\|H\|_{2\to2}  \le\sqrt{\|H\|_1\|H\|_\infty}  =\|H\|_\infty,$$ which proves the upper bound.

###### ref-3414efd3b152c57d

###### ref-1f177f725440b585

> **Proposition: Local phase no-go** \[prop:tpc68-local-star-extremizer\] For every row $m$, $$\boxed{  \sup_{\substack{\|x\|_2\le1\\                  \operatorname{supp}x\subseteq\mathcal N_H(m)}}  |(Hx)_m|  =s_m(H).}  \label{eq:tpc68-local-star-sharp}$$ If $s_m(H)>0$, equality is obtained by taking $$x_n=\frac{\overline{H_{mn}}}{s_m(H)}  \qquad(n\in\mathcal N_H(m)).$$

> **Proof** The upper bound is Cauchy–Schwarz. If $s_m(H)=0$, the zero vector is admissible and gives equality. Otherwise the displayed vector has norm one and gives $$(Hx)_m=\frac1{s_m(H)}  \sum_{n\in\mathcal N_H(m)}|H_{mn}|^2=s_m(H).$$

Thus cancellation in one scalar row sum $\sum_nH_{mn}$, or against any other distinguished row vector, cannot prove a uniform operator estimate. The later coefficient vector is free to align with the conjugate star. A valid star route must control the $\ell^2$-mass of every actual row star.

## Pair defects imply star bounds

For the pair-defect matrix $P$ of [\[eq:tpc68-optimal-pair-defect\]](TPC-068.md#ref-60da7ef45d4eca29), set $$s_m(P):=\left(\sum_{n\ne m}P_{mn}^2\right)^{1/2},  \qquad s_*(P):=\max_m s_m(P),$$ and let $$\Delta_P:=\max_m|\{n\ne m:P_{mn}>0\}|.$$

###### ref-c509fa55f840639e

###### ref-41b5a390d517bcec

> **Corollary: Pair-to-star certificate** \[cor:tpc68-pair-to-star\] One has $$s_m(H)\le s_m(P),  \qquad  \|H\|\le\sqrt{\Delta_P}\,s_*(P).  \label{eq:tpc68-pair-to-star}$$

> **Proof** The first inequality follows entry by entry from [\[eq:tpc68-entry-below-defect\]](TPC-068.md#ref-cb71e2a309b42dcd). The support of $H$ is contained in the support of $P$, so $\Delta_H\le\Delta_P$. Apply [\[thm:tpc68-star-sandwich\]](TPC-068.md#ref-fffef82dba63a732).

## The Perron certificate

The star estimate still pays the worst degree by Cauchy–Schwarz. The full nonnegative pair matrix permits optimal row weights; the matrix-norm and Perron facts used below are standard [`\citep{HornJohnson2013}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-068.md#cite-25e2af110ba05633).

###### ref-742efa34713472e3

###### ref-af1b558c8e2f180b

> **Theorem: Pair-majorant/Perron certificate** \[thm:tpc68-Perron-certificate\] Let $P$ be any finite symmetric nonnegative matrix such that $$|H_{mn}|\le P_{mn}  \qquad\text{for all }m,n.$$ Then $$\boxed{  \|H\|_{2\to2}  \le\rho(P)  =  \min_{a_m>0}  \max_m\frac{\sum_nP_{mn}a_n}{a_m}.}  \label{eq:tpc68-Perron-CW}$$ The minimum formula is valid even when $P$ is reducible.

> **Proof** For every $x$, $$|Hx|\le P|x|$$ coordinatewise. Hence $$\|Hx\|_2\le\|P|x|\|_2  \le\|P\|_{2\to2}\|x\|_2.$$ Since $P$ is real symmetric and nonnegative, $\|P\|_{2\to2}=\rho(P)$.
>
> For completeness, fix $a>0$ and put $$c(a):=\max_m\frac{(Pa)_m}{a_m}.$$ The weighted row-sum bound gives $\rho(P)\le c(a)$, so $$\rho(P)\le\inf_{a>0}c(a).$$ Conversely, for every $\lambda>\rho(P)$, $$a_\lambda:=(\lambda I-P)^{-1}\mathbf1  =\sum_{k\ge0}\lambda^{-k-1}P^k\mathbf1  >0.$$ It satisfies $Pa_\lambda=\lambda a_\lambda-\mathbf1<\lambda a_\lambda$, so $$\inf_{a>0}c(a)\le\lambda  \qquad(\lambda>\rho(P)).$$ Letting $\lambda\downarrow\rho(P)$ proves equality of the infimum with $\rho(P)$.
>
> It remains to verify attainment. Permute $P$ into the orthogonal direct sum of the connected components of its undirected support graph. On each nonzero irreducible block choose a strictly positive Perron eigenvector, and on every isolated zero block choose any positive entry. Concatenating these vectors gives $a>0$ for which $$\max_m\frac{(Pa)_m}{a_m}  =\max_C\rho(P_C)=\rho(P).$$

> **Remark: Optimality is relative to the majorant** The right side of [\[eq:tpc68-Perron-CW\]](TPC-068.md#ref-af1b558c8e2f180b) is the best diagonal weighted-Schur aggregation of the chosen pair certificates. It need not equal $\|H\|$, because replacing $H$ by $P$ has already discarded all phase cancellation between different row pairs.

###### ref-5debb91a1f8eb83b

###### ref-d84a3e762693be98

> **Corollary: Uniform pair bound plus actual degree** \[cor:tpc68-pair-degree\] If $$P_{mn}\le p_*  \quad(m\ne n),  \qquad  \Delta_P\le\Delta,$$ then $$\|H\|\le\rho(P)\le\Delta p_*.  \label{eq:tpc68-pair-degree-bound}$$

> **Proof** Take $a_m=1$ in [\[eq:tpc68-Perron-CW\]](TPC-068.md#ref-af1b558c8e2f180b).

This recovers the phase-blind degree-times-pair barrier isolated in TPC–62, but the defect $P_{mn}$ may be substantially smaller than the absolute sum of the original collision terms because opposite two-affine signs were paired first.

# Even operator walks and closed-walk signs

Pair and Perron certificates take absolute values before different row pairs interact. Powers of the signed matrix provide a later place to take absolute values.

## Power-row certificate

###### ref-278bb09fb45a0d88

For an integer $q\ge1$, define $$\Omega_{2q}(H)  :=  \max_m\sum_n|(H^{2q})_{mn}|.  \label{eq:tpc68-Omega-power}$$

###### ref-a3c2d739775319e5

###### ref-08770aaa48c4a116

> **Theorem: Even operator-walk certificate** \[thm:tpc68-even-walk-certificate\] For every finite Hermitian matrix $H$ and every $q\ge1$, $$\boxed{  \|H\|_{2\to2}  \le\Omega_{2q}(H)^{1/(2q)}.}  \label{eq:tpc68-even-walk-bound}$$

> **Proof** The matrix $H^{2q}$ is Hermitian, and its maximum absolute row and column sums agree. Therefore $$\|H\|^{2q}  =\|H^{2q}\|_{2\to2}  \le\sqrt{\|H^{2q}\|_1\|H^{2q}\|_\infty}  =\Omega_{2q}(H).$$ Taking $2q$-th roots proves the result.

The case $q=1$ already includes interference between two-edge walks: $$(H^2)_{mn}=\sum_kH_{mk}H_{kn}.$$ It is not identical to the star estimate. In particular, $(H^2)_{mm}=s_m(H)^2$, while off-diagonal entries may cancel.

## Even moments

Let $r=\dim S_M$.

###### ref-0b7bed9e85dbd882

###### ref-ce71ab844ddb6111

> **Theorem: Even-moment certificate** \[thm:tpc68-even-moment\] For every $q\ge1$, $$\boxed{  \|H\|  \le\bigl(\operatorname{tr}H^{2q}\bigr)^{1/(2q)}  \le r^{1/(2q)}\|H\|.}  \label{eq:tpc68-even-moment-bound}$$

> **Proof** If $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r$ are the real eigenvalues of $H$, then $$\operatorname{tr}H^{2q}=\sum_{\nu=1}^r|\lambda_\nu|^{2q}.$$ This sum is at least $\max_\nu|\lambda_\nu|^{2q}$ and at most $r$ times that maximum.

For a fixed finite matrix, the middle quantity decreases to $\|H\|$ as $q\to\infty$. In an asymptotic family whose dimension grows with $X$, using $q=q_X\to\infty$ requires estimates uniform in both $X$ and $q_X$. A fixed-moment estimate cannot silently be extrapolated to that regime.

## Literal decorated-walk expansion

For $m\ne n$, abbreviate each literal summand in [\[eq:tpc68-literal-pair-list\]](TPC-068.md#ref-9e3765597c03d4f1) by $$a_{mn}(\xi):=\varepsilon_\xi^{mn}v_\xi^{mn},  \qquad \xi\in\mathcal C_{mn}.$$ For a collision pair $\xi=(w,w')$, define $$j(\xi):=j(w)=j(w'),$$ where equality follows from the common complete native key. Set $\mathcal C_{mm}=\varnothing$, consistently with $H_{mm}=0$.

###### ref-2c2b6d6446138919

###### ref-00d57e8f2fdc9dd6

###### ref-41cb26059f17f85f

> **Proposition: Exact decorated-walk identity** \[prop:tpc68-decorated-walk\] For every integer $k\ge1$, $$(H^k)_{mn}  =  \sum_{\substack{m=m_0,m_1,\ldots,m_k=n\\                  \xi_\ell\in\mathcal C_{m_{\ell-1}m_\ell}}}  \prod_{\ell=1}^k  a_{m_{\ell-1}m_\ell}(\xi_\ell).  \label{eq:tpc68-decorated-walk-expansion}$$ Consequently $$\operatorname{tr}H^{2q}  =  \sum_{\substack{m_0=m_{2q}\\                  m_0,m_1,\ldots,m_{2q-1}\\                  \xi_\ell\in\mathcal C_{m_{\ell-1}m_\ell}}}  \prod_{\ell=1}^{2q}  \varepsilon_{\xi_\ell}^{m_{\ell-1}m_\ell}  v_{\xi_\ell}^{m_{\ell-1}m_\ell}.  \label{eq:tpc68-literal-closed-walk-moment}$$ Both identities sum the complete literal native collision lists on every edge.

> **Proof** Expand the matrix product $$(H^k)_{mn}  =\sum_{m_1,\ldots,m_{k-1}}  H_{mm_1}H_{m_1m_2}\cdots H_{m_{k-1}n}$$ and then expand every entry by [\[eq:tpc68-literal-pair-list\]](TPC-068.md#ref-9e3765597c03d4f1). Summing the diagonal at $k=2q$ gives [\[eq:tpc68-literal-closed-walk-moment\]](TPC-068.md#ref-41cb26059f17f85f).

###### ref-76fa8d6d5f9ef214

> **Proposition: Row gauges telescope on closed walks** \[prop:tpc68-gauge-telescoping\] Let $u_m\in\mathbb C$ with $|u_m|=1$, let $U=\operatorname{diag}(u_m)$, and put $\widehat H=U^*HU$. In every closed-walk term of $\operatorname{tr}\widehat H^{2q}$, the product of the row-gauge factors is one. Hence $$\operatorname{tr}\widehat H^{2q}=\operatorname{tr}H^{2q}.$$ For the real self-adjoint Möbius row gauge $U_d=U_d^*=U_d^{-1}$, the same statement applies to $U_dHU_d$.

> **Proof** An edge $m_{\ell-1}\to m_\ell$ contributes $\overline{u_{m_{\ell-1}}}u_{m_\ell}$. Their product around a closed walk is $$\prod_{\ell=1}^{2q}  \overline{u_{m_{\ell-1}}}u_{m_\ell}=1$$ because $m_{2q}=m_0$. The trace identity also follows from unitary invariance.

###### ref-2814086aa0fc5974

###### ref-a8885ce51b6cc9b4

> **Proposition: Pure same-residual walks have no target-sign gain** \[prop:tpc68-pure-residual-walk-sign\] Consider one decorated closed-walk term in [\[eq:tpc68-literal-closed-walk-moment\]](TPC-068.md#ref-41cb26059f17f85f). If every decorated edge $(w_\ell,w'_\ell)$ is a same-residual collision, then $$\prod_{\ell=1}^{2q}  \varepsilon_{\xi_\ell}^{m_{\ell-1}m_\ell}=1.  \label{eq:tpc68-pure-residual-sign-product}$$ Therefore pure $H_{\rm res}$ walks have no cancellation from the target Möbius signs themselves. Their literal complex weights may still cancel, and walks containing $H_{\rm erase}$ edges retain unequal-residual sign information.

> **Proof** By [\[prop:tpc68-same-residual-sign-freeze\]](TPC-068.md#ref-71a3ab90856ce077), the edge sign is $$\varepsilon_{\xi_\ell}^{m_{\ell-1}m_\ell}  =\mu(d_{m_{\ell-1}})\mu(d_{m_\ell}).$$ Every vertex gauge occurs twice in the closed product, so the product is one.

The telescoping proposition explains why the common residual/source gauge is not the arithmetic cancellation sought here. It disappears from every closed walk automatically. The signs $$\prod_{\ell=1}^{2q}  \mu(m_{\ell-1}j(\xi_\ell)+h_0)  \mu(m_\ell j(\xi_\ell)+h_0)$$ in [\[eq:tpc68-literal-closed-walk-moment\]](TPC-068.md#ref-41cb26059f17f85f) are the genuine target signs. The proposition above identifies the pure same-residual subfamily on which they are trivial. Bounding the remaining complete weighted sum is an L2 problem.

## Why a support walk count is insufficient

Taking absolute values term by term in [\[eq:tpc68-literal-closed-walk-moment\]](TPC-068.md#ref-41cb26059f17f85f) reduces the method to a positive walk count and loses its intended advantage. Conversely, a random-sign heuristic for the decorated walks is not a proof for the one literal Möbius array. A valid operator-walk input must estimate the complete fixed-$h_0$ weighted sum, uniformly over all start rows needed by $\Omega_{2q}$, or the complete closed-walk sum needed by $\operatorname{tr}H^{2q}$.

# The signed native certificate lattice

The preceding theorems take absolute values at different stages. Their conclusions can be compared only after each is evaluated on the same complete literal matrix.

###### ref-ca91c6ea1e8c4872

###### ref-b814a6464a0ea248

###### ref-9653d43a99ea6523

###### ref-ab6e27210850858e

> **Definition: Available certificates** \[def:tpc68-certificate-family\] For the gauged alias operator $H$, define $$\begin{aligned}  C_{\rm star}  &:=\sqrt{\Delta_H}\,s_*(H),  \label{eq:tpc68-C-star}\\  C_{\rm pair}  &:=\rho(P),  \label{eq:tpc68-C-pair}\\  C_{{\rm walk},q}  &:=\Omega_{2q}(H)^{1/(2q)},  \label{eq:tpc68-C-walk}\\  C_{{\rm mom},q}  &:=\bigl(\operatorname{tr}H^{2q}\bigr)^{1/(2q)}  \qquad(q\ge1).  \label{eq:tpc68-C-moment}\end{aligned}$$ Here $P$ may be the optimal literal pairing-defect matrix or any other proved symmetric nonnegative pair majorant. > **Theorem: Exact native eigenvalue certificate** \[thm:tpc68-native-certificate-lattice\] For the atomic-normalized literal missing-native Gram $K=I+H$, $$\begin{aligned}  \kappa_M  &\le1+\mathcal C_X,  \label{eq:tpc68-kappa-lattice}\\  \alpha_M  &\ge\max\{0,1-\mathcal C_X\}.  \label{eq:tpc68-alpha-lattice}\end{aligned}$$ Every individual term in [\[eq:tpc68-master-certificate\]](TPC-068.md#ref-ab6e27210850858e) may replace $\mathcal C_X$.

> **Proof** shows that $\|H\|$ is at most every listed certificate. Hence $\|H\|\le\mathcal C_X$. Since $$\lambda_{\max}(H)\le\|H\|,  \qquad  \lambda_{\min}(H)\ge-\|H\|,$$ the eigenvalue identities [\[eq:tpc68-upper-native-eigenvalue\]](TPC-068.md#ref-ceea57754986f250)–[\[eq:tpc68-lower-native-eigenvalue\]](TPC-068.md#ref-83699259997f825d) give the two bounds. Positivity of $K$ supplies the additional zero in [\[eq:tpc68-alpha-lattice\]](TPC-068.md#ref-1fe3279b90cd7210).

## Full-support components

Let the actual collision graph of $H$ have connected components $\mathscr C_X$. After a simultaneous permutation of rows, $$H=\bigoplus_{C\in\mathscr C_X}H_C,  \qquad  K=\bigoplus_{C\in\mathscr C_X}(I_C+H_C).$$

###### ref-64e31787bf8c4405

###### ref-57117802a09c6969

###### ref-61df2dc470b93a9e

###### ref-77f78dabacd5552f

> **Proposition: Worst-component rule** \[prop:tpc68-worst-component\] One has $$\begin{aligned}  \|H\|&=\max_{C\in\mathscr C_X}\|H_C\|,  \label{eq:tpc68-component-norm}\\  \kappa_M&=\max_{C\in\mathscr C_X}  \lambda_{\max}(I_C+H_C),  \label{eq:tpc68-component-upper}\\  \alpha_M&=\min_{C\in\mathscr C_X}  \lambda_{\min}(I_C+H_C).  \label{eq:tpc68-component-lower}\end{aligned}$$ Thus a uniform theorem must include every actual component. Selecting a favorable component or deleting a difficult row changes the operator.

> **Proof** These are the standard norm and extremal-eigenvalue formulas for a finite orthogonal direct sum.

## Quantifier-safe finite certificates

The pairing $\tau$, Abel ordering, Perron weight $a$, power $q$, and component decomposition may all be chosen after the finite literal matrix is known. They still yield coefficient-uniform operator inequalities because none depends on the vector to which $H$ is later applied. What is forbidden is to choose or alter the physical carrier, erase extra output labels, delete active rows, or rebuild the certificate separately for each later coefficient vector while advertising one uniform operator bound.

# The shorted boundary for full reassembly

The preceding sections control the literal missing-native map relative to its own atomic diagonal. Full reassembly introduces a represented map and coefficient directions invisible to its denominator. We now state the exact L0 relation, following the shorted-Gram framework of TPC–60. The underlying finite-dimensional shorted-operator identity is classical [`\citep{AndersonTrapp1975}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-068.md#cite-b8c5a03e9fd5d376).

> **Theorem: Exact direct-minus-nuisance formula** \[thm:tpc68-direct-minus-nuisance\] The shorted operator is positive semidefinite and $$\boxed{  \delta(A,M)=\lambda_{\min}(G_{\rm sh}).}  \label{eq:tpc68-delta-shorted}$$ If $$G_{\rm dir}=I+H_{\rm dir},  \qquad  \|H_{\rm dir}\|\le c,  \qquad  \|C_N\|\le\eta,  \label{eq:tpc68-direct-penalty-hypotheses}$$ then $$\boxed{  \delta(A,M)\ge\max\{0,1-c-\eta\}.}  \label{eq:tpc68-direct-penalty-bound}$$ If $W_{\rm nuis}=\{0\}$, then $C_N=0$ and no shorting penalty occurs.

> **Proof** For $z=s+n$, with $s\in S_A$ and $n\in N_A$, the denominator is $\|A_{S_A}s\|^2$, while minimization over $n$ gives $$\inf_{n\in N_A}\|B_{S_A}s+B n\|^2  =\|(I-P_{W_{\rm nuis}})B_{S_A}s\|^2.$$ Hence $$\delta(A,M)  =  \inf_{s\ne0}  \frac{\langle  B_{S_A}^*(I-P_{W_{\rm nuis}})B_{S_A}s,s\rangle}  {\langle R_As,s\rangle}.$$ Rayleigh–Ritz after the substitution $x=R_A^{1/2}s$ proves [\[eq:tpc68-delta-shorted\]](TPC-068.md#ref-b335423d1a166961) and the positivity of $G_{\rm sh}$. Under [\[eq:tpc68-direct-penalty-hypotheses\]](TPC-068.md#ref-e256a6366f0eab5f), $$\lambda_{\min}(G_{\rm sh})  \ge1-\|H_{\rm dir}\|-\|C_N\|  \ge1-c-\eta.$$ Positivity adds the maximum with zero. If $W_{\rm nuis}=\{0\}$, its projection vanishes.

> **Corollary: Canonical perturbation plus nuisance penalty** \[cor:tpc68-canonical-perturbation\] Suppose $$\|M_{S_A}s\|\le\varepsilon\|A_{S_A}s\|  \qquad(s\in S_A).  \label{eq:tpc68-relative-missing-bound}$$ Then $$\|G_{\rm dir}-I\|\le2\varepsilon+\varepsilon^2.  \label{eq:tpc68-Gdir-perturbation}$$ Consequently, if also $\|C_N\|\le\eta$, then $$\delta(A,M)  \ge  \max\{0,1-2\varepsilon-\varepsilon^2-\eta\}.  \label{eq:tpc68-safe-reassembly}$$

# Five separating countermodels

The examples below are finite-dimensional. Their purpose is not to model the full arithmetic carrier, but to show exactly which inference fails without additional physical input.

###### ref-bc527092e57f8b16

\[ex:tpc68-erasure-gauge\] Let $$T:\mathbb C\longrightarrow\mathbb C^2,  \qquad Tz=(z,z),$$ and let $\mathsf E(z_1,z_2)=z_1+z_2$. The diagonal residual gauge $$U=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$$ is unitary, so $T$ and $UT$ have the same residual norm and the same support magnitudes. Nevertheless, $$\|\mathsf ETz\|^2=4|z|^2,  \qquad  \|\mathsf EUTz\|^2=0.$$ Thus the location of a residual unit phase is a coordinate choice only before erasure. Once two residual coordinates meet, their relative phase changes the native Gram.

###### ref-f23cdcd98ec3c8df

\[ex:tpc68-small-pairs-large-operator\] For $r\ge2$, let $$H_r:=r^{-1/2}(J_r-I_r),  \qquad K_r:=I_r+H_r.$$ Every off-diagonal pair has magnitude $r^{-1/2}\to0$, and $K_r\succeq0$ because the eigenvalues of $H_r$ are $$\frac{r-1}{\sqrt r},  \qquad -\frac1{\sqrt r}\quad(r-1\text{ times}).$$ However $$\|H_r\|=\frac{r-1}{\sqrt r}\asymp\sqrt r.$$ This is the degree-times-pair obstruction in its simplest positive-Gram form.

###### ref-1d17342c55d1cc2e

\[ex:tpc68-scalar-star-fails\] On vertices $0,1,2,3,4$, let $H$ be the Hermitian star with $$(H_{01},H_{02},H_{03},H_{04})=(1,-1,i,-i)$$ and $H_{n0}=\overline{H_{0n}}$. Then $$\sum_{n=1}^4H_{0n}=0,  \qquad  s_0(H)=2,  \qquad  \|H\|=2.$$ Indeed, a Hermitian weighted star has nonzero eigenvalues $\pm s_0(H)$. For $0<a\le1/2$, the matrix $I+aH$ is positive semidefinite, while the distinguished scalar row sum remains zero and $\|aH\|=2a$. Cancellation against the all-ones vector therefore says nothing uniform about the star.

###### ref-780eb69a39883720

\[ex:tpc68-same-support-different-phase\] Let $r=2^k$ with $k\ge2$, let $S_r$ be a symmetric Sylvester Hadamard matrix, and put $$Q_r:=S_r-\operatorname{diag}(S_r).$$ Both $J_r-I_r$ and $Q_r$ have complete off-diagonal support and every off-diagonal entry has magnitude one. But $$\|J_r-I_r\|=r-1,$$ whereas, using $S_r^2=rI_r$, $$\|Q_r\|\le\|S_r\|+\|\operatorname{diag}(S_r)\|  \le\sqrt r+1.$$ After multiplying both matrices by $a=(r-1)^{-1}$, they still have identical support and entry magnitudes; both $I+a(J_r-I_r)$ and $I+aQ_r$ are positive semidefinite. Indeed, $$a\|Q_r\|  \le\frac{\sqrt r+1}{r-1}\le1  \qquad(r\ge4),$$ while the spectrum of $a(J_r-I_r)$ is $\{1,-(r-1)^{-1},\ldots,-(r-1)^{-1}\}$. Also $$\|Q_r\|  \ge\frac{\|Q_r\|_{\rm F}}{\sqrt r}  =\sqrt{r-1},$$ because $Q_r$ has $r(r-1)$ unit-modulus off-diagonal entries. Their defect norms differ by a factor of order $\sqrt r$. A support graph, codegree, and entry magnitudes cannot recover the missing phase information.

###### ref-da5b29e9acfc3167

\[ex:tpc68-shorting-kills\] Let $$\mathcal K=\mathbb C^2,\qquad\mathcal Y=\mathbb C,$$ and define $$A(z_1,z_2)=z_1,\qquad  M(z_1,z_2)=z_2,\qquad  B(z_1,z_2)=z_1+z_2.$$ Then $S_A=\mathbb C(1,0)$, and on $S_A$ the full map agrees exactly with the represented map: $$G_{\rm dir}=I.$$ But $N=\mathbb C(0,1)$ and $B(N)=\mathbb C$, so $$C_N=I,\qquad G_{\rm sh}=0,\qquad\delta(A,M)=0.$$ Equivalently, the nuisance choice $z_2=-z_1$ annihilates the full output without changing the denominator. A canonical representative estimate cannot replace the shorted quotient.

## Logical separation

prevents the residual unitary gauge from being called cancellation. prevents a small pair estimate from being promoted without aggregation. forces an $\ell^2$-star or stronger uniform object. separates literal phases from support geometry. forces the nuisance penalty in full reassembly. These obstructions are independent: repairing one does not repair the others.

## Endpoint ledger

The exact L0 identities and the L1 carrier crosswalk have zero exponent cost. Every positive exponent in $\delta,\tau,\sigma,\nu_M,\sigma_M,\lambda_{\rm rep}$, every block summation, and every comparison between reference energies must be charged once in the common endpoint ledger. If the literal physical family forces total loss at least $1/400$, the endpoint route must stop. Reallocating the same loss among pair, star, walk, or reassembly notation does not repair the budget.

# Conclusions, kill criteria, and the next decision

## What is proved

For the complete literal missing-native map at one fixed nonzero shift:

1.  physical atomic normalization gives a positive Gram $K=I+H$ with a Hermitian zero-diagonal alias defect;

2.  the common source/residual row gauge exposes the exact signs $\mu(mj+h_0)\mu(nj+h_0)$ without changing any spectral quantity;

3.  on a same-residual row-pair collision this target sign freezes to $\mu(d_m)\mu(d_n)$, so there is no pair-internal Möbius oscillation in $H_{\rm res}$; correspondingly, every pure $H_{\rm res}$ closed walk has target-sign product one;

4.  sign-reversing partial matchings and ordered Abel sums give exact complex-weight pair certificates;

5.  row stars satisfy the exact sandwich $s_*\le\|H\|\le\sqrt{\Delta_H}s_*$, and the local extremizer forbids replacing the star by a distinguished scalar row sum;

6.  a symmetric pair majorant gives the reducible-safe Perron optimum $\|H\|\le\rho(P)$;

7.  even power rows and even traces retain cancellation at the level of complete decorated operator walks, but genuinely new signed information comes only from walks containing $H_{\rm erase}$ edges or from literal complex-weight phases; and

8.  every certificate bounds both extremal eigenvalues of $K$, while the full represented-plus-missing problem is exactly a direct Gram minus the positive nuisance penalty $C_N$.

These are L0 theorems. Their identification with the actual fixed-$h_0$ missing carrier, complete native output labels, and raw atomic masses is L1.

## What remains unproved

The paper does not prove:

-   a power saving for one literal pair defect uniformly over all colliding active row pairs;

-   an $\ell^2$-star saving that beats half the actual degree exponent;

-   a fixed- or growing-length signed operator-walk estimate with constants uniform in every physical block;

-   the required missing-mass saving or represented lower-frame comparison in the same scope;

-   a bound $C_N=o(1)$ for the nuisance shorting penalty; or

-   any parity estimate, prime-pair theorem, or twin-prime conclusion.

## Kill criteria

The following stops have different strengths.

1.  **Wrong object.** If a proposed proof erases $\gamma$, side, external block, opposite row, or any label still orthogonal in the native range, or replaces complete outputs by reduced cells, it does not estimate the operator in this paper.

2.  **Wrong quantifier.** If the carrier, partition, pairing, or row deletion depends on the later coefficient vector or extremizing eigenvector, it is not a coefficient-uniform certificate.

3.  **Wrong physical regime.** A fixed-pair result, average over pairs, average over shifts, density-one row result, or frozen/smoothed family does not imply the required growing-row theorem at the prescribed fixed $h_0$.

4.  **Pair-route stop.** Failure of $\tau\ge\delta$, or a pair majorant with large $\rho(P)$, kills only the pair/Perron certificate. It does not rule out cancellation between complete stars or operator walks.

5.  **Star-route stop.** A star bound that does not beat the target after multiplication by $\sqrt{\Delta_H}$—equivalently, $\sigma<\delta/2$ in the exponent model of [\[prop:tpc68-conditional-star\]](TPC-068.md#ref-74411facf9ed76a0)—kills only the star certificate. It does not rule out a later even-walk or trace estimate.

6.  **Walk-route stop.** An unsigned support-walk count or a nonuniform fixed-moment estimate is not evidence for [\[eq:tpc68-L2-walk-hypothesis\]](TPC-068.md#ref-e1f926dab69dda51). Failure of one moment length does not kill every other length.

7.  **Hard uniform stop.** If the actual shorted Gram $G_{\rm sh}$ has a kernel, equivalently if some $z$ satisfies $Az\ne0$ and $(A+M)z=0$, then $\delta(A,M)=0$. No canonical-representative estimate can repair that uniform reassembly claim. A distinguished physical vector remains a logically separate question.

8.  **Endpoint stop.** If the total literal loss is at least $1/400$, the current endpoint implication must be abandoned even if every intermediate qualitative statement is correct.

## Route decision after TPC–68

TPC–67 and the present paper now expose two independent finite-dimensional doors on actual coefficients: $$\text{all-row determinant mass}  \qquad\text{and}\qquad  \text{signed native operator walks}.$$ The determinant door needs physical matching, pivot, and cycle-activity input. The signed-operator door needs one of the literal pair, star, or walk premises above, followed by missing-mass and nuisance control. A useful next paper should attack one *specified* fixed-$h_0$ decorated walk or star family and either prove a uniform saving or produce an actual coefficient counterexample that closes that door. More support-only repackaging would not advance the proof chain.

## Status line

TPC–68 closes a necessary operator interface and identifies the exact location of the genuine two-affine signs after residual gauge removal. It does not break the arithmetic parity barrier. A negative result for one certificate family would still be publishable as a sharp route obstruction, but it must not be presented as evidence for or against the twin-prime conjecture itself.
