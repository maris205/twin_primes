## Abstract

TPC–70 and TPC–71 reduce the first signed cross-key coefficient of the missing-native Gram to a Möbius sum on primitive cofactor coordinates. The general divisor-lifted certificate of TPC–71 allows an arbitrary finitely supported plane kernel and consequently contains diagonal dilations, restricted Mertens sums, and an exact large-diagonal tail. The literal physical kernel has more structure. Its common content is defined canonically by $q=(S_m,S_n)$, and its remaining coordinates $u=S_m/q$, $v=S_n/q$ are coprime. We prove that the support-faithful zero completion is therefore supported on the primitive lattice. Every diagonal dilation $K^{[d]}(x,y)=K(dx,dy)$ with $d\ge2$ vanishes identically, the exact diagonal tail is zero for every cutoff $D\ge1$, and the physical divisor lift collapses to ordinary two-dimensional Abel summation at $d=1$.

For one anchor $\mathfrak a$, with $M(x)=\sum_{n\le x}\mu(n)$, the exact formula is $$\mathfrak X_{\mathfrak a}  =  \mu(a_{\mathfrak a})\mu(b_{\mathfrak a})  \sum_{x,y\ge1}  M(x)M(y)\Delta_{12}K_{\mathfrak a}^{0}(x,y).$$ We also aggregate all row pairs and anchors before taking absolute values, obtaining a scalar trace kernel and a sharper weighted-plaquette certificate for $\operatorname{tr}(ER)$. An $\ell^1$-valued version retains every anchor and shows that a global cost estimate, rather than uniform anchorwise little-oh notation, is sufficient for trace-level summability.

The tail collapse does not make the variation problem disappear. We prove that mixed variation is exactly the coefficient mass in a unique terminal-rectangle decomposition and that diagonal downsampling contracts this norm. More importantly, the physical sum depends only on values at primitive sites. Any analytic completion at nonprimitive sites gives the same physical sum but may trade a smaller $d=1$ variation against nonzero higher diagonal slices and tail. We define the resulting completion gauge and prove its exact invariance and certificate. The canonical completion is tail-free, but it need not minimize its $d=1$ mixed variation within its completion class. Elementary examples separate tail freedom, low variation, signed cancellation, anchor summation, and operator control. Thus the auxiliary diagonal-tail gate for the support-faithful zero extension is closed exactly, while the mass-normalized completion and weighted-plaquette gates remain open. No operator saving, fixed-$2$ estimate, parity-breaking statement, or prime-pair conclusion is drawn.

# Introduction

The primitive-plane route began with a four-corner coefficient in the first mixed trace. TPC–69 shows that the first surviving signed term is cross-key and that its residual sign is carried by the odd erasure boundary [`\citep{WangTPC69}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-073.md#cite-04567db5550964e9). TPC–70 then gives exact cofactor coordinates: after extracting the common erasure content $q$, the surviving sign is a product $\mu(a)\mu(b)\mu(u)\mu(v)$ that is frozen along each affine prime ray [`\citep{WangTPC70}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-073.md#cite-9d0361df6408698e). TPC–71 applies coprimality inversion and two-dimensional Abel summation to a general finitely supported plane kernel [`\citep{WangTPC71}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-073.md#cite-f2e0afa0bd3d4064). Its master certificate contains $$\sum_{d\le D}  \mathfrak M_d(U/d)\mathfrak M_d(V/d)  \mathcal V_\square(K^{[d]})  +  \mathcal T_D(K).$$ TPC–72 carries the mass-normalized variation, the representation-dependent diagonal tail before a completion is fixed, and complete anchor summation as open gates; it also warns that a four-corner trace coefficient is not a two-corner erasure entry [`\citep{WangTPC72}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-073.md#cite-d21b73d8ae01e89b).

The present paper re-examines only the literal support of the kernel before trying to estimate it. The physical coordinates are not an arbitrary pair followed by a coprimality mask. They are defined by $$q=(S_m,S_n),\qquad S_m=qu,\qquad S_n=qv.$$ Consequently $(u,v)=1$ identically. If a kernel is extended by zero at coordinates which do not occur physically, then it is already supported on the primitive lattice. For $d\ge2$, the point $(dx,dy)$ cannot be primitive. Hence every higher diagonal slice vanishes.

This observation has two different consequences which must not be conflated.

1.  The support-faithful physical completion is tail-free. Its exact Abel certificate has one slice and needs no growing-$d$ restricted-Mertens theorem.

2.  Values away from the primitive lattice are invisible to the physical signed sum. They may therefore be introduced as an analytic completion. Such a completion can fill hard coprimality holes and reduce variation, but its higher diagonal slices and tail return and must be charged.

Thus variation and diagonal tail are not independent invariants of the primitive physical datum. They form a coupled analytic completion gauge. Tail freedom is canonical for the support-faithful zero extension; low variation is a separate theorem.

## Main results

The paper proves seven groups of statements.

1.  The exact $q$-coordinates make every literal anchored kernel primitive-supported after the $q$-sum and the removal of the same-residual direction.

2.  The canonical zero completion annihilates all $d\ge2$ diagonal slices and every exact diagonal tail with cutoff $D\ge1$.

3.  The anchored coefficient and the complete scalar mixed trace have exact $d=1$ Abel identities and weighted-plaquette majorants.

4.  A Banach-valued, anchor-labelled version retains the complete growing anchor family and replaces uniform anchorwise little-oh notation by one global cost inequality.

5.  Every finitely supported kernel has a unique terminal-rectangle expansion. Mixed variation is precisely the $\ell^1$-mass of its rectangle charges, and diagonal downsampling is contractive.

6.  Every analytic completion of the same primitive datum gives the same physical sum. Taking the infimum of the full divisor-variation cost over declared completions is an exact sufficient certificate.

7.  Counterexamples show that zero completion need not be variation-optimal, completion costs can be inflated arbitrarily, primitive support need not cancel, and scalar trace cancellation does not control the erasure operator.

## Logical level

The finite-support identities and completion calculus are L0. The identification of the literal fixed-$h_0$ kernel with its canonical primitive zero completion is L1: it retains all complete native keys, actual support, literal coefficients, and raw atomic normalization. The paper does not estimate the resulting physical weighted plaquette cost relative to the anchored or trace mass. That missing inequality is the next L2 gate. In particular, eliminating an auxiliary diagonal tail is not a parity breakthrough.

## Rows, anchors, and erasure coordinates

Fix a finite physical scale, one nonzero integer shift $h_0$, two distinct active rows $m<n$ satisfying $$(mn,h_0)=1,$$ and squarefree row divisors $$d_m\mid m,\qquad d_n\mid n,\qquad  d_m=ga,\qquad d_n=gb,\qquad(a,b)=1.$$ An active row $r$ is a canonical source row with positive raw atomic mass $W_r$. On the active row space, the atomic-normalized native Gram has the inherited decomposition $$G_{\rm nat}=I+R+E,$$ where $R$ retains common-output pairs with equal residual labels and $E$ retains those with unequal residual labels [`\citep{WangTPC68}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-073.md#cite-b8c461e9c6be3261). A complete native key retains the direct side, external block, opposite row, $\gamma$-coordinate, physical time, and every other orthogonal output label; equality of times alone is not used as equality of keys.

###### ref-8507debebabe5eb2

For the row pair $(m,n)$, a complete same-residual anchor $\mathfrak a$ contains a complete key $\iota_R$, its time $j_R$, and both active row corners $$mj_R+h_0=P_mR_m,\qquad  nj_R+h_0=P_nR_n,\qquad  d_mR_m=d_nR_n.$$ At an actual erasure key $\iota_E$, with time $j_E$, the corresponding targets are $$mj_E+h_0=Q_mS_m,\qquad  nj_E+h_0=Q_nS_n,\qquad  d_mS_m\ne d_nS_n.$$ Here $P_m,P_n,Q_m,Q_n$ are the literal largest-prime labels and $R_m,R_n,S_m,S_n$ their cofactors. At a present corner, let $\beta_{\iota,r}$ denote the literal coefficient after every physical mask and profile. The residual-sign-free four-corner amplitude is $$\mathcal A(\iota_E,\iota_R;m,n)  =  \frac{  \overline{\beta_{\iota_E,m}}\beta_{\iota_E,n}  \overline{\beta_{\iota_R,n}}\beta_{\iota_R,m}  }{W_mW_n},  \label{eq:literal-four-corner-amplitude}$$ and is zero if a required corner is absent [`\citep{WangTPC70}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-073.md#cite-9d0361df6408698e).

###### ref-651727158205bb13

###### ref-54ff65737d874c22

Every target and residual label used below lies on the inherited squarefree physical support. In particular, at an erasure key $$mj_E+h_0=Q_mS_m,\qquad nj_E+h_0=Q_nS_n,$$ and $$\mu(mj_E+h_0),\ \mu(nj_E+h_0),\  \mu(d_mS_m),\ \mu(d_nS_n)\in\{-1,1\}.$$ The analogous nonvanishing conditions are retained at the same-residual anchor. Put $$H=h_0(n-m).$$ At an actual erasure key, the two target cofactors are $S_m,S_n$. Their canonical content and primitive coordinates are $$q=(S_m,S_n),\qquad S_m=qu,\qquad S_n=qv.  \label{eq:canonical-erasure-coordinates}$$ The determinant identity of TPC–70 [`\citep{WangTPC70}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-073.md#cite-9d0361df6408698e) gives $$q\mid |H|,  \qquad  nuQ_m-mvQ_n=H/q,$$ and the unequal-residual condition is $$(u,v)\ne(b,a).  \label{eq:excluded-anchor-direction}$$ Every physical coordinate in [\[eq:canonical-erasure-coordinates\]](TPC-073.md#ref-fdd12eb58011cd0a) satisfies $$(u,v)=1.  \label{eq:physical-primitivity}$$

Let $k_{\mathfrak a,q}(u,v)$ be the TPC–70 complete literal ray sum of [\[eq:literal-four-corner-amplitude\]](TPC-073.md#ref-8507debebabe5eb2) at these coordinates. It contains every physical time, complete native key, largest-prime condition, endpoint, mask, profile, complex phase, and the four-corner normalization $1/(W_mW_n)$. It is zero when the corresponding actual ray is absent. Aggregate the exact contents before taking absolute values: $$k_{\mathfrak a}(u,v)  =  \mathbf 1_{(u,v)\ne(b,a)}  \sum_{q\mid|H|}k_{\mathfrak a,q}(u,v),  \qquad (u,v)=1.  \label{eq:primitive-anchor-datum}$$ Different $q$-slices may cancel at the same primitive coordinate. Accordingly, $$\operatorname{supp}k_{\mathfrak a}  \subseteq  \bigcup_{q\mid|H|}\operatorname{supp}k_{\mathfrak a,q},$$ and equality is not asserted.

## Canonical zero completion

Write $$\mathcal P_2=\{(u,v)\in\mathbb N^2:(u,v)=1\}.$$

###### ref-f340d537311c0f66

###### ref-1753dd164f77aba3

> **Definition: Support-faithful completion** \[def:canonical-zero-completion\] The canonical physical zero completion of [\[eq:primitive-anchor-datum\]](TPC-073.md#ref-64282a6e40ec64e7) is $$K_{\mathfrak a}^{0}(u,v)  =  \begin{cases}   k_{\mathfrak a}(u,v),&(u,v)\in\mathcal P_2,\\   0,&(u,v)\notin\mathcal P_2,  \end{cases}  \qquad u,v\ge1.  \label{eq:canonical-zero-completion}$$ It is zero both at absent primitive rays and at every nonprimitive coordinate.

The superscript $0$ denotes zero completion, not the removal of a physical atom. The same-residual direction $(b,a)$ is already absent from the literal erasure datum.

###### ref-c464523de5fe7f27

###### ref-4b758cea719a4474

> **Theorem: Literal primitive support** \[thm:literal-primitive-support\] For every physical anchor $\mathfrak a$, $$\operatorname{supp}K_{\mathfrak a}^{0}\subseteq\mathcal P_2.  \label{eq:literal-primitive-support}$$ The property is preserved by exact $q$-aggregation, finite sums over anchors, and multiplication by anchor-dependent scalars.

> **Proof** Equation [\[eq:physical-primitivity\]](TPC-073.md#ref-54ff65737d874c22) follows from the definition $q=(S_m,S_n)$. Thus no actual ray has a nonprimitive coordinate. Definition [\[def:canonical-zero-completion\]](TPC-073.md#ref-f340d537311c0f66) assigns zero to every remaining site. Pointwise sums and scalar multiplication cannot create support outside $\mathcal P_2$.

> **Remark: Canonical means support-faithful** \[rem:not-variation-minimal\] The completion is canonical because it inserts no coefficient at an absent physical coordinate. This does not say that it minimizes mixed variation among all analytic completions. That different optimization problem is treated in [5](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-073.md#src-b166003a3d64f534).

###### ref-62e6970ade9fd5eb

Let $$\varepsilon_{\mathfrak a}=\mu(a)\mu(b).$$ The frozen-sign theorem of TPC–70 [`\citep{WangTPC70}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-073.md#cite-9d0361df6408698e) gives the exact anchored scalar coefficient $$\boxed{  \mathfrak X_{\mathfrak a}  =  \varepsilon_{\mathfrak a}  \sum_{u,v\ge1}  \mu(u)\mu(v)K_{\mathfrak a}^{0}(u,v).}  \label{eq:anchored-physical-sum}$$ The explicit coprimality condition may be omitted because [\[eq:literal-primitive-support\]](TPC-073.md#ref-4b758cea719a4474) already enforces it. # Tail collapse and single-slice Abel summation

###### ref-81289d6e01400f5b

For a finitely supported kernel $F:\mathbb N^2\to\mathbb C$, define $$F^{[d]}(x,y)=F(dx,dy).$$ TPC–71 [`\citep{WangTPC71}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-073.md#cite-f2e0afa0bd3d4064) introduces the exact diagonal tail $$\mathcal T_D(F)  =  \sum_{d>D}|\mu(d)|  \sum_{x,y\ge1}  |\mu(dx)\mu(dy)F(dx,dy)|.  \label{eq:diagonal-tail}$$

###### ref-4017e1a6090e4402

###### ref-54099d01494dc19c

###### ref-d216f7600500b435

> **Theorem: Primitive-support collapse** \[thm:primitive-support-collapse\] If $F$ is supported on $\mathcal P_2$, then $$F^{[d]}\equiv0\qquad(d\ge2).  \label{eq:higher-slices-vanish}$$ Consequently, $$\mathcal T_D(F)=0\qquad(D\ge1).  \label{eq:tail-vanishes}$$

> **Proof** For $d\ge2$ and $x,y\ge1$, $$\gcd(dx,dy)=d\gcd(x,y)\ge d>1.$$ Hence $(dx,dy)\notin\mathcal P_2$, so $F(dx,dy)=0$. Every summand in [\[eq:diagonal-tail\]](TPC-073.md#ref-81289d6e01400f5b) has $d>D\ge1$, and therefore vanishes.

###### ref-b11b6344befc4612

> **Corollary: Physical diagonal hierarchy collapses** \[cor:physical-diagonal-collapse\] For every physical anchor $\mathfrak a$ and every $D\ge1$, $$(K_{\mathfrak a}^{0})^{[d]}\equiv0\quad(d\ge2),  \qquad  \mathcal T_D(K_{\mathfrak a}^{0})=0.$$ Thus the support-faithful physical branch requires neither a growing diagonal cutoff nor a restricted-Mertens estimate uniform in $d$.

## Exact weighted-plaquette identity

Set $$M(x)=\sum_{n\le x}\mu(n).$$ For a zero-extended finite kernel, write $$\begin{aligned}  \Delta_1F(x,y)&=F(x,y)-F(x+1,y),\\  \Delta_2F(x,y)&=F(x,y)-F(x,y+1),\\  \Delta_{12}F(x,y)  &=F(x,y)-F(x+1,y)-F(x,y+1)+F(x+1,y+1).\end{aligned}$$

###### ref-83d020c25f3acef4

###### ref-d6159af41aefad22

> **Theorem: Finite two-dimensional Abel identity** \[thm:tail-free-abel\] If $F:\mathbb N^2\to\mathbb C$ is finitely supported, then $$\boxed{  \sum_{u,v\ge1}\mu(u)\mu(v)F(u,v)  =  \sum_{x,y\ge1}M(x)M(y)\Delta_{12}F(x,y).}  \label{eq:tail-free-abel}$$

> **Proof** All sums are finite. Apply one-dimensional summation by parts in $u$ and then in $v$.

> **Remark** Primitive support is not needed for the Abel identity itself. It is needed at the physical interface: it permits the coprimality mask to be omitted in [\[eq:anchored-physical-sum\]](TPC-073.md#ref-62e6970ade9fd5eb) and makes every $d\ge2$ slice of the TPC–71 divisor lift vanish separately.

###### ref-cd04b2bd92c673e7

###### ref-a0f26467e7200f46

> **Definition: Weighted plaquette cost** \[def:weighted-plaquette-cost\] For a finitely supported kernel $F$, define $$\mathcal C_\square(F)  =  \sum_{x,y\ge1}  |M(x)M(y)|\,|\Delta_{12}F(x,y)|.  \label{eq:weighted-plaquette-cost}$$

###### ref-16f495bd2a7d7f41

###### ref-89a1c461e4cf4db4

###### ref-ab9f35552f5457cd

> **Corollary: Exact anchored certificate** \[cor:exact-anchored-certificate\] For every physical anchor, $$\begin{aligned}  \mathfrak X_{\mathfrak a}  &=  \varepsilon_{\mathfrak a}  \sum_{x,y\ge1}  M(x)M(y)\Delta_{12}K_{\mathfrak a}^{0}(x,y),  \label{eq:anchored-Abel-identity}\\  |\mathfrak X_{\mathfrak a}|  &\le \mathcal C_\square(K_{\mathfrak a}^{0}).  \label{eq:anchored-weighted-cost}\end{aligned}$$

> **Proof** Combine [\[eq:anchored-physical-sum\]](TPC-073.md#ref-62e6970ade9fd5eb) with [\[thm:tail-free-abel\]](TPC-073.md#ref-83d020c25f3acef4), then take absolute values.

###### ref-d4abe837bec6e893

> **Remark: Sharper than a product of maxima** \[rem:weighted-is-sharper\] If $F$ is supported in $[1,U]\times[1,V]$, then $$\mathcal C_\square(F)  \le  \mathfrak M(U)\mathfrak M(V)\mathcal V_\square(F),$$ where $$\mathfrak M(U)=\max_{x\le U}|M(x)|,  \qquad  \mathcal V_\square(F)=\sum_{x,y}|\Delta_{12}F(x,y)|.$$ The weighted cost retains the actual location of every plaquette and can be strictly smaller. Neither quantity is estimated for the physical kernel here.

> **Remark: What tail has not disappeared** Equation [\[eq:tail-vanishes\]](TPC-073.md#ref-d216f7600500b435) concerns only the auxiliary diagonal tail introduced by coprimality inversion. It says nothing about the geometric short-ray mass, outer physical endpoints, the ultra-tail of the original packet, or downstream boundary reconstruction.

# Mixed variation as canonical rectangle charge

The remaining canonical cost is concentrated in $\Delta_{12}K^0$. We record its exact geometric meaning.

###### ref-576075b1330ae67c

For $r,s\ge1$, define the terminal rectangle $$R_{r,s}(u,v)=\mathbf 1_{u\le r}\mathbf 1_{v\le s}.  \label{eq:terminal-rectangle}$$ It is a rectangle anchored at $(1,1)$ and terminating at $(r,s)$.

###### ref-ba7c090a90c91fa8

###### ref-aa4b26e6d7ec5ac6

###### ref-0dda7d3724ef0bc6

###### ref-e7e93f1c812ecc03

> **Theorem: Unique terminal-rectangle expansion** \[thm:terminal-rectangle-expansion\] Every finitely supported $F:\mathbb N^2\to\mathbb C$ has the exact expansion $$\boxed{  F(u,v)  =  \sum_{r,s\ge1}  \Delta_{12}F(r,s)R_{r,s}(u,v).}  \label{eq:terminal-rectangle-expansion}$$ The coefficients are unique. Moreover, $$\Delta_{12}R_{r,s}(x,y)=\mathbf 1_{(x,y)=(r,s)}  \label{eq:rectangle-unit-charge}$$ and hence $$\mathcal V_\square(F)  =  \sum_{r,s\ge1}|\Delta_{12}F(r,s)|  \label{eq:rectangle-charge-mass}$$ is exactly the $\ell^1$-mass of the canonical rectangle charges.

> **Proof** Finite support permits two successive telescoping sums: $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_{r\ge u}\sum_{s\ge v}\Delta_{12}F(r,s)  &=  \sum_{r\ge u}\sum_{s\ge v}  \bigl(  F(r,s)-F(r+1,s)\\  &\hspace{3cm}  -F(r,s+1)+F(r+1,s+1)  \bigr)\\  &=F(u,v).\end{aligned}$$ This is [\[eq:terminal-rectangle-expansion\]](TPC-073.md#ref-aa4b26e6d7ec5ac6). Direct substitution gives [\[eq:rectangle-unit-charge\]](TPC-073.md#ref-0dda7d3724ef0bc6). Applying $\Delta_{12}$ to any terminal-rectangle expansion therefore recovers every coefficient, proving uniqueness and [\[eq:rectangle-charge-mass\]](TPC-073.md#ref-e7e93f1c812ecc03).

###### ref-0e5cc8459f07feda

###### ref-61094187d69416bf

> **Corollary: Weighted rectangle form of the physical coefficient** \[cor:weighted-rectangle-form\] For every physical anchor, $$\mathfrak X_{\mathfrak a}  =  \varepsilon_{\mathfrak a}  \sum_{r,s\ge1}  M(r)M(s)\,  \Delta_{12}K_{\mathfrak a}^{0}(r,s).  \label{eq:weighted-rectangle-form}$$ Thus the canonical Abel problem is exactly a discrepancy estimate for the literal terminal-rectangle charges against the weight $M(r)M(s)$.

> **Remark** Low mixed variation means that the physical datum admits a sparse canonical superposition of large terminal rectangles. Sparse support in the point basis is a different property and can produce maximal rectangle charge.

## Diagonal downsampling

The next identity is useful both for the general TPC–71 certificate and for later analytic completions.

###### ref-01b9d5c73d937ea9

> **Theorem: Downsampling contraction** \[thm:downsampling-contraction\] Let $F^{[d]}(x,y)=F(dx,dy)$. Then $$\Delta_{12}F^{[d]}(x,y)  =  \sum_{r=dx}^{d(x+1)-1}  \sum_{s=dy}^{d(y+1)-1}  \Delta_{12}F(r,s).  \label{eq:downsampling-block-charge}$$ Consequently, $$\boxed{  \mathcal V_\square(F^{[d]})  \le  \mathcal V_\square(F).}  \label{eq:downsampling-contraction}$$

> **Proof** Telescoping over the displayed $d$-by-$d$ block gives $$\begin{aligned}  &\sum_{r=dx}^{d(x+1)-1}  \sum_{s=dy}^{d(y+1)-1}\Delta_{12}F(r,s)\\  &\qquad =  F(dx,dy)-F(d(x+1),dy)-F(dx,d(y+1))  +F(d(x+1),d(y+1)),\end{aligned}$$ which is $\Delta_{12}F^{[d]}(x,y)$. The blocks indexed by $(x,y)\in\mathbb N^2$ are disjoint. Apply the triangle inequality and sum.

> **Remark** For the canonical physical completion, [\[thm:primitive-support-collapse\]](TPC-073.md#ref-4017e1a6090e4402) is much stronger: $F^{[d]}$ is identically zero for $d\ge2$. The contraction theorem is needed when a nonprimitive analytic completion is deliberately introduced.

## Point and rectangle extremes

For $p,q\ge1$, write $$e_{p,q}(u,v)=\mathbf 1_{(u,v)=(p,q)}.$$ If $p,q>1$, a point mass $ce_{p,q}$ has four nonzero rectangle charges and $$\mathcal V_\square(ce_{p,q})=4|c|.$$ By contrast, a constant terminal rectangle $cR_{U,V}$ has one charge and $$\mathcal V_\square(cR_{U,V})=|c|.$$ These are exact identities, not asymptotic estimates. They explain why filling nonprimitive holes may trade pointwise sparsity for rectangular coherence.

# Analytic completions and the coupled certificate gauge

The physical datum is prescribed only on the primitive lattice, including zeros at absent primitive rays. Values at nonprimitive coordinates do not change the physical sum. They can nevertheless change every term of a triangle-inequality certificate.

## Declared finite completions

Fix $U,V\in\mathbb N$ and a rectangle $$B_{U,V}=[1,U]\times[1,V]$$ containing the primitive physical datum $$k:\mathcal P_2\cap B_{U,V}\longrightarrow\mathbb C.$$

###### ref-1e041d3dd63195d2

> **Definition: Completion class** \[def:completion-class\] Let $\operatorname{Ext}_{U,V}(k)$ be the affine space of kernels $F:\mathbb N^2\to\mathbb C$ such that $$\operatorname{supp}F\subseteq B_{U,V},  \qquad  F(u,v)=k(u,v)  \quad\bigl((u,v)\in\mathcal P_2\cap B_{U,V}\bigr).$$ The values at nonprimitive sites are free analytic variables. The support-faithful zero completion $$K^0(u,v)=  \begin{cases}   k(u,v),&(u,v)\in\mathcal P_2\cap B_{U,V},\\   0,&\text{otherwise}  \end{cases}$$ belongs to this class.

###### ref-97c30ff2830315bd

Define the primitive signed functional $$\mathcal S_{\rm prim}(k)  =  \sum_{\substack{u\le U,\ v\le V\\(u,v)=1}}  \mu(u)\mu(v)k(u,v).  \label{eq:primitive-functional}$$

###### ref-4ad33239bd8d8e44

###### ref-c119fac80accee45

> **Theorem: Completion invariance** \[thm:completion-invariance\] For every $F\in\operatorname{Ext}_{U,V}(k)$, $$\boxed{  \mathcal S_{\rm prim}(k)  =  \sum_{\substack{u,v\ge1\\(u,v)=1}}  \mu(u)\mu(v)F(u,v).}  \label{eq:completion-invariance}$$ Thus the physical functional is constant on the complete affine class $\operatorname{Ext}_{U,V}(k)$.

> **Proof** The summation in [\[eq:completion-invariance\]](TPC-073.md#ref-c119fac80accee45) samples only primitive coordinates, where every member of the class equals $k$.

## The full completion cost

###### ref-1108893a5cfc2f29

For $d\ge1$, put $$M_d(x)=\sum_{\substack{n\le x\\(n,d)=1}}\mu(n),  \qquad  \mathfrak M_d(T)  =\max_{1\le n\le\lfloor T\rfloor}|M_d(n)|,  \qquad  F^{[d]}(x,y)=F(dx,dy).$$ We set $\mathfrak M_d(T)=0$ for $T<1$. For $D\ge1$, define the exact weighted completion cost $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal C_D(F)  :={}&  \sum_{d\le D}|\mu(d)|  \sum_{x,y\ge1}  |M_d(x)M_d(y)|  |\Delta_{12}F^{[d]}(x,y)|  \notag\\  &+  \mathcal T_D(F),  \label{eq:full-completion-cost}\end{aligned}$$ where $\mathcal T_D$ is the exact tail in [\[eq:diagonal-tail\]](TPC-073.md#ref-81289d6e01400f5b).

###### ref-420a0d893a46c1f2

###### ref-f0da0243deee23ab

###### ref-53e0d07ee74cc6ef

> **Theorem: Completion-gauge certificate** \[thm:completion-gauge\] For every declared completion class and every $D\ge1$, $$\boxed{  |\mathcal S_{\rm prim}(k)|  \le  \inf_{F\in\operatorname{Ext}_{U,V}(k)}\mathcal C_D(F).}  \label{eq:completion-gauge}$$ For the canonical zero completion, $$\mathcal C_D(K^0)=\mathcal C_\square(K^0)  \qquad(D\ge1).  \label{eq:canonical-cost-collapse}$$

> **Proof** Fix $F\in\operatorname{Ext}_{U,V}(k)$. By [\[thm:completion-invariance\]](TPC-073.md#ref-4ad33239bd8d8e44), its primitive sum is the physical functional. The exact TPC–71 diagonal divisor lift [`\citep{WangTPC71}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-073.md#cite-f2e0afa0bd3d4064) followed by two-dimensional Abel summation gives $$\mathcal S_{\rm prim}(k)  =  \sum_{d\le D}\mu(d)  \sum_{x,y\ge1}  M_d(x)M_d(y)\Delta_{12}F^{[d]}(x,y)  +\mathcal R_D(F),  \qquad  |\mathcal R_D(F)|\le\mathcal T_D(F).$$ Taking absolute values gives $$|\mathcal S_{\rm prim}(k)|\le\mathcal C_D(F).$$ Take the infimum. For $K^0$, all $d\ge2$ slices and the tail vanish by [\[thm:primitive-support-collapse\]](TPC-073.md#ref-4017e1a6090e4402); the $d=1$ term is exactly [\[eq:weighted-plaquette-cost\]](TPC-073.md#ref-a0f26467e7200f46).

###### ref-69988f29476c92f7

> **Remark: Infimum versus a physical construction** \[rem:completion-quantifier\] The infimum is an exact finite-dimensional analytic benchmark. To use a completion in a uniform physical theorem, one must construct it before any later extremizing coefficient vector is selected and control its cost uniformly in all literal labels. An optimizer chosen after seeing the operator extremizer changes the quantifier. Moreover, nonprimitive values are analytic fill-ins and are never added to the physical reference mass.

> **Remark: No separate monotonicity** The $d=1$ variation and the higher-diagonal tail can move in opposite directions when a completion changes. Neither term is an invariant of the primitive datum. Only the original physical functional and a proved bound for the complete cost are invariant conclusions.

## A coarse completion norm

###### ref-b56a55ae4031213a

The weighted cost is the full location-sensitive deterministic majorant. A coarser but sometimes convenient completion cost is $$\mathcal E_D(F)  :=  \sum_{d\le D}|\mu(d)|  \mathfrak M_d(U/d)\mathfrak M_d(V/d)  \mathcal V_\square(F^{[d]})  +  \mathcal T_D(F).  \label{eq:coarse-completion-cost}$$ Then $\mathcal C_D(F)\le\mathcal E_D(F)$. The two displayed pieces in [\[eq:coarse-completion-cost\]](TPC-073.md#ref-b56a55ae4031213a) must be evaluated for the same completion; minimizing them independently would splice incompatible analytic objects.

# Aggregation over contents, anchors, and row pairs

All index sets at a finite physical scale are finite. We may therefore aggregate exact scalar kernels before applying absolute values.

## The $q$-sum

###### ref-8b3d12d2c417c912

Let $K_{\mathfrak a,q}^{0}$ be the canonical zero completion of the $q$-slice, with the excluded direction removed. Linearity gives $$K_{\mathfrak a}^{0}  =  \sum_{q\mid|H|}K_{\mathfrak a,q}^{0},  \qquad  \Delta_{12}K_{\mathfrak a}^{0}  =  \sum_{q\mid|H|}  \Delta_{12}K_{\mathfrak a,q}^{0}.  \label{eq:q-aggregation-difference}$$

###### ref-ab60246f1599c2f7

###### ref-b178ea653b21c9df

###### ref-bdf1e6ffa084aff2

> **Proposition: Post-aggregation sharpness** \[prop:q-aggregation-sharpness\] One has $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal C_\square(K_{\mathfrak a}^{0})  &\le  \sum_{q\mid|H|}\mathcal C_\square(K_{\mathfrak a,q}^{0}),  \label{eq:q-weighted-subadditivity}\\  \mathcal V_\square(K_{\mathfrak a}^{0})  &\le  \sum_{q\mid|H|}\mathcal V_\square(K_{\mathfrak a,q}^{0}).  \label{eq:q-variation-subadditivity}\end{aligned}$$ Both inequalities can be strict in the ambient class of finite primitive-supported kernels, including the case in which the left side is zero and the right side is positive. This does not assert that every literal physical family realizes such exact slice cancellation.

> **Proof** Apply the triangle inequality pointwise to [\[eq:q-aggregation-difference\]](TPC-073.md#ref-8b3d12d2c417c912), first with weights $|M(x)M(y)|$, then without them. Ambient strictness follows by taking two nonzero slices $F$ and $-F$.

Thus the exact $q$-sum precedes absolute values. The divisor bound $\tau(|H|)=|H|^{o(1)}$ controls the number of slices only; it supplies no signed cancellation.

## Scalar trace kernel

###### ref-332b49c0deebb7b0

For each row pair $m<n$, let $\mathcal R_{m,n}$ be its complete set of actual same-residual anchors. Extend every finite kernel by zero to $\mathbb N^2$ and define $$K_{\rm tr}^{0}(u,v)  =  \sum_{m<n}  \sum_{\mathfrak a\in\mathcal R_{m,n}}  \varepsilon_{\mathfrak a}K_{\mathfrak a}^{0}(u,v).  \label{eq:scalar-trace-kernel}$$ Although the coordinate labels originate from different row pairs, the right side is only a scalar reindexing of a finite sum. It is not an operator identification.

###### ref-0f3e242b0d498bab

###### ref-be5938bac5e6fbc0

###### ref-f4042ea21e309417

###### ref-819c162962879929

> **Theorem: Global trace Abel summation** \[thm:global-trace-Abelization\] The complete first mixed trace satisfies $$\begin{aligned}  \operatorname{tr}(ER)  &=  2\operatorname{Re}  \sum_{u,v\ge1}  \mu(u)\mu(v)K_{\rm tr}^{0}(u,v)  \label{eq:trace-global-plane}\\  &=  2\operatorname{Re}  \sum_{x,y\ge1}  M(x)M(y)\Delta_{12}K_{\rm tr}^{0}(x,y).  \label{eq:trace-global-Abel}\end{aligned}$$ Consequently, $$\boxed{  |\operatorname{tr}(ER)|  \le  2\mathcal C_\square(K_{\rm tr}^{0})  \le  2\sum_{m<n}\sum_{\mathfrak a\in\mathcal R_{m,n}}  \mathcal C_\square(K_{\mathfrak a}^{0}).}  \label{eq:global-trace-certificate}$$

> **Proof** TPC–70 [`\citep{WangTPC70}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-073.md#cite-9d0361df6408698e) gives $$\operatorname{tr}(ER)  =  2\operatorname{Re}  \sum_{m<n}\sum_{\mathfrak a\in\mathcal R_{m,n}}  \mathfrak X_{\mathfrak a}.$$ Insert [\[eq:anchored-physical-sum\]](TPC-073.md#ref-62e6970ade9fd5eb) and interchange finite sums to obtain [\[eq:trace-global-plane\]](TPC-073.md#ref-be5938bac5e6fbc0). The kernel in [\[eq:scalar-trace-kernel\]](TPC-073.md#ref-332b49c0deebb7b0) remains primitive-supported, so [\[thm:tail-free-abel\]](TPC-073.md#ref-83d020c25f3acef4) gives [\[eq:trace-global-Abel\]](TPC-073.md#ref-f4042ea21e309417). Apply the triangle inequality once after full scalar aggregation, or before anchor aggregation for the second bound.

## Anchor-safe labelled form

###### ref-deec6668c4f2babf

Let $\mathcal I_X$ be the finite set of all triples $(m,n,\mathfrak a)$, and define the $\ell^1(\mathcal I_X)$-valued kernel $$\mathbf K^{0}(u,v)  =  \bigl(  \varepsilon_{\mathfrak a}K_{\mathfrak a}^{0}(u,v)  \bigr)_{(m,n,\mathfrak a)\in\mathcal I_X}.  \label{eq:anchor-labelled-kernel}$$

###### ref-b3dbf3a28e02cd59

###### ref-7b122136b1d23cbe

> **Proposition: Anchor-safe global certificate** \[prop:anchor-safe-certificate\] One has the exact vector identity $$\sum_{u,v}\mu(u)\mu(v)\mathbf K^{0}(u,v)  =  \bigl(\mathfrak X_{\mathfrak a}\bigr)_{(m,n,\mathfrak a)\in\mathcal I_X}$$ and $$\boxed{  \sum_{m<n}\sum_{\mathfrak a\in\mathcal R_{m,n}}  |\mathfrak X_{\mathfrak a}|  \le  \sum_{m<n}\sum_{\mathfrak a\in\mathcal R_{m,n}}  \mathcal C_\square(K_{\mathfrak a}^{0}).}  \label{eq:anchor-safe-global-certificate}$$

> **Proof** Apply the Banach-valued two-dimensional Abel identity in $\ell^1(\mathcal I_X)$. The norm of the mixed difference is the sum of the coordinatewise absolute values, giving the right side of [\[eq:anchor-safe-global-certificate\]](TPC-073.md#ref-7b122136b1d23cbe).

> **Remark: Uniform anchors are not required** A sufficient trace-level theorem may prove that the total cost on all anchors is small relative to the declared total post-$q$-aggregation mass. Individual bad anchors are allowed if their aggregate cost is negligible. This is weaker than a uniform anchorwise estimate and stronger than fixed-anchor little-oh notation with no domination.

> **Remark: Still not an operator theorem** Cancellation in $K_{\rm tr}^{0}$ can occur across anchors and row pairs. Neither [\[eq:global-trace-certificate\]](TPC-073.md#ref-819c162962879929) nor [\[eq:anchor-safe-global-certificate\]](TPC-073.md#ref-7b122136b1d23cbe) bounds a two-corner entry, row star, Perron radius, or operator norm. A direct-entry or row-local bridge remains necessary.

# Countermodels and non-substitution laws

The following finite examples separate the conclusions proved above from the estimates which remain open.

## Zero completion need not minimize variation

###### ref-effe6a6d4385dc5c

Fix $U,V\ge3$ and prescribe the primitive datum $$k(u,v)=1  \qquad  ((u,v)=1,\ u\le U,\ v\le V).$$ Its canonical zero completion is $$K^0(u,v)=\mathbf 1_{u\le U,v\le V}\mathbf 1_{(u,v)=1}.$$ The constant rectangle $$F(u,v)=\mathbf 1_{u\le U,v\le V}$$ belongs to the same completion class. By [\[thm:terminal-rectangle-expansion\]](TPC-073.md#ref-ba7c090a90c91fa8), $$\mathcal V_\square(F)=1.$$ The zero completion is much rougher. Put $L=\min(U,V)$. For every $2\le n\le L-1$, $$K^0(n,n)=K^0(n+1,n+1)=0,  \qquad  K^0(n+1,n)=K^0(n,n+1)=1,$$ and hence $$\Delta_{12}K^0(n,n)=-2.$$ Therefore $$\mathcal V_\square(K^0)\ge2(L-2).  \label{eq:primitive-mask-variation-lower}$$ The completion $F$ reduces the $d=1$ variation, but $F^{[d]}$ is nonzero for many $d$ and its diagonal tail need not vanish. This is the basic variation-tail trade-off.

> **Remark** The example does not show that the full cost $\mathcal C_D(F)$ is smaller than $\mathcal C_\square(K^0)$ at every scale. It shows only that zero completion is not variation-minimal, which is the precise claim needed to prevent a false optimization statement.

## Completion cost can be inflated arbitrarily

Take the zero primitive datum $k=0$, a prime $p\le\min(U,V)$, and $$F_A=Ae_{p,p}.$$ The point $(p,p)$ is nonprimitive, so $F_A\in\operatorname{Ext}_{U,V}(0)$ and the physical functional is zero. For $D=1$, the exact tail contains the term $d=p,x=y=1$, giving $$\mathcal T_1(F_A)\ge |A|.$$ Thus $$\mathcal C_1(F_A)\ge|A|$$ can be arbitrarily large while the physical datum and physical sum remain zero. A large cost for an arbitrary analytic completion is not a physical obstruction.

## Primitive support and tail freedom do not cancel

Let $(u_0,v_0)=1$ and $$K^0=ce_{u_0,v_0}.$$ It is primitive-supported and has zero diagonal tail, but $$\left|  \sum_{u,v}\mu(u)\mu(v)K^0(u,v)  \right|  =  |c|\,|\mu(u_0)\mu(v_0)|.$$ When $u_0,v_0$ are squarefree this equals the full $\ell^1$-mass $|c|$. Tail freedom is therefore an exact bookkeeping improvement, not an arithmetic cancellation theorem.

## Slice and anchor cancellation are different

In the ambient finite-kernel class, two formal $q$-slices $F$ and $-F$ have zero post-$q$-aggregation kernel although the sum of the two separate costs is positive. Likewise, an abstract anchor-compatible model may have $$\varepsilon_{\mathfrak a_1}K_{\mathfrak a_1}^{0}=F,  \qquad  \varepsilon_{\mathfrak a_2}K_{\mathfrak a_2}^{0}=-F.$$ Then $K_{\rm tr}^{0}=0$ and the scalar trace certificate vanishes, while the anchor-labelled $\ell^1$ cost is twice the cost of $F$. Consequently: $$\begin{aligned}  \text{separate \(q\)-cost}  &\not\equiv  \text{post-\(q\)-aggregation cost},\\  \text{scalar trace cancellation}  &\not\equiv  \text{anchorwise or row-local control}.\end{aligned}$$

## A vanishing mixed carrier does not control the erasure operator

The abstract example $$R=0,  \qquad  E=  \begin{pmatrix}  0&1\\  1&0  \end{pmatrix}$$ has $$\operatorname{tr}(ER)=0,  \qquad  \|E\|=1.$$ In this abstract model the mixed carrier $ER$ vanishes identically, while the erasure operator is nonzero. No scalar completion theorem can remove the need for a genuine two-corner, row-local, or complete-moment operator carrier.

## Quantifier-adaptive completion is invalid

For each fixed primitive datum, finite-dimensional optimization can choose a completion with small cost. A coefficient-uniform operator theorem requires a single declared completion rule valid for the entire physical coefficient class. Choosing a different completion after the maximizing coefficient vector or row is known reverses the max–min order. The pointwise infimum in [\[eq:completion-gauge\]](TPC-073.md#ref-f0da0243deee23ab) is therefore an analytic benchmark; it is not automatically a uniform L2 theorem.

# The reduced physical gate

The canonical physical branch now has no diagonal-cutoff choice. Its remaining trace-level problem is a weighted plaquette estimate. From this point, let $X\to\infty$ through one declared physical family. The active rows, anchors, kernels, and reference scales all depend on $X$; the index is suppressed, and every $o(\,\cdot\,)$ statement is with respect to this limit.

## Reference masses

###### ref-7448e988f762b7bc

For one anchor define the exact post-$q$-aggregation mass $$\mathfrak W_{\mathfrak a}  =  \sum_{u,v\ge1}|K_{\mathfrak a}^{0}(u,v)|.  \label{eq:anchored-physical-mass}$$ Absolute values occur after the exact $q$-sum. This mass is neither the sum of pre-aggregation ray masses nor automatically the raw global Gram mass. Let $\mathfrak A_{\rm tr}>0$ be one explicitly declared physical reference scale for the complete first mixed trace.

###### ref-23aacddeb4b3e2b4

###### ref-6dd0a5ee8cdd5c88

###### ref-4c83a17f7a794a80

> **Proposition: Canonical physical closure criterion** \[prop:canonical-physical-criterion\] If $$\sum_{m<n}\sum_{\mathfrak a\in\mathcal R_{m,n}}  \mathcal C_\square(K_{\mathfrak a}^{0})  =  o(\mathfrak A_{\rm tr}),  \label{eq:global-weighted-plaquette-target}$$ then $$\operatorname{tr}(ER)=o(\mathfrak A_{\rm tr}).  \label{eq:trace-saving}$$ The conclusion remains valid if the left side of [\[eq:global-weighted-plaquette-target\]](TPC-073.md#ref-6dd0a5ee8cdd5c88) is replaced by the sharper $\mathcal C_\square(K_{\rm tr}^{0})$.

> **Proof** Use [\[eq:global-trace-certificate\]](TPC-073.md#ref-819c162962879929).

This is an exact sufficient condition, not a theorem that the physical cost is small. It allows a nonuniform anchor family because the quantifier is global and the total cost is explicit.

## Completion route

###### ref-06fcd25cc443031e

For each anchor choose a declared analytic completion $$F_{\mathfrak a}  \in  \operatorname{Ext}_{U_{\mathfrak a},V_{\mathfrak a}}(k_{\mathfrak a})$$ before any later operator extremizer is selected. The general sufficient condition is $$\sum_{m<n}\sum_{\mathfrak a\in\mathcal R_{m,n}}  \mathcal C_{D_{\mathfrak a}}(F_{\mathfrak a})  =  o(\mathfrak A_{\rm tr}).  \label{eq:completion-physical-target}$$ Here variation and diagonal tail refer to the same completion. A future physical construction may, for example, move a common coordinate factor into an existing $q$-label and fill only those nonprimitive sites compatible with literal content data. An arbitrary smooth interpolation is an L0 analytic device until its coefficient norm, support, and quantifier dependence are controlled.

## Exact stop rules

The route has the following logically different outcomes.

1.  If [\[eq:global-weighted-plaquette-target\]](TPC-073.md#ref-6dd0a5ee8cdd5c88) fails by a proved lower obstruction on a nonnegligible physical family, only the canonical tail-free certificate is closed.

2.  To close the full completion route, one needs a lower obstruction for every declared admissible completion rule, or for the infimum in [\[eq:completion-gauge\]](TPC-073.md#ref-f0da0243deee23ab), at the same physical mass scale.

3.  Failure of an ambient tail estimate closes only that ambient specialization. The auxiliary diagonal tail of the canonical zero extension is already zero.

4.  A large zero-completion variation does not prove that the original signed sum is large. Literal complex phases, a structured completion, or a different signed sector may still cancel.

5.  A completed estimate for $\operatorname{tr}(ER)$ remains trace-level. It does not imply an entrywise erasure bound, a row star, a Perron estimate, or an operator norm.

6.  The geometric short-ray mass, physical endpoint cells, represented lower frame, missing-mass comparison, nuisance shorting, and final boundary reconstruction are untouched.

## Exponent interpretation

The classical prime number theorem gives $M(x)=o(x)$ [`\citep{IwaniecKowalski2004}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-073.md#cite-49dfea87eaada156). It does not by itself provide the fixed polynomial saving required by the strict endpoint ledger. For a coarse anchorwise estimate, one would need quantitative control of $$\mathfrak M(U_{\mathfrak a})  \mathfrak M(V_{\mathfrak a})  \mathcal V_\square(K_{\mathfrak a}^{0})$$ relative to the literal reference mass. Calling $M(x)=o(x)$ an endpoint power saving would be incorrect.

The next block will alternate among the independent branches rather than refine one branch indefinitely.

## Dynamic branching rules

The numbering describes dependencies, not guaranteed positive outcomes.

1.  If TPC–75 proves that every admissible completion has excessive physical cost, the Abel branch is recorded as a negative certificate theorem and the signed effort moves to direct entries, literal phases, or higher one-erasure polygons.

2.  If the direct-entry carrier closes in TPC–74, it has priority over anchor observability because it also sees orphan erasure edges. Otherwise TPC–76 retains the exact observable/orphan decomposition.

3.  A positive trace theorem never substitutes for TPC–79. Operator aggregation begins only after a two-corner, row-local, or complete-moment carrier exists.

4.  The activation and represented papers proceed independently of the primitive-plane branch. Failure of one branch does not supply a hypothesis on the other.

5.  TPC–81 attempts a merge only if all inputs use literal coefficients, the same fixed $h_0$, raw physical normalization, actual support, and one common uniform or distinguished coefficient scope.

6.  Any actual family forcing total fixed-power loss at least $1/400$ closes the present endpoint implication, including equality.

At every stage, success means a literal exponent improvement, closure of a necessary interface, or an exact actual-carrier obstruction. A numerical trend, a smooth surrogate, a fixed anchor, or an averaged shift is not reported as fixed-shift arithmetic progress.

# Claim ledger and conclusion

## Exact conclusions

The paper proves the following.

1.  The literal $q$-aggregated physical kernel has canonical support on the primitive lattice.

2.  Its support-faithful zero completion has no $d\ge2$ diagonal slice and no exact diagonal tail for any $D\ge1$.

3.  The anchored coefficient, scalar global trace kernel, and anchor-labelled kernel have exact ordinary $d=1$ Abel identities.

4.  The weighted plaquette cost gives the coefficient-faithful, location-sensitive canonical certificate and can be aggregated before or after retaining anchor labels.

5.  Mixed variation equals the $\ell^1$-mass of the unique terminal-rectangle coefficients, and diagonal downsampling is contractive.

6.  The physical signed functional is invariant under analytic completion at nonprimitive sites.

7.  The complete divisor-variation cost of one completion is a valid certificate; its infimum is an exact analytic benchmark.

8.  Canonical zero completion is tail-free but need not be variation-optimal.

## Statements not proved

The paper does not prove:

-   that $\mathcal C_\square(K_{\mathfrak a}^{0})$ is small relative to $\mathfrak W_{\mathfrak a}$;

-   that the global cost in [\[eq:global-weighted-plaquette-target\]](TPC-073.md#ref-6dd0a5ee8cdd5c88) is $o(\mathfrak A_{\rm tr})$;

-   a uniformly controlled low-cost analytic completion for the literal physical family;

-   a fixed-power Möbius saving for the physical first mixed trace;

-   a direct two-corner representation of $E_{m,n}$;

-   an entrywise, row-star, Perron, or operator-norm saving;

-   a represented lower frame, missing-mass estimate, or nuisance-angle estimate;

-   closure of geometric short rays, physical endpoints, or the final packet tail;

-   the strict endpoint inequality for one complete chain;

-   any result using a property special to $h_0=2$; or

-   a parity-breaking, prime-pair, or twin-prime theorem.

## Audit levels

| Result                                                  | Scope                                                    | Level   |
|:--------------------------------------------------------|:---------------------------------------------------------|:--------|
| Rectangle decomposition, downsampling, completion gauge | All finite plane kernels and declared completion classes | L0      |
| Primitive support and diagonal-tail collapse            | Literal $q$-canonical fixed-$h_0$ anchored kernel        | L1      |
| Global scalar and anchor-labelled trace identities      | Complete actual anchor family, trace scope only          | L1      |
| Mass-normalized weighted plaquette saving               | Required for the actual growing physical family          | Open L2 |
| Trace-to-operator promotion                             | Requires a new two-corner, row-local, or moment carrier  | Open L2 |

## Conclusion

The auxiliary diagonal tail isolated in TPC–71 and carried as an open certificate gate in TPC–72 is not present for the canonical primitive zero completion. That closure is exact and useful: it removes a cutoff, all growing-$d$ uniformity, and one possible source of endpoint loss. The result does not make the arithmetic easy. It concentrates the canonical route in one literal object, $$\sum_{x,y}|M(x)M(y)|  |\Delta_{12}K_{\mathfrak a}^{0}(x,y)|,$$ and exposes analytic completion as a coupled variation-tail optimization, not as free smoothing. A natural next question is whether primitive coordinates can be attached directly to the two-corner erasure entry. The plane and represented branches remain available in parallel if that door closes.
