## Abstract

Let $A_X(n)=A_{C_X}(n)$ be the actual signed normalized-determinant coefficient of the TPC-32 matched shell at one fixed nonzero shift $h_0$, with $$C_X=\lfloor J_X\rfloor,\qquad  N_{0,X}=J_XQ_X^2, \qquad  Q_X=X^{267/400+o(1)},\quad  J_X=X^{133/400+o(1)}.$$ Write $$S_X=\#\operatorname{supp}A_X,\quad  L_X=\|A_X\|_\infty,\quad  M_X=\|A_X\|_1,\quad  D_X=\|A_X\|_2^2.$$ We prove the sharp finite-sequence inequalities $$\frac{M_X^2}{S_X}\le D_X\le L_XM_X.$$ The verified physical support and singleton bounds of TPC-32 give $$S_X\ll Q_X,\qquad  L_X\ll X^{o(1)}\frac{N_{0,X}}{Q_X}.$$ It follows that $$M_X\ge X^{-\mu+o(1)}N_{0,X}  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad  D_X\ge X^{-2\mu+o(1)}  \frac{N_{0,X}^2}{Q_X},$$ whereas $$D_X\ge X^{-\lambda+o(1)}  \frac{N_{0,X}^2}{Q_X}  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad  M_X\ge X^{-\lambda+o(1)}N_{0,X}.$$ Thus subpower survival of the natural determinant energy is equivalent to subpower survival of the actual post-bin $\ell^1$ mass. The same energy hypothesis forces $$S_X\ge X^{-\lambda-o(1)}Q_X.$$ The mass-to-energy exponent is sharp, and the reverse mass and support exponents are sharp in the nontrivial range $X^{-\lambda}Q_X\to\infty$, which contains the physical endpoint range $\lambda<1/400$.

The word post-bin is essential. A raw atomic mass, a pre-bin absolute mass, or the absolute matched majorant may upper-bound $M_X$, but none can lower-bound it: opposite atoms in one determinant fiber can cancel completely. The result therefore closes an exact $\mathrm{L1}$ equivalence between two literal statistics; it does not prove that either statistic survives at its natural scale, and it gives no zero-frequency cancellation estimate. In particular, it proves no $\mathrm{L2}$ arithmetic input, parity result, prime-pair lower bound, or twin-prime conclusion.

# Introduction

The TPC-81 transfer architecture isolates the determinant energy $$D_X=\sum_n|A_{C_X}(n)|^2$$ as one of the two remaining arithmetic statistics in the matched fixed-shift route [`\citep{WangTPC81}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-083.md#cite-ff65f956e7cf0d6d). TPC-82 then records the zero-slack relation between energy survival and distinguished zero-mode cancellation [`\citep{WangTPC82}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-083.md#cite-cfeb22890911c08a). A lower bound for $D_X$ is not presently known at the natural scale. Before returning to the more elaborate pre-bin completion machinery, it is useful to ask a strictly post-bin question:

> How much of the determinant energy problem is already equivalent to survival of the actual post-bin $\ell^1$ mass?

For the literal coefficient, the answer is exact and asymmetric. A lower $\ell^1$ mass with loss $\mu$ yields an energy lower bound with loss $2\mu$. Conversely, an energy lower bound with loss $\lambda$ yields an $\ell^1$ lower bound with the same loss $\lambda$. At subpower resolution, the two survival statements are equivalent. This uses only two physical upper bounds already proved in TPC-32: the determinant support has size $O(Q_X)$, and a single determinant bin has amplitude at most $X^{o(1)}N_{0,X}/Q_X$ [`\citep{WangTPC32}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-083.md#cite-49ad82103eb75eb7).

The result is elementary, but it closes a useful interface. It replaces the broad question “does the energy survive binning?” by the equivalent subpower question “does a natural amount of $\ell^1$ mass survive after binning?” This is not a replacement of arithmetic by norm interpolation. TPC-32 supplies only the upper bound $$M_X\ll X^{o(1)}N_{0,X};$$ the matching lower bound remains open. Moreover, $M_X$ is formed after all literal atoms with the same determinant have been summed. A large raw absolute mass gives no such lower bound.

The paper has five main conclusions.

1.  We prove the sharp universal inequalities $$M^2/S\le D\le LM$$ for a finite complex sequence, including all equality cases.

2.  We insert the actual TPC-32 support and singleton bounds at $C_X=\lfloor J_X\rfloor$, obtaining the two-sided exponent transfer.

3.  We prove that natural determinant energy forces a quantitatively large actual support. Thus energy cannot survive at its natural scale on a subpower-small fraction of the available determinant range.

4.  We give extremizing model sequences showing that the factors $2\mu$, $\lambda$, and the support exponent cannot be improved from the stated data.

5.  We separate the actual post-bin mass from every pre-bin or raw absolute majorant and give exact cancellation counterexamples.

The distinction from the distinguished zero frequency is equally important. The mass $$M_X=\sum_n|A_X(n)|$$ forgets the phases between different determinant bins. The zero mode $$Z_X=\sum_n A_X(n)$$ depends on those phases. Two sequences can have identical $(S,L,M,D)$ and opposite zero-mode behavior. Therefore the present equivalence advances the energy side only; it neither proves nor models zero-frequency cancellation.

All physical statements keep one prescribed $h_0\ne0$, the actual coefficient $A_{C_X}$, the complete matched aggregation, and the global TPC-32 normalization. No assertion is specialized to $h_0=2$.

> **Proposition: Verified TPC-32 envelope** \[prop:tpc32-envelope\] For the literal coefficient in [\[eq:literal-A\]](TPC-083.md#ref-88a096bbe46ac23c), $$\begin{aligned}  S_X&\ll Q_X,\label{eq:support-upper}\\  M_X&\ll X^{o(1)}N_{0,X},\label{eq:mass-upper}\\  L_X&\ll  X^{o(1)}\frac{N_{0,X}}{Q_X},\label{eq:atom-upper}\\  D_X&\ll  X^{o(1)}\frac{N_{0,X}^2}{Q_X}. \label{eq:energy-upper}\end{aligned}$$

> **Proof** TPC-32 proves that the determinant support is contained in a fixed multiple of $[ -Q_X,Q_X]\cap(\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\})$, giving [\[eq:support-upper\]](TPC-083.md#ref-6f1e6a2b5a9582fd). It also constructs a post-fiber absolute majorant $A_{C_X}^+$ with $$\sum_n A_{C_X}^+(n)\ll X^{o(1)}N_{0,X}$$ and $$\max_n A_{C_X}^+(n)  \ll X^{o(1)}Q_X(C_X+J_X).$$ Since $C_X\le J_X$ and $N_{0,X}/Q_X=J_XQ_X$, the second estimate is $$\max_n A_{C_X}^+(n)  \ll X^{o(1)}\frac{N_{0,X}}{Q_X}.$$ The literal inequalities $$|A_X(n)|\le A_{C_X}^+(n)$$ give [\[eq:mass-upper\]](TPC-083.md#ref-7ecb409774fd5942) and [\[eq:atom-upper\]](TPC-083.md#ref-7f760c7563f33363). Finally, $$D_X\le L_XM_X$$ gives [\[eq:energy-upper\]](TPC-083.md#ref-29a407fd38fd120f); this is also the energy estimate stated in TPC-32 [`\citep{WangTPC32}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-083.md#cite-49ad82103eb75eb7).

# A sharp finite-sequence theorem

The algebra is independent of arithmetic and is stated over $\mathbb C$.

###### ref-0109fe21d2277528

###### ref-38b057ae322c7890

###### ref-7bc1d0608497a14f

> **Theorem: Mass–energy sandwich** \[thm:mass-energy-sandwich\] Let $a=(a_n)$ be a nonzero finitely supported complex sequence and write $$S=\#\operatorname{supp}a,\qquad  L=\|a\|_\infty,\qquad  M=\|a\|_1,\qquad  D=\|a\|_2^2.$$ Then $$\boxed{  \frac{M^2}{S}\le D\le LM.} \label{eq:mass-energy-sandwich}$$ Equivalently, $$M\ge\frac{D}{L},  \qquad  S\ge\frac{D}{L^2}. \label{eq:reverse-sandwich}$$ Equality holds in either inequality of [\[eq:mass-energy-sandwich\]](TPC-083.md#ref-38b057ae322c7890) if and only if $|a_n|$ is constant on $\operatorname{supp}a$. In that case both inequalities are equalities.

> **Proof** Cauchy–Schwarz on the exact support gives $$M^2  =\left(\sum_{n\in\operatorname{supp}a}|a_n|\right)^2  \le  S\sum_{n\in\operatorname{supp}a}|a_n|^2  =SD.$$ This is the first inequality. For the second, $$D=\sum_n|a_n|^2  \le L\sum_n|a_n|  =LM.$$ The first formula in [\[eq:reverse-sandwich\]](TPC-083.md#ref-7bc1d0608497a14f) is a rearrangement of $D\le LM$. Also $$D=\sum_{n\in\operatorname{supp}a}|a_n|^2\le SL^2,$$ which gives the second formula.
>
> Equality in Cauchy–Schwarz occurs exactly when the vector $(|a_n|)_{n\in\operatorname{supp}a}$ is proportional to the all-ones vector. Equality in $D\le LM$ occurs exactly when each nonzero $|a_n|$ equals $L$. These are the same constant-modulus condition. This is the classical equality case of the elementary norm inequalities [`\citep{HardyLittlewoodPolya1952}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-083.md#cite-375fda39f3e33f31).

> **Remark: Phases are unrestricted** The theorem uses only the moduli of the post-bin entries. The phases of the nonzero $a_n$ can be arbitrary. Consequently equality in [\[eq:mass-energy-sandwich\]](TPC-083.md#ref-38b057ae322c7890) says nothing about the zero mode $\sum_na_n$.

###### ref-73b9e5abda120208

> **Corollary: Effective support** \[cor:effective-support\] The quantity $$S_{\rm eff}(a):=\frac{M^2}{D}$$ satisfies $$1\le S_{\rm eff}(a)\le S.$$ It equals $S$ precisely for constant-modulus support.

> **Proof** The upper bound is the first inequality of [\[eq:mass-energy-sandwich\]](TPC-083.md#ref-38b057ae322c7890). The lower bound follows from $M^2\ge D$, since $$\left(\sum_n|a_n|\right)^2  \ge\sum_n|a_n|^2.$$ The equality characterization follows from [\[thm:mass-energy-sandwich\]](TPC-083.md#ref-0109fe21d2277528).

> **Theorem: Two-sided post-bin exponent transfer** \[thm:two-sided-transfer\] Let $A_X=A_{C_X}$ be the fixed-$h_0$ matched coefficient of [2](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-083.md#src-9223d5a1267e46ec), and assume the verified envelope [\[prop:tpc32-envelope\]](TPC-083.md#ref-5c219b63844530fe).
>
> 1.  If, for some fixed $\mu\ge0$, $$M_X\ge X^{-\mu+o(1)}N_{0,X}, \label{eq:mass-lower-mu}$$ then $$\boxed{  D_X\ge  X^{-2\mu+o(1)}  \frac{N_{0,X}^2}{Q_X}.} \label{eq:energy-from-mass}$$
>
> 2.  If, for some fixed $\lambda\ge0$, $$D_X\ge  X^{-\lambda+o(1)}  \frac{N_{0,X}^2}{Q_X}, \label{eq:energy-lower-lambda}$$ then $$\boxed{  M_X\ge  X^{-\lambda+o(1)}N_{0,X}.} \label{eq:mass-from-energy}$$
>
> 3.  Under [\[eq:energy-lower-lambda\]](TPC-083.md#ref-009e3e1cc69a83c8), the exact determinant support satisfies $$\boxed{  S_X\ge X^{-\lambda-o(1)}Q_X.} \label{eq:support-from-energy}$$

> **Proof** By [\[eq:support-upper\]](TPC-083.md#ref-6f1e6a2b5a9582fd) and the first half of [\[eq:mass-energy-sandwich\]](TPC-083.md#ref-38b057ae322c7890), $$D_X\ge\frac{M_X^2}{S_X}  \gg  X^{-2\mu+o(1)}  \frac{N_{0,X}^2}{Q_X},$$ which proves (i), after absorbing fixed constants into $X^{o(1)}$.
>
> For (ii), use $D_X\le L_XM_X$ and [\[eq:atom-upper\]](TPC-083.md#ref-7f760c7563f33363): $$M_X  \ge\frac{D_X}{L_X}  \gg  \frac{  X^{-\lambda+o(1)}N_{0,X}^2/Q_X  }{  X^{o(1)}N_{0,X}/Q_X  }  =  X^{-\lambda+o(1)}N_{0,X}.$$
>
> Finally, $D_X\le S_XL_X^2$. Hence $$S_X  \ge\frac{D_X}{L_X^2}  \gg  \frac{  X^{-\lambda+o(1)}N_{0,X}^2/Q_X  }{  X^{o(1)}N_{0,X}^2/Q_X^2  }  =  X^{-\lambda-o(1)}Q_X.$$ This proves (iii).

> **Definition: Subpower natural survival** \[def:subpower-survival\] We say that the post-bin mass survives at its natural scale if $$M_X\ge X^{-o(1)}N_{0,X}. \label{eq:natural-mass}$$ We say that the determinant energy survives at its natural scale if $$D_X\ge  X^{-o(1)}\frac{N_{0,X}^2}{Q_X}. \label{eq:natural-energy}$$ These are lower-bound statements. Their matching upper bounds are already contained in [\[prop:tpc32-envelope\]](TPC-083.md#ref-5c219b63844530fe).

> **Corollary: Subpower equivalence** \[cor:subpower-equivalence\] For the literal matched coefficient, $$\boxed{  M_X\ge X^{-o(1)}N_{0,X}  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad  D_X\ge  X^{-o(1)}\frac{N_{0,X}^2}{Q_X}.} \label{eq:subpower-equivalence}$$

> **Proof** If $M_X\ge X^{-o(1)}N_{0,X}$, then $$D_X\ge\frac{M_X^2}{S_X}  \ge X^{-o(1)}\frac{N_{0,X}^2}{Q_X}$$ by [\[eq:support-upper\]](TPC-083.md#ref-6f1e6a2b5a9582fd). > **Corollary: Natural energy forces broad actual support** \[cor:support-forced\] If [\[eq:natural-energy\]](TPC-083.md#ref-9c51098c2db17ebb) holds, then $$S_X\ge X^{-o(1)}Q_X.$$ Together with $S_X\ll Q_X$, the actual support then occupies the available determinant scale up to subpower loss.

> **Proof** Repeat the proof of part (iii) of [\[thm:two-sided-transfer\]](TPC-083.md#ref-0d0d4b889160d26d) with the subpower lower bound [\[eq:natural-energy\]](TPC-083.md#ref-9c51098c2db17ebb).

> **Remark: The asymmetry is real** A mass loss $\mu$ may be spread evenly across $Q_X$ bins, so the energy loss doubles to $2\mu$. In the reverse direction, energy can be carried by only $X^{-\lambda}Q_X$ bins at the maximal allowed amplitude, giving mass loss $\lambda$. The model sequences in [5](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-083.md#src-89146472713240e0) make both mechanisms exact.

# Sharpness at every exponent

The constant-modulus equality cases of [\[thm:mass-energy-sandwich\]](TPC-083.md#ref-0109fe21d2277528) already show sharpness in finite dimension. We now match the arithmetic normalization and show that the exponent transfers cannot be improved from only $$S_X\ll Q_X,\qquad  L_X\ll X^{o(1)}N_{0,X}/Q_X.$$ Rounding an integer support size changes only fixed or subpower factors and will be suppressed.

###### ref-2d83d0f78cd56cfc

> **Proposition: Sharpness of mass-to-energy loss** \[prop:sharp-mass-energy\] Fix $\mu\ge0$. There are finite sequences with $$S_X\asymp Q_X,\qquad  L_X\le\frac{N_{0,X}}{Q_X},$$ for which $$M_X=X^{-\mu}N_{0,X}, \qquad  D_X\asymp  X^{-2\mu}\frac{N_{0,X}^2}{Q_X}.$$ Thus the exponent $2\mu$ in [\[eq:energy-from-mass\]](TPC-083.md#ref-abf41dc501e221ed) is best possible.

> **Proof** Choose $S_X=\lceil Q_X\rceil$ nonzero coordinates and give each coordinate modulus $$t_X=\frac{X^{-\mu}N_{0,X}}{S_X}.$$ Then $$M_X=S_Xt_X=X^{-\mu}N_{0,X}$$ and $$D_X=S_Xt_X^2 =\frac{X^{-2\mu}N_{0,X}^2}{S_X} \asymp X^{-2\mu}\frac{N_{0,X}^2}{Q_X}.$$ Also $t_X\ll N_{0,X}/Q_X$. Arbitrary unit phases may be attached to the coordinates.

###### ref-526182a309cba533

###### ref-6676a4531913171e

###### ref-4c9933bbf8eb5cdf

> **Proposition: Sharpness of energy-to-mass and support losses** \[prop:sharp-energy-mass\] Fix $\lambda\ge0$ in a range for which $X^{-\lambda}Q_X\to\infty$. There are finite sequences satisfying $$L_X=\frac{N_{0,X}}{Q_X}$$ and $$\begin{aligned}  S_X&\asymp X^{-\lambda}Q_X,\label{eq:sharp-support}\\  M_X&\asymp X^{-\lambda}N_{0,X},\label{eq:sharp-mass}\\  D_X&\asymp  X^{-\lambda}\frac{N_{0,X}^2}{Q_X}. \label{eq:sharp-energy}\end{aligned}$$ Consequently, neither the mass exponent $\lambda$ nor the support exponent $\lambda$ in [\[thm:two-sided-transfer\]](TPC-083.md#ref-0d0d4b889160d26d) can be improved from the stated envelope.

> **Proof** Choose $$S_X=\lfloor X^{-\lambda}Q_X\rfloor$$ coordinates, all of modulus $N_{0,X}/Q_X$. Then [\[eq:sharp-support\]](TPC-083.md#ref-526182a309cba533)–[\[eq:sharp-energy\]](TPC-083.md#ref-4c9933bbf8eb5cdf) follow by direct calculation. This sequence has constant modulus on its support and therefore attains equality on both sides of [\[eq:mass-energy-sandwich\]](TPC-083.md#ref-38b057ae322c7890).

> **Remark: Very sparse range** If $X^{-\lambda}Q_X$ is bounded, the lower support conclusion is at most a constant-scale statement. The theorem remains valid, but there is no stronger power-scale support conclusion to extract from the envelope alone.

Let $M=1$ and spread this mass uniformly over $K$ coordinates. Then $D=1/K$. Allowing $K$ to exceed the physical $O(Q_X)$ determinant range would make energy arbitrarily smaller than the scale in [\[eq:energy-from-mass\]](TPC-083.md#ref-abf41dc501e221ed). Thus the first transfer uses the verified physical support interval, not merely finiteness.

A one-point sequence of amplitude $T$ has $$D=T^2,\qquad M=T,\qquad L=T.$$ Without an upper bound for $L$ in the physical normalization, an energy lower bound does not determine the normalized mass scale $N_{0,X}$. The reverse transfer therefore genuinely uses the TPC-32 singleton estimate.

# Why raw and pre-bin masses cannot be substituted

###### ref-91aca472f9b6ae66

The distinction between a post-bin coefficient and an atomic majorant is exact. Let $\mathcal Y_X$ be a native pre-bin key set, let $\nu_X:\mathcal Y_X\to\mathbb Z$ be the determinant label, and write the literal atomic contribution as $b_X(y)$. In the simplest unit-multiplier model, $$A_X(n)=  \sum_{\substack{y\in\mathcal Y_X\\\nu_X(y)=n}}b_X(y). \label{eq:atomic-binning}$$ The same discussion applies with archived multipliers absorbed into $b_X(y)$.

###### ref-5d5bd67d4f30ac67

###### ref-6e029f9385613add

Define the fiberwise absolute majorant and raw absolute mass by $$A_X^+(n)=  \sum_{\substack{y\in\mathcal Y_X\\\nu_X(y)=n}}|b_X(y)|,  \qquad  M_{{\rm raw},X}=\sum_{y\in\mathcal Y_X}|b_X(y)|. \label{eq:raw-majorant}$$ Then $$M_X=\sum_n|A_X(n)|  \le\sum_nA_X^+(n)  =M_{{\rm raw},X}. \label{eq:one-way-raw}$$

###### ref-79d31467606d2500

> **Proposition: No reverse raw-mass inequality** \[prop:no-raw-reverse\] There is no positive universal constant $c$ such that $$M_X\ge c\,M_{{\rm raw},X}$$ for all literal determinant aggregations, even when the raw support, raw absolute values, determinant fiber, and global normalization are fixed.

> **Proof** Put two atoms in the same determinant fiber and take their contributions to be $1$ and $-1$. Then $$M_{{\rm raw},X}=2,\qquad A_X(n)=0,\qquad M_X=D_X=0.$$ Scaling both atoms makes the raw mass arbitrarily large while the post-bin coefficient remains zero.

###### ref-ad975e064969abde

> **Corollary: Majorants give upper bounds only** \[cor:majorant-upper-only\] The two TPC-32 majorant statistics $$\sum_nA_{C_X}^+(n)  \quad\text{and}\quad  \max_nA_{C_X}^+(n)$$ validly imply upper bounds for $M_X$ and $L_X$. They do not imply a lower bound for $M_X$, $D_X$, or $S_X$.

> **Proof** The upper implications follow from $|A_X(n)|\le A_{C_X}^+(n)$. The counterexample in [\[prop:no-raw-reverse\]](TPC-083.md#ref-79d31467606d2500) rules out every positive reverse implication.

> **Remark: No channelwise renormalization** The cancellation in [\[prop:no-raw-reverse\]](TPC-083.md#ref-79d31467606d2500) occurs before the post-bin statistics are measured. Renormalizing each atom, channel, triplet, or component separately would erase that cancellation and change the coefficient. The only allowed $M_X$ is the norm of the already aggregated $A_X$ in the original global normalization.

## Mass and energy do not control the zero mode

Let $$Z_X=\sum_nA_X(n)$$ be the distinguished zero-frequency coefficient.

###### ref-83db393d3506c8ce

> **Proposition: Identical norm data, different zero modes** \[prop:norms-not-zero-mode\] For every even $S$ and every $L>0$, there are two sequences with identical $(S,L,M,D)$ but zero modes of magnitudes $SL$ and $0$, respectively.

> **Proof** On a support of size $S$, take $$a_n=L$$ for every coordinate and take $$b_n=(-1)^nL.$$ Both sequences satisfy $$\#\operatorname{supp}=S,\qquad  \|\cdot\|_\infty=L,\qquad  \|\cdot\|_1=SL,\qquad  \|\cdot\|_2^2=SL^2.$$ The first zero mode is $SL$, while the second is zero.

> **Remark: No flatness theorem** shows that even exact knowledge of all four post-bin statistics in [\[eq:SLMD\]](TPC-083.md#ref-e30de0a6e01c9591) does not prove the TPC-32 distinguished-frequency flatness condition. The current paper concerns energy survival, not cancellation among determinant bins.

## Observation versus premise

Numerically observing $M_X$ or $D_X$ on a finite cutoff is useful for locating possible loss. It is not a growing-$X$ lower bound. Likewise, an exact upper bound for the raw majorant is not evidence that the post-bin mass is close to that upper bound. A valid arithmetic input must be stated directly for $M_X$ or $D_X$ with the fixed $h_0$, the literal coefficient, and the original quantifiers.

# The exponent and endpoint audit

## What exponent enters the energy ledger

###### ref-d007521e56d778b4

Suppose a future argument proves $$M_X\ge X^{-\mu+o(1)}N_{0,X}.$$ certifies the determinant-energy loss $$\lambda_D\le2\mu \label{eq:lambda-from-mu}$$ in the notation $$D_X\ge  X^{-\lambda_D+o(1)}  \frac{N_{0,X}^2}{Q_X}.$$ This is a one-way certified upper bound on the loss exponent; the actual energy may be larger. Conversely, an independently proved energy loss $\lambda_D$ certifies a post-bin mass loss at most $\lambda_D$.

###### ref-7abc8d667085bf5a

The TPC-82 zero-slack ledger compares this energy loss with a distinguished-zero-mode gain $\eta_Z$: $$2\eta_Z\ge\lambda_D. \label{eq:zero-slack-import}$$ Thus, if the mass route is the only energy input and there are no additional energy losses, the sufficient compatibility condition is $$\eta_Z\ge\mu.$$ If an auxiliary squared-energy loss $\lambda_{\rm aux}$ is present, the recorded condition becomes $$2\eta_Z\ge2\mu+\lambda_{\rm aux}.$$ The present paper proves none of these lower or cancellation hypotheses; it only identifies their exact transfer.

## The strict physical loss rule

###### ref-17534c5cce5947a5

The $1/400$ appearing at the high-row TPC-32 flatness endpoint is not an allowance that may be reused for mass, energy, dictionary, or cover losses. In the full physical comparison, every fixed-power loss must be derived in the same literal carrier and counted once. Writing their complete sum as $\Lambda_{\rm phys}$, the operational rule remains $$\boxed{  \Lambda_{\rm phys}<\frac1{400}  \quad\text{to continue};\qquad  \Lambda_{\rm phys}\ge\frac1{400}  \quad\text{to stop}.} \label{eq:strict-loss-boundary}$$ Equality is in the stopping case. An open term cannot be assigned zero loss, and [\[eq:lambda-from-mu\]](TPC-083.md#ref-d007521e56d778b4) cannot be counted twice under both the mass and energy names.

## The L0/L1/L2 ledger

###### ref-ab011af435f580e1

> **Definition: Stage labels** \[def:stage-labels\]
>
> 1.  $\mathrm{L0}$ is finite algebra independent of the physical carrier.
>
> 2.  $\mathrm{L1}$ is an exact interface retaining the fixed nonzero $h_0$, literal coefficient, complete native aggregation, actual support, global normalization, and original quantifiers.
>
> 3.  $\mathrm{L2}$ is a new asymptotic arithmetic estimate for that literal growing carrier, strong enough to reduce a positive exponent in the physical ledger.

###### ref-997d517b4257a760

> **Proposition: Stage classification of this paper** \[prop:stage-classification\] is an exact $\mathrm{L0}$ theorem. form an exact $\mathrm{L1}$ equivalence for the actual TPC-32 matched coefficient. This paper proves no unconditional $\mathrm{L2}$ estimate.

> **Proof** The finite theorem uses no arithmetic. The transfer inserts the verified literal support and singleton bounds while preserving every item in [\[def:stage-labels\]](TPC-083.md#ref-ab011af435f580e1)(2). However, neither [\[eq:mass-lower-mu\]](TPC-083.md#ref-e67d4da987b4b0c7) nor [\[eq:energy-lower-lambda\]](TPC-083.md#ref-009e3e1cc69a83c8) is proved here for any growing physical packet. Hence no new asymptotic lower bound is available to enter the $\mathrm{L2}$ ledger.

> **Remark: What would count as the next advance** A proof of $$M_X\ge X^{-o(1)}N_{0,X}$$ for the actual fixed-$h_0$ coefficient would, by [\[cor:subpower-equivalence\]](TPC-083.md#ref-14d6be8c31b046bf), close the natural determinant-energy gate. A lower bound for a raw majorant, an averaged shift, a selected subsequence of cutoffs, or a renormalized component would not.

# Final claim boundary

###### ref-de3cda5cb76e75e9

###### ref-62cee2d0cdeb7f51

###### ref-552ce1013cfd8f55

> **Theorem: Post-bin mass–energy equivalence** \[thm:final-equivalence\] For the actual TPC-32 coefficient $$A_X=A_{\lfloor J_X\rfloor}$$ at one fixed nonzero $h_0$, with $N_{0,X}=J_XQ_X^2$, the following are equivalent: $$\begin{aligned}  M_X&\ge X^{-o(1)}N_{0,X}, \label{eq:final-mass}\\  D_X&\ge  X^{-o(1)}\frac{N_{0,X}^2}{Q_X}. \label{eq:final-energy}\end{aligned}$$ More generally, a mass loss $\mu$ certifies energy loss $2\mu$, and an energy loss $\lambda$ certifies mass loss $\lambda$. The energy hypothesis also forces $$S_X\ge X^{-\lambda-o(1)}Q_X.$$ The mass-to-energy exponent is sharp under the verified support envelope. The reverse mass and support exponents are sharp whenever $X^{-\lambda}Q_X\to\infty$, in particular throughout the relevant endpoint range $\lambda<1/400$.

> **Proof** This is \[[thm:two-sided-transfer](TPC-083.md#ref-0d0d4b889160d26d),[cor:subpower-equivalence](TPC-083.md#ref-14d6be8c31b046bf)\] together with the extremizers of \[[prop:sharp-mass-energy](TPC-083.md#ref-2d83d0f78cd56cfc),[prop:sharp-energy-mass](TPC-083.md#ref-0670224f84230e51)\].

The theorem reduces one literal energy question to one literal post-bin mass question. It does not estimate either quantity from below. In particular, the TPC-32 absolute majorant and every pre-bin/raw absolute mass remain upper data only.

#### Explicit exclusions.

This paper proves no lower bound for $M_X$ or $D_X$, no estimate for the distinguished zero mode $Z_X$, no fixed-shift Möbius cancellation theorem, and no result specialized to $h_0=2$. It does not prove a breach of sieve parity, a Hardy–Littlewood asymptotic, a prime-pair lower bound, or the twin-prime conjecture. Its complete contribution is the sharp $\mathrm{L1}$ equivalence and its stop rules.

#### Next gate.

The next positive task is to prove the following lower bound directly for the literal post-bin mass: $$M_X\ge X^{-o(1)}N_{0,X}.$$ The alternative is an exact obstruction showing that the physical fiber geometry permits a fixed-power or complete post-bin mass loss. Either outcome must be stated for the same $h_0$, actual support, and global normalization. The full-route loss remains subject to [\[eq:strict-loss-boundary\]](TPC-083.md#ref-17534c5cce5947a5).
