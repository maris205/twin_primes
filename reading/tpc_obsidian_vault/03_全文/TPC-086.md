## Abstract

Let $A:\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z\to\mathbb C$ have support $S$, let $D=\sum_n|A(n)|^2$, and let $Z=\widehat A(0)=\sum_nA(n)$. When $A(0)=0$, Fourier inversion gives the exact identity $$Z=-\sum_{r\ne0}\widehat A(r).$$ Thus the distinguished zero mode can be recovered from the nonzero frequencies, but only as a coherent signed reassembly. We prove a stronger support-aware theorem. If $K=(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)\setminus S$, then every weight $w$ supported on $K$ with $\sum_Kw=1$ gives $$Z=-\sum_{r\ne0}m_w(r)\widehat A(r),\qquad  m_w(r)=\sum_{k\in K}w(k)\mathrm e^{2\pi i rk/q}.$$ The unique minimum-$\ell^2$ kernel among zero-set averages is obtained by taking $w$ uniform on $K$. Its squared norm is $$\sum_{r\ne0}|m_K(r)|^2=\frac{|S|}{q-|S|},$$ and Parseval then recovers exactly the sharp physical inequality $$|Z|^2\le |S|D.$$ Positive-density overpadding gives more. A fixed $k$-fold interval filter supported in the zero arc has Wiener norm $O_k(1)$ and multiplier decay $$|m_k(r)|\ll_k d_q(r,0)^{-k}.$$ For the literal TPC-32 packet this yields, unconditionally, $$|Z_X|\ll_k  \max_{0<d_q(r,0)\le R}|\widehat A_X(r)|  +  X^{o(1)}N_{0,X}R^{1/2-k}.$$ At the critical row, the triangular case $k=2$ reduces the full $q_X=X^{267/400+o(1)}$ frequency problem to the low window $R=X^{133/600+\varepsilon}$ for sufficiently small fixed $\varepsilon>0$. We also derive an exact multiplier-weighted exceptional-frequency budget and prove that mean-square dispersion alone cannot improve the sharp support bound. Applied at one fixed nonzero shift, the result corrects an overly strong “nonzero frequencies cannot see the zero mode” formulation: coherent nonzero-frequency control can close the zero-mode gate, but generic frequency estimates and unweighted exceptional-set counts by themselves cannot. The Fourier theorems are $\mathrm{L0}$, and their literal attachment is $\mathrm{L1}$. No new $\mathrm{L2}$ Möbius-correlation estimate is proved.

# Introduction

The high-row fixed-shift route isolates a literal determinant coefficient $$A_X(n)=A_{C_X}(n),\qquad  Z_X=\sum_nA_X(n),\qquad  D_X=\sum_n|A_X(n)|^2.$$ TPC-81 and TPC-82 record $D_X$ and $Z_X$ as the two remaining arithmetic statistics in the conditional flatness transfer [`\citep{WangTPC81,WangTPC82}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-086.md#cite-ff65f956e7cf0d6d); [\[WangTPC82\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-086.md#cite-cfeb22890911c08a). TPC-83 reduces natural determinant energy to survival of the actual post-bin $\ell^1$ mass [`\citep{WangTPC83}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-086.md#cite-c5e5f0238c876731). TPC-85 then audits several operations that do not manufacture cancellation: Fourier centering, determinant-fiber conditional expectation, and content inversion [`\citep{WangTPC85}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-086.md#cite-d8d9f98f6854badf).

There is, however, a subtle correction to a common exclusion. The physical TPC-32 Fourier modulus is chosen large enough that the determinant support embeds without wrap, and the literal determinant bin $n=0$ vanishes. Fourier inversion at that structural zero expresses $Z_X$ as a sum of all nonzero Fourier modes. Consequently a sufficiently coherent theorem for those modes can control $Z_X$. What fails is the unproved passage from generic, mean-square, or all-but-exceptional frequency information to that particular signed sum.

This paper makes the distinction exact. Its main results are:

1.  every zero coordinate gives an exact nonzero-frequency reconstruction of the zero mode;

2.  among kernels obtained by averaging structural zeros, the unique minimum-$\ell^2$ kernel averages them uniformly;

3.  the optimal kernel and Parseval recover the sharp support inequality $|Z|^2\le |S|D$, with no loss;

4.  positive-density zero-arc padding produces fixed-order smooth filters with bounded Wiener norm and polynomial frequency decay;

5.  the resulting tail estimate reduces the literal zero-mode problem to a mesoscopic low-frequency window;

6.  exceptional frequencies must be measured by their mass under the reconstruction multiplier, not merely by cardinality;

7.  total nonzero-frequency energy alone gives no improvement over the sharp support bound; and

8.  the literal TPC-32 packet satisfies all finite hypotheses, but none of the finite identities supplies new arithmetic cancellation.

The point is not to relabel the zero-mode problem as a Fourier problem. The exact identity instead says what a valid Fourier input must estimate. It also prevents two opposite errors: declaring that nonzero frequencies are logically irrelevant, and declaring that a generic dispersion estimate automatically controls their coherent sum.

An $\mathrm{L1}$ statement attaches that algebra to the literal fixed-$h_0$ carrier with complete native aggregation, actual support, and global normalization. # Exact reconstruction from structural zeros

###### ref-889da5bd3f1e330b

###### ref-e4902223e87fb174

Let $G=\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z$, where $q\ge2$, and write $$e_q(x)=\mathrm e^{2\pi i x/q}.$$ For $A:G\to\mathbb C$, use the unnormalized Fourier transform $$\widehat A(r)=\sum_{n\in G}A(n)e_q(-rn),  \qquad r\in G. \label{eq:dft}$$ Then $$A(n)=\frac1q\sum_{r\in G}\widehat A(r)e_q(rn), \qquad  \sum_{r\in G}|\widehat A(r)|^2  =q\sum_{n\in G}|A(n)|^2. \label{eq:inversion-parseval}$$ Put $$Z=\widehat A(0)=\sum_nA(n),\qquad  D=\sum_n|A(n)|^2.$$

###### ref-927a89830480f21f

###### ref-7910644fda724d17

###### ref-2da344475059621f

> **Proposition: One-zero coherent reconstruction** \[prop:one-zero\] If $A(k)=0$ for some $k\in G$, then $$\boxed{  Z=-\sum_{r\ne0}\widehat A(r)e_q(rk).} \label{eq:one-zero}$$ In particular, if $A(0)=0$, then $$Z=-\sum_{r\ne0}\widehat A(r). \label{eq:zero-coordinate}$$

> **Proof** Insert $n=k$ in Fourier inversion and multiply by $q$: $$0=Z+\sum_{r\ne0}\widehat A(r)e_q(rk).$$

> **Remark: What the identity does and does not say** proves that nonzero Fourier modes determine the zero mode once a structural zero is known. It does not bound the coherent sum on the right. A mean square, a pointwise estimate on generic $r$, or an exceptional-set count must still be combined with the exact phases $e_q(rk)$.

###### ref-352397d96c0f613c

###### ref-b7099c9d9a07da67

The single-zero identity is not optimal when $A$ has many zero coordinates. Let $$S=\operatorname{supp}A,\qquad s=|S|,\qquad K=G\setminus S,\qquad k=|K|=q-s.$$ Assume $K\ne\varnothing$. For a weight $w:K\to\mathbb C$ satisfying $$\sum_{x\in K}w(x)=1, \label{eq:weight-normalization}$$ define $$m_w(r)=\sum_{x\in K}w(x)e_q(rx). \label{eq:multiplier}$$

###### ref-0c824521a4c6813e

###### ref-af3096a227610d40

###### ref-ea06b5b9576eeebe

> **Theorem: Weighted zero-set reconstruction** \[thm:weighted-reconstruction\] For every $w$ satisfying [\[eq:weight-normalization\]](TPC-086.md#ref-352397d96c0f613c), $$\boxed{  Z=-\sum_{r\ne0}m_w(r)\widehat A(r).} \label{eq:weighted-reconstruction}$$ Moreover, $$\sum_{r\ne0}|m_w(r)|^2  =q\sum_{x\in K}|w(x)|^2-1. \label{eq:multiplier-parseval}$$

> **Proof** Multiply the inversion identity $A(x)=0$, $x\in K$, by $w(x)$ and sum over $K$. The $r=0$ term is $Z$ because $\sum_Kw=1$; the remaining terms give [\[eq:weighted-reconstruction\]](TPC-086.md#ref-af3096a227610d40). Parseval applied to the zero-extension of $w$ gives $$\sum_{r\in G}|m_w(r)|^2=q\sum_{x\in K}|w(x)|^2.$$ Since $m_w(0)=1$, removing $r=0$ proves [\[eq:multiplier-parseval\]](TPC-086.md#ref-ea06b5b9576eeebe).

# The optimal support-averaged kernel

###### ref-4a21225f04f29dc3

###### ref-3c1ea4f00ae4d0ba

> **Theorem: Unique minimum-norm reconstruction** \[thm:optimal-kernel\] Among all complex weights $w$ supported on $K$ and satisfying $\sum_Kw=1$, the unique minimizer of $$\sum_{r\ne0}|m_w(r)|^2$$ is the uniform weight $$w_K(x)=\frac1k,\qquad x\in K.$$ Writing $m_K=m_{w_K}$, one has $$\boxed{  \sum_{r\ne0}|m_K(r)|^2  =\frac{s}{q-s}.} \label{eq:optimal-norm}$$

> **Proof** Cauchy–Schwarz and $\sum_Kw=1$ give $$1\le k\sum_{x\in K}|w(x)|^2,$$ with equality exactly when $w(x)=1/k$ on $K$. Insert the minimum $\sum_K|w|^2=1/k$ into [\[eq:multiplier-parseval\]](TPC-086.md#ref-ea06b5b9576eeebe): $$\min_w\sum_{r\ne0}|m_w(r)|^2  =\frac qk-1=\frac{s}{q-s}.$$

###### ref-89e630bc0e81fbb8

###### ref-11be505c81258175

> **Theorem: Optimal Fourier proof of the support bound** \[thm:sharp-support-bound\] For every $A:G\to\mathbb C$ with nonempty complement of its support, $$\boxed{|Z|^2\le sD.} \label{eq:sharp-support-bound}$$ The constant $s$ is sharp for every prescribed nonempty support set $S$.

> **Proof** Use [\[thm:weighted-reconstruction\]](TPC-086.md#ref-0c824521a4c6813e) with $w=w_K$ and apply Cauchy–Schwarz: $$|Z|^2  \le  \frac{s}{q-s}  \sum_{r\ne0}|\widehat A(r)|^2.$$ By Parseval, $$\sum_{r\ne0}|\widehat A(r)|^2=qD-|Z|^2.$$ Therefore $$|Z|^2\le\frac{s}{q-s}(qD-|Z|^2),$$ which rearranges to [\[eq:sharp-support-bound\]](TPC-086.md#ref-11be505c81258175). If $A(n)=c\ne0$ on $S$ and is zero off $S$, then $|Z|^2=s^2|c|^2=sD$, proving sharpness.

> **Remark: No gain was hidden in Fourier space** The direct physical proof of [\[eq:sharp-support-bound\]](TPC-086.md#ref-11be505c81258175) is Cauchy–Schwarz on $S$. The value of [\[thm:sharp-support-bound\]](TPC-086.md#ref-89e630bc0e81fbb8) is diagnostic: it proves that the minimum-norm use of all structural zeros gives exactly, and not more than, the sharp physical support inequality. Any improvement must use arithmetic information about the coherent Fourier sum, not a different normalization of the same finite data.

###### ref-46e9e79fed9fe333

> **Corollary: Mean-square dispersion is insufficient** \[cor:mean-square-insufficient\] Knowledge of $s$, $D$, and the exact nonzero-frequency energy $$\sum_{r\ne0}|\widehat A(r)|^2=qD-|Z|^2$$ cannot yield a universal improvement over [\[eq:sharp-support-bound\]](TPC-086.md#ref-11be505c81258175). Constant-modulus, common-phase coefficients on $S$ attain equality.

> **Proof** The displayed identity is Parseval, and the extremizers in the proof of [\[thm:sharp-support-bound\]](TPC-086.md#ref-89e630bc0e81fbb8) have the claimed data.

# Stable reconstruction from a zero arc

The minimum-$\ell^2$ kernel in [\[thm:optimal-kernel\]](TPC-086.md#ref-4a21225f04f29dc3) is optimal for arbitrary zero sets. When the complement of the support contains a long cyclic interval, a different choice has bounded Wiener norm and polynomial Fourier decay.

###### ref-d57748d9c8049683

###### ref-238a52c75026d950

Let $C\subset G$ be a cyclic interval of length $H$ on which $A$ vanishes. Fix an integer $k\ge2$, independent of $q$, and choose $L$ so that $$k(L-1)+1\le H. \label{eq:filter-support-condition}$$ Let $u_L$ be the uniform probability measure on an interval of length $L$. Translate the $k$-fold cyclic convolution $u_L^{*k}$ so that its support lies in $C$, and call the resulting probability measure $p_k$. Define $$m_k(r)=\sum_{x\in C}p_k(x)e_q(rx).$$ Translation changes only the phase of $m_k$, while convolution gives $$|m_k(r)|  =  \left|  \frac1L\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}e_q(rj)  \right|^k. \label{eq:filter-multiplier}$$

###### ref-b8a0b5525b95f38d

###### ref-bc4db125a790c954

###### ref-f51912beab497682

###### ref-e33e9ac0bcdbe5a5

> **Theorem: Stable zero-arc filter** \[thm:stable-zero-arc\] Assume $H\ge\delta q$ for a fixed $\delta>0$, and choose $L$ maximal subject to [\[eq:filter-support-condition\]](TPC-086.md#ref-d57748d9c8049683). For every fixed $k\ge2$, $$\begin{aligned}  Z&=-\sum_{r\ne0}m_k(r)\widehat A(r),  \label{eq:filtered-reassembly}\\  \sum_{r\ne0}|m_k(r)|&\ll_{k,\delta}1,  \label{eq:wiener-bound}\\  |m_k(r)|&\ll_{k,\delta}  (1+d_q(r,0))^{-k},  \label{eq:filter-decay}\end{aligned}$$ where $d_q(r,0)=\min(r,q-r)$ for the representative $0\le r<q$.

> **Proof** The first identity is [\[thm:weighted-reconstruction\]](TPC-086.md#ref-0c824521a4c6813e) with $w=p_k$. Since $H\ge\delta q$ and $k$ is fixed, maximality gives $L\asymp_{k,\delta}q$. The geometric-sum formula yields $$\left|  \frac1L\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}e_q(rj)  \right|  \le  \min\left\{1,\frac{q}{2L\,d_q(r,0)}\right\},$$ after changing an absolute constant. Raising to the $k$-th power gives [\[eq:filter-decay\]](TPC-086.md#ref-e33e9ac0bcdbe5a5). Summing $\min(1,Cd^{-k})$ over the two frequencies at each distance $d$ proves [\[eq:wiener-bound\]](TPC-086.md#ref-f51912beab497682), because $k>1$.

###### ref-d9a0cddb3726286b

###### ref-d74a6098e34deecd

> **Proposition: Exact triangular Wiener norm** \[prop:triangular-wiener\] For $k=2$, $$\sum_{r\ne0}|m_2(r)|=\frac qL-1. \label{eq:triangular-wiener}$$ Hence positive-density padding gives a constant Wiener norm.

> **Proof** By [\[eq:filter-multiplier\]](TPC-086.md#ref-238a52c75026d950), $$|m_2(r)|=|\widehat u_L(r)|^2.$$ Parseval gives $\sum_r|\widehat u_L(r)|^2=q\|u_L\|_2^2=q/L$. The $r=0$ term is one.

###### ref-abb60ebdcbb73e8f

> **Proposition: Order-optimality of the Wiener norm** \[prop:wiener-optimality\] Suppose coefficients $c_r$, $r\ne0$, satisfy $$\widehat A(0)  =-\sum_{r\ne0}c_r\widehat A(r)$$ for every $A$ supported in a fixed nonempty allowed arc. Then $$\sum_{r\ne0}|c_r|\ge1.$$ Thus [\[eq:wiener-bound\]](TPC-086.md#ref-f51912beab497682) has the optimal constant order.

> **Proof** Take $A$ to be a point mass at any allowed coordinate $n$. Then $\widehat A(0)=1$ and $|\widehat A(r)|=1$, so the triangle inequality gives the claim.

###### ref-6c1d5de125913cb0

###### ref-ef15b3aa24cd786c

> **Theorem: Low-frequency reduction** \[thm:low-frequency-reduction\] Under the hypotheses of [\[thm:stable-zero-arc\]](TPC-086.md#ref-b8a0b5525b95f38d), for $1\le R<q/2$, $$\boxed{  |Z|  \ll_{k,\delta}  \max_{0<d_q(r,0)\le R}|\widehat A(r)|  +R^{1/2-k}\sqrt{qD}.} \label{eq:abstract-low-frequency}$$

> **Proof** On $0<d_q(r,0)\le R$, use the maximum and [\[eq:wiener-bound\]](TPC-086.md#ref-f51912beab497682). On the complementary frequencies, Cauchy–Schwarz, [\[eq:filter-decay\]](TPC-086.md#ref-e33e9ac0bcdbe5a5), and Parseval give $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_{d_q(r,0)>R}|m_k(r)\widehat A(r)|  &\le  \left(\sum_{d_q(r,0)>R}|m_k(r)|^2\right)^{1/2}  \left(\sum_r|\widehat A(r)|^2\right)^{1/2}\\  &\ll_{k,\delta}  \left(\sum_{d>R}d^{-2k}\right)^{1/2}\sqrt{qD}\\  &\ll_{k,\delta}R^{1/2-k}\sqrt{qD}.\end{aligned}$$

> **Remark: Why positive-density padding matters** If $H/q\to0$, then $L/q\to0$, and both the Wiener norm and the decay constant incur a factor depending on $q/H$. Such a loss must be entered into the physical ledger. The low-frequency theorem below therefore makes the harmless stronger auxiliary choice that leaves a zero arc of positive density.

# A multiplier-weighted exceptional-frequency budget

Let $m=m_w$ be any exact reconstruction multiplier from [\[thm:weighted-reconstruction\]](TPC-086.md#ref-0c824521a4c6813e). For $E\subseteq G\setminus\{0\}$, put $$M_E^2=\sum_{r\in E}|m(r)|^2,\qquad  H_E^2=\sum_{r\in E}|\widehat A(r)|^2,$$ and let $E^c$ denote the complement inside $G\setminus\{0\}$.

###### ref-86834d5c38e1ad7a

###### ref-0dc5c5953eefa8bf

###### ref-372b58e296b7f529

> **Theorem: Exact exceptional-frequency ledger** \[thm:exceptional-ledger\] For every $E\subseteq G\setminus\{0\}$, $$|Z|\le M_EH_E+M_{E^c}H_{E^c}. \label{eq:exceptional-l2}$$ If in addition $$|\widehat A(r)|\le U  \qquad(r\in E^c),$$ then $$|Z|  \le  M_EH_E  + U\sum_{r\in E^c}|m(r)|  \le  M_EH_E+U\sqrt{|E^c|}\,M_{E^c}. \label{eq:exceptional-pointwise}$$

> **Proof** Split the exact sum $$Z=-\sum_{r\ne0}m(r)\widehat A(r)$$ over $E$ and $E^c$. Apply Cauchy–Schwarz on both pieces to get [\[eq:exceptional-l2\]](TPC-086.md#ref-0dc5c5953eefa8bf). On $E^c$, the pointwise hypothesis gives the first bound in [\[eq:exceptional-pointwise\]](TPC-086.md#ref-372b58e296b7f529); a second Cauchy–Schwarz gives the last bound.

###### ref-5101549b18ba52f1

> **Corollary: A sufficient coherent transfer criterion** \[cor:coherent-criterion\] Suppose a family $A_X$ has target scale $T_X>0$. If for some exceptional sets $E_X$, $$M_{E_X}H_{E_X}=o(T_X)  \quad\text{and}\quad  M_{E_X^c}H_{E_X^c}=o(T_X),$$ then $Z_X=o(T_X)$. The same conclusion follows from the two terms on the right of [\[eq:exceptional-pointwise\]](TPC-086.md#ref-372b58e296b7f529).

> **Proof** Apply [\[thm:exceptional-ledger\]](TPC-086.md#ref-86834d5c38e1ad7a).

> **Remark: Cardinality is not the correct currency** The exceptional contribution is controlled by $M_EH_E$, not by $|E|$ alone. A small set can carry a large fraction of the reassembly multiplier or of the Fourier energy. Conversely a large set may be harmless if one of those weighted masses is small. Accordingly, an “all but few frequencies” theorem needs an explicit multiplier-weighted return to the literal coefficient.

###### ref-f0c96264414e6fe5

\[ex:bounded-exceptional-set\] Let $q$ be a large prime, put $s=\lfloor q/8\rfloor$, and define $$A(n)=e_q(n)\mathbf1_{\{1,\ldots,s\}}(n).$$ The coefficient has a zero arc of positive density and $A(0)=0$. Its zero mode is a geometric sum with $$|Z|\asymp s.$$ Moreover, $$\widehat A(r)  =\sum_{n=1}^{s}e_q((1-r)n),$$ so $\widehat A(1)=s$, while $$|\widehat A(r)|  \ll  \min\left\{s,\frac{q}{d_q(r,1)}\right\}.$$ For every fixed $\varepsilon>0$, only $O_\varepsilon(1)$ frequencies exceed $\varepsilon s$, yet one of them carries the natural zero-mode scale. Therefore an all-but-bounded-exceptional-set pointwise theorem does not control $Z$ unless the exceptional contribution is returned.

###### ref-c3839eae16b666b5

> **Proposition: No automatic gain from the optimal zero-set kernel** \[prop:no-automatic-gain\] For the uniform zero-set multiplier $m_K$, using only $$M_E^2+M_{E^c}^2=\frac{s}{q-s}, \qquad  H_E^2+H_{E^c}^2=qD-|Z|^2,$$ the best universal consequence of [\[eq:exceptional-l2\]](TPC-086.md#ref-0dc5c5953eefa8bf) is the sharp support bound $|Z|^2\le sD$.

> **Proof** Cauchy–Schwarz in $\mathbb C^2$ gives $$M_EH_E+M_{E^c}H_{E^c}  \le  \left(\frac{s}{q-s}\right)^{1/2}  (qD-|Z|^2)^{1/2}.$$ This is exactly the inequality used in [\[thm:sharp-support-bound\]](TPC-086.md#ref-89e630bc0e81fbb8), and its rearrangement is $|Z|^2\le sD$. Equality is attained by the common-phase extremizers there, so no smaller universal constant follows.

# The literal fixed-shift determinant interface

###### ref-74910130528fea1f

###### ref-a65d5a612a6959ab

Fix one nonzero integer $h_0$. On the high-$\beta$ TPC-32 row, $$Q_X=X^{267/400+o(1)},\qquad  J_X=X^{133/400+o(1)},\qquad  C_X=\lfloor J_X\rfloor. \label{eq:critical-scales}$$ Let $$A_X(n)=A_{C_X}(n)$$ be the literal matched normalized-determinant coefficient after all three physical channels and every native contribution in a determinant fiber have been summed. Write $$S_X=\operatorname{supp}A_X,\quad s_X=|S_X|,\quad  Z_X=\sum_nA_X(n),\quad D_X=\sum_n|A_X(n)|^2.$$ TPC-32 proves $$A_X(0)=0,\qquad s_X\ll Q_X, \label{eq:physical-zero-support}$$ and places the integer support in a fixed multiple of $[-Q_X,Q_X]$ [`\citep{WangTPC32}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-086.md#cite-49ad82103eb75eb7).

###### ref-a9ec9ab743540c76

Strengthen the harmless auxiliary padding choice. Put $$Y_X=\left\lceil4B_{\mathcal D}Q_X+4\right\rceil$$ and use Bertrand’s postulate to choose a prime $$Y_X<q_X<2Y_X. \label{eq:physical-modulus}$$ The integer support is then injective modulo $q_X$, residue $0$ represents only the literal bin $n=0$, and $q_X\asymp Q_X$ may be arranged with constants depending only on the fixed determinant range. Since that containing arc has length at most $2B_{\mathcal D}Q_X+1<q_X/2$, its complement has length $H_X>q_X/2$. This stronger choice changes no physical coefficient; it only adds zero coordinates to the auxiliary Fourier embedding.

###### ref-7794241363f0d0dd

###### ref-4982bb507fc365d7

Define the padded cyclic coefficient and its Fourier transform by $$\widetilde A_X(a)  =  \sum_{\substack{n\in\mathbb Z\\n\equiv a\;(\bmod q_X)}}A_X(n),  \qquad  \widehat{\widetilde A}_X(r)  =  \sum_{a\in\mathbb Z/q_X\mathbb Z}\widetilde A_X(a)e_{q_X}(-ra). \label{eq:padded-cyclic-coefficient}$$ No wrap gives $$\sum_a\widetilde A_X(a)=Z_X,\qquad  \sum_a|\widetilde A_X(a)|^2=D_X,\qquad  \widetilde A_X(0)=A_X(0)=0. \label{eq:no-wrap-identities}$$ For readability, the remainder of this section writes $\widehat A_X=\widehat{\widetilde A}_X$ and uses $\operatorname{supp}A_X$ for the corresponding cyclic support.

###### ref-ca5eb2849989d4ac

###### ref-7e95c45ee63a1cec

###### ref-1f7203561589f00a

###### ref-a65ae2a0d1a84915

###### ref-a0cadccf34e1b8a9

> **Theorem: Literal coherent-reassembly interface** \[thm:literal-interface\] For the physical packet above, $$\boxed{  Z_X=-\sum_{r=1}^{q_X-1}\widehat A_X(r).} \label{eq:literal-one-zero}$$ If $K_X=(\mathbb Z/q_X\mathbb Z)\setminus\operatorname{supp}A_X$ and $$m_X(r)=\frac1{|K_X|}\sum_{k\in K_X}e_{q_X}(rk),$$ then $$\begin{aligned}  Z_X&=-\sum_{r=1}^{q_X-1}m_X(r)\widehat A_X(r),  \label{eq:literal-optimal-reassembly}\\  \sum_{r=1}^{q_X-1}|m_X(r)|^2  &=\frac{s_X}{q_X-s_X},  \label{eq:literal-multiplier-mass}\\  |Z_X|^2&\le s_XD_X.  \label{eq:literal-support-bound}\end{aligned}$$ The multiplier is determined by the actual zero set after complete physical aggregation.

> **Proof** The padding and [\[eq:physical-zero-support\]](TPC-086.md#ref-a65d5a612a6959ab) verify the hypotheses of \[[prop:one-zero](TPC-086.md#ref-927a89830480f21f),[thm:optimal-kernel](TPC-086.md#ref-4a21225f04f29dc3),[thm:sharp-support-bound](TPC-086.md#ref-89e630bc0e81fbb8)\].

> **Remark: Correction to an earlier route exclusion** Equation [\[eq:literal-one-zero\]](TPC-086.md#ref-7e95c45ee63a1cec) shows that a theorem for nonzero frequencies can control $Z_X$ if it controls their exact coherent sum. Therefore “nonzero frequency” is not by itself a kill condition. What remains insufficient is a result that leaves an uncontrolled exceptional multiplier mass, averages away the fixed $h_0$, replaces the literal phases or support, or supplies only unsigned mean-square dispersion.

## Mesoscopic low-frequency reduction

###### ref-88e1b89c33cc22b4

###### ref-d29d8a9bf102beca

The verified TPC-32 upper envelopes include $$s_X\ll Q_X,\qquad  D_X\ll X^{o(1)}\frac{N_{0,X}^2}{Q_X},  \qquad N_{0,X}=J_XQ_X^2.$$ Since $q_X\asymp Q_X$, $$q_XD_X\ll X^{o(1)}N_{0,X}^2. \label{eq:physical-fourier-energy}$$ Apply the fixed-order zero-arc filter of [\[thm:low-frequency-reduction\]](TPC-086.md#ref-6c1d5de125913cb0) to obtain, for $1\le R<q_X/2$, $$\boxed{  |Z_X|  \ll_k  \max_{0<d_{q_X}(r,0)\le R}|\widehat A_X(r)|  +X^{o(1)}N_{0,X}R^{1/2-k}.} \label{eq:physical-low-frequency}$$

###### ref-2fae402cfa4044c1

###### ref-ee3a0eaec94868b6

###### ref-721ddae3d66b6c93

###### ref-527309308a7ca730

> **Theorem: Critical-window criterion** \[thm:critical-window\] Write $$Q_X=X^{\beta+o(1)},\qquad J_X=X^{1-\beta+o(1)}$$ and fix $\eta_Z\ge0$, $k\ge2$, and $R_X=X^{\rho+o(1)}<q_X/2$. Suppose $$\max_{0<d_{q_X}(r,0)\le R_X}|\widehat A_X(r)|  \le X^{-\eta_Z+o(1)}Q_X^2 \label{eq:low-window-hypothesis}$$ and $$(k-\tfrac12)\rho>1-\beta+\eta_Z. \label{eq:window-condition}$$ Then $$|Z_X|\le X^{-\eta_Z+o(1)}Q_X^2. \label{eq:zero-mode-conclusion}$$

> **Proof** The first term of [\[eq:physical-low-frequency\]](TPC-086.md#ref-d29d8a9bf102beca) has the required size by [\[eq:low-window-hypothesis\]](TPC-086.md#ref-ee3a0eaec94868b6). The second, divided by the target scale, is $$X^{o(1)}  \frac{J_XQ_X^2R_X^{1/2-k}}  {X^{-\eta_Z}Q_X^2}  =  X^{1-\beta+\eta_Z-(k-1/2)\rho+o(1)},$$ which has a fixed-power saving under [\[eq:window-condition\]](TPC-086.md#ref-721ddae3d66b6c93).

###### ref-bc6d23ec453730ca

###### ref-7f68b90abe1248d5

> **Corollary: Triangular critical row** \[cor:triangular-critical\] At $\beta=267/400$, with $\eta_Z=0$ and $k=2$, it is enough to prove [\[eq:low-window-hypothesis\]](TPC-086.md#ref-ee3a0eaec94868b6) on $$R_X=X^{133/600+\varepsilon} \label{eq:triangular-window}$$ for every sufficiently small fixed $\varepsilon>0$; explicitly one may take $$0<\varepsilon<\frac{267}{400}-\frac{133}{600}  =\frac{107}{240}.$$ This window then satisfies $R_X<q_X/2$ for large $X$ and is polynomially shorter than $q_X=X^{267/400+o(1)}$.

> **Proof** Here $$(k-\tfrac12)\rho  =\frac32\left(\frac{133}{600}+\varepsilon\right)  =\frac{133}{400}+\frac32\varepsilon  >1-\beta.$$

> **Remark: Higher fixed order** For every fixed $k\ge2$, the sufficient window condition is $$\rho>\frac{1-\beta+\eta_Z}{k-1/2}.$$ The order $k$ must remain fixed unless every $k$-dependent constant is audited. Equality gives no fixed-power tail reserve.

## What remains arithmetic

###### ref-00b8f1c637ecd2fb

###### ref-cd0a1d147e46c433

The support bound alone gives $$|Z_X|\ll X^{o(1)}Q_X^2J_X, \label{eq:trivial-physical-scale}$$ which is the natural absolute-mass scale. The TPC-32 zero-mode target is instead $$|Z_X|\le X^{o(1)}Q_X^2, \label{eq:physical-target}$$ or a fixed-power improvement thereof. Thus finite reassembly does not create cancellation. Its advance is a rigorous localization: the missing arithmetic estimate can be confined to [\[eq:low-window-hypothesis\]](TPC-086.md#ref-ee3a0eaec94868b6), while the high-frequency tail is absorbed by the already proved energy upper bound.

###### ref-99d98a1f5a1c1e40

> **Corollary: Exact form of a valid Fourier advance** \[cor:valid-fourier-advance\] A nonzero-frequency argument closes [\[eq:physical-target\]](TPC-086.md#ref-cd0a1d147e46c433) if it proves, for a literal reconstruction multiplier $m$, $$\left|  \sum_{r=1}^{q_X-1}m(r)\widehat A_X(r)  \right|  \le X^{o(1)}Q_X^2.$$ More generally, the low-window hypothesis of [\[thm:critical-window\]](TPC-086.md#ref-2fae402cfa4044c1) or the two-term bound of [\[thm:exceptional-ledger\]](TPC-086.md#ref-86834d5c38e1ad7a) closes the target at the corresponding scale.

> **Proof** Use the exact reconstruction identity associated with $m$, then apply \[[thm:critical-window](TPC-086.md#ref-2fae402cfa4044c1),[thm:exceptional-ledger](TPC-086.md#ref-86834d5c38e1ad7a)\] as applicable.

> **Remark: The minimal packet-level input** The pointwise maximum in [\[eq:low-window-hypothesis\]](TPC-086.md#ref-ee3a0eaec94868b6) is a convenient sufficient condition, not the minimal one. The minimal new input bounds the complete packet-level signed sum $$\sum_{0<d_{q_X}(r,0)\le R_X}  m_k(r)\widehat A_X(r)$$ at the target scale. Taking absolute values over native columns before their outer reassembly may lose a fixed power and must not be silently substituted.

> **Remark: Affine twisted form and resonances** Expanding a literal low-frequency coefficient retains the TPC-85 fixed-shift affine pair $$D_t=D_0+st,\qquad U_t=U_0+at,\qquad  sU_0-aD_0=h_0,$$ now with the additive normalized-determinant character $$e_{q_X}\!\left(-r\Delta^\#_{\alpha,\gamma}(j)\right).$$ Both polarizations, all three matched channels, the exact content cutoff, joint mask, and complete outer sum remain present. A nonzero $r$ does not guarantee oscillation in every native column: resonant slopes, short fibers, and bounded fibers need their own return estimate. No such estimate is proved here.

> **Remark: This is not an arithmetic estimate** is an equivalence of interfaces. It does not prove the displayed upper bound. In particular it neither converts a generic dispersion theorem into a zero-mode estimate nor removes the fixed-shift affine Möbius correlation isolated in TPC-85.

# Claim boundary and next gate

###### ref-574bb29886012b20

> **Theorem: Audited conclusion** \[thm:audited-conclusion\] For the actual high-$\beta$ TPC-32 coefficient at one fixed nonzero $h_0$, the distinguished zero mode is exactly a coherent linear functional of the nonzero determinant frequencies. Averaging over the complete actual zero set gives the unique minimum-$\ell^2$ kernel within the zero-set-averaged class and recovers the sharp bound $$|Z_X|^2\le s_XD_X.$$ Positive-density zero-arc padding also gives [\[eq:physical-low-frequency\]](TPC-086.md#ref-d29d8a9bf102beca); at the critical row the triangular filter reduces the missing pointwise input to $R_X=X^{133/600+\varepsilon}$ for sufficiently small fixed $\varepsilon>0$. For any exceptional set, [\[thm:exceptional-ledger\]](TPC-086.md#ref-86834d5c38e1ad7a) gives the exact sufficient multiplier-weighted return condition.

> **Proof** Combine \[[thm:literal-interface](TPC-086.md#ref-ca5eb2849989d4ac),[thm:low-frequency-reduction](TPC-086.md#ref-6c1d5de125913cb0),[thm:critical-window](TPC-086.md#ref-2fae402cfa4044c1),[thm:exceptional-ledger](TPC-086.md#ref-86834d5c38e1ad7a)\].

#### What is proved.

The finite reconstruction, optimizer, zero-arc filter, sharp support inequality, low-window reduction, and exceptional-frequency ledger are exact $\mathrm{L0}$ results. Their application to the padded literal determinant coefficient and its inherited energy envelope is $\mathrm{L1}$. The correction from “nonzero frequencies are irrelevant” to “a short low-frequency coherent reassembly is the remaining gate on the zero-mode branch” is therefore rigorous.

#### What is not proved.

There is no new $\mathrm{L2}$ estimate for the coherent sum, no lower bound for $D_X$, no fixed-shift Chowla theorem, and no transfer from averaged shifts or generic frequencies. The paper proves no parity breakthrough, no prime-pair lower bound, and no twin-prime result. Nothing is specialized to $h_0=2$.

#### Two independent ledgers.

If a later theorem proves $$|Z_X|\le X^{-\eta_Z+o(1)}Q_X^2,\qquad  D_X\ge X^{-\lambda_D+o(1)}Q_X^3J_X^2,$$ then the TPC-82 flatness transfer uses $$\lambda_D\le2\eta_Z.$$ Separately, every fixed-power degradation actually entering the full physical comparison must be counted once in $$\Lambda_{\rm phys}<\frac1{400}.$$ Equality in the second ledger stops the route; the first ledger is not another copy of the $1/400$ budget.

#### Stop rules.

The tail condition $(k-\tfrac12)\rho>1-\beta+\eta_Z$ is strict when a fixed-power reserve is claimed. If the padding has $H_X/q_X\to0$, its filter loss must be recalculated. If $k$ grows with $X$, its constants must be audited. An almost-all-frequency input is insufficient unless its multiplier-weighted exceptional contribution is returned. A resonant or short-fiber sector cannot be deleted.

#### Next gate.

The positive route on the zero-mode branch is now precise: estimate the literal low-frequency multiplier sum, or its fixed-$h_0$ determinant-character-twisted affine Möbius expansion. A logarithmically averaged or almost-all-scale theorem may be used only after a quantitative Tauberian and quantifier transfer to the same growing packet. The next paper audits that transfer.
