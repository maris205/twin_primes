## Abstract

Let $a=(a_\omega)_{\omega\in\Omega}\ne0$ be a complex coefficient on its prescribed finite support, let $S=\#\Omega$, and put $$Z=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}a_\omega,\qquad  D=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}|a_\omega|^2,\qquad  \rho(a)=\frac{|Z|}{\sqrt{SD}}.$$ Thus $\rho(a)$ is the cosine of the projective angle between $a$ and the constant vector. If $$a=\frac{Z}{S}\mathbf 1+a_\perp,$$ then the exact orthogonal decomposition is $$D=\frac{|Z|^2}{S}+\|a_\perp\|_2^2,\qquad  \mathfrak F(a):=\frac{|Z|^2}{D}=S\rho(a)^2.$$

We determine the sharp perturbation law for this angle. If $\|a-b\|_2\le\delta\|b\|_2$, $0\le\delta<1$, and $r=\rho(b)$, then, for support dimension at least two, $$L(r,\delta)\le\rho(a)\le U(r,\delta),$$ where, with $\alpha=\arccos r$ and $\gamma=\arcsin\delta$, $$L(r,\delta)=\cos\!\bigl(\min\{\tfrac{\pi}{2},\alpha+\gamma\}\bigr),  \qquad  U(r,\delta)=\cos\!\bigl((\alpha-\gamma)_+\bigr).$$ Both endpoints are attained. In particular, $$|\rho(a)-\rho(b)|\le\delta,$$ with optimal constant $1$. The frequently obtained estimate $$\rho(a)\le\frac{\rho(b)+\delta}{1-\delta}$$ is valid, but is not sharp; its denominator is an artifact of separately estimating the numerator and norm.

This exact stability law identifies a critical surrogate barrier. To transfer an upper target $\rho\ll J^{-1}$, relative $\ell^2$ error $O(J^{-1})$ is sufficient and, uniformly over surrogates, necessary in order. Merely $o(1)$ error is insufficient: there are two-dimensional examples with $\rho(b_J)=0$, relative error $\delta_J\to0$, and $\rho(a_J)=\delta_J\gg J^{-1}$. Uniformly preserving a two-sided $\rho\asymp J^{-1}$ law from the norm hypothesis alone additionally requires a fixed margin between the error constant and the surrogate’s lower constant.

Finally, we attach the theorem to the literal fixed-$h_0$ TPC-32 determinant packet. On its actual support $S_X\ll Q_X$, a same-frame surrogate satisfying $$\rho(b_X)\le\frac{X^{o(1)}}{J_X},  \qquad  \frac{\|A_X-b_X\|_2}{\|b_X\|_2}  \le\frac{X^{o(1)}}{J_X}$$ would certify $$\mathfrak F(A_X)\le X^{1/400+o(1)}$$ at the critical scales. This is an exact $\mathrm{L1}$ stability certificate, not a construction of such a surrogate and not a new $\mathrm{L2}$ estimate. The determinant-energy ledger $\lambda_D\le2\eta_Z$ and the independent physical-loss ledger $\Lambda_{\rm phys}<1/400$ are kept separate.

# Introduction

The TPC-32 matched shell reduces one high-row fixed-shift problem to the distinguished zero-frequency concentration of a literal post-bin coefficient $A_X$ [`\citep{WangTPC32}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-089.md#cite-49ad82103eb75eb7). Writing $$Z_X=\sum_nA_X(n),\qquad  D_X=\sum_n|A_X(n)|^2,$$ the statistic is $$\mathfrak F_X=\frac{|Z_X|^2}{D_X}.$$ TPC-81 and TPC-82 separate the two arithmetic obligations behind this quotient: determinant energy must survive, and the signed zero mode must cancel [`\citep{WangTPC81,WangTPC82}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-089.md#cite-ff65f956e7cf0d6d); [\[WangTPC82\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-089.md#cite-cfeb22890911c08a). TPC-88 then factors the same statistic into post-bin magnitude spreading and relative $\ell^1$ phase cancellation [`\citep{WangTPC88}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-089.md#cite-c309474acaf11792).

The present paper asks a different, operational question:

> If the literal coefficient is replaced by an analytically or numerically tractable surrogate, how accurately must that surrogate approximate the coefficient before its zero-mode geometry transfers?

There is a canonical Hilbert-space formulation. On the actual support $\Omega$, let $$e=\frac{\mathbf 1}{\sqrt{\#\Omega}}.$$ For a nonzero coefficient $a$, define $$\rho(a)=\frac{|\langle a,e\rangle|}{\|a\|_2}.$$ This is the cosine of the projective angle from $a$ to the constant line. Since $$\langle a,e\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\#\Omega}}\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}  a_\omega,$$ one has the exact identity $$\mathfrak F(a)=\#\Omega\,\rho(a)^2.$$ Thus the zero-frequency gate is a principal-angle gate.

The principal angle is especially well suited to surrogate analysis because it is invariant under nonzero scalar renormalization. More importantly, its perturbation law is exact. If $a$ lies in the relative ball $$\|a-b\|_2\le\delta\|b\|_2,\qquad \delta<1,$$ then the projective angle between $a$ and $b$ is at most $\arcsin\delta$. This yields sharp upper and lower envelopes for $\rho(a)$, and in particular $$|\rho(a)-\rho(b)|\le\delta.$$ The constant is best possible.

This elementary fact has a nontrivial consequence for the TPC program. The critical zero-mode scale is $1/J_X$, not merely a quantity tending to zero. Therefore a surrogate with relative $\ell^2$ error $o(1)$ may preserve its energy scale while completely failing to preserve the required zero-mode scale. Uniform transfer requires $O(1/J_X)$ accuracy. A two-dimensional tangent construction shows that this order cannot be improved.

## Main contributions

The paper establishes the following.

1.  It identifies determinant flatness with the squared principal-angle cosine on the prescribed actual support: $$D=\frac{|Z|^2}{S}+D_\perp,\qquad  \mathfrak F=S\rho^2.$$

2.  It gives the sharp two-sided perturbation envelope for $\rho$ under relative $\ell^2$ error, including all boundary cases and equality constructions.

3.  It audits the simpler quotient estimate $$\rho(a)\le\frac{\rho(b)+\delta}{1-\delta}$$ and shows that the exact projective result is stronger.

4.  It proves sharp transfer inequalities for determinant flatness, coherent energy, orthogonal energy, and the zero mode.

5.  It proves that $O(1/J)$ relative accuracy is the correct uniform threshold for transferring $\rho\ll1/J$, whereas $o(1)$ is not enough.

6.  It shows that preserving a two-sided $\rho\asymp1/J$ statement needs a constant margin; an error of the same size as the lower signal can erase that signal exactly.

7.  It gives a literal same-support $\mathrm{L1}$ certificate for the fixed-$h_0$ TPC-32 coefficient.

8.  It isolates zero padding as a normalization trap: $\rho$ changes under padding although $\mathfrak F$ does not.

9.  It keeps the determinant-energy and physical-loss ledgers independent.

## Audit levels

We use the established terminology. An $\mathrm{L0}$ result is finite Hilbert-space algebra or an abstract finite counterexample. An $\mathrm{L1}$ result places that algebra on the literal fixed-$h_0$ coefficient with its actual bins and original normalization. An $\mathrm{L2}$ result is a new growing-$X$ arithmetic estimate for that coefficient. All angle and perturbation theorems below are $\mathrm{L0}$. The same-frame implication for $A_X$ is $\mathrm{L1}$. No surrogate satisfying its hypotheses is constructed, and no $\mathrm{L2}$ estimate is claimed.

# Exact principal-angle geometry

Let $\Omega$ be a nonempty finite set, put $S=\#\Omega$, and equip $\mathbb C^\Omega$ with the inner product $$\langle x,y\rangle  =\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}x_\omega\overline{y_\omega},$$ linear in the first variable. Let $$e=\frac{\mathbf 1}{\sqrt S},\qquad  P_0x=\langle x,e\rangle e,\qquad  P_\perp=I-P_0.$$ The line $\mathbb Ce$ is the constant mode on the prescribed frame.

> **Definition: Literal finite statistics** \[def:literal-finite-statistics\] For $a\in\mathbb C^\Omega$, define $$Z(a)=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}a_\omega,\qquad  D(a)=\|a\|_2^2,\qquad  D_\perp(a)=\|P_\perp a\|_2^2.$$ For $a\ne0$, define $$\rho(a)  =\frac{|Z(a)|}{\sqrt{S D(a)}}  =\frac{|\langle a,e\rangle|}{\|a\|_2}, \qquad  \mathfrak F(a)=\frac{|Z(a)|^2}{D(a)}. \label{eq:rho-flat-def}$$ The zero vector is treated as a separate branch; no quotient by $D(0)$ is used.

> **Theorem: Orthogonal zero-mode decomposition** \[thm:orthogonal-decomposition\] For every $a\in\mathbb C^\Omega$, $$a=\frac{Z(a)}{S}\mathbf 1+P_\perp a \label{eq:vector-decomposition}$$ and $$\boxed{  D(a)=\frac{|Z(a)|^2}{S}+D_\perp(a).} \label{eq:energy-decomposition}$$ If $a\ne0$, then $$\boxed{  \rho(a)^2  =\frac{|Z(a)|^2/S}{D(a)}  =1-\frac{D_\perp(a)}{D(a)},  \qquad  \mathfrak F(a)=S\rho(a)^2.} \label{eq:principal-flatness}$$

> **Proof** Since $$\langle a,e\rangle=\frac{Z(a)}{\sqrt S},$$ one has $$P_0a=\frac{Z(a)}{\sqrt S}\frac{\mathbf 1}{\sqrt S}  =\frac{Z(a)}{S}\mathbf 1.$$ The decomposition [\[eq:vector-decomposition\]](TPC-089.md#ref-40685e6c173e6bb6) is orthogonal. Pythagoras gives [\[eq:energy-decomposition\]](TPC-089.md#ref-ec14765af0786cd2), and division by $D(a)>0$ gives [\[eq:principal-flatness\]](TPC-089.md#ref-16bba093db6b4270).

###### ref-fd4fedc1418f4843

> **Corollary: Equality cases** \[cor:principal-equality-cases\] For every nonzero $a$, $$0\le\rho(a)\le1,\qquad 0\le\mathfrak F(a)\le S.$$ Moreover, $$\rho(a)=0  \Longleftrightarrow Z(a)=0,$$ and $$\rho(a)=1  \Longleftrightarrow  a\in\mathbb C\mathbf 1\setminus\{0\}.$$

> **Proof** These statements follow from [\[eq:principal-flatness\]](TPC-089.md#ref-16bba093db6b4270). The equality $\rho(a)=1$ is equivalent to $P_\perp a=0$.

###### ref-b635329f3c21f3e1

> **Remark: Scale and phase invariance** \[rem:scale-phase-invariance\] For every $c\in\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}$, $$\rho(ca)=\rho(a),\qquad  \mathfrak F(ca)=\mathfrak F(a).$$ Thus $\rho$ is naturally a function on complex projective space, not on a chosen normalization of the coefficient.

## Relation to the two-level factorization

For comparison with TPC-88, define, for $a\ne0$, $$M(a)=\|a\|_1,\qquad  \mathfrak c(a)=\frac{|Z(a)|}{M(a)},\qquad  S_{\mathrm{eff}}(a)=\frac{M(a)^2}{D(a)}.$$

###### ref-48d8dbe245e6ae52

###### ref-551e0351f5e48a48

> **Proposition: Exact dictionary** \[prop:tpc88-dictionary\] For every nonzero $a\in\mathbb C^\Omega$, $$\boxed{  \rho(a)  =\mathfrak c(a)\sqrt{\frac{S_{\mathrm{eff}}(a)}{S}},  \qquad  \mathfrak F(a)  =\mathfrak c(a)^2S_{\mathrm{eff}}(a)  =S\rho(a)^2.} \label{eq:tpc88-dictionary}$$ In particular, $\rho(a)\le\mathfrak c(a)$.

> **Proof** The first identity follows by inserting $M(a)/\sqrt{D(a)}=\sqrt{S_{\mathrm{eff}}(a)}$ into [\[eq:rho-flat-def\]](TPC-089.md#ref-57631688dc0524e2). The remaining identities follow by squaring. Since $S_{\mathrm{eff}}(a)\le S$ by Cauchy–Schwarz, $\rho(a)\le\mathfrak c(a)$.

> **Remark: Different normalization questions** The coefficient $\mathfrak c$ measures signed cancellation relative to the actual post-bin $\ell^1$ mass. The coefficient $\rho$ measures the angle to the constant line relative to $\ell^2$ energy and actual support. They are equivalent up to subpower factors only when $S_{\mathrm{eff}}/S=X^{o(1)}$; they should not be interchanged without that input.

> **Proposition: Zero-padding law** \[prop:zero-padding\] Let $a\in\mathbb C^\Omega\setminus\{0\}$, and extend $a$ by zero to a larger frame $\widetilde\Omega\supset\Omega$ of size $\widetilde S=S+K$. Denote the padded vector by $\widetilde a$. > **Proof** Padding changes neither the sum nor the squared norm, proving [\[eq:padding-invariants\]](TPC-089.md#ref-1cd8cac91820bfd7). Apply $\mathfrak F=S\rho^2$ on the two frames to obtain [\[eq:padding-rho\]](TPC-089.md#ref-88373d7397f21f54).

# Sharp relative-$\ell^2$ perturbation theory

For nonzero vectors $x,y$ in a complex Hilbert space, define their projective angle by $$\angle(x,y)  =\arccos\frac{|\langle x,y\rangle|}  {\|x\|_2\|y\|_2}  \in[0,\pi/2].$$ This angle is unchanged by multiplying either vector by a nonzero complex scalar. It is the standard one-dimensional principal angle and the Fubini–Study distance between the corresponding projective points; see, for example, [`\citet{Bhatia1997,StewartSun1990}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-089.md#cite-8535808c9519fe06); [\[StewartSun1990\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-089.md#cite-74825d7a79d85798).

###### ref-fe79d8490eadeab8

###### ref-9b5f32dcc5cb1978

> **Lemma: A relative ball is a projective cone** \[lem:relative-ball-cone\] Let $b\ne0$ and suppose $$\|a-b\|_2\le\delta\|b\|_2,\qquad 0\le\delta<1.$$ Then $a\ne0$ and $$\boxed{  \angle(a,b)\le\arcsin\delta.} \label{eq:projective-cone}$$

> **Proof** The reverse triangle inequality gives $\|a\|_2\ge(1-\delta)\|b\|_2>0$. Let $\widehat b=b/\|b\|_2$. Since $a/\|b\|_2$ belongs to the complex line $\mathbb Ca$, $$\operatorname{dist}(\widehat b,\mathbb Ca)  \le  \left\|\widehat b-\frac{a}{\|b\|_2}\right\|_2  \le\delta.$$ Orthogonal projection onto $\mathbb Ca$ gives $$\operatorname{dist}(\widehat b,\mathbb Ca)  =  \sqrt{1-  \frac{|\langle a,b\rangle|^2}  {\|a\|_2^2\|b\|_2^2}}  =  \sin\angle(a,b).$$ Because the angle lies in $[0,\pi/2]$, this proves [\[eq:projective-cone\]](TPC-089.md#ref-9b5f32dcc5cb1978).

> **Theorem: Sharp principal-angle envelope** \[thm:sharp-envelope\] Let $\Omega$ have size $S\ge2$, let $a,b\in\mathbb C^\Omega$ with $b\ne0$, and suppose $$\|a-b\|_2\le\delta\|b\|_2,\qquad 0\le\delta<1. \label{eq:relative-error-hyp}$$ Put $$r=\rho(b),\qquad  \alpha=\arccos r,\qquad  \gamma=\arcsin\delta.$$ Then $$\boxed{  L(r,\delta)\le\rho(a)\le U(r,\delta),} \label{eq:sharp-envelope}$$ where $$\begin{aligned}  L(r,\delta)  &=  \cos\!\left(  \min\left\{\frac{\pi}{2},\alpha+\gamma\right\}\right)  \notag\\  &=  \bigl(  r\sqrt{1-\delta^2}  -\delta\sqrt{1-r^2}  \bigr)_+, \label{eq:L-envelope}\\  U(r,\delta)  &=  \cos\!\left((\alpha-\gamma)_+\right) \notag\\  &=  \begin{cases}  r\sqrt{1-\delta^2}  +\delta\sqrt{1-r^2},  &\delta<\sqrt{1-r^2},\\[2mm]  1,&\delta\ge\sqrt{1-r^2}.  \end{cases} \label{eq:U-envelope}\end{aligned}$$ For every $r\in[0,1]$ and $\delta\in[0,1)$, both endpoints allowed by [\[eq:sharp-envelope\]](TPC-089.md#ref-c926284d0a955634) are attained by vectors in a two-dimensional subspace. Hence the envelope is sharp.

> **Proof** Let $e=\mathbf 1/\sqrt S$. Since $$\rho(x)=\cos\angle(x,e),$$ put $\theta=\angle(a,e)$. The projective angle obeys the triangle inequality. By [\[lem:relative-ball-cone\]](TPC-089.md#ref-fe79d8490eadeab8), $$\max\{0,\alpha-\gamma\}  \le\theta\le  \min\left\{\frac{\pi}{2},\alpha+\gamma\right\}.$$ The cosine is decreasing on $[0,\pi/2]$, which gives the first forms of $L$ and $U$. The addition formulas give [\[eq:L-envelope\]](TPC-089.md#ref-10e578e4cec10dcf) and the first branch of [\[eq:U-envelope\]](TPC-089.md#ref-758cf362317d545e). The lower expression is positive exactly when $\delta<r$; the upper ray reaches the constant line exactly when $\delta\ge\sqrt{1-r^2}$.
>
> It remains to prove sharpness. Normalize $\|b\|_2=1$, multiply by a global phase, and choose a unit vector $f\perp e$ so that $$b=\cos\alpha\,e+\sin\alpha\,f.$$ Such an $f$ exists because $S\ge2$. When $\sin\alpha=0$, choose any unit vector orthogonal to $e$.
>
> If $\alpha\ge\gamma$, define $$u_+  =  \cos(\alpha-\gamma)e+\sin(\alpha-\gamma)f,  \qquad  a_+=\cos\gamma\,u_+.$$ Then $a_+$ is the orthogonal projection of $b$ onto $\mathbb Cu_+$, so $$\|a_+-b\|_2=\sin\gamma=\delta$$ and $\rho(a_+)=\cos(\alpha-\gamma)=U(r,\delta)$. If $\alpha<\gamma$, the constant ray is within distance $\sin\alpha\le\delta$ of $b$; its projection $a_+=\cos\alpha\,e$ attains $\rho(a_+)=1$.
>
> For the lower endpoint, if $\alpha+\gamma\le\pi/2$, define $$u_-  =  \cos(\alpha+\gamma)e+\sin(\alpha+\gamma)f,  \qquad  a_-=\cos\gamma\,u_-.$$ Again $\|a_--b\|_2=\delta$, and $\rho(a_-)=\cos(\alpha+\gamma)=L(r,\delta)$. If $\alpha+\gamma>\pi/2$, the orthogonal ray $\mathbb Cf$ is within distance $\cos\alpha=r\le\delta$ of $b$; the projection $a_-=\sin\alpha\,f$ attains $\rho(a_-)=0$. The endpoint cases follow by continuity or the same formulas.

> **Remark: The one-dimensional exception** If $S=1$, every nonzero vector lies on the constant line and $\rho\equiv1$. The inequalities above remain valid as bounds, but their lower endpoint need not be attainable. All nontrivial sharpness phenomena begin in dimension two.

###### ref-44da418e0112ec88

###### ref-98974aae1b37978b

> **Corollary: Optimal additive stability** \[cor:optimal-additive-stability\] Under the hypotheses of [\[thm:sharp-envelope\]](TPC-089.md#ref-fa524cf7e35e4211), $$\boxed{  |\rho(a)-\rho(b)|\le\delta.} \label{eq:rho-one-lipschitz}$$ The constant $1$ is optimal for every $0<\delta<1$.

> **Proof** The angle $\angle(a,e)$ differs from $\angle(b,e)$ by at most $\gamma=\arcsin\delta$. Among two angles in $[0,\pi/2]$ at distance at most $\gamma$, the largest possible difference of their cosines is $\sin\gamma=\delta$. Equivalently, this follows directly from [\[eq:L-envelope\]](TPC-089.md#ref-10e578e4cec10dcf)–[\[eq:U-envelope\]](TPC-089.md#ref-758cf362317d545e).
>
> For sharpness choose orthonormal $e,f$, put $b=f$, and set $$u=\delta e+\sqrt{1-\delta^2}\,f,\qquad  a=\sqrt{1-\delta^2}\,u.$$ Then $$\|a-b\|_2=\delta\|b\|_2,\qquad  \rho(b)=0,\qquad  \rho(a)=\delta.$$

## The quotient bound and its exact audit

###### ref-1f34ea9120941d27

###### ref-d05359852e5f8952

###### ref-129b257538bc424a

###### ref-e89717eb097c28c5

> **Corollary: Elementary quotient bounds** \[cor:quotient-bounds\] Under [\[eq:relative-error-hyp\]](TPC-089.md#ref-d72815f51c65456b), $$\frac{(\rho(b)-\delta)_+}{1+\delta}  \le\rho(a)\le  \min\left\{  1,\frac{\rho(b)+\delta}{1-\delta}  \right\}. \label{eq:quotient-bounds}$$ In particular, $$\boxed{  \rho(a)\le  \frac{\rho(b)+\delta}{1-\delta}.} \label{eq:requested-quotient-bound}$$ These bounds are valid but generally not sharp. The stronger consequence of the exact envelope is $$(\rho(b)-\delta)_+\le\rho(a)  \le\min\{1,\rho(b)+\delta\}. \label{eq:stronger-additive-bounds}$$

> **Proof** Cauchy–Schwarz gives $$|Z(a)-Z(b)|  =  |\langle a-b,\mathbf 1\rangle|  \le\sqrt S\,\|a-b\|_2  \le\sqrt S\,\delta\|b\|_2.$$ Also, $$(1-\delta)\|b\|_2  \le\|a\|_2  \le(1+\delta)\|b\|_2.$$ Combining the upper numerator estimate with the lower norm estimate, and the lower numerator estimate with the upper norm estimate, proves [\[eq:quotient-bounds\]](TPC-089.md#ref-d05359852e5f8952). Equation [\[eq:stronger-additive-bounds\]](TPC-089.md#ref-e89717eb097c28c5) is [\[cor:optimal-additive-stability\]](TPC-089.md#ref-44da418e0112ec88).

> **Remark: Where $1-\delta$ came from** The denominator in [\[eq:requested-quotient-bound\]](TPC-089.md#ref-129b257538bc424a) is produced by optimizing the numerator and denominator independently. Those two triangle inequalities cannot generally be saturated in the same direction. The projective-cone argument retains their joint geometry and removes the artificial denominator.

> **Proposition: Complete elementary transfer ledger** \[prop:elementary-transfer-ledger\] Under [\[eq:relative-error-hyp\]](TPC-089.md#ref-d72815f51c65456b), $$\begin{aligned}  (1-\delta)^2D(b)  &\le D(a)\le(1+\delta)^2D(b), \label{eq:energy-transfer}\\  |Z(a)-Z(b)|  &\le\sqrt S\,\delta\sqrt{D(b)}, \label{eq:zero-mode-transfer}\\  \left|  \sqrt{D_\perp(a)}-\sqrt{D_\perp(b)}  \right|  &\le\delta\sqrt{D(b)}. \label{eq:perp-energy-transfer}\end{aligned}$$ Moreover, $$S L(\rho(b),\delta)^2  \le\mathfrak F(a)\le  S U(\rho(b),\delta)^2, \label{eq:exact-flatness-transfer}$$ and hence $$\boxed{  \bigl(\sqrt{\mathfrak F(b)}-\sqrt S\,\delta\bigr)_+^2  \le\mathfrak F(a)\le  \min\left\{  S,\bigl(\sqrt{\mathfrak F(b)}+\sqrt S\,\delta\bigr)^2  \right\}.} \label{eq:additive-flatness-transfer}$$

> **Proof** The norm triangle inequality proves [\[eq:energy-transfer\]](TPC-089.md#ref-a66ebfc57ae49f1e). Cauchy–Schwarz proves [\[eq:zero-mode-transfer\]](TPC-089.md#ref-ac11e6e4fbc1804e). Since $P_\perp$ is a contraction, $$\|P_\perp(a-b)\|_2\le\|a-b\|_2,$$ and the reverse triangle inequality proves [\[eq:perp-energy-transfer\]](TPC-089.md#ref-e0e886082b8dc485). Finally use $\mathfrak F=S\rho^2$, [\[thm:sharp-envelope\]](TPC-089.md#ref-fa524cf7e35e4211), and [\[cor:optimal-additive-stability\]](TPC-089.md#ref-44da418e0112ec88).

###### ref-6f6dfad90361e563

> **Remark: Different accuracy scales** \[rem:different-accuracy-scales\] Any $\delta=o(1)$ transfers the $\ell^2$-energy scale multiplicatively through [\[eq:energy-transfer\]](TPC-089.md#ref-a66ebfc57ae49f1e). It does not necessarily transfer a much smaller angle $\rho\asymp1/J$. The latter task requires error at the angle’s own scale. This distinction is the source of the critical surrogate barrier in the next section.

# The critical surrogate-accuracy threshold

The perturbation theorem is most transparent for a general target scale $\tau\to0$. The TPC application later sets $\tau=1/J$.

###### ref-721f87b5f7efb968

###### ref-eaa6c4c195dc9af0

###### ref-0841f0fcbbf0ffd8

> **Theorem: One-sided target transfer** \[thm:one-sided-target-transfer\] Let $a_j,b_j$ be nonzero vectors on the same frames of dimension at least two, let $\tau_j>0$ satisfy $\tau_j\to0$, and fix constants $C,K\ge0$. Suppose $$\|a_j-b_j\|_2\le\delta_j\|b_j\|_2.$$ $$\rho(b_j)\le C\tau_j,\qquad  \delta_j\le K\tau_j \label{eq:one-sided-transfer-hyp}$$ implies, for all sufficiently large $j$, $$\boxed{ \rho(a_j)\le(C+K)\tau_j.} \label{eq:one-sided-transfer-conc}$$ If $C+K>0$, the asymptotic coefficient $C+K$ cannot be uniformly reduced using only [\[eq:one-sided-transfer-hyp\]](TPC-089.md#ref-eaa6c4c195dc9af0).

> **Proof** Apply [\[eq:stronger-additive-bounds\]](TPC-089.md#ref-e89717eb097c28c5). For sharpness, take $$r_j=C\tau_j,\qquad \delta_j=K\tau_j$$ for all sufficiently large $j$, and choose the upper equality case from [\[thm:sharp-envelope\]](TPC-089.md#ref-fa524cf7e35e4211). Since $r_j,\delta_j\to0$, the first branch of [\[eq:U-envelope\]](TPC-089.md#ref-758cf362317d545e) applies and gives $$\frac{\rho(a_j)}{\tau_j}  =  C\sqrt{1-K^2\tau_j^2}  +K\sqrt{1-C^2\tau_j^2}  \longrightarrow C+K.$$ Thus no smaller uniform asymptotic coefficient follows from the two scalar bounds alone.

###### ref-e3808e2093ae282e

> **Corollary: Critical $J^{-1}$ sufficiency** \[cor:J-sufficiency\] If $$\rho(b_J)=O(J^{-1}),\qquad  \frac{\|a_J-b_J\|_2}{\|b_J\|_2}=O(J^{-1}),$$ then $$\rho(a_J)=O(J^{-1}).$$ The same statement holds with $O(J^{-1})$ replaced throughout by $J^{-1}X^{o(1)}$ along a critical TPC scale $J=J_X$.

> **Proof** Apply [\[thm:one-sided-target-transfer\]](TPC-089.md#ref-721f87b5f7efb968) with $\tau_J=J^{-1}$. A sum of two subpower multiples of $J_X^{-1}$ is again a subpower multiple of $J_X^{-1}$.

###### ref-0d0d4b889160d26d

###### ref-d9df1b51c5f6996b

###### ref-10e5fdf1f269e9b3

> **Theorem: Two-sided scale transfer with margin** \[thm:two-sided-transfer\] Suppose, for fixed $0<c\le C$ and $0\le\vartheta<1$, $$\frac{c}{J}\le\rho(b_J)\le\frac{C}{J},  \qquad  \delta_J\le\frac{\vartheta c}{J}. \label{eq:two-sided-margin-hyp}$$ Then $$\boxed{  \frac{(1-\vartheta)c}{J}  \le\rho(a_J)\le  \frac{C+\vartheta c}{J}.} \label{eq:two-sided-margin-conc}$$ Thus a controlled $O(J^{-1})$ error preserves $\rho\asymp J^{-1}$. In particular, $\delta_J=o(J^{-1})$ preserves every fixed two-sided $\rho(b_J)\asymp J^{-1}$ law.

> **Proof** Use $$\rho(b_J)-\delta_J  \le\rho(a_J)\le  \rho(b_J)+\delta_J$$ from [\[cor:optimal-additive-stability\]](TPC-089.md#ref-44da418e0112ec88).

###### ref-5aafdae9d3861e96

> **Remark: Upper scale versus two-sided scale** \[rem:upper-vs-two-sided\] An error $K/J$ with arbitrary fixed $K$ always preserves the upper order $O(1/J)$. To preserve a lower bound as well, the error constant must stay below the surrogate’s lower constant. Saying only “$O(1/J)$” suppresses this necessary margin.

## Sharp countermodels

###### ref-95126eaef525c0eb

###### ref-4c4d77e2547afc8e

###### ref-fef100894007f3f6

\[ex:o1-not-enough\] Let $e,f$ be orthonormal in $\mathbb C^2$, let $\delta_J\to0$, and put $$b_J=f,\qquad  u_J=\delta_Je+\sqrt{1-\delta_J^2}\,f,\qquad  a_J=\sqrt{1-\delta_J^2}\,u_J. \label{eq:tangent-countermodel}$$ Then $$\frac{\|a_J-b_J\|_2}{\|b_J\|_2}  =\delta_J,\qquad  \rho(b_J)=0,\qquad  \rho(a_J)=\delta_J. \label{eq:tangent-countermodel-values}$$ Choosing $\delta_J=J^{-1/2}$ gives a surrogate with relative error $o(1)$ and perfect zero mode, while $$\rho(a_J)=J^{-1/2}\gg J^{-1}.$$ Thus structure-free $o(1)$ relative approximation does not transfer the critical scale.

> **Remark: Actual two-point support** Take $$e=2^{-1/2}(1,1),\qquad  f=2^{-1/2}(1,-1).$$ For every sufficiently small positive $\delta_J$, both coordinates of $a_J$ and $b_J$ are nonzero. Hence the countermodel already lives on one common actual support of size two; it is not a padding artifact.

###### ref-fd07e0c032276fba

\[ex:J-sharp-order\] In [\[ex:o1-not-enough\]](TPC-089.md#ref-95126eaef525c0eb), choose $\delta_J=K/J$ for a fixed $K>0$. Then $$\rho(b_J)=0,\qquad  \rho(a_J)=\frac{K}{J}.$$ Consequently the perturbation contribution enters at exactly the $J^{-1}$ scale. A uniform theorem that tolerates $\delta_J\gg J^{-1}$ cannot conclude $\rho(a_J)=O(J^{-1})$.

###### ref-08d1f4885d960a61

\[ex:lower-signal-erased\] Fix $c>0$, let $r_J=c/J<1$, and set $$b_J=r_Je+\sqrt{1-r_J^2}\,f,\qquad  a_J=\sqrt{1-r_J^2}\,f.$$ Then $$\rho(b_J)=\frac{c}{J},\qquad  \rho(a_J)=0,\qquad  \frac{\|a_J-b_J\|_2}{\|b_J\|_2}  =\frac{c}{J}.$$ Thus an error with the same leading constant as the lower signal can destroy two-sided comparability. The margin in [\[thm:two-sided-transfer\]](TPC-089.md#ref-0d0d4b889160d26d) is not cosmetic.

###### ref-62b993bef252e2a0

> **Theorem: Sharp order-of-accuracy barrier** \[thm:sharp-accuracy-barrier\] Consider all same-frame pairs $(a_J,b_J)$ in dimension at least two. Uniform transfer of $$\rho(b_J)=O(J^{-1})  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad  \rho(a_J)=O(J^{-1})$$ from a relative-error hypothesis $$\|a_J-b_J\|_2\le\delta_J\|b_J\|_2$$ with a prescribed tolerance $\delta_J\ge0$ holds if $\delta_J=O(J^{-1})$, and fails in general whenever $J\delta_J$ is unbounded. In particular, failure can occur even when $\delta_J=o(1)$.

> **Proof** Sufficiency is [\[cor:J-sufficiency\]](TPC-089.md#ref-e3808e2093ae282e). If $J\delta_J$ is unbounded, pass to a subsequence on which it tends to infinity and put $$\varepsilon_J  =  \min\left\{\frac12,\sqrt{\frac{\delta_J}{J}}\right\}.$$ Eventually $\delta_J>J^{-1}$, so $\varepsilon_J\le\delta_J$, while $$J\varepsilon_J  =  \min\left\{\frac J2,\sqrt{J\delta_J}\right\}  \longrightarrow\infty.$$ Use the tangent construction of [\[ex:o1-not-enough\]](TPC-089.md#ref-95126eaef525c0eb) with actual relative error $\varepsilon_J$ on that subsequence. Then $\rho(b_J)=0$, whereas $$J\rho(a_J)=J\varepsilon_J$$ is unbounded. The remaining indices may be filled by any exact pair $a_J=b_J\perp e$.

> **Remark: What the barrier does not say** The theorem is a uniform worst-case statement. A special surrogate may transfer with weaker norm accuracy if one separately proves that its error is almost orthogonal to the constant mode, or proves a signed estimate for $Z(a-b)$ stronger than Cauchy–Schwarz. Such information is additional structure, not a consequence of $\delta=o(1)$.

# Literal fixed-shift crosswalk

###### ref-74910130528fea1f

###### ref-e128310887943550

###### ref-201a9736467e48a6

###### ref-8e46f74481e13b12

Fix one nonzero integer $h_0$. At the high-$\beta$ TPC-32 endpoint, retain the scales $$Q_X=X^{267/400+o(1)},\qquad  J_X=X^{133/400+o(1)},\qquad  C_X=\lfloor J_X\rfloor. \label{eq:critical-scales}$$ Let $$A_X(n)=A_{C_X}(n)$$ be the literal signed normalized-determinant coefficient after all three matched channels and every native contribution in each determinant bin have been aggregated [`\citep{WangTPC32}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-089.md#cite-49ad82103eb75eb7). Define $$\Omega_X=\operatorname{supp}A_X,\qquad  S_X=\#\Omega_X,\qquad  Z_X=\sum_{n\in\Omega_X}A_X(n),\qquad  D_X=\sum_{n\in\Omega_X}|A_X(n)|^2. \label{eq:literal-statistics}$$ TPC-32 gives the imported support envelope $$S_X\ll X^{o(1)}Q_X. \label{eq:literal-support-envelope}$$ When $A_X\ne0$, view $$a_X=(A_X(n))_{n\in\Omega_X}\in\mathbb C^{\Omega_X}$$ and define $$\rho_X  =  \frac{|Z_X|}{\sqrt{S_XD_X}},  \qquad  \mathfrak F_X  =  \frac{|Z_X|^2}{D_X}  =  S_X\rho_X^2. \label{eq:literal-rho-flat}$$

###### ref-7efc51e1280873aa

> **Definition: Literal same-frame surrogate** \[def:literal-surrogate\] A literal same-frame surrogate is a nonzero vector $$b_X\in\mathbb C^{\Omega_X}$$ whose coordinate $b_X(n)$ corresponds to the same normalized determinant bin $n$ as $A_X(n)$. Define $$\delta_X  =  \frac{\|a_X-b_X\|_2}{\|b_X\|_2},  \qquad  \rho(b_X)  =  \frac{\left|\sum_{n\in\Omega_X}b_X(n)\right|}  {\sqrt{S_X}\|b_X\|_2}. \label{eq:literal-surrogate-statistics}$$ No separately chosen surrogate support or channel-wise renormalization is permitted in these definitions.

###### ref-101193343367adba

###### ref-98e43882e9b591e0

###### ref-8c1b8639b584f9bd

###### ref-50485145f694efe7

> **Theorem: Literal surrogate stability certificate** \[thm:literal-surrogate-certificate\] Assume $A_X\ne0$, $S_X\ge2$, and $\delta_X<1$. Then $$|\rho_X-\rho(b_X)|\le\delta_X. \label{eq:literal-rho-stability}$$ In particular, if $$\rho(b_X)\le\frac{X^{o(1)}}{J_X},  \qquad  \delta_X\le\frac{X^{o(1)}}{J_X}, \label{eq:literal-surrogate-hypotheses}$$ then $$\boxed{  \rho_X\le\frac{X^{o(1)}}{J_X},  \qquad  \mathfrak F_X\le X^{1/400+o(1)}.} \label{eq:literal-surrogate-conclusion}$$

> **Proof** Equation [\[eq:literal-rho-stability\]](TPC-089.md#ref-98e43882e9b591e0) is [\[cor:optimal-additive-stability\]](TPC-089.md#ref-44da418e0112ec88) on the frame $\Omega_X$. The hypotheses [\[eq:literal-surrogate-hypotheses\]](TPC-089.md#ref-8c1b8639b584f9bd) therefore give $$\rho_X\le\frac{X^{o(1)}}{J_X}.$$ Using [\[eq:literal-support-envelope\]](TPC-089.md#ref-201a9736467e48a6), $$\mathfrak F_X  =S_X\rho_X^2  \le  X^{o(1)}\frac{Q_X}{J_X^2}.$$ The critical exponents in [\[eq:critical-scales\]](TPC-089.md#ref-74910130528fea1f) give $$\frac{Q_X}{J_X^2}  =  X^{(267-266)/400+o(1)}  =  X^{1/400+o(1)}.$$

###### ref-7466d9106bb7a80b

> **Remark: Logical status of the certificate** \[rem:certificate-status\] is a deterministic implication. It does not construct $b_X$, bound $\delta_X$, or prove its zero-mode angle. Verifying [\[eq:literal-surrogate-hypotheses\]](TPC-089.md#ref-8c1b8639b584f9bd) for the growing literal fixed-shift family is precisely additional arithmetic or structural work.

###### ref-12782416fc1bad7a

> **Remark: A numerical surrogate must resolve the small angle** \[rem:numerical-resolution\] A computation showing only $\delta_X=o(1)$ can accurately reproduce the determinant energy while missing the critical zero-mode angle by many powers of $J_X$. To serve as a rigorous certificate without further signed error structure, it must control the global relative $\ell^2$ error at $X^{o(1)}/J_X$ resolution on the literal frame.

## Direct zero-mode error scale

> **Proposition: Why $J^{-1}$ produces a $Q^2$ zero-mode error** \[prop:direct-zero-mode-scale\] Suppose a same-frame surrogate has $$S_X\ll X^{o(1)}Q_X,\qquad  D(b_X)\ll X^{o(1)}Q_X^3J_X^2,$$ and $$\delta_X\le\frac{X^{o(1)}}{J_X}.$$ Then $$|Z(a_X)-Z(b_X)|  \le X^{o(1)}Q_X^2. \label{eq:direct-zero-mode-scale}$$

## Surrogate-accuracy ledger

The surrogate parameter $$\delta_X=\frac{\|a_X-b_X\|_2}{\|b_X\|_2}$$ has two distinct jobs:

1.  $\delta_X=o(1)$ transfers the determinant-energy scale by [\[eq:energy-transfer\]](TPC-089.md#ref-a66ebfc57ae49f1e);

2.  $\delta_X\le X^{o(1)}/J_X$ transfers the critical principal-angle scale by [\[eq:literal-rho-stability\]](TPC-089.md#ref-98e43882e9b591e0).

The second is strictly stronger. Neither hypothesis is presently verified for a literal TPC-32 surrogate.

> **Proposition: No automatic ledger conversion** \[prop:no-ledger-conversion\] The identities and perturbation inequalities of this paper do not imply any of the following conversions:
>
> 1.  a determinant-energy loss $\lambda_D$ is a physical reassembly loss;
>
> 2.  the approximation exponent in $\delta_X$ is a saving in $\eta_Z$;
>
> 3.  a numerical surrogate error may be subtracted from $\Lambda_{\rm phys}$;
>
> 4.  the inequalities [\[eq:determinant-ledger\]](TPC-089.md#ref-84330fbb25eb9c38) and [\[eq:physical-loss-ledger\]](TPC-089.md#ref-270fca19b4f98b68) may be added into one budget.

> **Proof** The determinant ledger compares the literal post-bin zero mode with literal post-bin energy. The surrogate ledger compares two vectors on that same coefficient space. The physical ledger records losses in the upstream and downstream arithmetic reassembly. No theorem above identifies any pair of these quantities. Therefore no conversion follows.

> **Remark: What a valid crosswalk would require** To enter a surrogate construction into the physical ledger, one would need a literal theorem that tracks its approximation error through every mask, determinant bin, polarization, and outer normalization. To enter determinant-energy loss into the physical ledger, one would need a theorem expressing that loss as an independent consumption of the final saving. Until such statements are proved, the ledgers remain separate and double counting is forbidden.

# Claim boundary and next gate

###### ref-574bb29886012b20

> **Theorem: Audited conclusion** \[thm:audited-conclusion\] Let $a\ne0$ be a finite complex coefficient on its prescribed support of size $S$. Then $$D(a)=\frac{|Z(a)|^2}{S}+D_\perp(a),\qquad  \mathfrak F(a)=S\rho(a)^2.$$ If $a$ is approximated by $b\ne0$ with $$\|a-b\|_2\le\delta\|b\|_2,\qquad\delta<1,$$ then the sharp envelope $$L(\rho(b),\delta)\le\rho(a)\le  U(\rho(b),\delta)$$ holds, and in particular $$|\rho(a)-\rho(b)|\le\delta.$$ The constant is optimal. Consequently, $$\rho(b_J)=O(J^{-1}),\quad  \delta_J=O(J^{-1}) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad  \rho(a_J)=O(J^{-1}),$$ and this order of relative accuracy is uniformly sharp.
>
> For the literal fixed-$h_0$ TPC-32 coefficient on its actual support, the conditional same-frame hypotheses $$\rho(b_X),\delta_X  \le\frac{X^{o(1)}}{J_X}$$ certify $$\mathfrak F_X\le X^{1/400+o(1)}.$$

> **Proof** Combine \[[thm:orthogonal-decomposition](TPC-089.md#ref-ed83ee0ed015373c),[thm:sharp-envelope](TPC-089.md#ref-fa524cf7e35e4211),[cor:optimal-additive-stability](TPC-089.md#ref-44da418e0112ec88),[thm:sharp-accuracy-barrier](TPC-089.md#ref-62b993bef252e2a0),[thm:literal-surrogate-certificate](TPC-089.md#ref-101193343367adba)\].

#### What is proved.

The orthogonal decomposition, exact angle envelope, equality constructions, padding law, $J^{-1}$ accuracy barrier, and finite countermodels are unconditional $\mathrm{L0}$ results. Their placement on the actual fixed-$h_0$ determinant support is an exact $\mathrm{L1}$ stability certificate.

#### What is not proved.

No same-frame surrogate for $A_X$ is constructed. No bound $\delta_X\le X^{o(1)}/J_X$ is proved. No determinant-energy lower bound, distinguished zero-mode estimate, growing fixed-shift Möbius-correlation estimate, or full-block remainder estimate is proved. Thus there is no new $\mathrm{L2}$ arithmetic theorem.

#### Support rule.

The angle must be computed on the actual post-bin support of the literal coefficient. Zero padding changes $\rho$. A surrogate with a different grid or support is covered only after an exact map places it on the literal bins. Otherwise a separate union-frame theorem is required and the present certificate does not apply.

#### Degenerate branches.

If $A_X=0$, one may set $\mathfrak F_X=0$ by convention, but $\rho_X$ is left undefined and no division by $D_X$ is made. This branch does not certify determinant-energy survival. If $A_X\ne0$ and $S_X=1$, then $\rho_X=\mathfrak F_X=1$; the flatness upper target is numerically automatic for large $X$, while the nontrivial two-dimensional sharpness constructions are inapplicable.

#### Accuracy rule.

Relative error $o(1)$ is sufficient to transfer a nonzero determinant-energy scale, but not a $J^{-1}$ principal-angle scale. For a one-sided upper target, $O(J^{-1})$ is the sharp uniform order. For a two-sided $\asymp J^{-1}$ statement, the error constant must also remain strictly below the lower signal constant.

#### Ledger rule.

The determinant compatibility condition $$\lambda_D\le2\eta_Z$$ and the physical stop rule $$\Lambda_{\rm phys}<1/400$$ belong to different ledgers. The surrogate error belongs to a third stability interface. None may be added to, subtracted from, or substituted for another without a proved literal crosswalk.

#### Arithmetic claim boundary.

Nothing here specializes the prescribed nonzero $h_0$ to $2$, breaches the sieve parity barrier, proves a fixed-shift Hardy–Littlewood asymptotic, gives a prime-pair lower bound, or proves the twin-prime conjecture. The critical-angle certificate only states what an eventual surrogate theorem would have to resolve.

#### Next gate.

The most direct positive continuation is to construct a literal same-frame surrogate $b_X$ for which both $$\rho(b_X)\le\frac{X^{o(1)}}{J_X} \quad\text{and}\quad  \|A_X-b_X\|_2  \le\frac{X^{o(1)}}{J_X}\|b_X\|_2$$ can be proved. If only $\delta_X=o(1)$ is accessible, a different route must establish additional signed orthogonality of the error, for example a direct improvement of $$|Z(A_X-b_X)|  \le\sqrt{S_X}\|A_X-b_X\|_2.$$ Failure of both routes would itself identify a precise nonidentifiability barrier, rather than evidence for a prime-pair claim.
