## Abstract

The all-block cover hypothesis in TPC-18 is a physical asymptotic representation with a controlled $o(X)$ remainder, not a consequence of bounded overlap. We formulate a stronger exact sufficient certificate on the literal finite atom space. The columns of a weighted incidence matrix $B$ are the exact candidate block coefficients, and the zero-remainder route is the constrained synthesis equation $Bc=w$, where $w$ is the physical target coefficient. For unrestricted coefficients, the orthogonal residual $(I-BB^\dagger)w$ is the unique least-squares residual and, when nonzero, yields a sharp obstruction through an exact dual variational formula. For nonnegative coefficients, the corresponding cone projection gives both a quantitative residual and a Farkas certificate. More general convex coefficient rules whose attainable images are closed have an analogous support-function dual.

Two explicit families show why overlap bookkeeping alone is insufficient. Even a partition of all atoms into disjoint two-point blocks can stay a fixed relative distance $1/\sqrt{10}$ from the target, and a two-column system may admit a signed exact cover while having nonnegative distance $1/\sqrt2$. We also prove that a cover after collapsing native keys need not lift to a literal cover. Least-squares conditioning, residual stability, and the exact coefficient-amplification cost are recorded. Most importantly, only an exact literal identity transports the original global normalization without an additional remainder theorem.

Applied to TPC-18, the framework is an $\mathrm{L1}$ certificate schema for the exact zero-remainder route through the still-unproved all-block representation gate. It neither constructs that exact certificate nor controls a nonzero physical remainder, and it does not prove the primitive determinant-dispersion estimate. The determinant/zero-mode condition $\lambda_D\le 2\eta_Z$, the strict physical-loss rule $\Lambda_{\mathrm{phys}}<1/400$, and the TPC-18 all-block representation gate remain independent. No $\mathrm{L2}$ fixed-shift arithmetic estimate, parity breakthrough, or prime-pair conclusion is claimed.

# Introduction

The TPC-18 stopping argument isolates a primitive large-determinant/low-gcd residual inside each admissible symmetric tail block [`\citep{WangTPC18}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-091.md#cite-4abf939102bd1649). Its conditional global conclusion requires more than a good estimate on each block: the physical hard packet must be represented by the entire proposed block family, with the correct unit coefficients, overlaps, masks, and normalization, up to a controlled $o(X)$ physical remainder. That requirement was deliberately left as an all-block representation hypothesis.

A bounded-overlap partition is often enough for inequalities because one may estimate each atom a bounded number of times. It is not enough for an identity. If two blocks overlap, the shared atom receives the sum of both block coefficients. If the block weights cannot reproduce the target coefficient exactly, dividing by a support multiplicity does not repair signs, phases, masks, or nonconstant block profiles. This distinction is especially important when a later argument is calibrated to a single global main term. An exact coefficient identity is a sufficient, zero-remainder way to verify this hypothesis. It is stronger than the original asymptotic statement: a nonzero coefficient residual may also be admissible if a separate theorem proves that its physical image is $o(X)$.

The purpose of this paper is to turn that qualitative warning into an exact finite-dimensional certificate. Let $\Omega_X$ be the actual atom set and let $w_X$ be the literal coefficient of the physical packet. Each candidate block contributes one exact column $b_j$. The synthesis matrix $$B_X=(b_1\ \cdots\ b_m)$$ contains all overlap information. A zero-remainder cover is precisely $$B_Xc_X=w_X,$$ together with the coefficient restrictions demanded by the decomposition. Linear algebra then separates three questions:

1.  does the target lie in the column space?

2.  if signed coefficients are forbidden, does it lie in the allowed cone or polytope?

3.  if it does, what coefficient amplification is paid when the block estimates are reassembled?

## Main results

The first result is the exact subspace certificate. With $P_B=BB^\dagger$, $$\inf_c\|w-Bc\|_2=\|(I-P_B)w\|_2.$$ The residual is zero exactly when a cover exists. When it is nonzero, its normalized direction annihilates every candidate block while detecting the target, so it is a short dual certificate of failure.

The second result is the constrained analogue. For real nonnegative coefficients, a cover exists exactly when $$B^{\mathsf T}y\ge0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad  \langle y,w\rangle\ge0.$$ Failure therefore has a Farkas witness. Cone projection yields the quantitative distance formula $$\operatorname{dist}(w,B\mathbb R_+^m)  =  \max_{\substack{\|y\|_2\le1\\B^{\mathsf T}y\le0}}  \langle y,w\rangle.$$ A support-function formula handles any closed convex allowed coefficient image.

The third result consists of sharp countermodels. A disjoint partition of $2n$ atoms into $n$ two-point blocks has overlap one, covers every atom as a set, and yet may have relative least-squares defect exactly $1/\sqrt{10}$ for every $n$. A separate two-column example has an exact signed cover but no nonnegative cover, with cone distance $1/\sqrt2$. Thus neither bounded overlap nor full set coverage implies exact or asymptotically approximate coefficient coverage.

Finally, we state the only automatic normalization transfer. If $Bc=w$ exactly, then every linear physical statistic with its original normalizer satisfies the exact reassembly identity, and the block error is amplified by $\|c\|_1$. If $w=Bc+r$ only approximately, the physical remainder is the additional term carried by $r$. Small Euclidean residual alone does not remove it, although a separate physical bound on that term can verify the original asymptotic TPC-18 route.

## Claim levels

The projection identities, dual formulas, counterexamples, and conditioning bounds are unconditional $\mathrm{L0}$ statements. Their literal placement on the TPC-18 atom space gives an exact $\mathrm{L1}$ certificate schema. We do not construct the physical incidence matrix from the whole preceding normal form, prove its feasibility, or prove the primitive determinant-dispersion estimate. Consequently there is no new $\mathrm{L2}$ arithmetic theorem.

# The literal atom–block model

## Atoms, native keys, and weighted incidence

Fix $X$ and one prescribed nonzero shift $h_0$. Let $\Omega=\Omega_X$ be a finite set of *literal atoms*. An atom retains every key needed to reconstruct its physical contribution: source variables, divisor variables, dyadic masks, determinant row, residue data, and any provenance label. Two atoms with the same numerical determinant remain distinct when their other native keys differ.

Let $\mathbb K$ be either $\mathbb R$ or $\mathbb C$, and equip $\mathbb K^\Omega$ with the Euclidean inner product and norm. The target coefficient is $$w=(w_\omega)_{\omega\in\Omega}\in\mathbb K^\Omega.$$ The set $\Omega$ is the actual active universe: it contains both the support of $w$ and every atom emitted by a candidate block. Thus a column that produces an unwanted atom is represented by a nonzero entry in a row where $w_\omega=0$; such leakage cannot be deleted by restricting the matrix after the fact.

###### ref-ba396565017a2c7e

Let $\mathcal J=\{1,\dots,m\}$ index the candidate blocks. For block $j$, let $$I_{\omega j}=\mathbf 1_{\{\omega\text{ occurs in block }j\}}$$ be its support incidence and let $g_{\omega j}\in\mathbb K$ be its exact unit block coefficient. The *weighted incidence matrix* is $$B_{\omega j}=I_{\omega j}g_{\omega j}.  \label{eq:weighted-incidence}$$ Its $j$-th column $b_j$ is therefore the literal coefficient vector delivered by one unit of that block. In the unweighted set cover model $g_{\omega j}=1$, but the weighted form is essential when masks, signs, or phases vary inside a block.

###### ref-8e14be3784231bec

> **Definition: Overlap degree** \[def:overlap-degree\] The support overlap degree is $$\Delta(I)=\max_{\omega\in\Omega}  \sum_{j=1}^m I_{\omega j}.$$ It counts how many candidate columns touch an atom. It contains no information about the values $g_{\omega j}$, the target $w_\omega$, or the existence of a synthesis coefficient.

## Exact and constrained covers

###### ref-9048e7530ae00a0e

###### ref-a623b7d24e6d2aeb

###### ref-d36c0914a976a768

> **Definition: Literal cover** \[def:literal-cover\] Let $\varnothing\ne\mathscr C\subseteq\mathbb K^m$ be the allowed coefficient set. A vector $c\in\mathscr C$ is an *exact literal cover* when $$Bc=w.  \label{eq:exact-cover}$$ The cover defect is $$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cov}}(B,w;\mathscr C)  =  \operatorname{dist}(w,B\mathscr C)  =  \inf_{c\in\mathscr C}\|w-Bc\|_2.  \label{eq:cover-defect}$$

###### ref-6bf5087236084fa2

> **Remark: Membership versus zero distance** \[rem:membership-vs-distance\] Exact feasibility is the membership statement $$w\in B\mathscr C.$$ If $B\mathscr C$ is closed, this is equivalent to $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cov}}=0$. Without closedness, zero distance need not be attained and is not an exact cover. Every later use of $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cov}}=0$ as a binary certificate is therefore accompanied by closedness; otherwise the membership statement is used directly.

The relevant choices of $\mathscr C$ include: $$\mathbb K^m  \quad\text{(unrestricted signed or complex synthesis)},\qquad  \mathbb R_+^m  \quad\text{(nonnegative synthesis)},$$ as well as affine or polyhedral rules that encode a prescribed partition of unity. The coefficient rule is part of the theorem: an unrestricted solution does not certify a nonnegative cover.

###### ref-7d5cce057729d649

> **Remark: No multiplicity division** \[rem:no-multiplicity-division\] Replacing every target coefficient by $w_\omega/\sum_jI_{\omega j}$ is not an overlap correction. It changes the target and ignores the block profiles $g_{\omega j}$. The only exact correction in the present model is solving [\[eq:exact-cover\]](TPC-091.md#ref-a623b7d24e6d2aeb).

## Determinant compatibility is metadata, not coverage

For every atom $\omega$, let $\mathfrak d(\omega)$ denote its literal determinant key and let $\mathfrak p(\omega)$ denote the rest of its provenance. A candidate column is called *TPC-18 compatible* only if every nonzero entry:

1.  belongs to the same prescribed shift $h_0$;

2.  obeys the required block geometry and masks;

3.  carries its native pair $(\mathfrak d(\omega),\mathfrak p(\omega))$; and

4.  uses the same physical atomic normalization as $w$.

Compatibility is checked before $B$ is formed. A solution using an incompatible column is not a physical cover.

###### ref-bfc1677e79437342

> **Proposition: Compatibility and coverage are independent** \[prop:compatibility-independent\] Neither of the following implications is valid in general:
>
> 1.  every column is determinant-compatible $\Longrightarrow w\in\operatorname{ran}B$;
>
> 2.  $w\in\operatorname{ran}B$ $\Longrightarrow$ the columns are determinant-compatible.

> **Proof** For (i), take any compatible nonzero column $b_1$ and a target not in its one-dimensional span. For (ii), append to a valid synthesis system an algebraically identical column whose provenance metadata is wrong; its span is unchanged. Column-space membership alone cannot inspect metadata.

> **Remark: Determinant energy** The Gram matrix $G=B^*B$ records column overlap and, under an exact cover, gives $\|w\|_2^2=c^*Gc$. This identity is not a lower bound for the literal determinant energy in another coefficient space and does not estimate a distinguished zero mode. Such arithmetic comparisons remain separate inputs.

# Projection and dual certificates

## Unrestricted coefficients

###### ref-6434188175385688

Let $B^\dagger$ be the Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse and let $$P_B=BB^\dagger$$ be the orthogonal projection onto $\operatorname{ran}B$ [`\citep{GolubVanLoan2013}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-091.md#cite-be8a5d68f1cad0f1). Write $$q_*=P_Bw,\qquad  r_*=(I-P_B)w.  \label{eq:subspace-projection}$$

###### ref-c459092ad1bb2e46

###### ref-a0adac4dc0093a8c

###### ref-7418581f09a4ba70

> **Theorem: Exact subspace cover certificate** \[thm:subspace-certificate\] For $B\in\mathbb K^{n\times m}$ and $w\in\mathbb K^n$, $$\begin{aligned}  \inf_{c\in\mathbb K^m}\|w-Bc\|_2  &=\|r_*\|_2,  \label{eq:least-squares-distance}\\  \|r_*\|_2  &=  \max_{\substack{y\in\mathbb K^n\\                   \|y\|_2\le1,\ B^*y=0}}  \operatorname{Re}\langle y,w\rangle.  \label{eq:subspace-dual}\end{aligned}$$ In particular, the following are equivalent:
>
> 1.  $Bc=w$ for some $c\in\mathbb K^m$;
>
> 2.  $r_*=0$;
>
> 3.  $\langle y,w\rangle=0$ for every $y\in\ker B^*$.
>
> If $r_*\ne0$, then $$y_*=\frac{r_*}{\|r_*\|_2}$$ is a unit dual obstruction satisfying $$B^*y_*=0,\qquad  \operatorname{Re}\langle y_*,w\rangle=\|r_*\|_2.$$

> **Proof** The orthogonal decomposition $$w-Bc=r_*+(q_*-Bc), \qquad  r_*\perp\operatorname{ran}B,$$ gives $$\|w-Bc\|_2^2  =  \|r_*\|_2^2+\|q_*-Bc\|_2^2.$$ This proves [\[eq:least-squares-distance\]](TPC-091.md#ref-a0adac4dc0093a8c); equality is attained, for example, at $c=B^\dagger w$.
>
> If $B^*y=0$ and $\|y\|_2\le1$, then $$\operatorname{Re}\langle y,w\rangle  =  \operatorname{Re}\langle y,r_*\rangle  \le\|r_*\|_2.$$ When $r_*\ne0$, equality holds at $y_*$. When $r_*=0$, the maximum is zero because $y=0$ is feasible. This proves [\[eq:subspace-dual\]](TPC-091.md#ref-7418581f09a4ba70) and the equivalences.

###### ref-ffe63486b25168e8

> **Corollary: A posteriori lower bound** \[cor:dual-lower-bound\] Any vector $y$ with $B^*y=0$ and $\|y\|_2\le1$ certifies $$\|w-Bc\|_2  \ge  \operatorname{Re}\langle y,w\rangle$$ for every $c$. Thus a rational or interval-verified $y$ can certify a nonzero cover defect without computing a pseudoinverse.

## Nonnegative coefficients

Suppose now that $B$ and $w$ are real and that $\mathscr C=\mathbb R_+^m$. The attainable set $$K=B\mathbb R_+^m$$ is a finitely generated closed convex cone.

###### ref-0b85c97875fc1829

###### ref-71248fb6c0c356ef

> **Theorem: Farkas and quantitative cone certificates** \[thm:farkas-cone\] Exactly one of the following alternatives holds:
>
> 1.  there is $c\ge0$ such that $Bc=w$;
>
> 2.  there is $y\in\mathbb R^n$ such that $B^{\mathsf T}y\ge0$ and $\langle y,w\rangle<0$.
>
> Moreover, $$\operatorname{dist}(w,K)  =  \max_{\substack{y\in\mathbb R^n\\                   \|y\|_2\le1,\ B^{\mathsf T}y\le0}}  \langle y,w\rangle.  \label{eq:cone-distance-dual}$$ If $q=P_Kw$, $r=w-q$, and $r\ne0$, then $$B^{\mathsf T}r\le0,\qquad  \langle r,q\rangle=0,$$ so $-r$ is a Farkas obstruction and $r/\|r\|_2$ attains [\[eq:cone-distance-dual\]](TPC-091.md#ref-71248fb6c0c356ef).

> **Proof** The alternative is the finite-dimensional Farkas lemma [`\citep{Schrijver1986}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-091.md#cite-03e05d4b9df95d53). For the quantitative statement, the projection inequality for the closed convex cone $K$ gives $$\langle w-q,z-q\rangle\le0  \qquad(z\in K).$$ Taking $z=0$ and $z=2q$ yields $\langle r,q\rangle=0$. Taking $z=tBe_j$, $t\ge0$, gives $B^{\mathsf T}r\le0$. Hence the right side of [\[eq:cone-distance-dual\]](TPC-091.md#ref-71248fb6c0c356ef) is at least $\|r\|_2$. Conversely, if $B^{\mathsf T}y\le0$, then $\langle y,z\rangle\le0$ for all $z\in K$, and therefore $$\langle y,w\rangle  \le  \langle y,w-q\rangle  \le  \|r\|_2$$ for $\|y\|_2\le1$. This proves the formula. Finally, $$\langle-r,w\rangle  =  -\langle r,q+r\rangle  =  -\|r\|_2^2<0,$$ so $-r$ has the required Farkas signs.

## General convex rules with closed attainable images

For a nonempty set $\mathscr C\subseteq\mathbb K^m$, define its support function $$\sigma_{\mathscr C}(u)  =  \sup_{c\in\mathscr C}  \operatorname{Re}\langle u,c\rangle.$$ The projection and support-function formulation below is standard finite-dimensional convex analysis [`\citep{Rockafellar1970,BauschkeCombettes2017}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-091.md#cite-92a0199d3fd7bd68); [\[BauschkeCombettes2017\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-091.md#cite-5da48dee15c6c75c).

###### ref-14c855da8bc49317

###### ref-cdc574ddac054c89

> **Theorem: Convex allowed-set dual** \[thm:convex-dual\] Let $\mathscr C$ be nonempty and convex, and assume $B\mathscr C$ is closed. Then $$\operatorname{dist}(w,B\mathscr C)  =  \max_{\|y\|_2\le1}  \left\{  \operatorname{Re}\langle y,w\rangle  -  \sigma_{\mathscr C}(B^*y)  \right\}.  \label{eq:convex-distance-dual}$$ In particular, an allowed exact cover exists if and only if the right side is zero.

> **Proof** Put $K=B\mathscr C$, and let $q=P_Kw$. For every $\|y\|_2\le1$ and $z\in K$, $$\operatorname{Re}\langle y,w\rangle-\sigma_K(y)  \le  \operatorname{Re}\langle y,w-z\rangle  \le  \|w-z\|_2.$$ Taking the infimum in $z$ gives the upper bound by $\operatorname{dist}(w,K)$. If $w\notin K$, the projection inequality shows that $y=(w-q)/\|w-q\|_2$ supports $K$ at $q$; hence $\sigma_K(y)=\operatorname{Re}\langle y,q\rangle$, and equality holds. If $w\in K$, $y=0$ gives equality at zero. Finally, $\sigma_K(y)=\sigma_{\mathscr C}(B^*y)$.

> **Remark: Closedness** The image of an arbitrary closed convex set under a linear map need not be closed. The hypothesis in [\[thm:convex-dual\]](TPC-091.md#ref-14c855da8bc49317) is automatic for the coefficient sets used most often here: compact polytopes and polyhedra. It is stated explicitly rather than hidden in the projection notation.

# Bounded overlap does not imply coverage

Bounded overlap provides an upper-norm inequality. It does not provide a lower frame bound, surjectivity, or coefficient feasibility.

###### ref-713ae2bd9d780eff

###### ref-5025d100031c5774

> **Lemma: What overlap actually controls** \[lem:overlap-upper-bound\] Put $S_j=\{\omega\in\Omega:I_{\omega j}=1\}$. For the weighted incidence matrix [\[eq:weighted-incidence\]](TPC-091.md#ref-ba396565017a2c7e), $$\|Bc\|_2^2  \le  \Delta(I)  \sum_{\omega\in\Omega}\sum_{j=1}^m  I_{\omega j}|g_{\omega j}c_j|^2.  \label{eq:overlap-upper-bound}$$ In particular, if $|g_{\omega j}|\le G$, then $$\|Bc\|_2^2  \le  \Delta(I)G^2  \sum_{j=1}^m |c_j|^2|S_j|.$$

> **Proof** For each row $\omega$, Cauchy–Schwarz gives $$\left|  \sum_j I_{\omega j}g_{\omega j}c_j  \right|^2  \le  \left(\sum_jI_{\omega j}\right)  \sum_jI_{\omega j}|g_{\omega j}c_j|^2.$$ Sum over $\omega$. The last displayed specialization follows by interchanging the sums and using $\sum_\omega I_{\omega j}=|S_j|$.

> **Remark** The right side of [\[eq:overlap-upper-bound\]](TPC-091.md#ref-5025d100031c5774) is an upper synthesis bound. No reverse inequality follows from $\Delta(I)<\infty$: columns may be linearly dependent, miss directions, or conflict with the target weights.

## A disjoint full-support family with fixed residual

###### ref-13510c642b4a9a41

> **Theorem: Overlap one, but no asymptotic cover** \[thm:partition-counterexample\] For every $n\ge1$, there is an unweighted incidence matrix $B_n\in\{0,1\}^{2n\times n}$ and a positive target $w_n\in\mathbb R^{2n}$ such that:
>
> 1.  every atom lies in exactly one block, so $\Delta(I)=1$ and the blocks partition the full support;
>
> 2.  no signed, complex, or nonnegative exact cover exists;
>
> 3.  the exact least-squares defect is $$\operatorname{dist}(w_n,\operatorname{ran}B_n)=\sqrt{\frac n2};$$
>
> 4.  the relative defect is the fixed constant $$\frac{\operatorname{dist}(w_n,\operatorname{ran}B_n)}{\|w_n\|_2}   =\frac1{\sqrt{10}}.$$

> **Proof** Let $$b_j=e_{2j-1}+e_{2j},  \qquad  w_n=\sum_{j=1}^n(e_{2j-1}+2e_{2j}).$$ The columns have disjoint supports and every row is present once. For any $c=(c_j)$, $$\|w_n-B_nc\|_2^2  =  \sum_{j=1}^n  \left(|1-c_j|^2+|2-c_j|^2\right).$$ Each summand is uniquely minimized at $c_j=3/2$, where it equals $1/2$. The minimizer is nonnegative, so imposing $c\ge0$ does not improve or worsen the minimum. Hence the squared defect is $n/2$, whereas $\|w_n\|_2^2=5n$. The positive defect excludes every exact cover.

###### ref-f4c9c0fd87e9546d

> **Corollary: No approximate-cover consequence** \[cor:no-approx-from-overlap\] Even the conjunction of bounded overlap, full set coverage, positive target weights, and a disjoint block partition does not imply $$\operatorname{dist}(w_X,\operatorname{ran}B_X)=o(\|w_X\|_2).$$

## Signed feasibility does not imply allowed feasibility

###### ref-354866d62cb215bc

> **Theorem: A sharp two-column cone obstruction** \[thm:cone-counterexample\] Let $$B=  \begin{pmatrix}  1&0\\  1&1  \end{pmatrix},  \qquad  w=  \begin{pmatrix}  1\\0  \end{pmatrix}.$$ Then:
>
> 1.  every row occurs in a block and the overlap degree is two;
>
> 2.  $Bc=w$ has the unique signed solution $c=(1,-1)^{\mathsf T}$;
>
> 3.  no nonnegative cover exists;
>
> 4.  $$\operatorname{dist}(w,B\mathbb R_+^2)=\frac1{\sqrt2}.$$
>
> The vector $y=(-1,1)^{\mathsf T}$ is a Farkas certificate: $$B^{\mathsf T}y=(0,1)^{\mathsf T}\ge0,  \qquad  \langle y,w\rangle=-1<0.$$

> **Proof** The signed equation gives $c_1=1$ from the first row and $c_1+c_2=0$ from the second. For $c_1,c_2\ge0$, $$\|w-Bc\|_2^2  =  (1-c_1)^2+(c_1+c_2)^2.$$ At fixed $c_1$, the minimum in $c_2\ge0$ occurs at $c_2=0$. The remaining quadratic is minimized at $c_1=1/2$, with value $1/2$. The displayed Farkas signs are immediate.

## Native-key collapse can create a false cover

Let $A:\mathbb K^\Omega\to\mathbb K^\Gamma$ aggregate literal atoms into coarse classes, for example by summing all atoms with equal numerical determinant.

###### ref-26439f143502c869

###### ref-cb3c908c528a57cd

> **Proposition: Coarse feasibility need not lift** \[prop:coarse-does-not-lift\] The coarse equation $$ABc=Aw  \label{eq:coarse-cover}$$ is necessary but not sufficient for the literal equation $Bc=w$. The failure can occur with one block, one coarse key, and overlap one.

> **Proof** Take two literal atoms with the same coarse key, $$A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\end{pmatrix},  \qquad  B=e_1,  \qquad  w=e_2.$$ Then $AB=Aw=1$, so $c=1$ solves the coarse equation. But $Bc=e_1\ne e_2=w$, and the literal defect is $\|e_2-e_1\|_2=\sqrt2$. The two atoms may have the same determinant and different provenance; aggregation has hidden exactly that difference.

> **Remark: Union-frame rule** If a candidate column emits atoms outside $\operatorname{supp}w$, the correct universe is the union of target and column supports, with target coefficient zero on the extra rows. Deleting those rows can turn leakage into a false exact cover, just as [\[prop:coarse-does-not-lift\]](TPC-091.md#ref-26439f143502c869) turns a literal mismatch into a coarse identity.

###### ref-b65970b7bd2f145a

\[ex:three-row-overlap\] Let $$B=  \begin{pmatrix}  1&0\\  1&1\\  0&1  \end{pmatrix},  \qquad  w=(1,1,1)^{\mathsf T}.$$ The first and third rows force $c_1=c_2=1$, while the middle row would then equal $2$. Thus there is no exact cover although every atom is covered and the overlap is two. The least-squares solution is $c_1=c_2=2/3$, with residual $(1,-1,1)^{\mathsf T}/3$ and relative defect $1/3$. It gives a three-row binary example of why an ad hoc division by overlap multiplicity cannot replace the literal system.

# Least-squares stability and exact normalization

## Conditioning of the unrestricted certificate

Assume $B\ne0$, and let $\sigma_+(B)$ be its smallest positive singular value.

###### ref-7ce34456bf56dd13

###### ref-e0a92f38a147f9e4

###### ref-09d16bd4b4dd05d5

###### ref-f58b497b23f3666b

> **Proposition: Minimum-norm synthesis and target stability** \[prop:least-squares-stability\] The vector $$c^\dagger=B^\dagger w$$ is the unique minimum-norm least-squares coefficient, and $$\|c^\dagger\|_2  \le  \frac{\|P_Bw\|_2}{\sigma_+(B)}  \le  \frac{\|w\|_2}{\sigma_+(B)}.  \label{eq:min-norm-bound}$$ For two targets $w,w'$, $$\begin{aligned}  \left|  \operatorname{dist}(w,\operatorname{ran}B)-\operatorname{dist}(w',\operatorname{ran}B)  \right|  &\le\|w-w'\|_2,  \label{eq:residual-lipschitz}\\  \|B^\dagger w-B^\dagger w'\|_2  &\le  \frac{\|w-w'\|_2}{\sigma_+(B)}.  \label{eq:coefficient-lipschitz}\end{aligned}$$

> **Proof** The singular-value decomposition gives the minimum-norm property and $\|B^\dagger\|_{2\to2}=\sigma_+(B)^{-1}$, proving \[[eq:min-norm-bound](TPC-091.md#ref-e0a92f38a147f9e4),[eq:coefficient-lipschitz](TPC-091.md#ref-f58b497b23f3666b)\]. The distance to a fixed nonempty closed set is $1$-Lipschitz: $$\operatorname{dist}(w,\operatorname{ran}B)  \le  \|w-w'\|_2+\operatorname{dist}(w',\operatorname{ran}B),$$ and the reverse inequality follows by interchanging $w,w'$.

> **Remark: Residual stability versus coefficient stability** The residual defect is always stable with constant one. The synthesis coefficients may be unstable when $\sigma_+(B)$ is small. Therefore a numerically tiny residual is not by itself a well-conditioned symbolic certificate.

###### ref-37927d80b353b105

###### ref-8b113f24500fcc6a

###### ref-2f5bece48259c612

> **Proposition: Column perturbation with bounded coefficients** \[prop:column-perturbation\] Let $\widetilde B=B+E$. If $Bc=w$, then $$\operatorname{dist}(w,\operatorname{ran}\widetilde B)  \le  \|E\|_{2\to2}\|c\|_2.  \label{eq:column-perturbation}$$ More generally, if $\varnothing\ne\mathscr C$ is contained in the radius-$R$ ball, then $$\left|  \operatorname{dist}(w,B\mathscr C)  -  \operatorname{dist}(w,\widetilde B\mathscr C)  \right|  \le  R\|E\|_{2\to2}.  \label{eq:allowed-image-stability}$$ No uniform bound of this form follows for an unbounded coefficient cone without an a priori bound on the relevant coefficients.

> **Proof** Using the same coefficient $c$, $$\|w-\widetilde Bc\|_2=\|Ec\|_2,$$ which proves [\[eq:column-perturbation\]](TPC-091.md#ref-8b113f24500fcc6a). For every $c\in\mathscr C$, $$\|Bc-\widetilde Bc\|_2\le R\|E\|_{2\to2}.$$ Thus the two attainable sets have Hausdorff distance at most the right side, and their distances from $w$ differ by no more than that amount.

## The exact normalization identity

###### ref-106ed0acf0f16ba8

Let $\mathcal N_X>0$ be the original global normalizer and let $\Phi_{\omega,X}\in\mathbb C$ be the physical contribution of atom $\omega$. Define the normalized linear statistic $$\mathcal T_X(v)  =  \frac1{\mathcal N_X}  \sum_{\omega\in\Omega_X}  v_\omega\Phi_{\omega,X}.  \label{eq:physical-functional}$$ No atom-dependent renormalization is allowed in this definition.

###### ref-bb61f587f51a4c40

###### ref-2f640154a0b0209d

###### ref-385f6d43fee5c1d0

> **Theorem: Exact reassembly and coefficient cost** \[thm:exact-reassembly\] If $Bc=w$ exactly, then $$\mathcal T_X(w)  =  \sum_{j=1}^m c_j\mathcal T_X(b_j).  \label{eq:exact-physical-reassembly}$$ If, on the same normalization, $$\mathcal T_X(b_j)=M_j+O(E_j),$$ then $$\mathcal T_X(w)  =  \sum_{j=1}^m c_jM_j  +  O\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^m|c_j|E_j\right).  \label{eq:coefficient-amplification}$$ In particular, a uniform block error $E$ is amplified by at most $\|c\|_1E$.

> **Proof** Apply the same linear functional $\mathcal T_X$ to $Bc=w$. The error estimate follows from the triangle inequality.

###### ref-5831e8217ba7997b

> **Definition: Exact-cover amplification** \[def:cover-amplification\] For an allowed exact cover, define $$\tau_{\mathrm{cov}}(B,w;\mathscr C)  =  \inf_{\substack{c\in\mathscr C\\Bc=w}}\|c\|_1,$$ with value $+\infty$ when no exact cover exists. When block errors are nonuniform, the intrinsic cost is instead $$\inf_{\substack{c\in\mathscr C\\Bc=w}}  \sum_j|c_j|E_j.$$

> **Remark: Logarithmic estimates** If every block has error $O(X(\log X)^{-A})$ for arbitrary fixed $A$, then a cover cost $\|c_X\|_1=O((\log X)^C)$ preserves arbitrary logarithmic saving after increasing $A$. The weaker statement $\|c_X\|_1=X^{o(1)}$ is not enough for that conclusion: $\exp(\sqrt{\log X})=X^{o(1)}$ dominates every fixed power of $\log X$. The exact product $\|c_X\|_1E_X$ must be checked.

## Why an approximate cover is a different theorem

###### ref-29f69efc050b6f3c

###### ref-3e0bdf424d4717f4

> **Proposition: Residual remainder identity** \[prop:residual-remainder\] For an arbitrary coefficient $c$, put $r=w-Bc$. Then $$\mathcal T_X(w)  =  \sum_jc_j\mathcal T_X(b_j)  +  \mathcal T_X(r).  \label{eq:residual-remainder}$$ The Euclidean statement $\|r\|_2=o(\|w\|_2)$ does not, by itself, imply $\mathcal T_X(r)=o(1)$ or any prescribed physical error bound.

> **Proof** The identity follows by linearity. For the obstruction, let $\varepsilon_n\downarrow0$, take one block $B_n=e_2$, $c_n=1$, and $$w_n=e_2+\varepsilon_ne_1,\qquad r_n=w_n-B_nc_n=\varepsilon_ne_1.$$ Define $\mathcal T_n(v)=\varepsilon_n^{-1}v_1$. Then $\|r_n\|_2/\|w_n\|_2\to0$, but $\mathcal T_n(r_n)=1$. Equivalently, the only automatic estimate is $$|\mathcal T_X(r)|  \le  \|\mathcal T_X\|_{2\to\mathbb C}\|r\|_2,$$ and the physical operator norm has not been bounded by an incidence calculation.

###### ref-4d7721e41ba05e6c

> **Corollary: Exactness threshold for a pure cover argument** \[cor:exactness-threshold\] A block-cover argument transports the original physical statistic without a new remainder estimate only when $w\in B\mathscr C$ in the literal frame. When $B\mathscr C$ is closed, this is equivalent to $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cov}}=0$. If no exact allowed cover is used, closure requires an independent bound for $\mathcal T_X(r)$ on the explicitly identified residual.

> **Remark** This corollary does not say that approximate covers are useless. It says that they are not exact covers. A physical norm estimate for the residual could be valuable, but it is an additional arithmetic input and must be labeled as such.

# An exact sufficient certificate for the TPC-18 cover gate

TPC-18 proves a stopping dichotomy inside an admissible symmetric tail block and states a conditional global implication under two additional inputs [`\citep{WangTPC18}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-091.md#cite-4abf939102bd1649): a primitive generic determinant-dispersion estimate and an all-block representation of the upstream hard packet up to $o(X)$ in the physical statistic. The present section gives an exact zero-remainder certificate for the second input and records the distinct controlled-residual route.

## The physical incidence system

For a fixed nonzero $h_0$, let $w_X$ be the literal atomic coefficient of the hard packet inherited from the upstream normal form. Construct $\Omega_X$ from the union of its active atoms and the atoms of every proposed TPC-18 block. Let $b_{j,X}$ be the literal coefficient of the $j$-th candidate block, including both the prefix and complementary-tail pieces, and form $$B_X=(b_{1,X}\ \cdots\ b_{m_X,X}).$$ The upstream derivation also determines an allowed coefficient set $\mathscr C_X$. It may be a singleton, a partition-of-unity polytope, or another explicitly derived set.

###### ref-7ef40698eca85a2e

> **Definition: Exact TPC-18 full-block certificate** \[def:tpc18-certificate\] An exact full-block cover certificate at scale $X$ consists of:
>
> 1.  the complete literal row list $\Omega_X$, with native keys and zero target coefficients retained on every leakage row;
>
> 2.  the exact weighted incidence matrix $B_X$;
>
> 3.  an allowed coefficient $c_X\in\mathscr C_X$ satisfying $$B_Xc_X=w_X;$$
>
> 4.  a proof-producing exact verification of the equality, together with a rigorous bound for the coefficient amplification in the physical normalization.

> **Remark: The original unit cover** If the upstream normal form requires the unweighted sum of all candidate blocks, then $\mathscr C_X=\{\mathbf 1\}$, and the certificate is the single identity $$B_X\mathbf 1=w_X.$$ Finding some $c_X\in\mathbb R^{m_X}$ is not a substitute. Allowing different coefficients changes the decomposition unless that freedom has been derived upstream. Failure of the exact identity $B_X\mathbf 1=w_X$ does not by itself refute the original TPC-18 hypothesis: the separate controlled-residual route may still prove $\mathcal T_X(w_X-B_X\mathbf 1)=o(X)$.

###### ref-d35f45e00d05e055

###### ref-bd45aa8dcd9f06f0

> **Theorem: Conditional exact-cover transfer** \[thm:tpc18-cover-transfer\] Assume an exact TPC-18 full-block certificate in the sense of [\[def:tpc18-certificate\]](TPC-091.md#ref-7ef40698eca85a2e). Let the normalized physical functional be $\mathcal T_X$, and suppose each certified block has a decomposition $$\mathcal T_X(b_{j,X})=M_{j,X}+E_{j,X}.$$ Then $$\mathcal T_X(w_X)  =  \sum_{j=1}^{m_X}c_{j,X}M_{j,X}  +  O\!\left(  \sum_{j=1}^{m_X}|c_{j,X}||E_{j,X}|  \right).  \label{eq:tpc18-exact-transfer}$$ Consequently, if the upstream calibrated main term equals $\sum_jc_{j,X}M_{j,X}$ and the displayed weighted error is admissible, the all-block reassembly step is valid with no overlap correction beyond $B_Xc_X=w_X$.

> **Proof** This is [\[thm:exact-reassembly\]](TPC-091.md#ref-bb61f587f51a4c40) applied on the literal TPC-18 frame. The hypotheses about the calibrated main term and error are stated separately because they are not consequences of incidence feasibility.

###### ref-862af2923a4f883a

> **Proposition: Controlled-residual route** \[prop:tpc18-controlled-residual\] Let $c_X$ be the coefficient prescribed by the upstream decomposition, put $r_X=w_X-B_Xc_X$, and retain the original physical functional $\mathcal T_X$. Then $$\mathcal T_X(w_X)  =  \sum_jc_{j,X}\mathcal T_X(b_{j,X})  +  \mathcal T_X(r_X).$$ Thus a theorem proving $\mathcal T_X(r_X)=o(X)$, together with the required block estimates and the calibrated main term, can verify the original asymptotic all-block hypothesis even when $r_X\ne0$. Euclidean smallness of $r_X$ alone does not supply this theorem.

> **Proof** The identity is [\[prop:residual-remainder\]](TPC-091.md#ref-29f69efc050b6f3c). The remaining statements distinguish an assumed physical remainder estimate from the Euclidean incidence calculation.

###### ref-028210d3a8c47710

> **Corollary: A checkable failure certificate** \[cor:tpc18-failure-certificate\] If unrestricted synthesis is permitted and $$r_X=(I-B_XB_X^\dagger)w_X\ne0,$$ then $r_X/\|r_X\|_2$ certifies that the proposed block family cannot exactly cover the TPC-18 hard packet. If nonnegative synthesis is required, any Farkas vector from [\[thm:farkas-cone\]](TPC-091.md#ref-0b85c97875fc1829) certifies failure of that allowed exact cover. Such a certificate is a rigorous negative $\mathrm{L1}$ result for the zero-remainder route; it does not rule out a controlled physical residual and is not an arithmetic cancellation theorem.

## What this does not supply

###### ref-1fcb1b3a84ff9dc4

> **Proposition: Independent downstream inputs** \[prop:independent-downstream-inputs\] Even an exact, well-conditioned full-block certificate does not imply:
>
> 1.  the TPC-18 primitive generic determinant-dispersion estimate;
>
> 2.  a determinant-energy lower bound or distinguished zero-mode cancellation estimate for a later post-bin coefficient;
>
> 3.  the prefix estimate for a block outside the published TPC-17 geometry;
>
> 4.  an admissible bound for an approximate-cover residual;
>
> 5.  a fixed-shift Hardy–Littlewood asymptotic.

> **Proof** The certificate is the coefficient identity $B_Xc_X=w_X$. Items (i)–(iii) are analytic estimates on the columns, item (iv) is an estimate on a vector outside their exact synthesis, and item (v) requires the full upstream and downstream theorem chain. None is a linear-algebraic consequence of column-space membership.

| Literal cover                    | Does an allowed $c_X$ satisfy $B_Xc_X=w_X$ on all native rows?                         | Exact certificate schema; not instantiated globally |
| Coefficient cost                 | Is $\sum_j|c_{j,X}||E_{j,X}|$ within the physical error?                               | Exact accounting formula                            |
| Primitive determinant dispersion | Are the TPC-18 generic residual columns small at fixed $h_0$?                          | Independent unproved arithmetic input               |

: Set coverage, coefficient coverage, and arithmetic cancellation are different gates.

</div>

###### ref-4ac64cda519d0593

> **Remark: Present status** \[rem:present-tpc18-status\] This paper does not enumerate the full physical atom list of the TPC-16/TPC-18 normal form, exhibit an allowed exact $c_X$, or bound the physical image of a nonzero residual. Therefore the TPC-18 all-block representation remains an independent open gate. The result here is a rigorous format in which a future exact certificate, controlled-residual theorem, or negative exact-route certificate can be stated and checked.

# Separate feasibility, determinant, and physical ledgers

Several losses can occur near the same endpoint, but they measure different objects. We record them separately to prevent an incidence gain from being counted as arithmetic cancellation.

## The cover ledger

###### ref-01fd2dad722a63a3

The exact feasibility entry is binary: $$\boxed{w_X\in B_X\mathscr C_X.}  \label{eq:cover-ledger}$$ When $B_X\mathscr C_X$ is closed, this is equivalent to $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cov}}(B_X,w_X;\mathscr C_X)=0$. The original asymptotic TPC-18 representation also admits a distinct route in which the prescribed coefficient leaves $r_X\ne0$ but a new theorem proves $\mathcal T_X(r_X)=o(X)$. When it holds, the quantitative reassembly cost is the exact product $$\sum_j|c_{j,X}||E_{j,X}|,$$ minimized over allowed exact covers if there is a choice. If $\|c_X\|_1=X^{\lambda_{\mathrm{cov}}+o(1)}$ with $\lambda_{\mathrm{cov}}>0$, that amplification is a physical-interface loss only when it is not already present in the block error. It is never a zero-mode saving.

## The determinant/zero-mode ledger

###### ref-84330fbb25eb9c38

On a separate conditional post-bin branch, suppose $$S_X=X^{o(1)}Q_X,\qquad  |Z_X|\le X^{-\eta_Z+o(1)}Q_X^2,\qquad  D_X\ge  X^{-\lambda_D+o(1)}Q_X^3J_X^2.$$ These are hypotheses about a literal coefficient, not conclusions of the incidence model. Its principal angle $$\rho_X=\frac{|Z_X|}{\sqrt{S_XD_X}}$$ then satisfies $$\rho_X  \le  \frac{X^{\lambda_D/2-\eta_Z+o(1)}}{J_X}.$$ Thus the recorded estimates certify the critical angle scale under $$\boxed{\lambda_D\le2\eta_Z.}  \label{eq:determinant-ledger}$$ This is the inherited determinant/zero-mode compatibility rule [`\citep{WangTPC89}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-091.md#cite-77cd2665bece44cd). Solving $B_Xc_X=w_X$ neither improves $\lambda_D$ nor proves $\eta_Z>0$.

## The strict physical-loss ledger

###### ref-270fca19b4f98b68

Let $\Lambda_{\mathrm{phys}}$ be the sum of fixed-power losses actually incurred by the full physical reduction and downstream comparison, excluding every loss already represented in $\lambda_D-2\eta_Z$. The route remains viable only under the strict rule $$\boxed{\Lambda_{\mathrm{phys}}<\frac1{400}.}  \label{eq:physical-loss-ledger}$$ Equality or excess is a stop condition. If a polynomial exact-cover amplification is newly incurred, it is charged here once. It may not also be inserted into $\lambda_D$ or subtracted from $\eta_Z$.

###### ref-6b14aa569d078e63

> **Theorem: No automatic conversion among ledgers** \[thm:no-ledger-conversion\] None of the following implications follows from the results of this paper:
>
> 1.  $w_X\in B_X\mathscr C_X \Longrightarrow\lambda_D\le2\eta_Z$;
>
> 2.  $\lambda_D\le2\eta_Z \Longrightarrow w_X\in B_X\mathscr C_X$;
>
> 3.  bounded overlap $\Longrightarrow\Lambda_{\mathrm{phys}}=0$;
>
> 4.  a small Euclidean cover residual $\Longrightarrow$ a saving in $\eta_Z$;
>
> 5.  determinant-compatible column metadata $\Longrightarrow$ the TPC-18 generic determinant estimate.

> **Proof** The cover ledger concerns membership of one target vector in an allowed linear image. The determinant ledger compares a zero mode with an energy of a literal post-bin coefficient. The physical ledger accumulates losses in arithmetic reassembly, and the TPC-18 generic estimate bounds a fixed-shift correlation inside each compatible column. The counterexamples in [4](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E6%BA%90%E5%AE%9A%E4%BD%8D/TPC-091.md#src-4e90350a994949d4) already separate support overlap from linear feasibility, while [\[prop:residual-remainder\]](TPC-091.md#ref-29f69efc050b6f3c) separates Euclidean residual size from a physical statistic. No theorem identifies the remaining quantities, so no listed conversion follows.

| Ledger                       | Required statement                                               | Status here                                                    |
|:-----------------------------|:-----------------------------------------------------------------|:---------------------------------------------------------------|
| Literal zero-remainder cover | Exact membership $w_X\in B_X\mathscr C_X$ on all native rows     | Sufficient zero-remainder route; physical feasibility unproved |
| Coefficient amplification    | Weighted block error fits the target error                       | Exact formula; no physical $c_X$ constructed                   |

: The same gain or loss may not be moved between rows without a literal crosswalk theorem.

> **Remark: TPC-18 gate independence** The all-block representation in the first row is still its own gate. The present paper makes the exact zero-remainder route falsifiable by an exact residual or Farkas vector, but does not declare the original asymptotic gate closed: a separate controlled-residual route may remain. Even if the representation gate were closed, the last row would remain open.

# Audited conclusion and next certificate

###### ref-574bb29886012b20

> **Theorem: Audited finite-dimensional conclusion** \[thm:audited-conclusion\] Let $w$ be a literal finite atom coefficient, let $B$ contain the exact candidate block columns, and let $\mathscr C$ encode the allowed synthesis coefficients.
>
> 1.  For unrestricted coefficients, $$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cov}}   =   \|(I-BB^\dagger)w\|_2,$$ and, when the residual is nonzero, its normalized direction is an optimal dual obstruction.
>
> 2.  For nonnegative real coefficients, Farkas’ alternative is exact and $$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cov}}   =   \max_{\substack{\|y\|_2\le1\\B^{\mathsf T}y\le0}}   \langle y,w\rangle.$$
>
> 3.  For a closed convex attainable image, the support-function formula [\[eq:convex-distance-dual\]](TPC-091.md#ref-cdc574ddac054c89) is exact.
>
> 4.  Bounded overlap, even overlap one with every atom covered, does not imply an exact or $o(1)$-relative cover.
>
> 5.  An exact cover transports the original global normalization with coefficient cost $\sum_j|c_j|E_j$. An approximate cover leaves the independent physical remainder $\mathcal T_X(w-Bc)$.

> **Proof** Combine \[[thm:subspace-certificate](TPC-091.md#ref-c459092ad1bb2e46),[thm:farkas-cone](TPC-091.md#ref-0b85c97875fc1829),[thm:convex-dual](TPC-091.md#ref-14c855da8bc49317),[thm:partition-counterexample](TPC-091.md#ref-13510c642b4a9a41),[thm:exact-reassembly](TPC-091.md#ref-bb61f587f51a4c40),[prop:residual-remainder](TPC-091.md#ref-29f69efc050b6f3c)\].

#### What is proved.

The literal synthesis model, exact residual formulas, dual certificates, explicit finite counterexamples, target and column stability bounds, and coefficient-amplification identity are unconditional $\mathrm{L0}$ results. They provide an exact $\mathrm{L1}$ format for auditing a proposed TPC-18 cover.

#### What is not proved.

No complete physical atom list $w_X$, incidence matrix $B_X$, or allowed exact solution $c_X$ is constructed for the full TPC-18 normal form, and no nonzero residual is controlled in the physical statistic. No primitive generic determinant-dispersion estimate, prefix extension, determinant-energy survival theorem, or distinguished zero-mode estimate is proved. Hence the TPC-18 all-block representation and every $\mathrm{L2}$ arithmetic gate remain open.

#### Support and key rule.

The certificate must be checked on the union of actual target and candidate-block supports. Native atoms may not be merged merely because they have the same determinant. Every extra column atom is a row with target coefficient zero. A certificate after aggregation is only necessary unless a separate lifting theorem is proved.

#### Coefficient rule.

The allowed set $\mathscr C_X$ comes from the upstream decomposition. A signed solution does not certify a nonnegative cover, and an arbitrary solution does not certify the original unit all-block identity. Exact feasibility and coefficient amplification must both be verified.

#### Normalization rule.

Only $B_Xc_X=w_X$ gives pure algebraic transport under the original global normalizer. A least-squares residual, however small in $\ell^2$, requires a new physical remainder theorem. Such a theorem could verify the original asymptotic TPC-18 route, but the residual may not be discarded by bounded overlap or numerical conditioning.

#### Ledger rule.

The exact-cover condition $w_X\in B_X\mathscr C_X$ (equivalently $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cov}}=0$ when the attainable image is closed), the post-bin determinant rule $\lambda_D\le2\eta_Z$, and the strict endpoint rule $\Lambda_{\mathrm{phys}}<1/400$ are independent. Equality or excess in the last rule stops the route. No gain or loss is charged twice.

#### Arithmetic claim boundary.

All statements retain one prescribed nonzero $h_0$. Nothing specializes it to $2$, proves a fixed-shift Hardy–Littlewood asymptotic, gives a prime-pair lower bound, breaches the parity barrier, or implies the twin-prime conjecture.

#### Next exact task.

A genuine advance on this gate must export the upstream hard packet and all candidate TPC-18 blocks to the same literal row list, then do one of the following:

1.  produce an exact symbolic or interval-verified allowed coefficient $c_X$ with $B_Xc_X=w_X$ and acceptable amplification;

2.  produce a dual residual or Farkas vector proving that the proposed block family cannot exactly cover the packet; or

3.  isolate an explicit residual $r_X$ and prove a new physical bound for $\mathcal T_X(r_X)$.

The first would close only the exact zero-remainder interface. The second would rule out that block family only as an exact zero-remainder route; it would not exclude a separately controlled residual route. The third would define such a residual route. None, by itself, proves the independent TPC-18 determinant-dispersion input.
