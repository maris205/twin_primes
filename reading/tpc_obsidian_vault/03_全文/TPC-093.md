## Abstract

Let $A_X$ be the already aggregated fixed-$h_0$ determinant coefficient of TPC-32, embedded without wrap in $\mathbb Z/q_X\mathbb Z$, and let $\mathfrak m_{K,X}$ be the order-$K$ zero-arc reconstruction multiplier of TPC-86. We export the complete low-frequency functional $$\mathcal L_{K,R}(A_X)  =  \sum_{0<d_{q_X}(r,0)\le R}  \mathfrak m_{K,X}(r)\widehat A_X(r)$$ to the native affine variables of the two polarized physical columns. The first exact formula is row-native: $$\mathcal L_{K,R}(A_X)  =  \sum_{\alpha\ne\gamma}\sum_j  w_{\alpha,\gamma,j}\,  \mathbf 1_{G_{\alpha,\gamma}(j)\le C_X}  \mathcal K_{K,R,X}\!\left(  \frac{m_\alpha-m_\gamma}{G_{\alpha,\gamma}(j)}\right),$$ where $\mathcal K_{K,R,X}(y)=\sum_{0<d_q(r,0)\le R} \mathfrak m_{K,X}(r)e_q(-ry)$. No frequency, channel, or exceptional sector has been estimated or discarded.

After opening one ultra leg and applying the actual row-mask projector, every left column has $$D_\theta(t)=d_\theta+\sigma_\theta t,\qquad  U_\theta(t)=u_\theta+a_\theta t,\qquad  \sigma_\theta u_\theta-a_\theta d_\theta=h_0,$$ with the exact two-Möbius sign $\mu(D_\theta(t))\mu(U_\theta(t))$; the right polarization is symmetric. We prove a decorated-column principle showing that the small-content indicator and determinant character survive this pointwise disintegration. Exact content inversion then gives the complete multiplier-weighted expansion $$\sum_{\theta,c,\kappa,r}  c_{\theta,X}\mu(\kappa)\mathfrak m_{K,X}(r)  \mathbf 1_{c\kappa\mid P_\theta}  \sum_{t\in I_\theta}  \mu(D_\theta(t))\mu(U_\theta(t))  W_\theta(t)  \mathbf 1_{c\kappa\mid\sigma_\theta U_\theta(t)}  e_{cq_X}\!\left(  -r\varepsilon_\theta(M_\theta(t)-n_\theta)\right).$$ All masks and provenance factors are retained inside $c_{\theta,X}$ and $W_\theta$. An explicit child-to-source inverse and projector-weighted multiplicity identity prove that this key list reassembles the literal source coefficient.

Writing $b=c\kappa$, $$B_{\theta,b}=\frac{b}{(b,\sigma_\theta)},$$ the divisibility condition either has no solution or restricts $t$ to one class $t=\tau+B_{\theta,b}z$. On that class the determinant character has slope $\ell_\theta v_\theta\sigma_\theta B_{\theta,b}$. The unreduced forms $$D_\theta(\tau+Bz),\qquad U_\theta(\tau+Bz)$$ have determinant $Bh_0$, but the divisibility condition also gives $$U_\theta(\tau+Bz)=B\,V_{\theta,b}(z).$$ On the nonzero Möbius support, $B$ is squarefree and $$\mu(BV)=\mu(B)\mu(V)\mathbf 1_{(B,V)=1}.$$ After this exact content-factor extraction, the reduced pair $$D_{\theta,b}(z),\qquad V_{\theta,b}(z)$$ has slopes $(\sigma_\theta B,a_\theta)$ and determinant exactly $$\boxed{h_0.}$$ Physical primitivity also gives $(a_\theta,\sigma_\theta B)=1$ and $(a_\theta\sigma_\theta B,h_0)=1$. Thus the low window can be resolved into unmasked fixed-$h_0$ affine intervals, but only after retaining the extracted sign $\mu(B)$ and the new coprimality mask. Treating the unreduced $Bh_0$ pair as though its determinant were $h_0$ is the precise provenance error.

For every threshold $H_X$, we finally partition every resolved native term, without deletion, into short fibers, long resonant fibers satisfying $$N_\xi  \left\|  \frac{r_\xi\ell_\xi v_\xi\sigma_\xi B_\xi}  {c_\xi q_X}  \right\|_{\mathbb R/\mathbb Z}\le1,$$ and the complementary long generic fibers. This supplies the complete representation requested by TPC-92 Deliverable 1A and identifies the exact arithmetic inputs still missing. The finite reindexing and determinant law are $\mathrm{L0}$; their attachment to the literal packet is $\mathrm{L1}$. No $\mathrm{L2}$ cancellation, fixed-shift prime-pair lower bound, parity breach, or twin-prime conclusion is proved.

# Introduction

###### ref-8d533fec89f34d17

TPC-86 reduces the distinguished zero-mode problem to a coherent low-frequency window for the literal normalized-determinant coefficient [`\citep{WangTPC86}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-093.md#cite-41b0792996942846). At the critical row, $$Q_X=X^{267/400+o(1)},\qquad  J_X=X^{133/400+o(1)},\qquad  N_{0,X}=J_XQ_X^2,$$ and an order-$K$ zero-arc filter gives $$|Z_X|  \ll_K  \left|  \sum_{0<d_{q_X}(r,0)\le R_X}  \mathfrak m_{K,X}(r)\widehat A_X(r)  \right|  +  X^{o(1)}N_{0,X}R_X^{1/2-K}. \label{eq:intro-low-window}$$ The minimal arithmetic input is the signed multiplier-weighted sum, not a maximum over generic frequencies. TPC-92 therefore lists a literal export of that sum as its first next-stage deliverable [`\citep{WangTPC92}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-093.md#cite-8444c63105c2920e).

The word “export” carries more content than inserting an additive character in an abstract affine sum. The physical coefficient has:

-   two polarized cutoff legs;

-   an exact common-target content $G\le C_X=\lfloor J_X\rfloor$;

-   a normalized determinant $(m_\alpha-m_\gamma)/G$;

-   a joint row-pair mask;

-   an opposite row fixed outside each affine column;

-   row-gcd divisor layers and fixed-period factors;

-   long and bounded-length affine fibers; and

-   one outer reassembly under the original normalization.

Every one of these data affects either the phase or the coefficient.

This paper performs that export exactly. It also identifies a content-factor extraction that is invisible at determinant frequency zero. Once exact content inversion imposes $$c\kappa\mid \sigma U(t),$$ solving the divisibility condition restricts the affine parameter to one progression $t=\tau+Bz$. The unreduced reparametrized pair has determinant $Bh_0$, but the same divisibility also gives $$U(\tau+Bz)=B\,V(z).$$ Extracting this literal content factor restores determinant $h_0$: $$\begin{array}{ccl} \bigl(D(\tau+Bz),U(\tau+Bz)\bigr) &:&\det=Bh_0,\\[1mm] \bigl(D(\tau+Bz),V(z)\bigr) &:&\det=h_0. \end{array}$$ On the nonzero Möbius support, the extraction contributes the literal factor $\mu(B)$ and the mask $\mathbf 1_{(B,V)=1}$. Dropping either one is an invalid hybrid; retaining both gives a genuine fixed-$h_0$ affine family.

## Main results

The exact chain has four steps.

#### Row-native window.

We first keep the physical rows and prove that the complete low-window functional is obtained by replacing the determinant character with one finite kernel $$\mathcal K_{K,R,X}(y)  =  \sum_{0<d_{q_X}(r,0)\le R}  \mathfrak m_{K,X}(r)e_{q_X}(-ry).$$ This identity already returns every low frequency with the correct multiplier and phase.

#### Decorated affine columns.

The TPC-33 reflection and row-gcd projection are pointwise identities [`\citep{WangTPC33}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-093.md#cite-3dc89b1d29e24761). We prove that they remain valid after multiplication by the small-content determinant decoration. This produces an exact two-sign affine formula for both polarizations, with the opposite row and every separation key retained in the outer provenance tuple. The source-to-child construction, its inverse, and the projector-weighted child multiplicity are all displayed explicitly; losslessness is therefore a conclusion rather than an interface assumption.

#### Content-progression reduction.

We expand exact content by the TPC-32 common-divisor projector [`\citep{WangTPC32}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-093.md#cite-49ad82103eb75eb7). The resulting congruence is solved explicitly and the two-stage determinant law $$Bh_0\longrightarrow h_0$$ is proved after exact extraction of the forced factor $B$ from one affine value. This is an algebraic theorem, not a heuristic normalization.

#### Complete sector partition.

Every resolved term is assigned to exactly one of three sets: short, long resonant, or long generic. Resonance is measured by the actual phase increment on the resolved progression. The partition is an equality. In particular, neither exact resonances nor near-resonant long fibers are hidden inside an exceptional “generic” remainder.

# Decorated affine-column export

The TPC-33 column theorem was stated for the untwisted distinguished shell. Its proof uses pointwise row-gcd inversion, divisor reflection, and sign fusion. These operations commute with any scalar decoration attached to the original physical atom. We record that principle before applying it to the determinant character.

## A pointwise decoration lemma

###### ref-f54e1ca3bf828c83

> **Lemma: Decorated reindexing** \[lem:decorated-reindexing\] Let a finite sum be written as $$S=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}a(\omega),$$ and suppose a finite exact expansion and reindexing gives $$a(\omega)  =  \sum_{\xi\in\Xi(\omega)}a_\xi(\omega),\qquad  \{(\omega,\xi):\omega\in\Omega,\ \xi\in\Xi(\omega)\}  \longleftrightarrow\mathcal P.$$ For every function $F:\Omega\to\mathbb C$, $$\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}F(\omega)a(\omega)  =  \sum_{p\in\mathcal P}  F(\omega(p))a_p.$$ In particular, a pointwise projector or change of variables remains exact after multiplication by $F$, provided the provenance map $p\mapsto\omega(p)$ is retained.

> **Proof** Multiply the finite identity for $a(\omega)$ by $F(\omega)$, sum over $\omega$, and use the stated bijective reindexing. No estimate or interchange of an infinite sum is involved.

> **Remark: Why provenance is part of the statement** The conclusion fails if two distinct source atoms are merged before the value of $F$ is known. Equal affine forms or equal determinants do not imply equal determinant phases: the opposite row, exact content, and source polarization remain part of $\omega(p)$.

> 2.  For a fixed source atom and fixed $v\mid d,e$, $(\theta,t_{\omega,v})$ is its unique child on that projector layer. Hence the algebraic source multiplicity is exactly $\tau((d,e))$; the interval component introduces no further multiplicity.
>
> 3.  The multiplicity is projector-weighted, not a duplication of the physical atom: $$\sum_{v\mid d,e}\lambda_{G_X^{\rm row}}(v)  =  \mathbf 1_{(d,e)\le G_X^{\rm row}}. \label{eq:source-projector-multiplicity}$$ On the physical squarefree support the moving sign is reconstructed term by term as $$\begin{aligned}  &\mu(d)(-\mu(u)\log u)  \mathbf 1_{(d,e)\le G_X^{\rm row}} \notag\\  &\quad=  \sum_{v\mid d,e}  \bigl[-\mu(v)\lambda_{G_X^{\rm row}}(v)\bigr]  \mu(d/v)\mu(u)\log u\,  \mathbf 1_{(v,(d/v)u)=1}. \label{eq:source-sign-reassembly}\end{aligned}$$
>
> 4.  The labels $L$ and $R$ form a disjoint union and correspond to the two summands of [\[eq:row-polarization\]](TPC-093.md#ref-0f1e393e5ee67ef3). Thus both polarizations occur once before their intentional projector multiplicity.
>
> Consequently there is a concrete bijection $$\{(\omega,v):\omega\text{ is a retained source atom},\                  v\mid d_\omega,e_\omega\}  \longleftrightarrow  \{(\theta,t):\theta\in\Theta_X,\ t\in I_\theta\}.$$ The forward map is [\[eq:source-to-child\]](TPC-093.md#ref-5393d25befd3c9de), with its polarization and interval label, while [\[eq:child-to-source-left\]](TPC-093.md#ref-0221fe7717895b0b)–[\[eq:child-to-source-right\]](TPC-093.md#ref-84db58e9b99f359a) give its actual child-to-source inverse.

> **Proof** For a left source, $d=v(d/v)$ and $$\ell jv(d/v)+h_0=u\sigma.$$ Reduction modulo $\sigma$ gives [\[eq:source-canonical-residue\]](TPC-093.md#ref-98f3083859a4ab01), so $d/v=d_{\omega,v}+\sigma t_{\omega,v}$. Therefore $$vD_\theta(t_{\omega,v})=d.$$ Using the affine determinant identity at the same parameter gives $$\sigma U_\theta(t_{\omega,v})  =  \ell jvD_\theta(t_{\omega,v})+h_0  =  u\sigma,$$ and hence $U_\theta(t_{\omega,v})=u$. This proves [\[eq:child-to-source-left\]](TPC-093.md#ref-0221fe7717895b0b); it also proves uniqueness because the residue and the disjoint interval component are canonical. The right formula follows by the displayed exchange of rows.
>
> There is one algebraic child for each divisor of $(d,e)$, proving the multiplicity assertion. Reversing the two divisor sums in the definition of $\lambda_{G_X^{\rm row}}$ proves [\[eq:source-projector-multiplicity\]](TPC-093.md#ref-4e9fa2bf81c3f778). On physical support, $d$ and $u$ are squarefree and target primitivity gives $(d,u)=1$. Thus $$\mu(v)\mu(d/v)=\mu(d),\qquad  \mathbf 1_{(v,(d/v)u)=1}=1,$$ and [\[eq:source-sign-reassembly\]](TPC-093.md#ref-40abfc240a78c736) follows from [\[eq:source-projector-multiplicity\]](TPC-093.md#ref-4e9fa2bf81c3f778). Finally, the polarization labels and disjoint integer interval convention prove (iv) and the claimed child parametrization.

# Exact-content expansion and progression resolution

## Content inversion inside the low window

###### ref-c6576be9615a5e18

The content in [\[eq:affine-native-content\]](TPC-093.md#ref-6794e276a52b459f) is $$G_\theta(t)=\gcd(  \sigma_\theta U_\theta(t),P_\theta).$$ For $c\ge1$, exact common-divisor inversion gives $$\mathbf 1_{G_\theta(t)=c}  =  \sum_{\kappa\ge1}\mu(\kappa)  \mathbf 1_{c\kappa\mid\sigma_\theta U_\theta(t)}  \mathbf 1_{c\kappa\mid P_\theta}. \label{eq:content-exact-projector}$$ The sum is finite.

###### ref-59a06e212790d462

###### ref-18d240add4e3861e

> **Lemma: Phase-compatible content inversion** \[lem:phase-content-inversion\] For every native key $\theta$, affine parameter $t$, and frequency $r$, $$\begin{aligned}  &\mathbf 1_{G_\theta(t)\le C_X}  e_{q_X}\!\left(  -r\varepsilon_\theta  \frac{M_\theta(t)-n_\theta}{G_\theta(t)}\right) \notag\\  &\quad=  \sum_{1\le c\le C_X}\sum_{\kappa\ge1}  \mu(\kappa)  \mathbf 1_{c\kappa\mid P_\theta}  \mathbf 1_{c\kappa\mid\sigma_\theta U_\theta(t)}  e_{cq_X}\!\left(  -r\varepsilon_\theta  (M_\theta(t)-n_\theta)\right). \label{eq:phase-content-inversion}\end{aligned}$$

> **Proof** Fix $c$. If $c\kappa$ divides both targets, primitive target support implies $c\mid M_\theta(t)-n_\theta$, so the lifted character on the right is well defined. Summing $\mu(\kappa)$ over divisors of $G_\theta(t)/c$ vanishes unless $G_\theta(t)=c$. On the surviving term, $$e_{cq_X}\!\left(  -r\varepsilon_\theta(M_\theta(t)-n_\theta)\right)  =  e_{q_X}\!\left(  -r\varepsilon_\theta  \frac{M_\theta(t)-n_\theta}{c}\right).$$ Summing $c\le C_X$ proves the identity.

###### ref-0b30ace406f4a2e3

###### ref-944db83352ffea7f

> **Theorem: Complete content-expanded low window** \[thm:content-expanded-window\] With the notation of [\[thm:decorated-affine-export\]](TPC-093.md#ref-96a2038bdffe1eab), $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal L_{K,R}(A_X)  ={}&  \sum_{\theta\in\Theta_X}  \sum_{1\le c\le C_X}  \sum_{\kappa\ge1}  \sum_{r\in\mathscr W_X(R)}  c_{\theta,X}\mu(\kappa)  \mathfrak m_{K,X}(r)  \mathbf 1_{c\kappa\mid P_\theta}  \notag\\  &\quad\times  \sum_{t\in I_\theta}  \mu(D_\theta(t))\mu(U_\theta(t))  \chi_\theta(t)W_{\theta,X}(t)  \mathbf 1_{c\kappa\mid\sigma_\theta U_\theta(t)}  \notag\\  &\quad\times  e_{cq_X}\!\left(  -r\varepsilon_\theta  (M_\theta(t)-n_\theta)\right). \label{eq:content-expanded-window}\end{aligned}$$ Every sum is finite. In particular, the right side is one coherent outer sum; the $(c,\kappa,r)$ terms have not been replaced by their absolute values.

> **Proof** Expand $\mathcal K_{K,R,X}$ in [\[eq:decorated-affine-export\]](TPC-093.md#ref-f4c25cd8875f6ec6), apply [\[lem:phase-content-inversion\]](TPC-093.md#ref-59a06e212790d462), and interchange finite sums.

> **Remark: Why $c$ and $\kappa$ are different keys** The integer $c$ is the candidate exact content and determines the phase modulus $cq_X$. The integer $\kappa$ is a signed inclusion–exclusion variable. Replacing $c$ by $b=c\kappa$ in the phase would change the normalized determinant character.

## Solving the native divisibility condition

###### ref-378f93885f4fca1b

Fix an active tuple $(\theta,c,\kappa)$, put $$b=c\kappa,\qquad  g_{\theta,b}=(b,\sigma_\theta),\qquad  B_{\theta,b}=\frac{b}{g_{\theta,b}}. \label{eq:progression-B}$$

###### ref-4471feafa0c042e4

###### ref-023b255b8116bc50

> **Lemma: One progression or none** \[lem:one-progression\] The native divisibility condition satisfies $$b\mid\sigma_\theta U_\theta(t)  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad  B_{\theta,b}\mid U_\theta(t). \label{eq:progression-divisibility-reduction}$$ Moreover:
>
> 1.  if $(a_\theta,B_{\theta,b})>1$, there is no solution;
>
> 2.  if $(a_\theta,B_{\theta,b})=1$, there is one residue $\tau_{\theta,b}\pmod{B_{\theta,b}}$ such that every solution is $$t=\tau_{\theta,b}+B_{\theta,b}z,\qquad z\in\mathbb Z.$$

> **Proof** Write $b=gB$ and $\sigma_\theta=g\sigma'$. By the definition of $g$, $(B,\sigma')=1$. Therefore $$gB\mid g\sigma' U_\theta(t)  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad B\mid U_\theta(t),$$ which proves [\[eq:progression-divisibility-reduction\]](TPC-093.md#ref-023b255b8116bc50).
>
> The inherited primitive relations imply $(a_\theta,u_\theta)=1$: a common divisor would divide $\sigma_\theta u_\theta-a_\theta d_\theta=h_0$, contrary to $(a_\theta,h_0)=1$. Hence if a prime divides both $a_\theta$ and $B$, it does not divide $u_\theta$, and the congruence $u_\theta+a_\theta t\equiv0\pmod B$ is insoluble. When $(a_\theta,B)=1$, the slope is invertible modulo $B$, giving one residue class.

###### ref-46dbb822b4c6fe88

###### ref-f75e5d65a8a0460a

###### ref-729aa627dd423ffd

###### ref-7eda6b19a9992c1c

On the soluble branch, define $$\begin{aligned}  I_{\theta,b}^\ast  &=  \{z\in\mathbb Z:  \tau_{\theta,b}+B_{\theta,b}z\in I_\theta\}, \label{eq:progression-resolved-interval}\\  D_{\theta,b}^\ast(z)  &=  D_\theta(\tau_{\theta,b}+B_{\theta,b}z), \label{eq:progression-D}\\  U_{\theta,b}^\ast(z)  &=  U_\theta(\tau_{\theta,b}+B_{\theta,b}z), \label{eq:progression-U}\\  M_{\theta,b}^\ast(z)  &=  \ell_\theta v_\theta D_{\theta,b}^\ast(z). \label{eq:progression-M}\end{aligned}$$ The set $I_{\theta,b}^\ast$ is an interval for each interval component already contained in $\theta$, possibly empty. Put $$\Omega_{\theta,b}  =  \ell_\theta v_\theta\sigma_\theta B_{\theta,b}. \label{eq:progression-phase-slope}$$

> **Proof** The moving row satisfies $$M_{\theta,b}^\ast(z)  =  M_\theta(\tau_{\theta,b})  +  \ell_\theta v_\theta\sigma_\theta  B_{\theta,b}z.$$ Insert this identity in the character and separate the constant phase.

# Content-factor extraction and fixed-determinant preservation

The resolved formula [\[eq:fully-resolved-inner-sum\]](TPC-093.md#ref-6ab6af717dce6e60) still uses the value $U_{\theta,b}^\ast(z)$. The defining congruence forces a factor $B_{\theta,b}$ from that value. Extracting it is essential before invoking the fixed-determinant local theory of TPC-90.

## The two-stage determinant law

###### ref-9e9f7fa41133e187

###### ref-297fc64b9fd51507

###### ref-f534f53cf5e7feb0

On a soluble branch, put $B=B_{\theta,b}$, $\tau=\tau_{\theta,b}$, and define $$V_{\theta,b}(z)  =  \frac{U_\theta(\tau+Bz)}{B}. \label{eq:det-reduced-V}$$ This is integral because $B\mid U_\theta(\tau)$. More explicitly, $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal D_{\theta,b}(z)  &:=  D_\theta(\tau+Bz)  =  D_\theta(\tau)+\sigma_\theta Bz, \label{eq:det-reduced-D}\\  V_{\theta,b}(z)  &=  \frac{U_\theta(\tau)}B+a_\theta z. \label{eq:det-reduced-V-affine}\end{aligned}$$

###### ref-703009e48b970dd6

###### ref-5e7f9660c4f7d59e

###### ref-80f6a9c91d038943

###### ref-873b4552072780c6

> **Theorem: Content-progression determinant law** \[thm:content-progression-determinant\] Let a nonempty literal resolved term satisfy $$b=c\kappa\mid P_\theta,\qquad  b\mid\sigma_\theta U_\theta(t), \qquad  B=\frac b{(b,\sigma_\theta)}.$$ Then:
>
> 1.  the unreduced reparametrized pair $$\mathcal D_{\theta,b}(z),\qquad  U_\theta(\tau+Bz)$$ has coefficient determinant $Bh_0$;
>
> 2.  the second value factors exactly as $$U_\theta(\tau+Bz)=B\,V_{\theta,b}(z);$$
>
> 3.  the reduced pair $$\mathcal D_{\theta,b}(z)  =  D_\theta(\tau)+(\sigma_\theta B)z,\qquad  V_{\theta,b}(z)  =  \frac{U_\theta(\tau)}B+a_\theta z$$ has coefficient determinant exactly $h_0$: $$\boxed{  (\sigma_\theta B)\frac{U_\theta(\tau)}B  -  a_\theta D_\theta(\tau)  =  h_0;} \label{eq:det-preserved}$$
>
> 4.  on every nonempty literal branch, $$(a_\theta,\sigma_\theta B)=1,\qquad  (a_\theta\sigma_\theta B,h_0)=1; \label{eq:det-reduced-slope-primitivity}$$
>
> 5.  the reduced forms are individually primitive: $$\gcd(D_\theta(\tau),\sigma_\theta B)=1,\qquad  \gcd(U_\theta(\tau)/B,a_\theta)=1. \label{eq:det-reduced-form-primitivity}$$

###### ref-6cce8e7d16954133

> **Proof** The unreduced forms have origins $D_\theta(\tau),U_\theta(\tau)$ and slopes $\sigma_\theta B,a_\theta B$. Their determinant is $$(\sigma_\theta B)U_\theta(\tau)  -(a_\theta B)D_\theta(\tau)  =  B\{\sigma_\theta U_\theta(\tau)  -a_\theta D_\theta(\tau)\}  =  Bh_0.$$ The progression was chosen so that $B\mid U_\theta(\tau)$, proving the factorization. Dividing the second origin and slope by $B$ gives [\[eq:det-preserved\]](TPC-093.md#ref-5e7f9660c4f7d59e).
>
> For a nonempty term, $b$ divides both physical targets. Primitive target support therefore gives $$(b,h_0)=1 \label{eq:det-b-coprime-h}$$ and, on every contributing source atom, $b$ is coprime to the moving row and $j_\theta$. In particular $(B,h_0)=1$. Solubility gives $(a_\theta,B)=1$ by [\[lem:one-progression\]](TPC-093.md#ref-4471feafa0c042e4); combine this with $(a_\theta,\sigma_\theta)=1$ and $(a_\theta\sigma_\theta,h_0)=1$ from [\[eq:affine-native-determinant\]](TPC-093.md#ref-935c3c87518e2ea4) to obtain [\[eq:det-reduced-slope-primitivity\]](TPC-093.md#ref-80f6a9c91d038943).
>
> Any common divisor of $D_\theta(\tau)$ and $\sigma_\theta B$ divides the left side of [\[eq:det-preserved\]](TPC-093.md#ref-5e7f9660c4f7d59e), hence $h_0$; this contradicts $(\sigma_\theta B,h_0)=1$. The second primitive identity is proved in the same way using $(a_\theta,h_0)=1$.

All extracted factors and masks remain literal.

## The precise provenance boundary

We now isolate the exact obstruction to an untracked determinant change.

###### ref-61f6549bec3c9a04

> **Theorem: No silent determinant relabeling** \[thm:no-silent-relabeling\] After the substitution $t=\tau+Bz$, the unreduced pair has determinant $Bh_0$. Therefore it cannot be entered into a theorem whose hypothesis is determinant $h_0$ by simply retaining its two unreduced values and changing the determinant label. A valid fixed-$h_0$ entry must instead perform $$U(\tau+Bz)=B\,V(z)$$ and retain:
>
> 1.  the branch deletion $\mu^2(B)=0$;
>
> 2.  the outer sign $\mu(B)$;
>
> 3.  the coprimality mask $\mathbf 1_{(B,V(z))=1}$; and
>
> 4.  the original content, phase, polarization, and outer provenance keys.
>
> Dropping any one of (a)–(c) changes the literal coefficient.

> **Proof** The determinant statements follow from [\[thm:content-progression-determinant\]](TPC-093.md#ref-703009e48b970dd6). Let $\Xi_X(K,R)$ be the set of all tuples $$\xi=(\theta,c,\kappa,r) \label{eq:sector-index}$$ that occur in [\[thm:fixed-h-reduced-export\]](TPC-093.md#ref-d4bdb460ecd05bd4), including: $$1\le c\le C_X,\qquad  r\in\mathscr W_X(R),\qquad  b_\xi=c\kappa\mid P_\theta,$$ $$\mu^2(B_\xi)=1,\qquad  (a_\theta,B_\xi)=1,\qquad  I_\xi:=I_{\theta,b_\xi}^\ast\ne\varnothing.$$ Write $$\begin{aligned}  N_\xi&=\#I_\xi, \label{eq:sector-occupancy}\\  \Omega_\xi  &=  \ell_\theta v_\theta\sigma_\theta B_\xi, \label{eq:sector-phase-slope}\\  \delta_\xi  &=  \left\|  \frac{r\Omega_\xi}{cq_X}  \right\|_{\mathbb R/\mathbb Z}. \label{eq:sector-phase-distance}\end{aligned}$$ The complete outer coefficient is $$\mathfrak c_{\xi,X}  =  c_{\theta,X}\mu(\kappa)\mu(B_\xi)  \mathfrak m_{K,X}(r)  \zeta_{\theta,b_\xi,c,r}. \label{eq:sector-outer-coefficient}$$ Put $$\begin{aligned}  F_{\xi,X}(z)  ={}&  \mu(\mathcal D_{\theta,b_\xi}(z))  \mu(V_{\theta,b_\xi}(z))  \mathbf 1_{(B_\xi,V_{\theta,b_\xi}(z))=1}  \notag\\  &\times  \chi_\theta(\tau_{\theta,b_\xi}+B_\xi z)  W_{\theta,X}(\tau_{\theta,b_\xi}+B_\xi z). \label{eq:sector-inner-coefficient}\end{aligned}$$ Then the fully resolved exact formula is $$\boxed{  \mathcal L_{K,R}(A_X)  =  \sum_{\xi\in\Xi_X(K,R)}  \mathfrak c_{\xi,X}  \sum_{z\in I_\xi}  F_{\xi,X}(z)  e_{cq_X}(-r\varepsilon_\theta\Omega_\xi z).} \label{eq:sector-master-formula}$$ All variables on the right are components of $\xi$; the repeated symbols $c,r,\theta$ are not newly summed inside the inner sum.

## The three sectors

###### ref-1e1f7e1e4adbc6a6

###### ref-3fb69c886b26cf79

###### ref-510455fc62f3221a

Let $H_X\ge1$ be any chosen occupancy threshold. Define disjoint sets: $$\begin{aligned}  \Xi_{\mathrm{short}}  &=  \{\xi:N_\xi\le H_X\}, \label{eq:sector-short}\\  \Xi_{\mathrm{res}}  &=  \{\xi:N_\xi>H_X,\ N_\xi\delta_\xi\le1\}, \label{eq:sector-resonant}\\  \Xi_{\mathrm{gen}}  &=  \{\xi:N_\xi>H_X,\ N_\xi\delta_\xi>1\}. \label{eq:sector-generic}\end{aligned}$$ Call them the short, long resonant, and long generic sectors. The resonant sector includes both exact resonances and phases that move by at most one turn at the natural fiber resolution.

###### ref-b77f6f1ccd1dacec

> **Theorem: Complete native sector decomposition** \[thm:complete-sector-decomposition\] For every $H_X\ge1$, $$\Xi_X(K,R)  =  \Xi_{\mathrm{short}}\,\dot\cup\,  \Xi_{\mathrm{res}}\,\dot\cup\,  \Xi_{\mathrm{gen}}, \label{eq:sector-partition}$$ and $$\boxed{  \mathcal L_{K,R}(A_X)  =  \mathcal L_{\mathrm{short},X}  +  \mathcal L_{\mathrm{res},X}  +  \mathcal L_{\mathrm{gen},X},} \label{eq:sector-sum}$$ where each term is [\[eq:sector-master-formula\]](TPC-093.md#ref-ec1fbdef370786b8) restricted to the corresponding set. This identity retains both polarizations, every exact-content projector term, every low frequency, and every resolved fiber.

> **Proof** Every $\xi$ first satisfies exactly one of $N_\xi\le H_X$ and $N_\xi>H_X$. In the latter case it satisfies exactly one of $N_\xi\delta_\xi\le1$ and $N_\xi\delta_\xi>1$. Restrict the finite sum [\[eq:sector-master-formula\]](TPC-093.md#ref-ec1fbdef370786b8) to the resulting disjoint sets.

###### ref-258d459a3e5ec75b

###### ref-9bc674c120fd10f1

> **Proposition: One-step and observed resonance criteria** \[prop:exact-resonance\] The one-step multiplier of the determinant character equals one precisely when $$\delta_\xi=0  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad  cq_X\mid r\ell_\theta v_\theta  \sigma_\theta B_\xi. \label{eq:exact-resonance}$$ If $N_\xi\ge2$, this is also equivalent to the character being constant on the resolved interval $I_\xi$. If $N_\xi=1$, the character is vacuously constant on its single observed point for every value of $\delta_\xi$; such a singleton belongs to the short sector for every $H_X\ge1$.
>
> For all sufficiently large $X$, $c<q_X$. Since $0<d_{q_X}(r,0)<q_X/2$, an exact resonance in particular requires $$q_X\mid  \ell_\theta v_\theta\sigma_\theta B_\xi.$$ No theorem presently excludes that divisibility on every literal key.

> **Proof** The phase increment from $z$ to $z+1$ is $$e_{cq_X}(-r\varepsilon_\theta\Omega_\xi).$$ It equals one exactly under the divisibility in [\[eq:exact-resonance\]](TPC-093.md#ref-9bc674c120fd10f1). An integer interval with at least two points contains two consecutive integers, which proves the observed constancy assertion. The singleton assertion is immediate from [\[eq:sector-short\]](TPC-093.md#ref-1e1f7e1e4adbc6a6). The critical scales give $c\le C_X<q_X$, and the chosen representative of a nonzero low frequency is not divisible by the prime $q_X$. Euclid’s lemma gives the final necessary condition.

> **Remark: Near resonance is not exact resonance** The condition $N_\xi\delta_\xi\le1$ is quantitative and depends on the actual occupancy. It does not assert the exact divisibility in [\[eq:exact-resonance\]](TPC-093.md#ref-9bc674c120fd10f1). It records that additive oscillation alone is too slow on that particular interval to be treated as a uniformly generic phase.

# The literal arithmetic gate and endpoint ledger

## What the export reduces

###### ref-c264949a305b2b46

At the critical scales, choose $$R_X=X^{\varrho+o(1)}<q_X/2,\qquad  \varrho<\frac{267}{400},$$ and fixed $K\ge2$. For a desired zero-mode rate $$|Z_X|\le X^{-\eta_Z+o(1)}Q_X^2,$$ the inherited high-frequency tail has a fixed-power reserve when $$(K-\tfrac12)\varrho  >  \frac{133}{400}+\eta_Z. \label{eq:gate-tail-condition}$$ The often quoted second-order choice $$K=2,\qquad  R_X=X^{133/600+\varepsilon}$$ belongs to the zero-loss corner $\eta_Z=0$ for fixed $$0<\varepsilon<\frac{107}{240}.$$ For the same window and positive $\eta_Z$, one additionally needs $\eta_Z<3\varepsilon/2$.

> **Remark: The coherent certificate is weaker** Equation [\[eq:gate-coherent-sector-target\]](TPC-093.md#ref-1ce276f9ad5b4f4c) permits cancellation between the three returned sectors. Requiring three separate bounds may lose a fixed power. A proof may use either route, but must state which outer cancellations survive.

## Sector-specific tasks

#### Short fibers.

By [\[prop:resolved-occupancy\]](TPC-093.md#ref-8b11131c3d8ed237), large values of $\sigma_\theta v_\theta B_\xi$ automatically enter the short sector. These fibers have no asymptotic averaging parameter. They must be returned by an exact enumeration, an outer-energy estimate, or a physical counting theorem. Calling them negligible because each is short does not bound their number or outer coefficient mass.

#### Long resonant fibers.

Exact resonances satisfy [\[eq:exact-resonance\]](TPC-093.md#ref-9bc674c120fd10f1); near resonances satisfy $N_\xi\delta_\xi\le1$. The additive determinant phase provides no uniform power saving on this sector. A viable estimate must instead use the two-sign arithmetic, a structural outer cancellation, or a proved small multiplier/provenance mass.

#### Long generic fibers.

Here the additive phase makes more than one resolvable turn, and the reduced Möbius pair has the fixed determinant $h_0$. This is the natural sector for a large-sieve, bilinear, $TT^\ast$, or phase-sensitive affine-correlation theorem. Such a theorem still must be uniform in the growing slopes, the content-factor masks, prefixes, interval origins, and complete outer ledger.

#### Local squarefree split.

applies TPC-90 without changing the determinant. It classifies local square-power support but leaves the weighted Liouville product. The extracted $\mu(B_\xi)$ may participate in outer cancellation, but no such cancellation is proved by the factorization itself.

#### What is new.

The paper proves three exact advances. First, the full coherent TPC-86 window is now written in the native affine variables rather than represented by a schematic twisted column. Second, exact content resolution is reconciled with the fixed determinant: the unreduced progression has determinant $Bh_0$, but the forced factor $B$ in one Möbius argument can be extracted exactly, restoring determinant $h_0$ together with the literal sign and coprimality mask. Third, the resulting physical family is partitioned into explicit short, resonant, and generic sectors with no exceptional remainder.

#### Proof levels.

The Fourier reindexing, phase-compatible content inversion, progression solution, Möbius extraction, determinant identities, and sector partition are finite $\mathrm{L0}$ theorems. Their attachment to the already aggregated TPC-32 coefficient through the actual TPC-33 column decomposition is an $\mathrm{L1}$ theorem.

#### Finite regression certificate.

The standard-library script separately exercises both source polarizations, the explicit source–child inverse, projector and sign reassembly, phase-compatible content inversion, progression resolution, determinant reduction, and Möbius extraction on deterministic finite instances. This guards the literal bookkeeping; it is not evidence for an $\mathrm{L2}$ estimate.

#### What is not proved.

This paper proves no nontrivial upper bound for any of $$\mathcal L_{\mathrm{short},X},\qquad  \mathcal L_{\mathrm{res},X},\qquad  \mathcal L_{\mathrm{gen},X},$$ no new bound for $Z_X$, no post-bin mass or determinant-energy lower bound, and no growing fixed-$h_0$ Liouville/Möbius correlation estimate. It proves no $\mathrm{L2}$ saving.

#### The surviving arithmetic fork.

The next positive step may:

1.  control short fibers by their complete outer mass or an exact physical return;

2.  bound the multiplier/provenance mass of resonant fibers or use the fixed-$h_0$ sign correlation there;

3.  prove a phase-sensitive estimate for the long generic reduced pair; or

4.  estimate the complete signed sum of all three sectors without separate absolute bounds.

Any one-sector theorem is progress at its native level only after the other two sectors are returned.

#### Stop rules.

A generic-frequency theorem does not pass if it leaves resonant multiplier mass uncontrolled. A long-fiber theorem does not pass if the aggregate bounded-fiber contribution is unreturned. A determinant-$h_0$ theorem cannot be applied to the unreduced determinant-$Bh_0$ pair. After extracting $B$, the factors $\mu(B)$ and $\mathbf 1_{(B,V)=1}$ are mandatory. Any unavoidable fixed-power return loss reaching $1/400$ stops the physical branch.

#### Independent requirements.

The zero-mode and determinant-energy exponents still require $$\lambda_D\le2\eta_Z.$$ The separate physical ledger still requires $$\Lambda_{\rm phys}<1/400.$$ TPC-18 full-block reassembly and primitive determinant dispersion remain independent downstream gates; the affine export proves neither one.

#### Final boundary.

Nothing is specialized to $h_0=2$. The paper does not prove a fixed-shift Hardy–Littlewood asymptotic, breach the sieve parity barrier, give a prime-pair lower bound, or prove the twin-prime conjecture. Its contribution is a complete literal representation and a corrected fixed-determinant content reduction on which the next arithmetic estimate can be stated without hidden sectors.
