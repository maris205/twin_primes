## Abstract

TPC-93 resolves the exact-content condition $$b=c\kappa\mid \sigma U(t)$$ by writing $t=\tau+Bz$, where $B=b/(b,\sigma)$. This paper audits the two structures created by that substitution: the affine determinant and the additive phase.

For an individually primitive pair $$D(t)=d+\sigma t,\qquad U(t)=u+at,\qquad  \sigma u-ad=h_0\ne0,$$ a soluble content progression gives $$U(\tau+Bz)=B\,V(z).$$ The unreduced pair $(D(\tau+Bz),U(\tau+Bz))$ has determinant $Bh_0$, whereas the semi-reduced pair $(D(\tau+Bz),V(z))$ has determinant $h_0$. In the general abstract setting the first form can acquire the exact content $$H=\gcd(D(\tau),\sigma B)=\gcd(B,h_0).$$ After extracting it, the primitive pair has determinant $h_0/H$, and $$\mu(D(\tau+Bz))\mu(U(\tau+Bz)) = \mu(H)\mu(B)\mu(\widetilde D(z))\mu(V(z)) \,\mathbf 1_{(H,\widetilde D(z))=1} \,\mathbf 1_{(B,V(z))=1}.$$ Thus squareful $B$ vanishes identically, and the apparent growing determinant $Bh_0$ is a representation effect, not by itself a growing-determinant obstruction. On the literal canonical TPC-32 support, $b$ is a common target divisor and $(b,h_0)=1$; hence $H=1$ and the reduced primitive determinant is exactly the prescribed $h_0$.

The determinant character becomes $$\zeta_{\xi}\exp\!\left(  -2\pi i\,\frac{\varepsilon_\xi\widetilde r_\xi\ell_\xi v_\xi  \sigma_\xi B_\xi}{c_\xi q_X}z\right).$$ Here $\varepsilon_\xi\in\{+1,-1\}$ records the source polarization, and $\widetilde r_\xi$ is the unique least-absolute signed lift of the low-window class $r_\xi\bmod q_X$. Its exact conductor is $$q_\xi^\ast  =  \frac{c_\xi q_X}  {\gcd(c_\xi q_X,  \widetilde r_\xi\ell_\xi v_\xi\sigma_\xi B_\xi)}.$$ For prime $q_X$, $c_\xi<q_X$, and nonzero low-window $r_\xi$, there is a conductor gap: if $q_X$ divides the phase slope then $q_\xi^\ast\le c_\xi$, while otherwise $q_\xi^\ast\ge q_X$. Exact constancy, low conductor, archimedean resonance, and generic oscillation are therefore different predicates.

The literal common-target provenance is stronger: $$b_\xi\mid M_\xi(z)-n_\xi,\qquad  b_\xi\mid\ell_\xi v_\xi\sigma_\xi B_\xi.$$ Hence $c_\xi$ divides the phase slope, and the conductor is exactly $$q_\xi^\ast\in\{1,q_X\}.$$ It equals $1$ precisely on the constant branch $q_X\mid\ell_\xi v_\xi\sigma_\xi B_\xi$; otherwise it equals $q_X$. Thus no nonconstant low-conductor sector survives in the literal packet.

We give a mutually exclusive and exhaustive abstract partition into null, short, long constant, long low-conductor resonant/generic, and long high-conductor resonant/generic sectors, and prove its four-sector literal collapse. Each exceptional return is charged by $$\sum_{r\ {\rm in\ the\ sector}}  |\mathfrak m_{K,X}(r)|,$$ not by the number of frequencies. All determinant, conductor, and partition results are $\mathrm{L0}$; by the complete lossless provenance theorem established in TPC-93, their identification with that literal packet is $\mathrm{L1}$. No $\mathrm{L2}$ cancellation, fixed-shift prime-pair estimate, parity breach, or twin-prime conclusion is proved.

Put $b=c\kappa$, $g=(b,\sigma_\theta)$, and $B=b/g$. The content condition reduces to $B\mid U_\theta(t)$. On a soluble branch it selects one class $t=\tau+Bz$. Two questions must then be kept separate.

#### Which determinant is relevant?

If one merely substitutes $t=\tau+Bz$, both slopes gain a factor $B$, and the raw determinant is $Bh_0$. But the congruence itself forces $U(\tau+Bz)=BV(z)$. Extracting that factor returns a determinant-$h_0$ pair, together with an outer sign and a coprimality mask. A further fixed content can occur in the first form outside the canonical TPC-32 support. The exact answer is the finite palette $h_0/H$, $H=(B,h_0)$, rather than an uncontrolled growing determinant.

#### Which phase is relevant?

Content-factor extraction does not divide the determinant character. The phase increment remains $$\alpha_\xi  =  \frac{\varepsilon_\xi\widetilde r_\xi  \ell_\xi v_\xi\sigma_\xi B_\xi}  {c_\xi q_X}  \pmod 1.$$ Its rational conductor, its distance to the nearest integer, and the resolved fiber length encode three distinct mechanisms: periodicity, near constancy, and shortness.

## Main results

The first theorem gives the full two-stage normalization: $$\begin{array}{ccl} (D(\tau+Bz),U(\tau+Bz)) &:&\det=Bh_0,\\ (D(\tau+Bz),V(z)) &:&\det=h_0,\\ (\widetilde D(z),V(z)) &:&\det=h_0/H,\quad H=(B,h_0). \end{array}$$ It also gives the exact Möbius masks at both extractions. The literal common-target provenance of TPC-32 forces $H=1$ [`\citep{WangTPC32}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-094.md#cite-49ad82103eb75eb7).

The second theorem computes the exact additive conductor. Because the TPC-86 modulus is prime and the content $c$ is smaller than that modulus, every nonconstant phase has either conductor at most $c$ or conductor at least $q_X$; there is no middle range [`\citep{WangTPC86}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-094.md#cite-41b0792996942846). On the literal common-target branch one has the stronger divisibility $c\mid\Omega$, so the two possibilities collapse to conductor $1$ and conductor $q_X$.

The third theorem gives a disjoint and exhaustive sector partition. Its natural specialization uses a short-fiber threshold at least as large as the content cutoff. In that regime a long nonconstant low-conductor fiber cannot also be resonant. In the literal packet there is no nonconstant low-conductor fiber at all.

Finally, we formulate the exact multiplier-mass ledger. A set of exceptional frequencies is inexpensive only when its reconstruction-multiplier mass is inexpensive after the literal outer coefficients and fiber masses are restored. Cardinality by itself is not the relevant quantity.

> **Theorem: Prime-modulus conductor gap** \[thm:prime-modulus-conductor-gap\] Suppose $q_X$ is prime, $$1\le c\le C_X<q_X,  \qquad  0<|\widetilde r|<q_X/2.$$ Then $q_X\nmid\widetilde r$ and:

>

> **Proof** By [\[eq:signed-frequency-lift\]](TPC-094.md#ref-973f538cb95a20a6), $q_X\nmid\widetilde r$. Also $(c,q_X)=1$.
>

If $q_X\nmid\Omega_\xi$, then $q_X\nmid\widetilde r\Omega_\xi$, so $$(cq_X,\widetilde r\Omega_\xi)  =  (c,\widetilde r\Omega_\xi).$$ This gives [\[eq:high-conductor-formula\]](TPC-094.md#ref-cc7da277e8ec8f3c).

## Archimedean resonance

###### ref-993df7538e0e0a39

Let $N_\xi$ be the cardinality of the resolved $z$-interval. Define $$\mathcal R(\xi)  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad  N_\xi\|\alpha_\xi\|_{\mathbb R/\mathbb Z}\le1. \label{eq:archimedean-resonance}$$

###### ref-3b52743d1091e047

> **Lemma: Nonconstant resonance forces a conductor-length bound** \[lem:resonance-conductor-length\] If $q_\xi^\ast>1$ and $\mathcal R(\xi)$, then $$N_\xi\le q_\xi^\ast. \label{eq:resonance-length-bound}$$

> **Proof** In lowest terms $\alpha_\xi=p/q_\xi^\ast$ with $(p,q_\xi^\ast)=1$ and $p\not\equiv0\pmod{q_\xi^\ast}$. Therefore $$\|\alpha_\xi\|_{\mathbb R/\mathbb Z}\ge1/q_\xi^\ast.$$ Insert this in [\[eq:archimedean-resonance\]](TPC-094.md#ref-993df7538e0e0a39).

> **Remark: No automatic oscillatory saving** On a constant or resonant fiber the additive phase supplies no uniform cancellation. On a low-conductor fiber it is periodic, but the Möbius signs, coprimality masks, and smooth weights need not be constant on phase periods. Even on a high-conductor generic fiber, partial sums of the full weighted Möbius product still require a new arithmetic estimate. The conductor calculation alone is $\mathrm{L0}$, not $\mathrm{L2}$.

# Fiber length and an exhaustive sector partition

## Exact resolved length

Let the native interval be $$I_\theta=[L_\theta,U_\theta]\cap\mathbb Z,  \qquad N_\theta^{(0)}=|I_\theta|.$$ For a soluble branch, the resolved interval is $$I_{\theta,b}^\ast  =  \{z\in\mathbb Z:\tau_{\theta,b}+B_{\theta,b}z\in I_\theta\}.$$

###### ref-af6b5801ea9a254f

###### ref-881a306d65b2f155

###### ref-852ec4927146ec28

> **Proposition: Progression thinning** \[prop:progression-thinning\] The resolved cardinality is $$\begin{aligned}  N_{\theta,b}  =  \max\biggl\{0,\,  &\left\lfloor  \frac{U_\theta-\tau_{\theta,b}}{B_{\theta,b}}  \right\rfloor  -  \left\lceil  \frac{L_\theta-\tau_{\theta,b}}{B_{\theta,b}}  \right\rceil  +1\biggr\}, \label{eq:exact-resolved-length}\end{aligned}$$ and $$N_{\theta,b}  \le  \left\lceil  \frac{N_\theta^{(0)}}{B_{\theta,b}}  \right\rceil  \le  \frac{N_\theta^{(0)}}{B_{\theta,b}}+1. \label{eq:resolved-length-bound}$$ In particular:
>
> 1.  if $B_{\theta,b}\ge N_\theta^{(0)}$, the fiber has at most one point;
>
> 2.  for any integer $Y\ge1$, if $B_{\theta,b}\ge N_\theta^{(0)}/Y$, then $N_{\theta,b}\le Y$;
>
> 3.  if $q_X\nmid\ell_\theta v_\theta\sigma_\theta$, $q_X\mid\Omega_\xi$, and $N_\theta^{(0)}\le q_X$, then the fiber has at most one point.

> **Proof** The first formula is obtained by solving the two endpoint inequalities for $z$. An interval of $N_\theta^{(0)}$ consecutive integers contains at most $\lceil N_\theta^{(0)}/B\rceil$ members of one class modulo $B$, which proves [\[eq:resolved-length-bound\]](TPC-094.md#ref-852ec4927146ec28). Parts (i) and (ii) follow immediately. In part (iii), the divisibility of $\Omega_\xi=\ell_\theta v_\theta\sigma_\theta B$ forces $q_X\mid B$; now use part (i).

> **Remark: Short is a physical return, not a deletion** The estimate $N_{\theta,b}\le Y$ does not make the corresponding term zero. It identifies a short-fiber return whose complete outer coefficient, masks, and reconstruction-multiplier mass must still be bounded.

## General disjoint partition

Fix an integer short threshold $Y_X\ge1$ and a conductor threshold $Q_X^{\rm cond}\ge2$. Let $\Xi_X$ be the finite set of all resolved $(\theta,c,\kappa,r)$ keys before estimating any sector. Put in $\mathfrak N$ every insoluble key, empty interval, or squareful $B$ branch, as well as every key with $\mu(\kappa)=0$. Such terms are exactly zero.

For every remaining key define: $$\begin{aligned}  \mathfrak S  &=  \{\xi:N_\xi\le Y_X\},\\  \mathfrak C  &=  \{\xi:N_\xi>Y_X,\ q_\xi^\ast=1\},\\  \mathfrak L_{\rm res}  &=  \{\xi:N_\xi>Y_X,\ 2\le q_\xi^\ast\le Q_X^{\rm cond},               \ \mathcal R(\xi)\},\\  \mathfrak L_{\rm gen}  &=  \{\xi:N_\xi>Y_X,\ 2\le q_\xi^\ast\le Q_X^{\rm cond},               \ \neg\mathcal R(\xi)\},\\  \mathfrak H_{\rm res}  &=  \{\xi:N_\xi>Y_X,\ q_\xi^\ast>Q_X^{\rm cond},               \ \mathcal R(\xi)\},\\  \mathfrak H_{\rm gen}  &=  \{\xi:N_\xi>Y_X,\ q_\xi^\ast>Q_X^{\rm cond},               \ \neg\mathcal R(\xi)\}.\end{aligned}$$

###### ref-9bc21f19cf5855a4

###### ref-9f1f658cbe4b02cb

> **Theorem: Mutually exclusive complete sector partition** \[thm:complete-sector-partition\] One has the disjoint union $$\boxed{  \Xi_X  =  \mathfrak N\sqcup\mathfrak S\sqcup\mathfrak C  \sqcup\mathfrak L_{\rm res}\sqcup\mathfrak L_{\rm gen}  \sqcup\mathfrak H_{\rm res}\sqcup\mathfrak H_{\rm gen}.} \label{eq:complete-sector-partition}$$ Consequently the literal resolved low window is the exact sum of the six potentially nonzero sector contributions. No exceptional, constant, resonant, or short fiber is hidden in the generic term.

> **Proof** Every key is first either structurally null or nonnull. A nonnull key is short or long. A long key has conductor $1$, between $2$ and $Q_X^{\rm cond}$, or larger than $Q_X^{\rm cond}$. In each nonconstant conductor range exactly one of $\mathcal R(\xi)$ and its negation holds. These successive binary and trinary alternatives are disjoint and exhaustive.

## The natural prime-modulus specialization

###### ref-2d1f15e5b4898ef6

> **Corollary: Five active sectors under the prime-modulus gap** \[cor:natural-sector-collapse\] Assume the hypotheses of [\[thm:prime-modulus-conductor-gap\]](TPC-094.md#ref-0cc16f1f145c6ee6) and choose $$C_X\le Y_X,\qquad  C_X\le Q_X^{\rm cond}<q_X.$$ Then:
>
> 1.  $q_\xi^\ast\le Q_X^{\rm cond}$ is equivalent to $q_X\mid\Omega_\xi$;
>
> 2.  $q_\xi^\ast>Q_X^{\rm cond}$ is equivalent to $q_X\nmid\Omega_\xi$;
>
> 3.  $\mathfrak L_{\rm res}=\varnothing$.
>
> Thus the only active sectors are $$\mathfrak S,\quad\mathfrak C,\quad\mathfrak L_{\rm gen},\quad  \mathfrak H_{\rm res},\quad\mathfrak H_{\rm gen}.$$

> **Proof** The conductor gap gives (i) and (ii). If $\xi\in\mathfrak L_{\rm res}$, then $$N_\xi\le q_\xi^\ast\le C_X\le Y_X$$ by [\[lem:resonance-conductor-length\]](TPC-094.md#ref-3b52743d1091e047), contradicting the long condition $N_\xi>Y_X$.

###### ref-9175398ef58a4e8d

###### ref-7099604ce384c0e6

> **Corollary: Four active sectors on canonical support** \[cor:literal-four-sector-collapse\] Under all hypotheses and threshold choices of [\[cor:natural-sector-collapse\]](TPC-094.md#ref-2d1f15e5b4898ef6), use the literal common-target provenance proved in [\[prop:literal-content-divides-phase-slope\]](TPC-094.md#ref-14375fdf2e662553). Then $$\mathfrak L_{\rm res}=\mathfrak L_{\rm gen}=\varnothing.$$ The complete structurally nonnull partition is $$\boxed{  \Xi_X\setminus\mathfrak N  =  \mathfrak S\sqcup\mathfrak C\sqcup\mathfrak H_{\rm res}\sqcup\mathfrak H_{\rm gen}.} \label{eq:literal-four-sector-partition}$$ Every key in $\mathfrak C$ has a phase constant for all low-window frequencies. Every key in $\mathfrak H_{\rm res}\cup\mathfrak H_{\rm gen}$ has conductor exactly $q_X$.

> **Proof** Apply [\[thm:literal-binary-conductor\]](TPC-094.md#ref-1da22f6bf8df7fe3): the only conductors are $1$ and $q_X$. The first belongs to $\mathfrak C$ after short fibers are removed, and the second is above $Q_X^{\rm cond}<q_X$.

> **Remark: Why predicates must not be conflated** Constancy implies resonance, but is separated first because it has no additive variation at all. Low conductor and resonance can overlap for arbitrary thresholds, which is why both $\mathfrak L_{\rm res}$ and $\mathfrak L_{\rm gen}$ appear in the general partition. Shortness depends on $B$ and the native interval; it is not a frequency property. The hierarchical definition is what makes [\[eq:complete-sector-partition\]](TPC-094.md#ref-9f1f658cbe4b02cb) genuinely disjoint.

# Multiplier-mass accounting

We now state the accounting rule that turns the set partition into a valid physical return.

## Normalized branch sums

###### ref-ed36f79897a453ea

###### ref-98c3a4290d3eaca5

###### ref-470624f6b6644342

Let $$\beta=(\theta,c,\kappa)$$ be a soluble nonempty branch, with $b=c\kappa$, progression step $B_\beta$, and robustness content $H_\beta$. Combine the literal smooth weights and all native periodic masks with the two extraction masks into $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal W_{\beta,X}(z)  ={}&  \chi_\theta(\tau_\beta+B_\beta z)  W_{\theta,X}(\tau_\beta+B_\beta z) \notag\\  &\times  \mathbf 1_{(H_\beta,\widetilde D_\beta(z))=1}  \mathbf 1_{(B_\beta,V_\beta(z))=1}. \label{eq:normalized-branch-weight}\end{aligned}$$ Define $$S_{\beta,X}(r)  =  \sum_{z\in I_\beta^\ast}  \mu(\widetilde D_\beta(z))\mu(V_\beta(z))  \mathcal W_{\beta,X}(z)e(-\alpha_{\beta,r}z). \label{eq:normalized-branch-sum}$$ The exact outer coefficient, apart from the unit phase $\zeta_{\beta,r}$, is $$A_{\beta,X}  =  c_{\theta,X}\mu(\kappa)\mu(B_\beta)\mu(H_\beta)  \mathbf 1_{c\kappa\mid P_\theta}. \label{eq:normalized-outer-coefficient}$$ On the canonical TPC-32 branch, $H_\beta=1$.

## The correct exceptional cost

###### ref-6479936bc104e39a

###### ref-863e637743c2897c

###### ref-5022f5e310a0438f

> **Definition: Branchwise multiplier mass** \[def:branchwise-multiplier-mass\] For a branch $\beta$ and a sector $\mathfrak E$, put $$\mathfrak M_{\beta,X}(\mathfrak E)  =  \sum_{\substack{r\in\mathscr W_X(R_X)\\                  (\beta,r)\in\mathfrak E}}  |\mathfrak m_{K,X}(r)|. \label{eq:branchwise-multiplier-mass}$$ Also put $$\mathcal A_{\beta,X}  =  \sum_{z\in I_\beta^\ast}  |\mathcal W_{\beta,X}(z)|  \mu^2(\widetilde D_\beta(z))\mu^2(V_\beta(z)). \label{eq:branch-absolute-mass}$$

###### ref-c0ba346c91ca4fe3

###### ref-092776b7e897319f

> **Theorem: Multiplier-mass return inequality** \[thm:multiplier-mass-return\] For every sector $\mathfrak E$, $$\boxed{  |\mathcal L_X(\mathfrak E)|  \le  \sum_\beta  |A_{\beta,X}|\,  \mathcal A_{\beta,X}\,  \mathfrak M_{\beta,X}(\mathfrak E).} \label{eq:multiplier-mass-return}$$ In particular, a frequency-cardinality bound is not a replacement for the multiplier mass in [\[eq:multiplier-mass-return\]](TPC-094.md#ref-092776b7e897319f).

> **Proof** The unit phase has modulus one and $$|S_{\beta,X}(r)|\le\mathcal A_{\beta,X}.$$ Apply the triangle inequality to [\[eq:exact-sector-contribution\]](TPC-094.md#ref-e008f91a3d0e09fb), retaining the literal branch coefficient. The resulting inner sum is exactly [\[eq:branchwise-multiplier-mass\]](TPC-094.md#ref-863e637743c2897c).

> **Remark: Sufficient, not necessary** is the correct absolute return of one exceptional sector. Cancellation may also occur between its frequencies or outer branches, so the inequality is not claimed to be necessary. What is invalid is to discard the sector using only the number of frequencies without proving a bound for the weighted sum that actually occurs.

## Consequences of filter decay

###### ref-f28617d7963f79dc

###### ref-7fde431dc1e871a3

TPC-86 supplies $$|\mathfrak m_{K,X}(r)|  \ll_K(1+d_{q_X}(r,0))^{-K}.$$ Therefore $$\mathfrak M_{\beta,X}(\mathfrak E)  \ll_K  \sum_{\substack{r\in\mathscr W_X(R_X)\\                  (\beta,r)\in\mathfrak E}}  (1+d_{q_X}(r,0))^{-K}. \label{eq:multiplier-decay-mass}$$ If every frequency in the sector has $d_{q_X}(r,0)\ge R_0$, then $$\mathfrak M_{\beta,X}(\mathfrak E)  \ll_K R_0^{1-K}. \label{eq:far-frequency-mass}$$

###### ref-a216b7c12e4d3a97

There is also an exact description of the constant sector. When $q_X\mid\Omega_\beta$, write $\Omega_\beta=q_X\Omega_\beta'$ and put $$\delta_\beta  =  \frac{c}{(c,\Omega_\beta')}.$$ By [\[eq:constant-divisibility\]](TPC-094.md#ref-00c487660a70c0f7), the constant frequencies are exactly the nonzero signed lifts $\widetilde r$ divisible by $\delta_\beta$. Hence $$\mathfrak M_{\beta,X}(\mathfrak C)  \ll_K \delta_\beta^{-K}. \label{eq:constant-multiplier-mass}$$ This can be small when $\delta_\beta$ grows, but can be of constant order when $\delta_\beta=1$. It gives no cancellation inside the corresponding affine Möbius sum.

###### ref-e946c4028f17c25e

###### ref-7696b5e330e55a56

> **Corollary: No filter-mass discount for a literal constant branch** \[cor:no-literal-constant-discount\] On canonical common-target support, $$q_X\mid\Omega_\beta  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad  c\mid\Omega_\beta/q_X  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad  \delta_\beta=1.$$ Hence, if the branch is long, every low-window frequency belongs to $\mathfrak C$ and $$\mathfrak M_{\beta,X}(\mathfrak C)  =  \sum_{r\in\mathscr W_X(R_X)}  |\mathfrak m_{K,X}(r)|  \ll_K1. \label{eq:literal-constant-full-mass}$$ The bound is uniform but gives no decaying factor.

> **Proof** Canonical support gives $c\mid\Omega_\beta$. Since $(c,q_X)=1$, the additional divisibility $q_X\mid\Omega_\beta$ implies $c\mid\Omega_\beta/q_X$. Thus $\delta_\beta=1$. makes the phase constant for every $r$, and the filter decay is summable for $K\ge2$.

> **Remark: One heavy frequency can dominate** The decay in [\[eq:multiplier-decay-mass\]](TPC-094.md#ref-f28617d7963f79dc) distinguishes a singleton near frequency $1$ from many far frequencies. Their cardinalities point in the opposite direction from their possible costs. This is why the exceptional ledger must use $\mathfrak M_{\beta,X}$.

## Separate ledgers

No exponent is gained in this paper. The literal $\mathrm{L1}$ identification above imports the cited TPC-93 lossless-provenance theorem and its explicit inverse ledger. If later work proves a zero-mode saving $\eta_Z$ and a determinant-mass loss $\lambda_D$, the compatibility condition remains $$\lambda_D\le2\eta_Z.$$ Independently, all physical localization, short-fiber, completion, and reassembly losses must satisfy the strict endpoint rule $$\Lambda_{\rm phys}<\frac1{400}.$$ The same sector loss cannot be entered in both ledgers. Equality in the physical ledger is not a reserve.
