## Abstract

TPC-93 exports the complete low-frequency reconstruction window of the matched fixed-shift shell to primitive affine Möbius fibers. TPC-94 proves that every surviving literal phase has conductor exactly $1$ or the strong no-wrap prime $q_X$. The conductor-one branch was left as a potentially full-mass obstruction. We prove that the entire conductor-one sector is already affordable in the literal packet.

The harmless auxiliary prime can be chosen, still with $q_X\asymp Q_X$, larger than every physical row slope, every row difference, and the orbit diameter. A resolved key has phase slope $$\Omega_\xi=\ell_\xi v_\xi\sigma_\xi B_\xi .$$ Exact common-target provenance gives $$b_\xi\mid M_\xi-n_\xi,\qquad B_\xi\mid b_\xi.$$ The rows are distinct and their difference has modulus below $q_X$, so $B_\xi<q_X$. The other native factors $\ell_\xi,v_\xi$ are smaller than $q_X$, hence on every active literal key $$q_X\mid\Omega_\xi  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad  q_X\mid\sigma_\xi.$$ The opened-divisor relation $$m_\alpha j+h_0=u\sigma_\xi$$ then forces $q_X\mid m_\alpha j+h_0$. Since $q_X\nmid m_\alpha$ and the actual orbit interval has diameter less than $q_X$, each moving row admits at most one such orbit point.

Using the literal row $\ell^1$ bounds, the subpower divisor envelopes of both matched prefixes, the exact-content expansion mass, the row-gcd projector mass, and the bounded $\ell^1$ mass of the low-window reconstruction multiplier, we obtain $$\boxed{  |\mathcal L^{\rm const}_{K,R,X}|  \ll_{K,h_0,\mathscr D}  X^{o(1)}Q_X^2.}$$ At the critical scales this is $X^{o(1)}N_{0,X}/J_X$, exactly the required amplitude scale. The result removes the complete conductor-one sector from the list of possible polynomial obstructions. It does not bound the remaining short nonconstant, resonant, or high-conductor generic sectors and proves no new signed Möbius cancellation theorem or prime-pair result.

# Introduction

###### ref-43157d8277784f32

###### ref-3ead436cb8fdab8b

On the high-$\beta$ endpoint row, the physical scales are $$Q_X=X^{267/400+o(1)},\qquad  J_X=X^{133/400+o(1)},\qquad  N_{0,X}=J_XQ_X^2.$$ The zero-mode route isolated in TPC-85–TPC-88 asks for the amplitude scale $$X^{o(1)}Q_X^2  =  X^{o(1)}\frac{N_{0,X}}{J_X}. \label{eq:target-amplitude}$$ TPC-93 gives an exact low-frequency export of the literal matched shell [`\citep{WangTPC93}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-098.md#cite-2d5bd9665d7257b5). After exact-content resolution, one surviving affine key $\xi$ has two primitive forms of determinant $h_0$, a finite interval of affine parameters, and additive phase $$e_{c_\xi q_X}\!  \left(-r_\xi\varepsilon_\xi        \Omega_\xi z\right),  \qquad  \Omega_\xi  =\ell_\xi v_\xi\sigma_\xi B_\xi.$$ TPC-94 proves that literal common-target provenance gives $c_\xi\mid\Omega_\xi$; since $q_X$ is prime and $0<|r_\xi|<q_X$, the reduced conductor is exactly $1$ or $q_X$ [`\citep{WangTPC94}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-098.md#cite-61d27506f94490b2). Conductor $1$ is equivalent to $$q_X\mid\Omega_\xi. \label{eq:constant-condition}$$ The reconstruction filter gives this branch its full low-frequency multiplier mass, so Fourier decay alone supplies no discount.

The present paper uses a different feature: the no-wrap modulus is auxiliary. It may be enlarged by a fixed factor without changing a single physical coefficient. Once it dominates all rows, row differences, and orbit diameters, exact common-target provenance makes the progression step divide a nonzero row difference below $q_X$. The remaining native factors except the complementary divisor $\sigma_\xi$ are also below $q_X$. Consequently [\[eq:constant-condition\]](TPC-098.md#ref-3ead436cb8fdab8b) forces $q_X\mid\sigma_\xi$, and the original orbit target occupies one residue class modulo $q_X$. The actual orbit interval is shorter than $q_X$, hence at most one orbit point survives for each moving row.

## What is new

The main result, [\[thm:literal-constant-bound\]](TPC-098.md#ref-e37b5e301e0eb90a), is a literal growing-packet upper bound, not merely a finite-dimensional model. It uses no cancellation of Möbius signs: orbit sparsity alone replaces the usual $O(J_X)$ orbit count by $O(1)$ on the entire conductor-one branch. The two row $\ell^1$ bounds then give $X^{o(1)}Q_X^2$.

This is a one-sector closure. In particular:

-   the apparent $q_X\mid B_\xi$ alternative is proved absent on active literal support, rather than discarded;

-   no estimate is inferred for a nonconstant phase merely from its conductor $q_X$;

-   the bound has only soft reserve at [\[eq:target-amplitude\]](TPC-098.md#ref-43157d8277784f32); and

-   the determinant compatibility $\lambda_D\le2\eta_Z$ and the strict physical budget $\Lambda_{\rm phys}<1/400$ remain separate.

# The literal frame and a stronger harmless padding

## Rows, orbit points, and opened divisors

Fix $h_0\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}$. On one physical dyadic packet a row is $$\alpha=(\ell_\alpha,d_\alpha),\qquad  m_\alpha=\ell_\alpha d_\alpha\asymp_{\mathscr D}Q_X,$$ and its orbit target is $$N_\alpha(j)=m_\alpha j+h_0. \label{eq:physical-target}$$ The joint orbit support lies in a fixed union of intervals contained in an interval $\mathcal J_X$ of diameter $O_{\mathscr D}(J_X)$. The two row coefficients satisfy the imported bounds $$\sum_\alpha|\gamma_\alpha^{(i)}|  \ll_{\mathscr D}Q_X,  \qquad i=1,2. \label{eq:row-l1}$$ These are the literal row coefficients, including prime-source, squarefree-divisor, smooth, and residue factors [`\citep{WangTPC33}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-098.md#cite-3dc89b1d29e24761).

Opening the ultra increment on the moving row gives $$N_\alpha(j)=u\sigma,  \qquad T_X<u\le U_{0,X}, \label{eq:opened-factorization}$$ where $u$ carries the Möbius–logarithmic ultra coefficient and $\sigma$ is the complementary divisor. ## The strengthened no-wrap prime

###### ref-bd2722b86ac61df2

TPC-86 chooses an auxiliary prime $q_X\asymp Q_X$ large enough to embed the determinant support injectively [`\citep{WangTPC86}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-098.md#cite-41b0792996942846). Since all of the following quantities are $O_{\mathscr D}(Q_X)$, Bertrand’s postulate permits the same choice to satisfy $$\begin{split}  q_X>{}&  2\max_\alpha m_\alpha,\qquad  2\max_{\alpha,\gamma}|m_\alpha-m_\gamma|,\qquad  2\max_\alpha d_\alpha,\qquad  2\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal J_X), \end{split} \label{eq:strong-padding}$$ as well as the original determinant no-wrap inequalities. Enlarging the fixed comparison constant changes neither the physical packet nor any exponent.

###### ref-8e034cc841adb400

> **Lemma: Immediate consequences** \[lem:padding-consequences\] For all sufficiently large $X$, the choice [\[eq:strong-padding\]](TPC-098.md#ref-bd2722b86ac61df2) has $$q_X\nmid m_\alpha,\qquad q_X\nmid\ell_\alpha,\qquad  q_X\nmid v$$ on every active row and row-gcd layer. Moreover any residue class modulo $q_X$ meets $\mathcal J_X$ in at most one integer.

> **Proof** Every displayed positive integer is strictly between $0$ and $q_X$. The final assertion follows because two distinct representatives of one residue class differ by at least $q_X$, whereas $\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal J_X)<q_X$.

###### ref-106133c5975272bd

> **Lemma: Literal content steps are below $q_X$** \[lem:content-step-below-q\] Call a child *opened-active* when its two row targets, primitive masks, and exact-content divisibilities all hold before row-gcd projector reassembly; its parent is allowed to cancel after that reassembly. For every opened-active exact-content child, $$1\le B_\xi\le b_\xi<q_X.$$

> **Proof** Write $b_\xi=c_\xi\kappa_\xi$. This integer divides both physical targets $$M_\xi j_\xi+h_0,\qquad n_\xi j_\xi+h_0.$$ The primitive target provenance imported in TPC-93–TPC-94 gives $(b_\xi,j_\xi)=1$. Subtracting the targets therefore gives $$b_\xi\mid(M_\xi-n_\xi)j_\xi  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad  b_\xi\mid M_\xi-n_\xi.$$ The physical off-diagonal mask has $M_\xi\ne n_\xi$, while [\[eq:strong-padding\]](TPC-098.md#ref-bd2722b86ac61df2) gives $|M_\xi-n_\xi|<q_X$. Hence $b_\xi\le|M_\xi-n_\xi|<q_X$. Finally $B_\xi=b_\xi/(b_\xi,\sigma_\xi)\le b_\xi$.

> **Remark** The modulus is still only an auxiliary Fourier-padding device. does not impose a congruence on the original sum and does not select a subsequence of scales.

# The literal constant-phase equivalence

###### ref-9c003e2259cbabde

One soluble exact-content key has $$b=c\kappa,\qquad  B=\frac{b}{(b,\sigma)},$$ and phase slope $$\Omega=\ell v\sigma B. \label{eq:phase-slope}$$ Literal target provenance gives $c\mid\Omega$, and TPC-94 therefore reduces the algebraic phase conductor to $1$ or $q_X$. For every nonzero low frequency, conductor $1$ is exactly $q_X\mid\Omega$.

###### ref-9b1b3ffdc97a9b7e

###### ref-c3865b18a7ebd529

> **Theorem: Literal constant-phase equivalence** \[thm:constant-dichotomy\] On every opened-active literal key, $$q_X\mid\Omega  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad  q_X\mid\sigma. \label{eq:constant-equivalence}$$

> **Proof** gives $0<B<q_X$. The prime $q_X$ divides neither $\ell$ nor $v$ by [\[lem:padding-consequences\]](TPC-098.md#ref-8e034cc841adb400). Euclid’s lemma applied to $\Omega=\ell v\sigma B$ gives the forward implication in [\[eq:constant-equivalence\]](TPC-098.md#ref-c3865b18a7ebd529); the reverse implication is immediate.

###### ref-316c3b8f49f7f956

> **Corollary: No hidden progression-step alternative** \[cor:qB-return\] The general algebraic possibility $q_X\mid B$ is empty on opened-active literal support. Thus every opened-active conductor-one key, including a one-point key, retains the target divisibility $q_X\mid\sigma$.

> **Remark: Algebraic conductor versus interval constancy** On a singleton interval every character is pointwise constant, even when its algebraic conductor is $q_X$. The sector treated here is the conductor-one sector $q_X\mid\Omega$, not the larger class of all characters whose restriction happens to be constant on one point. Those nonconstant-conductor singletons remain in the short sector.

> **Remark: Why primality matters** For a composite padding modulus, its prime factors could split among $\ell,v,\sigma,B$, and [\[eq:constant-equivalence\]](TPC-098.md#ref-c3865b18a7ebd529) would not follow. The prime no-wrap choice is doing genuine arithmetic work here, although it remains an auxiliary embedding.

# One orbit point per moving row

The complement $\sigma$ in [\[eq:opened-factorization\]](TPC-098.md#ref-19e35e0783901851) is not a free affine slope. It remembers the physical target: $$m_\alpha j+h_0=u\sigma.$$ This provenance converts conductor degeneracy into orbit sparsity.

###### ref-ee72da8f6fdf2196

> **Theorem: Orbit sparsity on the constant branch** \[thm:orbit-sparsity\] Fix a moving physical row $\alpha$. Among all orbit points $j\in\mathcal J_X$, at most one can support a conductor-one key obtained by opening the ultra divisor on that row.

###### ref-ed5e8aefd2e6a041

> **Proof** By [\[thm:constant-dichotomy\]](TPC-098.md#ref-9b1b3ffdc97a9b7e), a conductor-one key satisfies $q_X\mid\sigma$. The factorization [\[eq:opened-factorization\]](TPC-098.md#ref-19e35e0783901851) then gives $$m_\alpha j+h_0\equiv0\pmod{q_X}. \label{eq:orbit-congruence}$$ Since $q_X\nmid m_\alpha$, this congruence has exactly one residue class $$j\equiv-h_0m_\alpha^{-1}\pmod{q_X}.$$ By [\[lem:padding-consequences\]](TPC-098.md#ref-8e034cc841adb400), $\mathcal J_X$ contains at most one integer in that class.

###### ref-45e9cf54a05f4ffc

> **Corollary: Both polarizations** \[cor:both-polarizations-sparse\] For the left-ultra polarization, each left moving row admits at most one constant-sector orbit point while the right row remains arbitrary. For the right-ultra polarization, the symmetric statement holds with the row labels exchanged.

###### ref-0c586c9c1e5bab40

> **Remark: Divisor choices do not restore the $J_X$ factor** \[rem:divisor-choices\] At the unique possible orbit point, several divisors $u$ can make $\sigma=(m_\alpha j+h_0)/u$ divisible by $q_X$. Their total absolute coefficient is $X^{o(1)}$ by [\[eq:ultra-envelope\]](TPC-098.md#ref-4f428ecb7fa544bc). Thus divisor multiplicity is a soft factor; it cannot restore $J_X$ distinct orbit points.

###### ref-f2da447acbced773

> **Proposition: Sharpness of the one-point statement** \[prop:one-point-sharp\] The conclusion of [\[thm:orbit-sparsity\]](TPC-098.md#ref-ee72da8f6fdf2196) cannot be improved to zero using only interval length and primitivity. For any prime $q\nmid m$ and any fixed $h_0$, an interval containing $-h_0m^{-1}\pmod q$ has one solution of $q\mid mj+h_0$.

> **Proof** Choose the canonical representative of the unique residue class and an interval containing it. No Möbius sign or target mask has been used, so nothing in the stated hypotheses forbids that point.

# A fully opened literal mass bound

## Why the moving ultra sum must be opened first

On the left-ultra polarization the signed factor $$C_{m_\alpha}(j)  =  \sum_{\substack{u\mid N_\alpha(j)\\T_X<u\le U_{0,X}}}  a_R(u)$$ contains both conductor-one children $$q_X\mid\sigma=\frac{N_\alpha(j)}u$$ and nonconstant children. Cancellation between those two classes can make $C_{m_\alpha}(j)$ smaller than the conductor-one subsum. Therefore $|C_{m_\alpha}(j)|$ is not an upper bound for the absolute constant-sector child mass. The row-gcd projector has the same issue: its children may cancel even when their absolute constant-sector mass is nonzero.

We consequently take total variation only after every relevant child has been opened. This order also includes projector children whose unexpanded parent is killed by the actual mask; such children cancel in the full projector identity but cannot be silently deleted from a sectorwise absolute majorant.

## Columnwise projector variation

###### ref-fcdbe80cac7981c0

###### ref-5647387bb146d133

Fix an opposite row $\gamma=(\ell_\gamma,e_\gamma)$. Let $\lambda_{G_X}(v)$ be the exact row-gcd projector coefficient. If the local coprimality indicator is also expanded, its coefficient mass is at most $2^{\omega(v)}$. Put $$\mathfrak P_\gamma  =  \sum_{v\mid e_\gamma}  |\lambda_{G_X}(v)|\,2^{\omega(v)}. \label{eq:column-projector-variation}$$ The literal TPC-33 divisor bounds give, uniformly in the fixed column, $$\mathfrak P_\gamma\le X^{o(1)}. \label{eq:column-projector-soft}$$ For example, on squarefree $e_\gamma$, the elementary bounds $|\lambda_{G_X}(v)|\le\tau(v)$ and $\tau(v)=2^{\omega(v)}$ give $\mathfrak P_\gamma\le5^{\omega(e_\gamma)}=X^{o(1)}$.

The point is the order of quantifiers: [\[eq:column-projector-soft\]](TPC-098.md#ref-5647387bb146d133) is used inside the sum over $\gamma$. It is never promoted to a low-rank decomposition of the full row matrix.

## The fully opened left majorant

###### ref-7e13d1d26bf83d29

Let $$\mathcal U_q(\alpha,j)  =  \left\{  u:  \begin{array}{c}  u\mid N_\alpha(j),\quad T_X<u\le U_{0,X},\\  q_X\mid N_\alpha(j)/u  \end{array}  \right\}.$$ After opening the ultra divisor, row-gcd projector, local coprimality children, exact-content children, finite residue labels, and interval components, the total variation of the left conductor-one sector is bounded by $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal V_L  \le{}&  X^{o(1)}  \sum_\gamma|\gamma_\gamma^{(2)}|  \mathfrak P_\gamma  \sum_\alpha|\gamma_\alpha^{(1)}|  \sum_{\substack{j\in\mathcal J_X\\                  q_X\mid N_\alpha(j)}}  \notag\\  &\qquad\times  \left(  \sum_{u\in\mathcal U_q(\alpha,j)}  |a_R(u)|  \right)  |\mathsf H_{m_\gamma}(j)|. \label{eq:fully-opened-left-majorant}\end{aligned}$$ Here the leading soft factor includes:

-   the exact-content total variation $$\sum_{\substack{c\mid G\\c\le C_X}}   \sum_{\kappa\mid G/c}|\mu(\kappa)|   \le\tau(G)^2=X^{o(1)};$$

-   at most two interval components and the finite periodic/residue separation; and

-   the total-variation norm of the absolutely convergent Mellin/BV representation.

The finite projective mass and the uniform Mellin/BV total-variation control are precisely the separation statements proved in TPC-25 and TPC-33, uniformly in $(\alpha,\gamma,j,u,v,c,\kappa)$ [`\citep{WangTPC25,WangTPC33,WangTPC93}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-098.md#cite-39e8dc14c55a2411); [\[WangTPC33\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-098.md#cite-3dc89b1d29e24761); [\[WangTPC93\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-098.md#cite-2d5bd9665d7257b5). Tonelli’s theorem applies to this nonnegative majorant. Thus continuous separation variables remain inside their total-variation integral and do not become fictitious finite native keys.

###### ref-680e47a1bdb59b29

> **Proposition: Fully opened polarization bound** \[prop:polarization-bound\] Each exact polarization has conductor-one child variation $$\mathcal V_L,\mathcal V_R  \ll_{\mathscr D,h_0}X^{o(1)}Q_X^2.$$

> **Proof** For each moving row $\alpha$, the $j$-sum in [\[eq:fully-opened-left-majorant\]](TPC-098.md#ref-7e13d1d26bf83d29) contains at most one point by [\[thm:orbit-sparsity\]](TPC-098.md#ref-ee72da8f6fdf2196). At that point, $$\sum_{u\in\mathcal U_q(\alpha,j)}|a_R(u)|  \le  \sum_{\substack{u\mid N_\alpha(j)\\T_X<u\le U_{0,X}}}  |\mu(u)|\log u  \le X^{o(1)}$$ by [\[eq:ultra-envelope\]](TPC-098.md#ref-4f428ecb7fa544bc), while $|\mathsf H_{m_\gamma}(j)|\le X^{o(1)}$ by [\[eq:prefix-envelope\]](TPC-098.md#ref-3ca99c3102c68532). Hence $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal V_L  &\le  X^{o(1)}  \sum_\gamma|\gamma_\gamma^{(2)}|\mathfrak P_\gamma  \sum_\alpha|\gamma_\alpha^{(1)}|\\  &\le  X^{o(1)}  \left(\sum_\gamma|\gamma_\gamma^{(2)}|\right)  \left(\sum_\alpha|\gamma_\alpha^{(1)}|\right)  \ll X^{o(1)}Q_X^2\end{aligned}$$ by [\[eq:column-projector-soft\]](TPC-098.md#ref-5647387bb146d133) and [\[eq:row-l1\]](TPC-098.md#ref-70fb90eeca7ec8be). The right-ultra term is identical after exchanging the rows.

## Restoring the reconstruction frequencies

###### ref-6821c04ad9cf7141

For fixed $K\ge2$, the translated low-window reconstruction multiplier obeys $$\sum_{0<d_{q_X}(r,0)\le R_X}  |\mathfrak m_{K,X}(r)|  \ll_K1. \label{eq:filter-l1}$$ The conductor-one predicate is independent of the nonzero low frequency after the literal divisibility $c_\xi\mid\Omega_\xi$ has been imposed. The orientation and fixed phase have modulus one. Thus restoring all frequencies costs only [\[eq:filter-l1\]](TPC-098.md#ref-6821c04ad9cf7141), not their cardinality.

###### ref-e37b5e301e0eb90a

###### ref-00957c3b4e905be0

> **Theorem: Endpoint-scale constant-sector bound** \[thm:literal-constant-bound\] Let $\mathcal L^{\rm const}_{K,R,X}$ be the sum of all fully opened literal TPC-93 low-window children that
>
> 1.  occur in either exact polarization, including children of projector parents that cancel only after reassembly;
>
> 2.  retain every native mask, exact-content label, interval component, prefix, and original coefficient; and
>
> 3.  have algebraic conductor $1$, equivalently $q_X\mid\Omega_\xi$.
>
> Then $$\boxed{  |\mathcal L^{\rm const}_{K,R,X}|  \ll_{K,h_0,\mathscr D}  X^{o(1)}Q_X^2.} \label{eq:main-constant-bound}$$

> **Proof** The absolute value of the sector sum is at most $\mathcal V_L+\mathcal V_R$ times the frequency multiplier $\ell^1$ mass. Apply [\[prop:polarization-bound\]](TPC-098.md#ref-680e47a1bdb59b29) and [\[eq:filter-l1\]](TPC-098.md#ref-6821c04ad9cf7141).

> **Remark: No quotient estimate** The proof works directly with opened physical children, fixed opposite columns, and their native total variation. # Exact finite regression
