## Abstract

TPC-98 returns the constant-phase branch at the endpoint scale, and TPC-99 rewrites the remaining conductor-$q_X$ resonance as a multiplicative incidence problem. A literal provenance audit shows that its weight is attached to a resolved branch $\beta=(\theta,c,\kappa)$, not to a free orbit point. The native key $\theta$ fixes $j_\theta,\sigma_\theta$, while the actual opened divisor and moving source row vary along the resolved $z$-fiber. We build the multiplier linearization only after respecting this indexing.

First refine the branch absolute mass exactly into opened atoms $$p=(\beta,z),\qquad  t_p=\tau_\beta+B_\beta z,\qquad  u_p=U_\theta(t_p),\qquad  m_p=M_\theta(t_p).$$ Their source identity is $$m_pj_\theta+h_0=\sigma_\theta u_p.$$ The branch length remains $N_\beta=\#I_\beta^\ast$, and its TPC-99 resonance width is $\mathsf H_\beta=H_{q_X}(N_\beta)$; neither quantity is recomputed from an opened-atom cell.

On the nonconstant branch, split the opened atoms according to $q_X\mid u_p$. If $q_X\mid u_p$, then $q_X\mid m_pj_\theta+h_0$. Strong no-wrap leaves at most one orbit point for each actual moving source row. Reassembling the source inverse, both polarizations, all content and projector children, masks, and low-frequency multiplier mass gives $$\boxed{\mathcal L^{q\mid u}_{K,R,X}  \ll X^{o(1)}Q_X^2.}$$

If $q_X\nmid u_p$, the native multiplier of $\beta$ satisfies $$\omega_\beta  =  \varepsilon_\beta\frac{\Omega_\beta}{c_\beta}  \equiv  A_p(m_pj_\theta+h_0)\pmod{q_X}, \quad  A_p=\varepsilon_\beta\ell_\beta v_\beta B_\beta  (c_\beta u_p)^{-1}.$$ The integer provenance is $\Omega_\beta/c_\beta =\kappa_\beta\ell_\beta v_\beta(\sigma_\beta/g_\beta)$; $\ell vB/c$ need not be integral. We augment every atom by its TPC-93 child-to-source inverse and form cells by deleting only the coordinate $j_\theta$. All other branch, $z$-fiber, source, content, mask, and width labels remain. Thus a cell and $j$ recover the unique atom. In fact the retained physical values $m_p,u_p,\sigma_p$ and the source identity already recover $j$, so these maximally literal cells are singletons. Their internal injectivity is therefore exact but vacuous; every distinct-atom collision remains in the cross-cell ledger.

The exact TPC-99 census is recovered because the opened weights satisfy $w_\beta=\sum_z w_{\beta,z}$. Its centered energy splits into an opened-atom diagonal ledger and an explicit cross-cell collision excess. Cross-cell pairs include different $z$’s of the same branch as well as different branches. The resulting principal, diagonal, and cross-cell estimates are sufficient gates, not theorems proved here. The finite identities are $\mathrm{L0}$; the complete $q_X\mid u_p$ return and literal crosswalk are $\mathrm{L1}$. No $\mathrm{L2}$ fixed-shift cancellation, parity breakthrough, or prime-pair theorem is claimed.

# Introduction

TPC-93 writes the literal low-frequency reconstruction term as primitive determinant-$h_0$ affine Möbius fibers while retaining the physical child-to-source inverse [`\citep{WangTPC93}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-100.md#cite-2d5bd9665d7257b5). TPC-94 resolves exact content and proves that a literal additive conductor is $1$ or the strong no-wrap prime $q_X$ [`\citep{WangTPC94}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-100.md#cite-61d27506f94490b2). TPC-98 returns the conductor-one branch at $X^{o(1)}Q_X^2$ [`\citep{WangTPC98}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-100.md#cite-b430ed4aad3480af). TPC-99 then writes nonconstant resonance as $$r\omega_\beta\in E_{\mathsf H_\beta},\qquad  \omega_\beta  =  \varepsilon_\beta\frac{\Omega_\beta}{c_\beta}  \in\mathbb F_{q_X}^{\times},$$ and isolates a centered multiplier-dispersion gate [`\citep{WangTPC99}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-100.md#cite-d0d31470086cb360).

The native indices impose a constraint on any further refinement. A TPC-93 key $$\theta=(L,\gamma,\ell,j,\sigma,v,\iota)$$ already fixes $j$ and $\sigma$. After content resolution, $\beta=(\theta,c,\kappa)$ fixes the progression and the resolved fiber $I_\beta^\ast$. Along that fiber, $$t=\tau_\beta+B_\beta z,$$ the actual source data are $$u_{\beta,z}=U_\theta(t),\qquad  m_{\beta,z}=M_\theta(t).$$ Thus $u$ and $m$ are *opened atom values*; neither is a fixed outer coordinate of $\beta$. The branch resonance width is computed before opening: $$N_\beta=\#I_\beta^\ast,\qquad  \mathsf H_\beta=H_{q_X}(N_\beta).$$

## The provenance-safe route

This paper follows four steps.

1.  Split the exact branch absolute mass losslessly as $w_\beta=\sum_{z\in I_\beta^\ast}w_{\beta,z}$, producing $r$-free atoms $p=(\beta,z)$.

2.  Apply the TPC-93 inverse to each atom, recording its actual polarization, source row pair, opened divisor, row-gcd child, masks, content labels, and original branch width.

3.  Split the atom mass by $q_X\mid u_{\beta,z}$. Return the divisible-$u$ submass on the source ledger, where the physical row-$\ell^1$ and divisor envelopes apply.

4.  On $q_X\nmid u_{\beta,z}$, linearize the branch multiplier using the actual source identity and group atoms only through a deletion map whose sole deleted coordinate is $j$.

This order matters. Splitting by a fictitious branchwise $u$, or assigning the full $z$-fiber mass independently to every opened atom, would change the TPC-99 census.

## What advances

The $q_X\mid u_{\beta,z}$ opened submass is returned at the target scale. On the complementary submass, every fine provenance cell has an affine multiplier $$\omega=A(mj+h_0)$$ with $A,m\ne0\bmod q_X$, hence only diagonal internal collisions. Because the retained source values already determine $j$, every such fine cell is a singleton. The complete centered energy is therefore an exact sum of a single-atom diagonal ledger and a cross-cell excess; no within-cell dispersion gain is inferred.

This is deliberately a fine decomposition. Two atoms belonging to the same $\beta$ but different $z$’s normally have different source values $u_{\beta,z},m_{\beta,z}$, so they lie in different cells while retaining the same $\omega_\beta$. Their collision is therefore present in the cross-cell term. No false dispersion is created by atomizing the branch weight.

## The augmented atom record

TPC-93 gives an explicit inverse from a child $(\theta,t)$ to the preprojector source atom. Augment $p=(\beta,z)$ by:

-   the child coordinate $t_p=\tau_\beta+B_\beta z$ and the inverse source atom, including polarization, actual moving and opposite rows, $j_p$, and the opened value $u_p$;

-   the projector divisor $v_p$, interval component, actual masks, prefix, residue, smooth, and reconstruction labels;

-   $c_\beta,\kappa_\beta,b_\beta,g_\beta,B_\beta$, the branch interval $I_\beta^\ast$, $N_\beta,\mathsf H_\beta$, and $z$; and

-   every label needed to reverse the exact-content and source reconstructions; the atom weight $w_{\beta,z}$ is attached as a coefficient payload, not used as a grouping label.

Redundant derived values such as $m_p,u_p,\sigma_p$ are retained on purpose; they are provenance checks, not new multiplicities.

###### ref-c4c6a7507dcf4a21

> **Definition: Fine provenance cell** \[def:fine-cell\] Flatten the augmented provenance record: $\beta$ is not kept as an opaque token that could secretly retain $j_\theta$. Let $\operatorname{del}_j(p)$ delete the single native coordinate $j_p$ and its redundant copies, while deleting no independent coordinate. The attached numerical weight is not part of this projection. A fine cell $C$ is one fiber of $\operatorname{del}_j$. Write $$P_C=\{p:\operatorname{del}_j(p)=C\},\qquad  J_C=\{j_p:p\in P_C\}.$$

###### ref-0f63b9655cdae0fe

> **Theorem: Lossless opened-atom disintegration** \[thm:lossless-atom-cells\] Assume the TPC-93 child-to-source inverse and the TPC-94 exact content reconstruction. Then:
>
> 1.  $(C,j_p)$ reconstructs the unique pair $(\beta,z)$, its $r$-free source atom, and its weight $w_{\beta,z}$;
>
> 2.  in fact $C$ already determines $$j_p=\frac{\sigma_pu_p-h_0}{m_p},$$ so every nonempty fine cell $P_C$ is a singleton;
>
> 3.  all $p\in P_C$ have common values $m_C,u_C,\ell_C,v_C,c_C,B_C,\varepsilon_C,\mathsf H_C$; and
>
> 4.  summing the cells gives exactly the second line of [\[eq:atomized-census\]](TPC-100.md#ref-8d0b6babd0e60b3d).

> **Proof** The flattened augmented record contains every coordinate of $(\beta,z)$, its TPC-93 inverse source record, and every reconstruction label. It contains no opaque branch identifier. The map $\operatorname{del}_j$ removes only the native $j$ coordinate and direct stored copies, so restoring that coordinate restores the original record and its attached coefficient. This proves (i). The retained physical coordinates $m_p,u_p,\sigma_p$ remain. Their source identity [\[eq:atom-source-identity\]](TPC-100.md#ref-2e099d06aaea3581) determines the displayed integer $j_p$, proving (ii). Every quantity in (iii) remains in the deletion record, either as a native coordinate or an explicitly retained source value. Finally, the cells form a disjoint partition of the opened atoms, so (iv) is just finite reindexing of [\[eq:atomized-census\]](TPC-100.md#ref-8d0b6babd0e60b3d).

> **Remark: No claim that $\theta$ fixes $u$** The cell fixes the *opened source value* $u_C=U_\theta(t_p)$ only after $z$ has been opened and the source inverse recorded. The branch $\beta$, and in particular $\theta$, does not fix $u$ or $m$ along its full resolved fiber.

###### ref-c6a5fd5adf22d4d1

> **Remark: The fine-cell injection is deliberately vacuous** \[rem:singleton-cells\] The singleton conclusion is not an equidistribution statement. It is the unavoidable price of retaining the complete source inverse while deleting only the displayed $j$-coordinate: the determinant identity algebraically recovers that coordinate. Consequently all collisions between distinct opened atoms, including same-$\beta$ different-$z$ collisions, occur in the cross-cell ledger.

# The opened ultra-value split

> **Theorem: One orbit point for a fixed actual source row** \[thm:atom-q-u-one-point\] Fix an actual moving source row $m$. Among orbit points $j\in\mathcal J_X$, at most one orbit coordinate can occur for opened atoms satisfying $$m_p=m,\qquad q_X\mid u_p.$$

> **Theorem: Atomwise affine multiplier formula** \[thm:atomwise-linearization\] Let $p=(\beta,z)$ be a nonconstant opened atom with $q_X\nmid u_p$. Define $$A_p  =  \varepsilon_\beta\ell_\beta v_\beta B_\beta  (c_\beta u_p)^{-1}  \in\mathbb F_{q_X}^{\times}. \label{eq:atom-A}$$ Then $$\boxed{  \omega_\beta  =  A_p(m_pj_p+h_0)  \quad\text{in }\mathbb F_{q_X}^{\times}.} \label{eq:atom-affine-omega}$$ Equivalently, $$A_p  =  \varepsilon_\beta\kappa_\beta\ell_\beta v_\beta  (u_pg_\beta)^{-1}. \label{eq:atom-A-second}$$

> **Proof** All factors in [\[eq:atom-A\]](TPC-100.md#ref-736911a8aecaa501) are nonzero modulo $q_X$. Reduce the branch multiplier and use the atom source identity: $$\begin{aligned}  \omega_\beta  &\equiv  \varepsilon_\beta\ell_\beta v_\beta B_\beta  c_\beta^{-1}\sigma_\beta\\  &\equiv  \varepsilon_\beta\ell_\beta v_\beta B_\beta  (c_\beta u_p)^{-1}(m_pj_p+h_0).\end{aligned}$$ This proves [\[eq:atom-affine-omega\]](TPC-100.md#ref-99cd7e06ec22d790). The identity $B_\beta g_\beta=c_\beta\kappa_\beta$ gives [\[eq:atom-A-second\]](TPC-100.md#ref-d64bdcbf74a2ae5a).

> **Remark: Dependence on $z$** The left side $\omega_\beta$ is constant for $z\in I_\beta^\ast$. The coefficient $A_p$ and source row $m_p$ generally vary with $z$, because $$u_p=U_\theta(\tau_\beta+B_\beta z),\qquad  m_p=M_\theta(\tau_\beta+B_\beta z).$$ No theorem here treats $u$ or $m$ as fixed on $\beta$.

By [\[thm:lossless-atom-cells\]](TPC-100.md#ref-0f63b9655cdae0fe)(i), the common cell record and this coordinate reconstruct the same $(\beta,z)$. Hence $p=p'$.
