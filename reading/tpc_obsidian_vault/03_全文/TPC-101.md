## Abstract

TPC-99 diagonalizes the multiplicative resonance carrier but bounds its nonprincipal part by the largest incomplete-character eigenvalue, costing $\sqrt q\log q$ at every resonance width. That estimate treats the low-window reconstruction weight as an arbitrary $L^2$ vector. In the literal problem this weight is fixed and has bounded Wiener mass. We exploit that asymmetry.

For $G=\mathbb F_q^\times$, $E_H=\{\pm1,\ldots,\pm H\}$, and $$\mathcal I_H(a,b)  =  \sum_{\omega,r\in G}  a(\omega)b(r)\mathbf 1_{E_H}(r\omega),$$ write $\mathcal R_H(a,b)$ for the nonprincipal part. We prove that the exact norm of this functional in the centered $a$ variable is $$\mathfrak S_H(b)^2  =  \frac1{q-1}  \sum_{\chi\ne\chi_0}  |\widehat{\mathbf 1_{E_H}}(\chi)|^2  |\widehat b(\overline\chi)|^2.$$ Consequently $$|\mathcal R_H(a,b)|  \le  \|b\|_1  \sqrt{\frac{2H(q-1-2H)}{q-1}}\,  \|a^\circ\|_2.$$ The factor is exact using only $\|b\|_1$: a point mass $b$ attains it. It vanishes at $H=(q-1)/2$, and for the literal width $H_q(N)$ it is at most $\sqrt{2q/N}$ whenever $N\ge3$.

Applied to the corrected frequency-free TPC-99 census and its fixed filter $b_X=|\mathfrak m_{K,X}|$, this replaces the uniform $Q_X^{3/2}$ centered-energy target by the weaker width-sensitive gate $$\sum_H  \sqrt{\frac{2H(q_X-1-2H)}{q_X-1}}\,  \|a_H^\circ\|_2  \ll X^{o(1)}Q_X^2.$$ After TPC-100 separately returns its $q_X\mid u$ subcarrier, its exact singleton-cell decomposition produces separate width-weighted atomic-diagonal and cross-cell gates. The singleton fact itself supplies no dispersion. We also prove an abstract principal-to-diagonal transfer under a stated maximum-atom bound. The finite-group theorem is $\mathrm{L0}$, and the literal crosswalk is $\mathrm{L1}$. The principal mass, literal atom cap, and cross-cell estimate are not proved here; no $\mathrm{L2}$ fixed-shift cancellation or prime-pair conclusion is claimed.

# Introduction

The literal low-window export retains the original fixed nonzero shift, source rows, exact-content branches, projector children, actual masks, and reconstruction multiplier [`\citep{WangTPC93,WangTPC94}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-101.md#cite-2d5bd9665d7257b5); [\[WangTPC94\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-101.md#cite-61d27506f94490b2). After the conductor-one branch is returned, TPC-99 identifies every remaining resonant nonconstant branch with an incidence condition $$r\omega_\beta\in E_H  \subset\mathbb F_{q_X}^{\times}.$$ Its multiplicative-character theorem is exact. The quantitative return used there, however, invokes the largest nonprincipal singular value $$\max_{\chi\ne\chi_0}  |\widehat{\mathbf 1_{E_H}}(\chi)|  \ll\sqrt q\log q$$ and then pays this worst value against the whole centered multiplier census [`\citep{WangTPC99}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-101.md#cite-d0d31470086cb360).

There is an unused asymmetry. The multiplier census $a_H$ is the unknown growing object, but the frequency weight $$b_X(r)  =  |\mathfrak m_{K,X}(r)|  \mathbf 1_{\{0<d_{q_X}(r,0)\le R_X\}}$$ is fixed by the zero-arc reconstruction and satisfies $\|b_X\|_1\ll_K1$. Instead of bounding the operator uniformly for all $b$, one may compute the exact norm of the scalar functional $a\mapsto\mathcal R_H(a,b_X)$. Cauchy–Schwarz then falls on the *whole* nonprincipal spectrum, whose squared mass is known exactly: $$\sum_{\chi\ne\chi_0}  |\widehat{\mathbf 1_{E_H}}(\chi)|^2  =2H(q-1-2H).$$ This exchanges a worst-eigenvalue cost for a Wiener-to-Hilbert–Schmidt transfer.

## What changes

Define $$g_q(H)  =  \sqrt{\frac{2H(q-1-2H)}{q-1}}.$$ The old nonprincipal return was $$|\mathcal R_H(a_H,b_X)|  \ll_K\sqrt q\log q\,\|a_H^\circ\|_2.$$ The new one is $$|\mathcal R_H(a_H,b_X)|  \ll_K g_q(H)\|a_H^\circ\|_2.$$ This is never worse up to harmless constants and is asymptotically better when $H=o(q)$; the improvement is polynomial when $H\le q^{1-\delta}$ for a fixed $\delta>0$, apart from logarithms. It also records the exact endpoint: $g_q((q-1)/2)=0$, because $E_H=G$ and the incidence operator is purely principal.

## What does not change

The principal term $$\frac{2H}{q-1}\|a_H\|_1\|b_X\|_1$$ is untouched. Nor does the theorem estimate the native census. TPC-100 decomposes its centered energy into an opened-atom diagonal ledger and a cross-cell collision excess [`\citep{WangTPC100}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-101.md#cite-3a25dec8d6d49e3b); the present theorem merely assigns the correct width cost to those quantities. Thus it opens a wider analytic door without relabeling an interface result as fixed-shift arithmetic cancellation.

# The exact fixed-filter functional norm

Let $q$ be an odd prime, put $G=\mathbb F_q^\times$, and write $n=q-1$. For $1\le H\le n/2$, set $$E_H=\{\pm1,\ldots,\pm H\}\subset G.$$ For $f:G\to\mathbb C$, use $$\widehat f(\chi)  =  \sum_{x\in G}f(x)\overline{\chi(x)},\qquad  f^\circ=f-\frac1n\Big(\sum_xf(x)\Big)\mathbf 1_G.$$ Parseval reads $$\sum_{\chi\in\widehat G}|\widehat f(\chi)|^2  =  n\sum_{x\in G}|f(x)|^2.$$

###### ref-6757392b27611713

TPC-99 proves the exact decomposition $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal I_H(a,b)  ={}&  \frac{2H}{n}  \left(\sum_\omega a(\omega)\right)  \left(\sum_r b(r)\right)  +\mathcal R_H(a,b), \label{eq:principal-remainder}\\  \mathcal R_H(a,b)  ={}&  \frac1n  \sum_{\chi\ne\chi_0}  \widehat{\mathbf 1_{E_H}}(\chi)  \widehat a(\overline\chi)  \widehat b(\overline\chi). \label{eq:exact-remainder}\end{aligned}$$

> **Definition: Fixed-filter spectral mass** \[def:fixed-filter-mass\] For $b:G\to\mathbb C$, define $$\mathfrak S_H(b)^2  =  \frac1n  \sum_{\chi\ne\chi_0}  |\widehat{\mathbf 1_{E_H}}(\chi)|^2  |\widehat b(\overline\chi)|^2. \label{eq:fixed-filter-mass}$$

###### ref-6b5dc212f172003a

###### ref-65375072bbc16435

> **Theorem: Exact fixed-filter norm** \[thm:exact-fixed-filter\] For every $a,b:G\to\mathbb C$, $$|\mathcal R_H(a,b)|  \le  \mathfrak S_H(b)\,\|a^\circ\|_2. \label{eq:exact-functional-bound}$$ For fixed $b$, the constant $\mathfrak S_H(b)$ is the exact operator norm of the functional $$a^\circ\longmapsto\mathcal R_H(a,b)$$ on the centered subspace of $\ell^2(G)$.

> **Proof** Cauchy–Schwarz in [\[eq:exact-remainder\]](TPC-101.md#ref-6757392b27611713) gives $$\begin{aligned}  |\mathcal R_H(a,b)|  &\le  \frac1n  \left(  \sum_{\chi\ne\chi_0}  |\widehat a(\overline\chi)|^2  \right)^{1/2}  \left(  \sum_{\chi\ne\chi_0}  |\widehat{\mathbf 1_{E_H}}(\chi)|^2  |\widehat b(\overline\chi)|^2  \right)^{1/2}\\  &=\|a^\circ\|_2\,\mathfrak S_H(b)\end{aligned}$$ by Parseval. Equality in Cauchy–Schwarz is attained by prescribing the nonprincipal Fourier coefficients of $a$ proportional to the complex conjugates of $$\widehat{\mathbf 1_{E_H}}(\chi)\widehat b(\overline\chi)$$ with the corresponding index reversal. Fourier inversion produces a centered $a$, proving exactness.

###### ref-c9d65f9b4248abe4

###### ref-c3478149950cbdbb

> **Theorem: Wiener-to-Hilbert–Schmidt transfer** \[thm:wiener-hs\] For every $b:G\to\mathbb C$, $$\begin{aligned}  \mathfrak S_H(b)  &\le  \|b\|_1  \sqrt{\frac{2H(q-1-2H)}{q-1}}, \label{eq:wiener-hs-mass}\\  |\mathcal R_H(a,b)|  &\le  \|b\|_1  \sqrt{\frac{2H(q-1-2H)}{q-1}}\,  \|a^\circ\|_2. \label{eq:wiener-hs-bound}\end{aligned}$$

> **Proof** The elementary Wiener bound gives $$|\widehat b(\chi)|\le\sum_{r\in G}|b(r)|=\|b\|_1.$$ TPC-99’s exact nonprincipal Hilbert–Schmidt ledger is $$\sum_{\chi\ne\chi_0}  |\widehat{\mathbf 1_{E_H}}(\chi)|^2  =  2H(q-1-2H).$$ Insert both facts into [\[eq:fixed-filter-mass\]](TPC-101.md#ref-b78c89f0d9f554ce), then use [\[thm:exact-fixed-filter\]](TPC-101.md#ref-6b5dc212f172003a).

> **Remark: No character-sum theorem is used** The factor in [\[eq:wiener-hs-bound\]](TPC-101.md#ref-c3478149950cbdbb) comes from the complete nonprincipal squared spectrum, not from Pólya–Vinogradov or a pointwise estimate for one character. It is therefore sensitive to the resonance width and to the purely principal endpoint.

> **Remark: Why the asymmetric norm is legitimate** The theorem does not claim a smaller operator norm for arbitrary pairs $(a,b)$. It fixes $b$, uses its actual Wiener mass, and takes the norm only in the unknown census $a$. This is precisely the asymmetry of the literal reconstruction filter.

# Sharpness and endpoint geometry

###### ref-f02b21a4a5b8dcc0

Put $$g_q(H)  =  \sqrt{\frac{2H(q-1-2H)}{q-1}}. \label{eq:gq}$$

###### ref-4fbc953c67bf4193

> **Proposition: Sharpness from a frequency atom** \[prop:delta-sharp\] Fix $r_0\in G$, and let $$b=B\mathbf 1_{\{r_0\}},\qquad B\in\mathbb C.$$ Then $$\mathfrak S_H(b)=|B|g_q(H)=\|b\|_1g_q(H).$$ Consequently [\[eq:wiener-hs-mass\]](TPC-101.md#ref-c9d65f9b4248abe4) cannot be improved uniformly using only $q,H$, and $\|b\|_1$.

> **Proof** For every multiplicative character, $$\widehat b(\chi)=B\overline{\chi(r_0)},$$ whose modulus is $|B|$. The assertion follows from [\[eq:fixed-filter-mass\]](TPC-101.md#ref-b78c89f0d9f554ce) and the exact Hilbert–Schmidt ledger.

###### ref-be8ae3e8081c835a

> **Proposition: The full-window endpoint is purely principal** \[prop:full-window\] If $H=(q-1)/2$, then $E_H=G$, $$g_q(H)=0,\qquad  \mathcal R_H(a,b)=0$$ for every $a,b$. Thus $$\mathcal I_H(a,b)  =  \left(\sum_\omega a(\omega)\right)  \left(\sum_r b(r)\right).$$

> **Proof** The indicator of $E_H$ is the constant function on $G$, so all of its nonprincipal Fourier coefficients vanish. The formula also follows directly from the fact that every pair $(r,\omega)$ is an edge.

> **Remark: Two-point fibers** For odd $q$, $$H_q(1)=H_q(2)=\frac{q-1}{2}.$$ Hence resolved fibers of length one or two have a full resonance window. Their multiplier distribution affects only the principal mass, not the centered spectral remainder. This exact endpoint was hidden by a uniform $\sqrt q\log q$ majorant.

###### ref-59534d89a4158325

> **Proposition: Comparison with the worst-eigenvalue route** \[prop:comparison\] For every $H$, $$g_q(H)\le\sqrt{2H}\le\sqrt q.$$ If $H=o(q)$, the ratio between $g_q(H)$ and a $\sqrt q\log q$ cost tends to zero polynomially whenever $H\le q^{1-\delta}$, apart from logarithms. At $H=(q-1)/2$, the ratio is exactly zero.

> **Proof** Drop the factor $(q-1-2H)/(q-1)\le1$ in [\[eq:gq\]](TPC-101.md#ref-f02b21a4a5b8dcc0); the second inequality uses $2H\le q-1$.

> **Remark: What sharpness means** is a sharp statement for the information $\|b\|_1$ alone. The actual reconstruction filter may have additional structure, so a still better filter-specific theorem is not ruled out. Conversely, one cannot simply delete $g_q(H)$ without using such new structure.

# Return to the frequency-free literal census

We use the corrected TPC-99 crosswalk. Its base key is $$\beta=(\theta,c,\kappa),$$ not the resolved key $(\theta,c,\kappa,r)$ containing a frequency. For each soluble nonconstant branch, put $$w_{\beta,X}  =  |A_{\beta,X}|\mathcal A_{\beta,X},$$ where $A_{\beta,X}$ is the exact outer coefficient and $\mathcal A_{\beta,X}$ is the uniform absolute mass of the resolved $z$-fiber. Thus $w_{\beta,X}$ is independent of $r$ and includes one copy of every source, content, projector, mask, and global-normalization factor.

For $$1\le N_\beta\le q_X,\qquad  H_\beta=H_{q_X}(N_\beta),\qquad  \omega_\beta  =  \varepsilon_\theta\frac{\Omega_\beta}{c}  \pmod{q_X},$$ TPC-99 defines $$a_H(\omega)  =  \sum_{\substack{\beta:H_\beta=H\\                   \omega_\beta=\omega}}  w_{\beta,X}. \label{eq:literal-census}$$ The branch length $N_\beta$ is the resolved $z$-fiber cardinality; $j$ is already retained in $\theta$. Branches with $N_\beta>q_X$ have no resonant nonzero frequency.

The fixed positive frequency weight is $$b_X(r)  =  |\mathfrak m_{K,X}(r)|  \mathbf 1_{\{0<d_{q_X}(r,0)\le R_X\}}, \qquad  B_X=\|b_X\|_1\ll_K1.$$ The phase of $\mathfrak m_{K,X}$ is lost only because this is the positive carrier, not the original signed low window.

> **Theorem: Width-sensitive literal carrier bound** \[thm:literal-width-bound\] Let $\mathcal C_{\mathrm{res},X}^{\mathrm{abs}}$ be the corrected TPC-99 positive nonconstant resonance carrier. Then $$\begin{aligned}  \mathcal C_{\mathrm{res},X}^{\mathrm{abs}}  \le{}&  B_X  \sum_H\frac{2H}{q_X-1}\|a_H\|_1 \notag\\  &+  B_X\sum_Hg_{q_X}(H)\|a_H^\circ\|_2. \label{eq:literal-width-bound}\end{aligned}$$ In particular, it is $O(X^{o(1)}Q_X^2)$ if $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_H\frac{H}{q_X}\|a_H\|_1  &\ll X^{o(1)}Q_X^2, \label{eq:new-principal-gate}\\  \sum_Hg_{q_X}(H)\|a_H^\circ\|_2  &\ll X^{o(1)}Q_X^2. \label{eq:new-centered-gate}\end{aligned}$$

> **Proof** TPC-99 gives the exact positive-census identity $$\mathcal C_{\mathrm{res},X}^{\mathrm{abs}}  =  \sum_H\mathcal I_H(a_H,b_X).$$ Apply [\[eq:principal-remainder\]](TPC-101.md#ref-753f2986591d102f) and then [\[thm:wiener-hs\]](TPC-101.md#ref-b250729aedc5ee6d) to every width. # Cell energy and a principal-to-diagonal transfer

###### ref-87c959be603e79d3

TPC-100 first returns the $q_X\mid u$ subcarrier at $O(X^{o(1)}Q_X^2)$. It then refines the remaining $q_X\nmid u$ mass into opened atoms and groups those atoms into lossless cells while retaining the parent branch length $N_\beta$. Write $a_H^{\mathrm{inv}}$ for this invertible-ultra census. For every exact width $H$, TPC-100 proves $$\|(a_H^{\mathrm{inv}})^\circ\|_2^2  =  \mathfrak D_H^\circ+\mathfrak X_H^\circ, \qquad  \mathfrak D_H^\circ\ge0, \label{eq:tpc100-energy}$$ where $\mathfrak D_H^\circ$ is the one-cell diagonal ledger and $\mathfrak X_H^\circ$ is the centered excess from collisions between distinct cells.

At the maximally literal TPC-100 resolution, a cell retains $(m,u,\sigma)$, so the source identity recovers $$j=\frac{\sigma u-h_0}{m}.$$ Every such fine cell is therefore a singleton. Consequently $$\mathfrak D_H^\circ  =  \frac{q_X-2}{q_X-1}  \sum_{\substack{p=(\beta,z):\,H_\beta=H\\                   q_X\nmid u_p}}  w_{\beta,z}^{\,2}.$$ This is an exact atomic diagonal ledger, not an intra-cell dispersion gain; every collision between distinct opened atoms remains in $\mathfrak X_H^\circ$.

> **Theorem: Width-weighted cell gates** \[thm:width-cell-gates\] After the $q_X\mid u$ return, the full principal condition [\[eq:new-principal-gate\]](TPC-101.md#ref-904f15ef1b461f5f) (or its restriction to the invertible census), together with the two estimates $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_Hg_{q_X}(H)\sqrt{\mathfrak D_H^\circ}  &\ll X^{o(1)}Q_X^2, \label{eq:weighted-diagonal-gate}\\  \sum_Hg_{q_X}(H)\sqrt{|\mathfrak X_H^\circ|}  &\ll X^{o(1)}Q_X^2 \label{eq:weighted-cross-gate}\end{aligned}$$ are sufficient to return the remaining invertible positive nonconstant resonance carrier. Adding the already returned $q_X\mid u$ piece gives $O(X^{o(1)}Q_X^2)$ for the complete positive nonconstant carrier.

> **Proof** By [\[eq:tpc100-energy\]](TPC-101.md#ref-87c959be603e79d3), $$\|(a_H^{\mathrm{inv}})^\circ\|_2  \le  \sqrt{\mathfrak D_H^\circ}  +  \sqrt{|\mathfrak X_H^\circ|}.$$ Multiply by $g_{q_X}(H)$, sum over $H$, and apply [\[thm:literal-width-bound\]](TPC-101.md#ref-dd745c0a8616623b).

> **Theorem: Principal-to-diagonal transfer** \[thm:principal-diagonal-transfer\] Define the principal mass without the bounded filter factor by $$\mathfrak P_X^{\mathrm{inv}}  =  \sum_H\frac{2H}{q_X-1}M_H^{\mathrm{inv}}.$$ Then $$\boxed{  \sum_Hg_{q_X}(H)\sqrt{\mathfrak D_H^\circ}  \le  \sqrt{  W_X\,|\mathcal H_X|\,(q_X-1)\,  \mathfrak P_X^{\mathrm{inv}}}.} \label{eq:principal-diagonal-transfer}$$ Consequently, if $$q_X\asymp Q_X,\qquad  W_X\le X^{o(1)},\qquad  \mathfrak P_X^{\mathrm{inv}}\le X^{o(1)}Q_X^2,$$ then [\[eq:weighted-diagonal-gate\]](TPC-101.md#ref-b0aa40ad6b9843f6) follows automatically.

> **Proof** The diagonal definition and nonnegativity give $$0\le\mathfrak D_H^\circ  \le\sum_{C,j}w_C(j)^2  \le W_XM_H^{\mathrm{inv}}.$$ Therefore Cauchy–Schwarz over the active widths yields $$\begin{aligned}  \sum_Hg_{q_X}(H)\sqrt{\mathfrak D_H^\circ}  &\le  \sqrt{W_X}  \sum_{H\in\mathcal H_X}  g_{q_X}(H)\sqrt{M_H^{\mathrm{inv}}}\\  &\le  \sqrt{  W_X|\mathcal H_X|  \sum_Hg_{q_X}(H)^2M_H^{\mathrm{inv}}}.\end{aligned}$$ Since $$g_{q_X}(H)^2\le2H,$$ the last sum is at most $(q_X-1)\mathfrak P_X^{\mathrm{inv}}$, proving [\[eq:principal-diagonal-transfer\]](TPC-101.md#ref-fc61b186fa0ef855). Finally $|\mathcal H_X|\le(q_X-1)/2$ and $q_X\asymp Q_X$. The full principal mass dominates $\mathfrak P_X^{\mathrm{inv}}$ because the opened weights are nonnegative.
