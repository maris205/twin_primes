## Abstract

A frozen-parameter or frequency-averaged estimate is not yet an estimate at one prescribed physical shift $h_0$. The missing operation is evaluation on the actual shift-profile subspace. We give its exact finite-dimensional cost.

Let $A:E\to\mathbb C^I$ be the declared observation map, let $\boldsymbol\Omega=\operatorname{diag}(p_i)$ with $p_i\ge0$, and let $\ell(x)=b_0^*x$ be the target functional. With $G=A^*\boldsymbol\Omega A$, a finite localization inequality $$|\ell(x)|^2\le K\,\|Ax\|_{\boldsymbol\Omega}^2  \qquad(x\in E)$$ exists if and only if $b_0\in\operatorname{ran}G$, equivalently $\ell$ annihilates $\ker A_{\boldsymbol\Omega}$, where $A_{\boldsymbol\Omega}=\boldsymbol\Omega^{1/2}A$. In that case the sharp constant is $$\boxed{\qquad K=b_0^*G^\dagger b_0.\qquad}$$ For a profile space $V=\operatorname{ran}B$, this becomes the Christoffel evaluation cost $$K_V(h_0)=b(h_0)^*   (B^*\boldsymbol\Omega B)^\dagger b(h_0).$$

The formula separates three regimes. The unrestricted profile space costs exactly $1/p_{h_0}$, reproducing the positivity-only localization loss. Constants cost $1$. A $d$-character subspace under uniform averaging costs exactly $d$, independent of the ambient number of shifts. Conversely, if the target does not annihilate the observation kernel, no finite averaged estimate can recover it.

This is a sharp conditional bridge, not a fixed-shift arithmetic estimate. It advances the interface only after the actual TPC shift profile is proved to lie in a controlled subspace. We prove no such regularity, no $\mathrm{L2}$ fixed-power saving, no parity breakthrough, and no prime-pair or twin-prime theorem.

# The missing return from averages

TPC-46 exhibits the sharp positivity cost $\sum_h\omega_h/\omega_{h_0}$ for recovering one shift from a weighted average with no regularity [`\citep{WangTPC46}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-115.md#cite-2f37865d719a8eb5). Later frozen and spectral routes improve averaged or model estimates, but TPC-MVP1 requires an actual prescribed physical shift [`\citep{WangMVP1}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-115.md#cite-934c301de52ef9a3). TPC-113 fixes physical conditioning, and TPC-114 identifies the residual/native Gram and its cancellation kernel [`\citep{WangTPC113,WangTPC114}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-115.md#cite-0988df134318b83f); [\[WangTPC114\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-115.md#cite-95a88dac6b7098cc). The present paper isolates the exact evaluation norm after those maps.

Let $E$ be a finite-dimensional Hilbert space and $$A:E\to\mathbb C^I,\qquad  \boldsymbol\Omega=\operatorname{diag}(p_i:i\in I),\qquad p_i\ge0.$$ Write $$\|z\|_{\boldsymbol\Omega}^2=z^*\boldsymbol\Omega z,\qquad  A_{\boldsymbol\Omega}=\boldsymbol\Omega^{1/2}A,\qquad G=A^*\boldsymbol\Omega A=A_{\boldsymbol\Omega}^*A_{\boldsymbol\Omega}.$$ The target is $\ell(x)=b_0^*x$.

# The exact localization theorem

###### ref-0ff4d7373a13d90e

> **Theorem: Sharp evaluation through a semidefinite observation** \[thm:sharp\] There exists a finite $K$ such that $$|b_0^*x|^2\le K\,x^*Gx\qquad(x\in E)$$ if and only if $$b_0\perp\ker G,  \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad  b_0\in\operatorname{ran}G.$$ When this holds, the least possible constant is $$\boxed{\quad K_{\rm loc}(A,\boldsymbol\Omega;b_0)  =b_0^*G^\dagger b_0.\quad}$$ If it fails, the sharp constant is $+\infty$.

> **Proof** Since $G=A_{\boldsymbol\Omega}^*A_{\boldsymbol\Omega}$, $\ker G=\ker A_{\boldsymbol\Omega}$. If $b_0$ does not annihilate this kernel, some $x\in\ker G$ has $b_0^*x\ne0$, so no finite inequality is possible.
>
> Assume $b_0\in\operatorname{ran}G$. On $(\ker G)^\perp$, put $y=G^{1/2}x$. Then $$b_0^*x=(G^{\dagger/2}b_0)^*y.$$ Cauchy–Schwarz gives $$|b_0^*x|^2  \le\|G^{\dagger/2}b_0\|^2\|G^{1/2}x\|^2  =(b_0^*G^\dagger b_0)(x^*Gx).$$ Equality holds for $y=G^{\dagger/2}b_0$, equivalently along $x=G^\dagger b_0$, unless $b_0=0$, when both sides vanish. Thus the constant is sharp.

> **Remark: Kernel condition, not invertibility** The observation need not be injective on all of $E$. Only the target must vanish on its invisible directions. Requiring a global inverse can therefore overstate the cost, while ignoring the kernel can assert an impossible de-aliasing.

###### ref-427b73690a76e307

> **Proposition: Hilbert-valued extension** \[prop:vector\] Let $\mathcal H$ be any Hilbert space and replace $A$ by $A\otimes I_{\mathcal H}$, and $b_0^*$ by $b_0^*\otimes I_{\mathcal H}$. Under the condition of [\[thm:sharp\]](TPC-115.md#ref-0ff4d7373a13d90e), $$\|(b_0^*\otimes I_{\mathcal H})X\|_{\mathcal H}^2  \le K_{\rm loc}  \|(A_{\boldsymbol\Omega}\otimes I_{\mathcal H})X\|^2,$$ with the same sharp constant.

> **Proof** Apply the scalar inequality to the scalar products of the $\mathcal H$-valued coefficients against arbitrary unit vectors, or diagonalize $G$ and sum the componentwise inequalities. Sharpness follows by tensoring a scalar extremizer with a unit vector.

# Profile spaces and Christoffel cost

Let $H$ be the finite shift set, let $p_h\ge0$ with $\sum_hp_h=1$, and let $B:\mathbb C^d\to\mathbb C^H$ describe a declared profile space: $$(Bc)(h)=b(h)^*c.$$ Evaluation at $h_0$ is $b(h_0)^*c$.

###### ref-972e2cdf6fafb26d

> **Corollary: Exact profile-space localization** \[cor:profile\] Put $$G_V=B^*\boldsymbol\Omega B,\qquad  \boldsymbol\Omega=\operatorname{diag}(p_h:h\in H).$$ If $b(h_0)\in\operatorname{ran}G_V$, then $$|(Bc)(h_0)|^2  \le K_V(h_0)\sum_{h\in H}p_h|(Bc)(h)|^2,$$ where the sharp constant is $$\boxed{\quad  K_V(h_0)=b(h_0)^*G_V^\dagger b(h_0).  \quad}$$ If $b(h_0)\notin\operatorname{ran}G_V$, no finite constant exists.

> **Proof** Apply [\[thm:sharp\]](TPC-115.md#ref-0ff4d7373a13d90e) with $A=B$ and $b_0=b(h_0)$.

###### ref-48c51cafad6e61a4

> **Proposition: Three exact regimes** \[prop:regimes\] The following constants are sharp.
>
> 1.  For the unrestricted profile space $\mathbb C^H$, $$K_{\mathbb C^H}(h_0)=\frac1{p_{h_0}}$$ when $p_{h_0}>0$; it is infinite when $p_{h_0}=0$.
>
> 2.  For the constant profile space, $K_{\rm const}(h_0)=1$.
>
> 3.  Let $H=\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z$, use uniform weights $1/N$, and let $V$ be the span of any $d$ distinct characters. Then $$K_V(h_0)=d  \qquad\text{for every }h_0.$$

> **Proof** For (i), $B=I$, $G_V=\boldsymbol\Omega$, and the target is $e_{h_0}$. For (ii), $B=\mathbf 1$, so $G_V=\sum_hp_h=1$. For (iii), character orthogonality gives $B^*(N^{-1}I)B=I_d$, while the evaluation vector $b(h_0)$ has $d$ unit-modulus entries. Hence $b(h_0)^*b(h_0)=d$.

> **Remark: The positivity cost is genuinely sharp** Writing $p_h=\omega_h/\sum_t\omega_t$, part (i) gives $$K_{\mathbb C^H}(h_0)=\frac{\sum_t\omega_t}{\omega_{h_0}},$$ exactly the TPC-46 positivity cost. It cannot be improved from an averaged inequality alone. Any improvement must prove that the actual profile lies in a smaller declared space, as in parts (ii) or (iii), or establish another explicit regularity inequality.

# Conditional fixed-shift transfer

###### ref-c37bf23cbac39d62

> **Theorem: What an averaged arithmetic estimate would imply** \[thm:conditional\] Let $F_X(h)\in\mathcal H_X$ be the literal physical shift profile. Assume:
>
> 1.  $F_X$ belongs to a declared Hilbert-valued profile space $V_X\otimes\mathcal H_X$;
>
> 2.  the averaged estimate $$\sum_h p_{X,h}\|F_X(h)\|_{\mathcal H_X}^2\le E_X$$ holds on the same active support and normalization; and
>
> 3.  the target evaluation annihilates the observation kernel.
>
> Then $$\boxed{\qquad  \|F_X(h_0)\|_{\mathcal H_X}^2  \le K_{V_X}(h_0)E_X.  \qquad}$$ If $K_{V_X}(h_0)=X^{\lambda+o(1)}$, localization spends $\lambda$ in an energy ledger and $\lambda/2$ in the corresponding amplitude ledger.

> **Proof** Apply \[[prop:vector](TPC-115.md#ref-427b73690a76e307),[cor:profile](TPC-115.md#ref-972e2cdf6fafb26d)\]. The exponent statement follows by taking square roots when converting squared norms to amplitudes.

> **Remark: Strict endpoint accounting** The localization cost is charged exactly once, after the native Gram and before the prescribed-shift conclusion. If the route uses the amplitude-normalized physical ledger of TPC-MVP1, the accumulated loss, including $\lambda/2$, must remain strictly below $1/400$. An endpoint equality is not a positive reserve.

# Sharp invisibility obstruction

###### ref-43d0b6e7172ee83e

> **Proposition: Indistinguishable profiles** \[prop:invisible\] If $x_1-x_2\in\ker A_{\boldsymbol\Omega}$ but $\ell(x_1)\ne\ell(x_2)$, then no procedure using only the weighted observation $A_{\boldsymbol\Omega}x$ can recover the target on both profiles. In particular, no finite localization inequality exists.

> **Proof** The observations of $x_1$ and $x_2$ coincide, whereas their target values differ. Their difference is the kernel witness in [\[thm:sharp\]](TPC-115.md#ref-0ff4d7373a13d90e).

For example, $A=(1\ \ 1)$ observes only $x_1+x_2$. Evaluation of the first coordinate does not annihilate $\ker A=\operatorname{span}\{(1,-1)\}$, so it is impossible. Evaluation of the observed sum itself has sharp cost $1$.

# Finite exact certificate and verdict

The script `experiments/tpc115_localization_certificate.py` checks with exact rational arithmetic:

1.  unrestricted weighted costs $6,3,2$ for weights $1/6,2/6,3/6$;

2.  constant-profile cost $1$;

3.  Walsh-character subspace costs $d=1,2,4,8$ on eight shifts; and

4.  the invisible and visible functionals for $A=(1\ \ 1)$.

The certificate validates the finite formulas, not an arithmetic profile hypothesis.

| Status           | Required certificate                                                                                                      | Consequence                                                                                             |
|:-----------------|:--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|:--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Go               | The actual profile belongs to $V_X$, the averaged estimate is physical, and $K_{V_X}(h_0)$ leaves strict endpoint reserve | Apply [\[thm:conditional\]](TPC-115.md#ref-c37bf23cbac39d62) and continue to high-frequency reassembly. |
| Stop observation | Target evaluation is nonzero on the actual observation kernel                                                             | No de-aliasing from this average is possible; retain more labels or change the observation.             |
| Open             | Only an averaged bound is known, with no verified profile space                                                           | Pay the full positivity cost or make no fixed-$h_0$ claim.                                              |

#### Established.

give the exact evaluation norm, its Hilbert-valued extension, and a sharp obstruction. These are $\mathrm{L0}$ finite-dimensional theorems. Attaching them to the literal fixed-shift profile is an explicit $\mathrm{L1}$ conditional interface.

#### Not established.

We do not prove that the actual TPC profile lies in a low-dimensional character or regularity space. We prove no bound for its Christoffel cost, no new averaged arithmetic estimate, and no prescribed-shift saving. There is no $\mathrm{L2}$ advance, parity breakthrough, Hardy–Littlewood asymptotic, prime-pair lower bound, or twin-prime theorem. Nothing is specialized to $h_0=2$.
