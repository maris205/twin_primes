## Abstract

TPC-117 gives the exact range test for a physical TPC-18 block matrix, while TPC-119 requires every final scalar contribution to remain connected to its original TPC-15 atom. These are not the same test. Starting from a literal native-leaf vector $z$, let $G$ be the declared leaf-to-physical grouping, $C$ the block-to-leaf synthesis and $B=GC$ the physical block matrix. We compare the canonical residuals $$r_{\rm leaf}=(I-CC^\dagger)z,  \qquad  r_{\rm phys}=(I-BB^\dagger)Gz.$$ They give the two exact decompositions $$z=CC^\dagger z+r_{\rm leaf},  \qquad  Gz=BC^\dagger z+Gr_{\rm leaf}      =BB^\dagger Gz+r_{\rm phys}.$$ Native-leaf cover implies physical cover, but the converse fails sharply when $G$ hides a provenance direction. Separate dual witnesses therefore stop the two cover claims, and the exact Gram crosswalk is $B^*B=C^*G^*GC$. We also give a coefficientwise test for identifying determinant fibers with zero-mode outer fibers; agreement on one physical vector is insufficient. These are $\mathrm{L0}$ identities and $\mathrm{L1}$ interfaces. The actual growing $G,C,z,B$ archive and its residual estimates remain absent, so the physical reassembly audit is $\textsc{not-testable}$. No $\mathrm{L2}$ arithmetic saving, endpoint gain or prime-pair theorem is claimed.

# Why two cover spaces are necessary

TPC-123 replaces the missing global leaf archive by an exact path-expanded object whose columns are original TPC-15 atoms [`\citep{WangTPC15,WangTPC123}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-124.md#cite-b0805195de3a7444); [\[WangTPC123\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-124.md#cite-6ff895d094f015b3). If that archive is complete, its retained terminal records form a vector $$z_X=M_X^{\rm ret}c_X\in\mathbb C^{\mathcal L_X}.$$ TPC-114 then groups residual labels into native physical labels, whereas TPC-117 asks whether an actual physical vector lies in the range of the TPC-18 block synthesis [`\citep{WangTPC114,WangTPC117}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-124.md#cite-95a88dac6b7098cc); [\[WangTPC117\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-124.md#cite-41bb314f911953d0). Grouping is generally noninjective. It follows that two different statements must be audited:

1.  every native leaf is synthesized by the declared blocks; and

2.  the grouped physical vector is synthesized by the grouped blocks.

The first is a provenance statement relevant to H8. The second is a physical range statement relevant to H6. Neither statement estimates the hard packet.

> **Definition: Claim levels** Finite Hilbert-space identities are $\mathrm{L0}$. Identifying their spaces and maps with the literal TPC records is $\mathrm{L1}$. A uniform growing fixed-$h_0$ arithmetic power saving is $\mathrm{L2}$.

# The literal reassembly diagram

Let $\mathcal L_X$, $\mathcal P_X$, and $\mathcal Q_X$ be finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces of native leaves, physical carriers, and block coefficients. The declared maps are $$C_X:\mathcal Q_X\to\mathcal L_X,\qquad  G_X:\mathcal L_X\to\mathcal P_X,\qquad  B_X=G_XC_X:\mathcal Q_X\to\mathcal P_X.$$ Put $z=z_X$, $G=G_X$, $C=C_X$, $B=B_X$, and $$w=Gz.$$ All adjoints and Moore–Penrose inverses refer to the declared physical Hilbert norms, not to a convenient surrogate norm [`\citep{BenIsraelGreville,WangTPC113}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-124.md#cite-9cca6c50d69939f8); [\[WangTPC113\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-124.md#cite-0988df134318b83f).

###### ref-cb95c9d8245776ef

###### ref-bf62ad8f1d61cf3e

> **Definition: Two canonical coefficients and residuals** Define $$\begin{aligned}  q_{\rm leaf}&=C^\dagger z,  &  r_{\rm leaf}&=(I-CC^\dagger)z,  \label{eq:leaf}\\  q_{\rm phys}&=B^\dagger w,  &  r_{\rm phys}&=(I-BB^\dagger)w.  \label{eq:phys}\end{aligned}$$

The coefficient vectors need not agree. The first is least squares before grouping; the second is least squares in the physical norm after grouping. Substituting one for the other without proving an isometry is a normalization change.

# The two-residual theorem

###### ref-c67666191f27d8f7

###### ref-d369c2ea4d4a2fe5

###### ref-32a892129f2ac8ce

###### ref-9bbbff8e04c10162

> **Theorem: Provenance–physical reassembly** \[thm:reassembly\] The following identities hold exactly: $$\begin{aligned}  z&=Cq_{\rm leaf}+r_{\rm leaf},  \label{eq:zdec}\\  w&=Bq_{\rm leaf}+Gr_{\rm leaf},  \label{eq:wleaf}\\  w&=Bq_{\rm phys}+r_{\rm phys}.  \label{eq:wphys}\end{aligned}$$ Moreover, $$r_{\rm leaf}\perp\operatorname{ran}(C),  \qquad  r_{\rm phys}\perp\operatorname{ran}(B).$$ In particular, $$r_{\rm leaf}=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad r_{\rm phys}=0.$$

> **Proof** The matrices $CC^\dagger$ and $BB^\dagger$ are the orthogonal projections onto $\operatorname{ran}(C)$ and $\operatorname{ran}(B)$, respectively. This proves [\[eq:zdec\]](TPC-124.md#ref-d369c2ea4d4a2fe5) and [\[eq:wphys\]](TPC-124.md#ref-9bbbff8e04c10162) together with the orthogonality statements. Applying $G$ to [\[eq:zdec\]](TPC-124.md#ref-d369c2ea4d4a2fe5) and using $GC=B$ proves [\[eq:wleaf\]](TPC-124.md#ref-32a892129f2ac8ce). If $r_{\rm leaf}=0$, then $w=BC^\dagger z\in\operatorname{ran}(B)$, so its orthogonal physical residual vanishes.

> **Remark: No reverse implication** The theorem does not assert $r_{\rm phys}=0\Rightarrow r_{\rm leaf}=0$. A component of $r_{\rm leaf}$ can lie in $\operatorname{ker}(G)$, or its grouped image can fall back into $\operatorname{ran}(B)$. Physical equality alone may therefore erase the missing native route.

###### ref-8389b36dd7e34a47

> **Theorem: Separate dual certificates** \[thm:dual\] The two exact cover conditions are $$\begin{aligned}  r_{\rm leaf}=0  &\iff  \langle y,z\rangle=0  \quad\text{for every }y\in\operatorname{ker}(C^*),\\  r_{\rm phys}=0  &\iff  \langle v,w\rangle=0  \quad\text{for every }v\in\operatorname{ker}(B^*).\end{aligned}$$ Thus one $y\in\operatorname{ker}(C^*)$ with $\langle y,z\rangle\ne0$ stops exact native-leaf cover, and one $v\in\operatorname{ker}(B^*)$ with $\langle v,w\rangle\ne0$ stops exact physical cover.

> **Proof** For any finite-dimensional operator $A$, $\operatorname{ran}(A)=\operatorname{ker}(A^*)^\perp$. Apply this to $C$ and $B$, then use \[[eq:leaf](TPC-124.md#ref-cb95c9d8245776ef),[eq:phys](TPC-124.md#ref-bf62ad8f1d61cf3e)\].

# Scalar evaluation and the native Gram

Let $a\in\mathcal P_X$ be the actual physical evaluator and let $\nu_X$ be the one inherited normalization.

###### ref-22c3046fb1cd27c9

> **Corollary: Exact scalar return** \[cor:scalar\] $$\boxed{  \nu_X\langle a,w\rangle  =  \nu_X\langle B^*a,q_{\rm phys}\rangle  +  \nu_X\langle a,r_{\rm phys}\rangle.}$$ There is also the provenance-resolving form $$\nu_X\langle a,w\rangle  =  \nu_X\langle B^*a,q_{\rm leaf}\rangle  +  \nu_X\langle G^*a,r_{\rm leaf}\rangle.$$

> **Proof** Pair [\[eq:wphys\]](TPC-124.md#ref-9bbbff8e04c10162) and [\[eq:wleaf\]](TPC-124.md#ref-32a892129f2ac8ce) with $a$ and use the adjoint identity.

###### ref-40cd61833db72aad

> **Proposition: Exact native Gram crosswalk** \[prop:gram\] The physical block Gram is $$\boxed{B^*B=C^*G^*GC.}$$ Consequently any determinant, singular value or condition number used after grouping must be computed from the literal $G$ and $C$. Replacing $G^*G$ by the identity requires an additional isometry theorem.

> **Proof** Use $B=GC$ and take adjoints.

This identity attaches the native Gram warning of TPC-114 to the physical range test of TPC-117. It does not say that a large or small condition number is an unavoidable endpoint exponent. TPC-113 and TPC-118 require the actual composite norm and a nonduplicated cost registry [`\citep{WangTPC113,WangTPC118}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-124.md#cite-0988df134318b83f); [\[WangTPC118\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-124.md#cite-fbda8e0b86a9d86d).

# The quotient observability operator

The two residuals are not unrelated. Let $$P_B=BB^\dagger$$ and restrict the operator $$H=(I-P_B)G\big|_{\operatorname{ker}(C^*)}:  \operatorname{ker}(C^*)\longrightarrow\operatorname{ker}(B^*).$$ This is the part of the physical grouping that remains visible after quotienting out the declared block range.

###### ref-cbe71acb3c094050

###### ref-f660657c57d5dc20

> **Theorem: Exact residual transfer** \[thm:residual-transfer\] For the residuals in [\[eq:leaf\]](TPC-124.md#ref-cb95c9d8245776ef)–[\[eq:phys\]](TPC-124.md#ref-bf62ad8f1d61cf3e), $$\boxed{r_{\rm phys}=Hr_{\rm leaf}.}  \label{eq:residual-transfer}$$ Consequently, $$\|r_{\rm phys}\|  \le  \|H\|\,\|r_{\rm leaf}\|  \le  \|G\|\,\|r_{\rm leaf}\|.$$

> **Proof** By [\[eq:wleaf\]](TPC-124.md#ref-32a892129f2ac8ce), $$w=Bq_{\rm leaf}+Gr_{\rm leaf}.$$ Apply $I-P_B$. The first term vanishes because it lies in $\operatorname{ran}(B)$, while the left side becomes $r_{\rm phys}$. Moreover $r_{\rm leaf}\in\operatorname{ker}(C^*)$, so the remaining term is exactly $Hr_{\rm leaf}$. The norm bounds follow from the definition and from $\|I-P_B\|\le1$.

Define the quotient observability constant $$\gamma_X  =  \inf_{\substack{r\in\operatorname{ker}(C^*)\\\|r\|=1}}\|Hr\|,$$ when $\operatorname{ker}(C^*)\ne\{0\}$. In finite dimension, $\gamma_X$ is the minimum modulus of the restricted operator $H$ on its full declared domain. Equivalently, it is the smallest singular value when zeros coming from $\operatorname{ker}(H)$ are included. Thus $\gamma_X>0$ precisely when $H$ is injective. The least *positive* singular value on $\operatorname{ker}(H)^\perp$ is a different quantity and does not give a reverse bound for all native residuals when $\operatorname{ker}(H)\ne\{0\}$. If $\operatorname{ker}(C^*)=\{0\}$, then $C$ is already onto the native-leaf space and $r_{\rm leaf}=0$ for every $z$, so no reverse-transfer constant is needed.

###### ref-a202c37300753f9d

> **Corollary: When physical cover detects provenance cover** \[cor:observable\] If $\operatorname{ker}(C^*)\ne\{0\}$ and $\gamma_X>0$, then $$\gamma_X\|r_{\rm leaf}\|  \le  \|r_{\rm phys}\|  \le  \|H\|\|r_{\rm leaf}\|,$$ and hence $$r_{\rm phys}=0\iff r_{\rm leaf}=0.$$ The same equivalence holds trivially when $\operatorname{ker}(C^*)=\{0\}$. If $\gamma_X=0$, there is a nonzero native residual direction that is physically invisible modulo $\operatorname{ran}(B)$.

> **Proof** When the kernel is zero, $r_{\rm leaf}=0$ and [\[thm:reassembly\]](TPC-124.md#ref-c67666191f27d8f7) applies. Otherwise, the lower bound is the definition of $\gamma_X$, applied to $r_{\rm leaf}$. The upper bound is [\[thm:residual-transfer\]](TPC-124.md#ref-cbe71acb3c094050). In finite dimension, $\gamma_X=0$ means that $H$ has a nonzero kernel vector.

> **Remark: A criterion, not a free endpoint gain** A growing lower bound for $\gamma_X$ could certify that physical residual control transfers back to native provenance control. The present paper neither supplies the actual operator $H_X$ nor proves such a lower bound. Also, $\gamma_X^{-1}$ would be a norm-conversion cost, not an arithmetic saving; it must be placed in the physical ledger only when the actual proof invokes this reverse transfer.

# A sharp provenance-camouflage example

###### ref-33ff941fa463118f

> **Proposition: Physical cover can hide a native residual** \[prop:camouflage\] There exist $G,C,z$ with $r_{\rm leaf}\ne0$ and $r_{\rm phys}=0$.

> **Proof** In standard Euclidean coordinates take $$C=  \begin{pmatrix}  1&0\\0&1\\0&0  \end{pmatrix},  \qquad  z=  \begin{pmatrix}0\\0\\1\end{pmatrix},  \qquad  G=  \begin{pmatrix}  1&0&0\\0&1&0  \end{pmatrix}.$$ Then $r_{\rm leaf}=z\ne0$, while $w=Gz=0$. Hence the physical vector lies in $\operatorname{ran}(GC)$ and $r_{\rm phys}=0$.

Here $H=0$ on $\operatorname{ker}(C^*)=\mathbb Ce_3$, so $\gamma_X=0$. The example is not a claim about the actual TPC archive. It proves that an H6 range certificate cannot be promoted to H8 without either the native-leaf audit or a positive quotient-observability certificate.

# Determinant fibers and zero-mode fibers

TPC-121 groups raw atoms into determinant bins, while TPC-122 orders literal outer fibers for signed-prefix control [`\citep{WangTPC121,WangTPC122}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-124.md#cite-d3e88dbca3ebc492); [\[WangTPC122\]](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-124.md#cite-af7e5d5ca960c85e). These are different declared dictionaries. Denote their finite index sets by $\mathcal D_X$ and $\mathcal Z_X$, respectively, and let $$Q_D:\mathcal L_X\to\mathbb C^{\mathcal D_X},  \qquad  Q_Z:\mathcal L_X\to\mathbb C^{\mathcal Z_X}$$ be their exact leaf-level aggregation matrices. A proposed identification $J:\mathbb C^{\mathcal D_X}\to\mathbb C^{\mathcal Z_X}$ must be proved, not inferred from similar symbols. The scalar determinant energy $D_X$ and scalar distinguished zero mode $Z_X$ retain their earlier meanings and are not index sets.

###### ref-f567f03d67edfbe8

> **Theorem: Coefficientwise fiber-intertwining test** \[thm:fibers\] Let $M:\mathbb C^{\mathcal A_X}\to\mathcal L_X$ be the complete native-leaf matrix. The two fiber descriptions agree on every original packet coefficient vector precisely when $$\boxed{(JQ_D-Q_Z)M=0.}$$ Agreement on one coefficient vector $c_X$, $(JQ_D-Q_Z)Mc_X=0$, is not sufficient for this matrix identity.

> **Proof** The two descriptions agree on every input if and only if the two linear maps $JQ_DM$ and $Q_ZM$ are equal, which is the displayed matrix condition. Equality after applying them to one vector tests only one element of their difference kernel.

> **Remark: No exponent transfer** Even if [\[thm:fibers\]](TPC-124.md#ref-f567f03d67edfbe8) passes, TPC-121 and TPC-122 provide only conditional formulas for $\lambda_D^{\rm cert}$ and $\eta_Z^{\rm cert}$. They do not prove their growing inputs, and their determinant reserve cannot be charged against the physical endpoint ledger.

# Typed invocation and route decisions

> **Definition: Paper-specific verdicts** $\textsc{go-interface}$ means that the indicated exact map and all its quantifiers are present. $\textsc{stop-declared-route}$ means that an exact dual witness, normalization mismatch or provenance defect stops the declared reassembly. It does not stop every possible representation. $\textsc{not-testable}$ means that the actual growing matrix or typed vector required to execute a test is absent.

\@P0.22P0.31Y@ Target & $\textsc{go-interface}$ input & Stop or incomplete outcome\
Native-leaf cover & Complete $z,C$ with $r_{\rm leaf}=0$ & Leaf dual witness gives $\textsc{stop-declared-route}$; missing $z,C$ gives $\textsc{not-testable}$.\
Physical exact cover & Complete $w,B$ with $r_{\rm phys}=0$ & Physical dual witness gives $\textsc{stop-declared-route}$; missing $w,B$ gives $\textsc{not-testable}$.\
Physical residual route & Same normalization and a theorem $\nu_X\langle a,r_{\rm phys}\rangle=o(X)$ & Nonzero residual alone stops exact cover, not the residual route.\
H8 reconnection & TPC-123 path conservation plus native-leaf cover and complete soft bound & Physical cover alone is insufficient.\
H9 endpoint & Actual growing norms and a complete TPC-118 registry & Unknown costs remain unknown, never zero.\

At present, TPC-123 supplies the schema rather than the actual growing leaf vector, and TPC-117 reports that the literal growing $B,w$ archive is unavailable. Hence $$\boxed{  \operatorname{Reassembly}_{124}  =  \textsc{not-testable}\ \text{from current artifacts}.}$$ This is an executable-data verdict, not an impossibility theorem.

#### Explicit exclusions.

This paper proves no complete physical cover for the TPC packet, no H8 conjunction, no frame or localization exponent, no endpoint below $1/400$, no determinant-energy or zero-mode exponent, no signed Möbius cancellation, no parity breakthrough, no Hardy–Littlewood asymptotic, and no prime-pair or twin-prime theorem.
