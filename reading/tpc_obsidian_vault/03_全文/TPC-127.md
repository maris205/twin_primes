## Abstract

Consider positive affine forms $$D(z)=d+sz,\qquad V(z)=u+az,\qquad su-ad=2,$$ on one literal TPC generic interval. On the primitive branches exported in TPC-93, $a,s$ are positive, coprime, and odd. The integer $$n=sV(z)=aD(z)+2=su+as z$$ therefore gives an order-preserving bijection from the affine parameter to one residue class modulo $as$. Complete multiplicativity of the Liouville function gives the exact identity $$\boxed{\;  \mu(D(z))\mu(V(z))  =  \mu^2(D(z))\mu^2(V(z))\lambda(as)  \lambda(n-2)\lambda(n).\;}$$ We insert this identity into the full literal TPC-108 coefficient, retaining its coprimality mask, periodic factor, physical weight, additive phase, interval origin, and outer key. Thus the determinant-two signed problem is exactly a shift-two Liouville correlation on a *growing arithmetic progression*, with two quotient-squarefree masks. The pullback itself has no exponent loss and preserves every prefix. It does not turn the block into an unrestricted correlation, and known fixed-form logarithmic theorems do not supply the required growing-modulus, ordinary-weight, all-prefix estimate. The algebra is $\mathrm{L0}$; its provenance-preserving attachment is $\mathrm{L1}$; no $\mathrm{L2}$ cancellation is proved.

Fix an interval $I\subset\mathbb Z$. Assume throughout that $$D(z)=d+sz>0,\qquad V(z)=u+az>0  \quad(z\in I), \label{eq:positive}$$ where $a,s\in\mathbb N$ and $$su-ad=2. \label{eq:det-two}$$ The resolved nonempty branches of TPC-93 additionally satisfy $$(a,s)=1,\qquad (as,2)=1,\qquad (d,s)=(u,a)=1 \label{eq:primitive}$$ [`\citep{WangTPC93}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-127.md#cite-2d5bd9665d7257b5). In particular, $a$ and $s$ are odd. The positivity convention in [\[eq:positive\]](TPC-127.md#ref-4e21dd7947162045) avoids extending $\lambda$ to signed integers. A branch on which a form changes sign must first be partitioned at its zero; this is an exact finite operation and is not an arithmetic estimate.

For one TPC-108 key $\xi$, the literal inner coefficient is $$\begin{aligned}  A_{\xi,X}(z)  ={}&  \mu(D(z))\mu(V(z))  \mathbf 1_{(B,V(z))=1} \notag\\  &\times  \chi(\tau+Bz)W_X(\tau+Bz), \label{eq:literal-A}\end{aligned}$$ and its generic transform is $$S_{\xi,X}(\alpha)  =  \sum_{z\in I}A_{\xi,X}(z)\mathrm e^{-2\pi i\alpha z}. \label{eq:literal-S}$$ All symbols in [\[eq:literal-A\]](TPC-127.md#ref-88a096bbe46ac23c) are literal: $B,\tau$, the fixed-period factor $\chi$, the physical multiplier $W_X$, and the interval are not replaced by averaged surrogates [`\citep{WangTPC108}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-127.md#cite-493205aca788aded).

# The exact shift-two frame

###### ref-ac5ede31c23a1a3a

###### ref-2812a7497531ffb3

###### ref-50a250fb94c99295

###### ref-4203745537f2185c

> **Theorem: Determinant-two pullback** \[thm:pullback\] Under [\[eq:positive\]](TPC-127.md#ref-4e21dd7947162045)–[\[eq:primitive\]](TPC-127.md#ref-01bd98f14129d0ba), put $$q=as,\qquad n_0=su=ad+2,\qquad n(z)=n_0+qz. \label{eq:n-frame}$$ Then:
>
> 1.  $$n(z)=sV(z)=aD(z)+2;$$
>
> 2.  $z\mapsto n(z)$ is an order-preserving bijection from $I$ onto $$\mathcal N_I  =  \{n_0+qz:z\in I\}  =  \{n\in n(I):n\equiv0\pmod s,\ n\equiv2\pmod a\}; \label{eq:progression}$$
>
> 3.  on this progression, $$D(z)=\frac{n(z)-2}{a},  \qquad  V(z)=\frac{n(z)}s;$$
>
> 4.  for every $z\in I$, $$\boxed{  \mu(D(z))\mu(V(z))  =  \lambda(q)\lambda(n(z)-2)\lambda(n(z))  \mu^2\!\left(\frac{n(z)-2}{a}\right)  \mu^2\!\left(\frac{n(z)}s\right).} \label{eq:mobius-pullback}$$

> **Proof** The determinant equation gives $$sV(z)-aD(z)  =s(u+az)-a(d+sz)=su-ad=2.$$ This proves (i), and [\[eq:n-frame\]](TPC-127.md#ref-2812a7497531ffb3) follows by expansion. Because $q>0$, the map is injective and order preserving. Its image obeys the two displayed congruences. Conversely, since $(a,s)=1$, those congruences select one residue modulo $as$, and the interval restriction selects exactly $n(I)$, proving (ii). Part (iii) is immediate.
>
> For every positive integer $m$, $\mu(m)=\mu^2(m)\lambda(m)$. Complete multiplicativity and $\lambda(t)^2=1$ give $$\begin{aligned}  \lambda(D(z))\lambda(V(z))  &=  \lambda(a)\lambda(s)  \lambda(aD(z))\lambda(sV(z))\\  &=\lambda(as)\lambda(n(z)-2)\lambda(n(z)).\end{aligned}$$ Multiplying by the two squarefree indicators proves [\[eq:mobius-pullback\]](TPC-127.md#ref-4203745537f2185c).

> **Remark: Why $h_0=2$ is used literally** For determinant $h_0$, the same calculation yields $sV=aD+h_0$ and a shift-$h_0$ Liouville pair. The present paper fixes $h_0=2$, and [\[eq:primitive\]](TPC-127.md#ref-01bd98f14129d0ba) then forces the physical progression modulus $as$ to be odd. No average over shifts and no replacement of $2$ by a convenient displacement occurs.

# Arithmetic geometry specific to determinant two

The pullback also gives two exact structural checks that are useful when later decompositions are proposed.

###### ref-2d8037dfbd60f9a1

###### ref-45d423322b72ddc6

> **Proposition: Common-divisor and parity classification** \[prop:gcd-classification\] For every $z\in I$, $$\gcd(D(z),V(z))\mid2.$$ Under $(as,2)=1$, the two affine values have the same parity. Consequently:
>
> 1.  if they are odd, their gcd is $1$;
>
> 2.  if they are even, their gcd is $2$;
>
> 3.  on the simultaneous squarefree support, the even–even case is impossible; hence $D(z),V(z)$ are odd and coprime, and $$\mu(D(z))\mu(V(z))  =\mu(D(z)V(z)). \label{eq:quadratic-compression}$$

> **Proof** Every common divisor divides $sV(z)-aD(z)=2$. Since $a$ and $s$ are odd, reduction of the same identity modulo $2$ gives $V(z)\equiv D(z)\pmod2$, proving the first two cases. If both values are squarefree and odd, they are coprime and multiplicativity of $\mu$ gives [\[eq:quadratic-compression\]](TPC-127.md#ref-45d423322b72ddc6). If both were squarefree and even, write $D=2D'$, $V=2V'$, with $D',V'$ odd. Dividing the determinant identity by $2$ would give $$sV'-aD'=1.$$ The left side is even because $a,s,D',V'$ are odd, a contradiction.

> **Remark: A single sign is not a linear sign** Equation [\[eq:quadratic-compression\]](TPC-127.md#ref-45d423322b72ddc6) compresses the active two-form sign to one Möbius value of a reducible quadratic polynomial. It does not turn it into $\mu(cz+r)$. Therefore a single-linear-form estimate cannot be inserted merely because only one $\mu$ symbol remains.

###### ref-08ebff950f4aaee6

> **Proposition: Ordered sequence isometry** \[prop:isometry\] Let $c_z$ be any complex sequence on $I$, and put $\widetilde c_{n(z)}=c_z$ on $\mathcal N_I$. Then, for every $1\le p<\infty$, $$\sum_{z\in I}|c_z|^p  =  \sum_{n\in\mathcal N_I}|\widetilde c_n|^p,$$ and the terminal maximum and endpoint-anchored variation satisfy $$\begin{aligned}  \max_{T\in I}\left|\sum_{\substack{z\in I\\z\le T}}c_z\right|  &=  \max_{M\in\mathcal N_I}  \left|\sum_{\substack{n\in\mathcal N_I\\n\le M}}  \widetilde c_n\right|,\\  |c_{\max I}|+\sum_{z,z+1\in I}|c_{z+1}-c_z|  &=  |\widetilde c_{\max\mathcal N_I}|  +\sum_{\substack{n,n+q\in\mathcal N_I}}  |\widetilde c_{n+q}-\widetilde c_n|.\end{aligned}$$

> **Proof** The map $z\mapsto n_0+qz$ is an order-preserving bijection, so each identity is a relabeling of the same finite list.

The last proposition shows that the sharp signed-prefix/BV norm of TPC-122 is transported exactly [`\citep{WangTPC122}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-127.md#cite-af7e5d5ca960c85e). Measuring variation between consecutive ambient integers instead of consecutive progression points would change the norm and is not authorized by the pullback.

# Complete coefficient transport

###### ref-5251416ee92c740a

Define, for $n\in\mathcal N_I$, $$\begin{aligned}  z(n)&=\frac{n-n_0}{q},\notag\\  \mathcal Q_{a,s}(n)  &=  \mu^2\!\left(\frac{n-2}{a}\right)  \mu^2\!\left(\frac ns\right),\notag\\  \mathcal W_{\xi,X}(n)  &=  \mathbf 1_{(B,n/s)=1}  \chi(\tau+Bz(n))W_X(\tau+Bz(n)). \label{eq:transported-data}\end{aligned}$$

> **Corollary: Literal block in the shift-two frame** \[cor:literal\] The transform [\[eq:literal-S\]](TPC-127.md#ref-dbc9b1e675ce3488) has the exact representation $$\boxed{  S_{\xi,X}(\alpha)  =  \lambda(q)  \sum_{n\in\mathcal N_I}  \mathcal Q_{a,s}(n)  \lambda(n-2)\lambda(n)  \mathcal W_{\xi,X}(n)  \mathrm e^{-2\pi i\alpha z(n)}.} \label{eq:literal-pullback}$$ Every initial $z$-prefix corresponds to the same initial $n$-prefix, and $$\sum_{z\in I}|A_{\xi,X}(z)|  =  \sum_{n\in\mathcal N_I}  \mathcal Q_{a,s}(n)|\mathcal W_{\xi,X}(n)|. \label{eq:mass-preserved}$$

> **Proof** Insert [\[eq:mobius-pullback\]](TPC-127.md#ref-4203745537f2185c) in [\[eq:literal-A\]](TPC-127.md#ref-88a096bbe46ac23c)–[\[eq:literal-S\]](TPC-127.md#ref-dbc9b1e675ce3488). The mask and all remaining factors become [\[eq:transported-data\]](TPC-127.md#ref-5251416ee92c740a). The map in [\[thm:pullback\]](TPC-127.md#ref-ac5ede31c23a1a3a) is an order-preserving bijection, which proves both the prefix assertion and [\[eq:mass-preserved\]](TPC-127.md#ref-3a8713f91f33dd6e).

> **Remark: The phase is not silently simplified** One may write $$\mathrm e^{-2\pi i\alpha z(n)}  =  \mathrm e^{-2\pi i\alpha(n-n_0)/q}$$ on $\mathcal N_I$. The quotient is integral there. We retain $z(n)$ in [\[eq:literal-pullback\]](TPC-127.md#ref-e66ae57d5e459e03) so that no choice of a representative for $\alpha/q$ changes the stated physical phase.

# What the pullback does and does not prove

###### ref-99d7c2baf50ee13b

> **Proposition: No algebraic exponent loss** \[prop:no-loss\] Let $$V_{\xi,X}=\sum_{z\in I}|A_{\xi,X}(z)|.$$ Any estimate proved for the right side of [\[eq:literal-pullback\]](TPC-127.md#ref-e66ae57d5e459e03) in its displayed progression, masks, weight, phase, and all-prefix quantifiers, $$|S_{\xi,X}(\alpha)|  \le X^{-\eta+o(1)}V_{\xi,X},$$ returns the same exponent $\eta$ to the original block.

> **Proof** The two sums are equal and their absolute comparison masses agree by [\[eq:mass-preserved\]](TPC-127.md#ref-3a8713f91f33dd6e).

###### ref-be053d0699b59cbd

> **Proposition: Growing-progression firewall** \[prop:firewall\] The identity [\[eq:literal-pullback\]](TPC-127.md#ref-e66ae57d5e459e03) does not by itself reduce the required estimate to an unrestricted sum $\sum_{n\le y}\lambda(n-2)\lambda(n)$. A valid returning theorem must retain at least:
>
> 1.  the actual, generally growing modulus $q=as$ and residue $n_0\bmod q$;
>
> 2.  both quotient-squarefree masks $\mathcal Q_{a,s}$;
>
> 3.  the actual interval origin and every required prefix;
>
> 4.  the coprimality and periodic masks in $\mathcal W_{\xi,X}$;
>
> 5.  the physical weight and additive phase; and
>
> 6.  uniformity over the predeclared literal outer family.

> **Proof** Each item occurs explicitly in [\[eq:literal-pullback\]](TPC-127.md#ref-e66ae57d5e459e03). Dropping one changes either the support or the coefficient. Equality to an unrestricted correlation would already fail for the constant coefficient when $q>1$, since only one residue class is sampled.

The logarithmically averaged two-point theorem applies to fixed affine forms with reciprocal weight and its own terminal-window quantifiers [`\citep{Tao2016LogChowla}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-127.md#cite-a15c9a9edf426c07). It is not an ordinary-weight, power-rate theorem uniform in the growing data of [\[prop:firewall\]](TPC-127.md#ref-be053d0699b59cbd). This observation is a type check, not a negative statement about that theorem.

# A finite determinant-two example

Take $$a=3,\qquad s=5,\qquad d=u=1.$$ Then $su-ad=2$, $q=15$, and $$D(z)=1+5z,\qquad V(z)=1+3z,\qquad n(z)=5+15z.$$ The first few values illustrate both exact zeros and surviving signs:

|  $z$|  $n$|  $D$|  $V$|  $\mu(D)\mu(V)$|
|----:|----:|----:|----:|---------------:|
|    0|    5|    1|    1|             $1$|
|    1|   20|    6|    4|             $0$|
|    2|   35|   11|    7|             $1$|
|    3|   50|   16|   10|             $0$|
|    4|   65|   21|   13|            $-1$|

For example, at $z=4$, $$\lambda(15)\lambda(63)\lambda(65)=-1  =\mu(21)\mu(13),$$ and both quotient-squarefree masks equal $1$. At $z=1$, the Liouville product is still a sign, but the quotient-squarefree mask vanishes because $V=4$. This is why that mask cannot be dropped after the shift-two sign has been exposed.

# Stop/go certificate

For a pulled-back block let $N=|I|$ and $q=as$.

#### GO.

Continue through this frame only if a later theorem is stated on the actual progression and controls all literal masks, weights, phases, origins, and prefixes. Because [\[prop:no-loss\]](TPC-127.md#ref-99d7c2baf50ee13b) is exact, a proved power saving then returns without an additional algebraic loss.

#### SHORT STOP.

If $N$ is below the predeclared long-block threshold, the arithmetic-progression reformulation creates no new averaging. The block remains in the short-fiber ledger.

#### UNIFORMITY STOP.

If the modulus, mask, or interval range lies outside a cited theorem’s uniformity, that theorem is not callable. One may not replace $q$ by a fixed modulus or the progression by all integers.

#### ARITHMETIC STOP.

The statement $$\max_{T\in I}  \left|  \sum_{\substack{n\in\mathcal N_I\\z(n)\le T}}  \mathcal Q_{a,s}(n)\lambda(n-2)\lambda(n)  \mathcal W_{\xi,X}(n)\mathrm e^{-2\pi i\alpha z(n)}  \right|  \ll X^{-\eta}V_{\xi,X}$$ with a fixed $\eta>0$ is a new fixed-shift arithmetic input. It is not proved here and must be labelled $\mathrm{L2}$ if established.

TPC-127 gives a canonical arithmetic coordinate for the signed determinant-two block. It is a strict improvement in definition: the remaining sign is now the literal shift-two Liouville product, but on a sparse growing progression and behind quotient-squarefree masks. The next finite task is to expand those masks with their actual congruence and tail costs. No parity breakthrough, prime-pair lower bound, Hardy–Littlewood asymptotic, or twin-prime conclusion is claimed.
