## Abstract

TPC-127 writes a primitive determinant-two Möbius pair as a shift-two Liouville product on a growing progression, multiplied by the quotient-squarefree mask $$\mu^2(D(z))\mu^2(V(z)).$$ We resolve this mask without appealing to a density heuristic. For $$Q_R(m)=\sum_{\substack{k\le R\\k^2\mid m}}\mu(k)$$ the truncated product has the exact CRT expansion $$Q_R(D(z))Q_R(V(z))  =  \sum_{\substack{k,\ell\le R\\  (k,s)=(\ell,a)=(k,\ell)=1}}  \mu(k)\mu(\ell)  \mathbf 1_{z\equiv r_{k,\ell}\ ({\rm mod}\ k^2\ell^2)}.$$ In the TPC-127 variable $n=sV=aD+2$, the same fiber is the unique solution of $$n\equiv2\pmod{ak^2},\qquad n\equiv0\pmod{s\ell^2},$$ and its modulus is $ask^2\ell^2\le asR^4$. If the affine values are at most $Y$ on an interval of length $N$, the total unweighted truncation error is $$\ll \tau_*(Y)\left(\frac NR+\sqrt Y\right), \qquad  \tau_*(Y)=\max_{m\le Y}\tau(m).$$ We also prove the component census $\ll N+R^2$, which replaces a wasteful $R^2N$ triangle bound. These are unconditional finite and elementary estimates. They isolate, but do not prove, the required uniform cancellation on the resulting progression fibers. Thus the paper is $\mathrm{L0}/\mathrm{L1}$, not an $\mathrm{L2}$ parity result.

# Primitive determinant-two setting

###### ref-6001079f3fa969f8

###### ref-01bd98f14129d0ba

Let $$D(z)=d+sz,\qquad V(z)=u+az,\qquad su-ad=2 \label{eq:forms}$$ be positive on an integer interval $I$ of length $N$. We use the primitive relations supplied by the literal TPC-93 export, $$(a,s)=1,\quad (as,2)=1,\quad (d,s)=1,\quad (u,a)=1. \label{eq:primitive}$$ They imply $a,s$ are odd. Put $$q=as,\qquad n(z)=sV(z)=aD(z)+2=su+qz.$$ TPC-127 proves $$\mu(D(z))\mu(V(z))  =  \lambda(q)\lambda(n(z)-2)\lambda(n(z))  \mu^2(D(z))\mu^2(V(z))$$ and transports the literal phase and weight without changing the prefix order [`\citep{WangTPC127}`](../99_%E7%BB%B4%E6%8A%A4/%E5%8F%82%E8%80%83%E6%96%87%E7%8C%AE/TPC-128.md#cite-82838990f4215d0c).

The purpose here is narrower: replace the two squarefree indicators by a finite family of exact congruence fibers and put every truncation cost in a visible ledger.

# Exact square-divisor expansion

###### ref-07b4d06ed0d14296

###### ref-96a0140ad9fad181

For $R\ge1$, define $$Q_R(m)=\sum_{\substack{k\le R\\k^2\mid m}}\mu(k). \label{eq:QR}$$ The full identity $$\mu^2(m)=\sum_{k^2\mid m}\mu(k) \label{eq:squarefree-identity}$$ follows prime by prime.

###### ref-fc5fb787f531399d

###### ref-33374626544dd362

> **Lemma: Compatibility of two square divisors** \[lem:compatibility\] For positive integers $k,\ell$, the simultaneous conditions $$k^2\mid D(z),\qquad \ell^2\mid V(z)$$ have an integer solution $z$ if and only if $$(k,s)=1,\qquad(\ell,a)=1,\qquad(k,\ell)=1. \label{eq:compatibility}$$ When soluble, they select one residue $$z\equiv r_{k,\ell}\pmod{k^2\ell^2}.$$

> **Proof** If a prime $p$ divides both $k$ and $s$, then $p\mid D(z)$ and $p\mid s$, hence $p\mid d$, contradicting $(d,s)=1$. The argument for $(\ell,a)$ is identical. If $p\mid(k,\ell)$, then $p^2$ divides both $D(z)$ and $V(z)$, and consequently divides $$sV(z)-aD(z)=2,$$ which is impossible.
>
> Conversely, if [\[eq:compatibility\]](TPC-128.md#ref-33374626544dd362) holds, the first congruence selects one residue modulo $k^2$, and the second selects one residue modulo $\ell^2$. Since $(k,\ell)=1$, the Chinese remainder theorem combines them uniquely modulo $k^2\ell^2$.

###### ref-67e32fc1fbe30d0d

###### ref-e2002ced7df44de4

###### ref-815430287c4b1204

###### ref-89e49d26aeee574f

> **Theorem: Exact truncated CRT expansion** \[thm:crt-expansion\] For every $z\in I$, $$\boxed{  Q_R(D(z))Q_R(V(z))  =  \sum_{\substack{k,\ell\le R\\  (k,s)=(\ell,a)=(k,\ell)=1}}  \mu(k)\mu(\ell)  \mathbf 1_{z\equiv r_{k,\ell}\ ({\rm mod}\ k^2\ell^2)}.} \label{eq:crt-z}$$ Equivalently, in the $n$-frame each nonempty term is the unique residue class solving $$n\equiv2\pmod{ak^2},\qquad  n\equiv0\pmod{s\ell^2}, \label{eq:crt-n}$$ with modulus $$q_{k,\ell}=ask^2\ell^2\le asR^4. \label{eq:modulus-inflation}$$

> **Proof** Expand the product in [\[eq:QR\]](TPC-128.md#ref-07b4d06ed0d14296) and use [\[lem:compatibility\]](TPC-128.md#ref-fc5fb787f531399d), proving [\[eq:crt-z\]](TPC-128.md#ref-e2002ced7df44de4). Since $aD=n-2$ and $sV=n$, the two square divisibilities are exactly [\[eq:crt-n\]](TPC-128.md#ref-815430287c4b1204). The compatibility conditions and $(a,s)=1$ make the two displayed moduli coprime, so their product is [\[eq:modulus-inflation\]](TPC-128.md#ref-89e49d26aeee574f).

> **Remark: No density substitution** The coefficient $\mu(k)\mu(\ell)$, the compatibility conditions, and the actual CRT residue all remain in [\[eq:crt-z\]](TPC-128.md#ref-e2002ced7df44de4). Replacing them by a squarefree density would not be an identity and would erase the sign needed for reassembly.

# A primitive truncation tail

Let $$Y\ge  \max_{z\in I}\{D(z),V(z),2\},\qquad  \tau_*(Y)=\max_{1\le m\le Y}\tau(m).$$

###### ref-8ea1cd516f6dcb0a

###### ref-03302d572c52af5a

> **Lemma: One-form square-divisor tail** \[lem:one-tail\] For a primitive positive affine form $L(z)=b+cz$ on $I$, with $(b,c)=1$ and $L(z)\le Y$, $$\sum_{z\in I}  \left|\mu^2(L(z))-Q_R(L(z))\right|  \le  \frac NR+\sqrt Y+1. \label{eq:one-tail}$$

> **Proof** By [\[eq:squarefree-identity\]](TPC-128.md#ref-96a0140ad9fad181), $$|\mu^2(L(z))-Q_R(L(z))|  \le  \sum_{\substack{k>R\\k^2\mid L(z)}}1.$$ If $(k,c)>1$, the congruence is insoluble by primitivity. Otherwise it selects at most $N/k^2+1$ integers of $I$. Since $k\le\sqrt Y$, $$\sum_{R<k\le\sqrt Y}\left(\frac N{k^2}+1\right)  \le \frac NR+\sqrt Y+1.$$

###### ref-98a3bae04e08c85b

###### ref-ff7e3482ca36c8fa

> **Theorem: Two-mask truncation bound** \[thm:tail\] For every complex sequence $w_z$ with $\|w\|_\infty=\max_{z\in I}|w_z|$, $$\begin{aligned}  &\left|  \sum_{z\in I}w_z  \left\{  \mu^2(D(z))\mu^2(V(z))  -  Q_R(D(z))Q_R(V(z))  \right\}  \right| \notag\\  &\qquad\le  \|w\|_\infty(1+\tau_*(Y))  \left(\frac NR+\sqrt Y+1\right). \label{eq:two-tail}\end{aligned}$$ In particular the right side is $$\|w\|_\infty  Y^{o(1)}\left(\frac NR+\sqrt Y\right).$$

> **Proof** Write $A=\mu^2(D)$, $B=\mu^2(V)$, $A_R=Q_R(D)$, and $B_R=Q_R(V)$. Then $$AB-A_RB_R=(A-A_R)B+A_R(B-B_R).$$ Here $|B|\le1$, while $$|A_R|\le\sum_{k^2\mid D}1\le\tau(D)\le\tau_*(Y).$$ Apply [\[lem:one-tail\]](TPC-128.md#ref-8ea1cd516f6dcb0a) to $D$ and $V$, multiply by $\|w\|_\infty$, and use the triangle inequality.

> **Remark: The $\sqrt Y$ floor is real for this proof** The $+1$ in the count of each square-divisor progression sums to $O(\sqrt Y)$. Removing that term uniformly in moving slopes and origins would require additional information. This paper does not hide it in $Y^{o(1)}N/R$.

# Component census and exponent ledger

###### ref-4bc241b44c9b1ccb

###### ref-829d8ea9b38b5dc7

> **Proposition: Mass-aware CRT census** \[prop:census\] Let $\mathcal C_R$ be the sum, over compatible $k,\ell\le R$, of the number of $z\in I$ in the corresponding CRT fiber. Then $$\mathcal C_R  \le  N\left(\sum_{k\ge1}\frac1{k^2}\right)^2+R^2  \ll N+R^2. \label{eq:census}$$

> **Proof** One residue modulo $k^2\ell^2$ contributes at most $N/(k^2\ell^2)+1$ points. Sum this upper bound and discard the compatibility restrictions.

The census matters because a uniform estimate relative to each component’s *own* absolute mass costs $N+R^2$, not the wasteful $R^2N$. Physical weights still have to be included in the corresponding weighted census; [\[prop:census\]](TPC-128.md#ref-4bc241b44c9b1ccb) alone does not prove that outer bound.

###### ref-7388b00925f9e5ff

###### ref-6d931359aac5ab27

Suppose, for an exponent audit, that $$N=X^{\nu+o(1)},\quad  Y\le X^{\upsilon+o(1)},\quad  as\le X^{\kappa+o(1)},\quad  R=X^{\rho+o(1)},$$ and put $$R_{\rm eff}=\min\{R,\sqrt Y\},  \qquad  \rho_{\rm eff}=\min\{\rho,\upsilon/2\}. \label{eq:effective-cutoff}$$ Only $k,\ell\le R_{\rm eff}$ can occur on the interval. Every retained correlation theorem must be uniform up to modulus exponent $$\kappa+4\rho_{\rm eff}. \label{eq:uniformity-cost}$$

###### ref-6a45aa3a648218b7

To state a relative tail exponent, the comparison mass must be declared. Let $\mathcal V_X$ be the actual absolute mass of the untruncated literal block and assume $$\|w\|_\infty=X^{o(1)},  \qquad  \mathcal V_X=X^{\omega+o(1)}.$$ If $R<\sqrt Y$, [\[thm:tail\]](TPC-128.md#ref-98a3bae04e08c85b) gives $$\eta_{\rm tail}  =  \min\left\{\omega-\nu+\rho,\,              \omega-\frac{\upsilon}{2}\right\}, \label{eq:tail-exponent}$$ provided the displayed minimum is positive. In the common mass-comparable case $\omega=\nu$, this reduces to $\min\{\rho,\nu-\upsilon/2\}$. If $R\ge\sqrt Y$, then $Q_R(m)=\mu^2(m)$ for every $m\le Y$, so the truncation tail is identically zero rather than being governed by [\[eq:tail-exponent\]](TPC-128.md#ref-6a45aa3a648218b7).

###### ref-76a8e7e86337fae0

###### ref-b34058677d120eba

For each retained CRT component $c$, let $\mathcal V_c$ be its actual absolute mass. Define the *weighted census cost* by the theorem-backed inequality $$\sum_c\mathcal V_c  \le X^{\ell_{\rm cen}+o(1)}\mathcal V_X. \label{eq:weighted-census}$$ If the literal weights are uniformly bounded and $\mathcal V_X=X^{\nu+o(1)}$, the point census gives the crude choice $$\ell_{\rm cen}=(2\rho_{\rm eff}-\nu)_+. \label{eq:census-cost}$$ Without those mass hypotheses, [\[eq:census-cost\]](TPC-128.md#ref-b34058677d120eba) is unavailable and $\ell_{\rm cen}$ must come from the actual weighted archive.

###### ref-6942bbf4ff930828

If each CRT component saves $X^{-\eta_{\rm corr}}$ relative to its own $\mathcal V_c$, the certificate returns $$\boxed{  \eta_{\rm block}^{\rm cert}  =  \begin{cases}  \min\{\eta_{\rm tail},        \eta_{\rm corr}-\ell_{\rm cen}\},        &R<\sqrt Y,\\  \eta_{\rm corr}-\ell_{\rm cen},        &R\ge\sqrt Y.  \end{cases}} \label{eq:block-certificate}$$ The formula is relative to the declared actual absolute mass. It is a loss ledger, not an assertion of the correlation or weighted-census inputs. It is a positive saving only when $\eta_{\rm tail}>0$ and $\eta_{\rm corr}>\ell_{\rm cen}$ in the first regime, or when $\eta_{\rm corr}>\ell_{\rm cen}$ in the exact-cutoff regime.

# Stop/go rules and claim boundary

#### TAIL STOP.

If $\nu\le\upsilon/2$, the elementary uniform tail [\[eq:two-tail\]](TPC-128.md#ref-ff7e3482ca36c8fa) gives no positive relative power in the mass-comparable regime while $R<\sqrt Y$. One must either prove a stronger weighted tail theorem, choose the exact cutoff $R\ge\sqrt Y$, or use a different decomposition. The exact cutoff removes the tail but caps the effective component range at $R_{\rm eff}=\sqrt Y$, with modulus and census costs $\kappa+2\upsilon$ and potentially $(\upsilon-\nu)_+$, respectively.

#### MODULUS STOP.

Choosing $R$ large enough to improve the tail inflates the actual modulus to $asR^4$. A theorem uniform only for fixed modulus is not callable on these fibers.

#### CENSUS STOP.

The bound [\[eq:census\]](TPC-128.md#ref-829d8ea9b38b5dc7) is unweighted. If the literal physical weights, the active absolute mass, or outer keys do not satisfy the mass-comparable hypotheses, the actual bound [\[eq:weighted-census\]](TPC-128.md#ref-76a8e7e86337fae0) must replace [\[eq:census-cost\]](TPC-128.md#ref-b34058677d120eba).

#### ARITHMETIC GO.

Proceed only with a proved estimate for the actual shift-two Liouville correlation on every retained CRT fiber, with the original phase, weight, origin, and prefix quantifiers. Such a theorem would be genuine $\mathrm{L2}$ input.
