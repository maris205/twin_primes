# Rudin–Shapiro witness

Let $P_0=Q_0=1$ and recursively set $$P_{m+1}(z)=P_m(z)+z^{2^m}Q_m(z),\qquad  Q_{m+1}(z)=P_m(z)-z^{2^m}Q_m(z).$$ The coefficients of $P_m$ are signs $\rho_n$, $0\le n<L=2^m$, and for $|z|=1$, $$|P_m(z)|^2+|Q_m(z)|^2=2L.$$ Consequently every additive Fourier sum of $f(n)=\rho_n$ is at most $\sqrt{2L}$.

> **Theorem: Sharp black-box failure** Fix any $\alpha_\star$. Put $g(n)=\rho_n \mathrm{e}(\alpha_\star n)$. Both $f$ and $g$ have uniform $O(\sqrt L)$ Fourier bounds, but $$\sum_{n<L}f(n)g(n)\mathrm{e}(-\alpha_\star n)=L.$$ Therefore factorwise uniform Fourier bounds, used as black-box premises, do not imply cancellation of the product at a prescribed atom.

> **Proof** Modulation translates the Fourier variable, so $g$ inherits the same supremum bound. Since $\rho_n^2=1$, every term in the displayed product sum equals one.
