AI-assisted work on a long-standing conjecture requires more than plausible proof fragments: it needs a persistent record of the mathematical objects, source dependencies, and unresolved obligations to which those fragments belong. We present a source-traceable, file-based framework designed to support human-supervised AI assistance in research on the twin prime conjecture. Organized by a seven-island map, it connects an existing manuscript archive and linked research wiki to an explicit task, review, and handoff protocol. The framework separates exact identities, proved estimates, method obstructions, conditional transfers, and finite diagnostics. This organization is intended to support source retrieval and bounded research tasks without promoting local or model evidence into a global arithmetic conclusion. A detailed mathematical case study retains the actual fixed-shift operator and develops its exact decomposition, coefficient-norm and growing-rank obstructions, full-range Kloosterman representation, and a collective boundary bound. A parallel moving-target formulation distinguishes Haar-almost-everywhere recurrence from the unresolved conclusion at the arithmetic seed $0$. These cases specify two different open bridges and the conditions that a proposed continuation must preserve. We also set out a prospective agenda for coefficient-sensitive analysis, distinguished-seed arguments, selective formalization, and controlled evaluation of AI research workflows. The contribution is a concrete research organization and mathematical interface framework, not an empirically validated autonomous prover. This paper does not prove the twin-prime conjecture.
