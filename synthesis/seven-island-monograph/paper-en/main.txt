       A Seven-Island Framework for Twin-Prime Research:
            Exact Structures, Analytic Obstructions,
                   and Two Bridging Problems


                                    12 September 2026
                                 English synthesis draft v0.1


                                             Abstract
        We organize a collection of fixed-shift prime-pair investigations into seven modules:
    arithmetic foundations, analytic cancellation, fixed atoms, actual-occurrence structures,
    nonautonomous dynamics, the twin-prime endpoint, and auxiliary geometric lifts. The
    purpose is to distinguish established structures from transfers that still require arithmetic
    input, while keeping the objects, quantifiers, and normalizations consistent. The analytic
    core is an arithmetic operator retaining unit masks, a smooth kernel, and diagonal deletion.
    We restate and prove its exact positive-form decomposition, norm and row-energy bounds,
    and the restrictions that the actual coefficient norms impose on coefficient-blind methods.
    At interval scale x and prime-modulus scale Q = x1/3 , a low-frequency compression yields
    (1/4 + o(1))Q2 negative directions. An exact Kloosterman representation explains why
    one-sided short support does not automatically supply a two-sided critical-range estimate.
    For an explicitly constructed smooth partition, the collective boundary contribution is
    bounded by xQ2 x−11/64+o(1) . On the dynamical side, the Chinese remainder theorem
    and Abel summation give a linear variance bound and Haar-almost-everywhere infinite
    recurrence for the actual moving sieve events, while leaving the return to the distinguished
    arithmetic seed 0 unproved. These results establish neither the required signed interior
    cancellation nor the twin-prime conjecture. They provide a source-traceable synthesis of
    structures and obstructions that separates two unresolved bridging problems from the
    available results.

Keywords: twin primes; Möbius correlations; reduced residue classes; Gram operators;
Kloosterman sums; distinguished seeds.

1     Introduction: a common problem behind local results
The twin-prime problem asks for infinitely many prime pairs at the fixed displacement 2.
Producing an expression that resembles a correlation sum, or a finite matrix with a small
norm, is not enough. If a method transfers the original counting problem to another space, its
coefficients, support, quantifiers, and normalization must travel with it. Omitting any of these
can produce a correct statement that does not apply to the original problem.
    The research archive contains successive local investigations numbered TPC1–TPC418,
together with prototypes, source-unavailable markers, and later unnumbered work. Its devel-
opment was problem-driven: an obstruction motivated a different representation, and a source
audit sometimes required the actual object to be identified again. A chronological condensation
would preserve this history without necessarily preserving the mathematical dependencies.
We instead use Figure 1 as a shared coordinate system and reconstruct a readable argument,
leaving material outside that argument in a traceable companion index.

1.1    One framework, two substantive gaps
The analytic route seeks to pass from local correlation structure to signed collective cancellation
with the complete coefficients. One target considered here has natural scale xQ2 . To be an
effective input to the specified endpoint interface, it must exhibit a power saving strictly
exceeding 1/400. Exact identities preserve the object but do not themselves supply that saving.
Islands 3 and 4 concern the identification of local atoms and actual occurrences; Island 2
concerns the required estimates.
    The dynamical gap is different. Adequate event mass and covariance control in an encoding
system do not show that the particular orbit selected by arithmetic belongs to the good set. A
statement about almost every initial point need not hold at a prescribed one. Calling that
point “typical” cannot remove the quantifier gap. A geometric lift that merely changes the
representation cannot supply the missing input either.

1.2    Results retained in this synthesis
The analytic exposition develops four groups of statements that can be checked separately:

    1. An exact decomposition and row-energy bounds for the actual operator, under specified
       fixed-shift coefficients, unit masks, and kernel conventions, without substituting a model
       matrix.

    2. A constant-vector norm lower bound, lower bounds for the actual coefficient norms,
       and a growing-dimensional obstruction to low-rank repairs. These restrict particular
       bounding methods, not the size of the actual mixed scalar.

    3. An exact full-range Kloosterman representation and the norm of a one-short/one-complete
       array interface, separating algebraic representation, support compression, and signed
       reassembly.

    4. An explicit partition with uniform derivative control and a paid collective boundary
       bound in the original mixed scalar, leaving the signed interior estimate open.

The dynamical section additionally gives the O(N ) variance and Haar-almost-everywhere
infinite-recurrence proof for the actual moving sieve events, separately from the unproved
assertion at seed 0. These are unified statements and reorganized proofs of archived results;
no first-discovery or optimality claim is made. The appendix distinguishes the evidentiary

1.3   Relation to existing mathematical inputs
Our prime-counting input is the classical prime number theorem and its partial-summation con-
sequences [12]. The second-moment estimate for the hybrid coefficient uses the unconditional
Mertens product baseline, stated at the beginning of [11]; none of that paper’s conditional
conclusions on bias distributions is used. Frequency separation, positive-semidefinite compres-
sion, finite Fourier orthogonality, and spectral min–max arguments are standard tools in the
structural proofs, not new prime-pair theorems.
    The critical-interval bilinear estimate of Blomer–Pascadi [10] supplies an external bench-
mark: with the appropriate lengths and coprimality conditions, a Kloosterman bilinear form
admits a local power saving. We use only the square-root-length specialization of their The-
orem 1.1 and examine whether the actual representation satisfies its input conditions. The
existence of that theorem is not an estimate for the complete arithmetic expression.
    This article is not an exhaustive literature survey, and the number of internal documents
is not a substitute for comparison with external work. It is a mathematical synthesis draft for
a delimited research program. Submission would still require contribution positioning, a fuller
bibliography, verification of all selected historical dependencies, and independent mathematical
scrutiny of the complete argument.

1.4   Reading guide
Section 2 fixes the endpoint and the transfer requirements. Section 3 treats the fixed atom and

separates metric recurrence, the distinguished seed, and geometric attachment. Section 9

2.1   The endpoint and auxiliary quantities

discovery of more pairs in a finite interval. A customary weighted auxiliary quantity is

    To justify the implication, one must remove proper prime powers. There are O( y log y)
pairs in which at least one coordinate is pk with k ≥ 2, and each weight is O(log2 y). Their
total contribution is o(y). Finitely many genuine prime pairs cannot account for a positive
linear main term. Even for a standard auxiliary quantity, its connection to the endpoint should
therefore be stated rather than inferred from its name.

2.4   What every transfer must retain
To compare expressions from different stages, we record a source specification as

                     O = (coefficients, h0 , support and order, parameter range,
                           quantifiers, normalization, loss budget).

This is bookkeeping against misapplication, not a new mathematical object. Similar notation
does not imply equal specifications. An arbitrarily large CRT origin is not an origin in the
prescribed physical interval; a prefix of a full prime shell is not an arbitrary prime selection;
recomputing local row energy is not compressing the original normalized matrix.
    We distinguish four evidence types: finite identities verified by expansion; uniform proposi-
tions with complete hypotheses and proofs; conditional consequences retaining their unproved
premises; and finite numerical diagnostics. Diagnostics do not carry infinite quantifiers, and a
successful program check does not make a conditional implication unconditional. The results
below should be read in this hierarchy, not by their order of appearance in the archive.

8.4   The role and limits of Island 7
A useful geometric lift must at least supply a time-preserving semiconjugacy, event pullback,

carrier. Island 7 is therefore an auxiliary representation route; a geometric picture cannot
replace the distinguished-orbit hitting requirement on Island 5.

9.3   What the synthesis establishes
The seven islands distinguish research objects and transfer tasks; they do not turn a sequence of
historical paper numbers into a closed proof chain. Basic arithmetic supplies local consistency.
The fixed atom and occurrence layer fix the coefficients, support, and prefixes. The analytic
layer supplies actual operator structure, growing-dimensional obstructions, and a boundary
contribution that can be paid for. The dynamical layer supplies metric hitting for the
actual moving targets and exact sieve geometry. These results have distinct mathematical
content: they should neither be merged into a proof of the twin-prime conjecture nor dismissed
indiscriminately as toy models.
    This manuscript is a research synthesis of existing results, not a final paper with completed
novelty assessment and submission positioning. A future version for external readers should
further compress historical interfaces, expand the systematic external related-work discussion,
and specify authorship and a target journal. The essential mathematical change cannot come
from editorial work: the analytic bridge still needs signed estimates sensitive to the actual
coefficients, and the dynamical bridge still needs a return to the distinguished arithmetic seed.
This paper does not prove the twin-prime conjecture.

The complete Kloosterman matrix K = (S(m, n; q))1≤m,n<q satisfies the directly verifiable
identity
                               KK∗ = q 2 I − (q + 1)J,
where J is the all-ones matrix. To verify it, expand the two Kloosterman sums. Summation
over n gives q1r=s − 1. The first contribution is q r̸=0 e((m − m′ )r/q) = q(q1m=m′ − 1), and
                                                   P

the second is 1, giving the stated matrix. Every row sums to 1. Thus any vector a with zero
coordinate sum satisfies ∥K∗ a∥2 = q∥a∥2 . Choosing a = em1 − em2 supported on two distinct
indices shows that scale q is still attained when one side has only two coordinates while the
other is complete and centered. One-sided short support therefore cannot replace the two-sided
critical-array hypotheses.

extremum. It shows that a universal bound knowing only support and centering cannot be
stronger. Likewise, finite matrix checks test only the chosen scales. A model with a common
growing-parameter theorem is stronger than a finite check, but still needs attachment of actual
coefficients, a fixed starting point, parameter ranges, and residuals before it can address the
original arithmetic problem. We do not reissue historical finite certificates as asymptotic
theorems or add synthetic experiments merely to increase the manuscript’s length.


B     Sources, deduplication, and the scope of verification
This paper selects core original sources from a standalone repository containing 420 TPC
directories and rewrites them by mathematical dependency rather than paper number. Every
proposition proved in the body remains subject to its stated hypotheses; classical inputs such
as the Chinese remainder theorem, the prime number theorem, and Mertens’ theorem are not
new discoveries here. The number of historical manuscripts is not the number of independent
contributions.
    The accompanying SOURCE_CATALOG.md/json enumerates all 420 directories, retaining
original names, primary-source hashes, and candidate island labels obtained by text matching.
These labels aid retrieval; they do not represent a manual semantic audit of all 420 full texts.
The CORE_SOURCE_MATRIX.md/json records the core files actually used here, their source line
ranges, SHA-256 hashes, and claim boundaries. Source proofs, historical interfaces, current
handoff state, and independent reviews are listed separately; status files do not replace proofs.
    The early structural interfaces in Section 3 come from original TeX sources. The main
operator results in Sections 4–7 come from the unnumbered research cycle of 9 September
2026. The CRT–Abel proof in Section 8 comes from the original moving-target proof, and
the replication-and-deletion structure comes from the exact physical-intertwining document.
Original papers, research manuscripts, handoff files, and maps remain unchanged. The new
synthesis is stored separately and adds no TPC paper number.
    The verifiable core consists of complete definitions of shared objects, step-by-step proofs,
and source hashes. Successful compilation, resolved citations, embedded fonts, and page-by-
page visual inspection verify presentation, not theorems. Independent reviews apply only to
their explicitly read scope; they certify neither the entire 420-directory collection nor novelty.
    This full English translation preserves the Chinese edition’s proofs and claim boundaries and
reproduces the supplied English map unchanged. The Chinese sources remain the provenance
baseline; no research route is reopened.
